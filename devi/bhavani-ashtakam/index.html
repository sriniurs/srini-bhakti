DEVĪ · ŚAṄKARA’S HYMN OF SURRENDER

Bhavānī Aṣṭakam

You Alone Are My Refuge · explained and illustrated

You alone are my refuge

HOW THIS WORKS: Bhavānī Aṣṭakam, eight verses of total surrender attributed to Ādi Śaṅkara, verse by verse: no father, no mother, no knowledge, no rituals, no virtue, only the refrain, “gatis tvaṃ gatis tvaṃ tvam ekā bhavāni”: you, you alone, Bhavānī, are my refuge.

WHERE WE ARE

Some hymns praise God’s glory. This one does the opposite: it lists everything the singer does not have. No family to rely on, no learning, no rituals, no virtue, no strength. And after each list comes the same line, sung softer each time: gatis tvaṃ gatis tvaṃ tvam ekā bhavāni, “you are my refuge, you are my refuge, you alone, Bhavānī.”

The Bhavānī Aṣṭakam is traditionally attributed to Ādi Śaṅkara. It is a favourite in homes because anyone can say it truthfully. This guide reads all eight verses.

WHO IS BHAVĀNĪ?

The Mother as refuge

The name. Bhavānī is “the consort of Bhava”, one of Śiva’s names: Pārvatī as the Mother of the universe and giver of life (bhava also means existence).

Tuljā Bhavānī. At Tuljāpur in Maharashtra she is worshipped as a fierce Durgā; she is the family goddess of many in Maharashtra and North Karnataka, and of the Marāṭhā ruler Śivājī by tradition, who is said to have received his sword Bhavānī from her.

One Mother. For the devotee she is the same Goddess as Pārvatī and Durgā, Cāmuṇḍeśvarī or Lalitā.

Why a mother? A child needs no qualifications to be cared for. The hymn approaches God that way.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 1

“You alone are my refuge”
na tāto na mātā na bandhur na dātāna putro na putrī na bhṛtyo na bhartāna jāyā na vidyā na vṛttir mamaivagatis tvaṃ gatis tvaṃ tvam ekā bhavāni
No father, no mother, no relative, no giver; no son, no daughter, no servant, no master; no wife, no learning, no livelihood is truly mine. You are my refuge, you are my refuge, you alone, Bhavānī.
WordMeaning
tātaḥfather
bandhuḥrelative
dātāgiver, patron
bhṛtyaḥservant
bhartāmaster, husband
vṛttiḥlivelihood
gatiḥrefuge, the way, the goal
tvam ekāyou alone
bhavāniO Bhavānī

Not ingratitude. Family is not denied; none of them can be the final refuge.

Bhavānī Aṣṭakam, attributed to Ādi Śaṅkara.
You alone.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 2

“You alone are my refuge”
bhavābdhāv apāre mahāduḥkhabhīruḥpapāta prakāmī pralobhī pramattaḥkusaṃsārapāśaprabaddhaḥ sadāhaṃgatis tvaṃ gatis tvaṃ tvam ekā bhavāni
In the shoreless ocean of becoming, terrified of great sorrow, I have fallen, full of desire, full of greed, careless; I am always bound by the noose of this wretched worldly life. You alone are my refuge, Bhavānī.
WordMeaning
bhava-abdhauin the ocean of becoming
apāreshoreless
prakāmīfull of desires
pralobhīgreedy
pramattaḥheedless
pāśanoose

Saṃsāra as an ocean. The same picture as many hymns: a sea with no far shore, and the soul drowning in its own desires.

Out of the ocean.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 3

“You alone are my refuge”
na jānāmi dānaṃ na ca dhyānayogaṃna jānāmi tantraṃ na ca stotramantramna jānāmi pūjāṃ na ca nyāsayogaṃgatis tvaṃ gatis tvaṃ tvam ekā bhavāni
I know nothing of charity, nor of meditation; I know no ritual system, no hymn or mantra; I know no pūjā and no nyāsa. You alone are my refuge, Bhavānī.
WordMeaning
dānamcharity
dhyāna-yogammeditation
tantramritual system
stotra-mantramhymns and mantras
nyāsaplacing mantras on the body

Said by Śaṅkara! The master of every ritual and text speaks as one who knows none: humility as the heart of devotion.

No technique needed.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 4

“You alone are my refuge”
na jānāmi puṇyaṃ na jānāmi tīrthaṃna jānāmi muktiṃ layaṃ vā kadācitna jānāmi bhaktiṃ vrataṃ vāpi mātargatis tvaṃ gatis tvaṃ tvam ekā bhavāni
I do not know merit, I do not know pilgrimage, I have never known liberation or absorption; I do not know devotion, nor vows, O Mother. You alone are my refuge, Bhavānī.
WordMeaning
puṇyammerit
tīrthampilgrimage place
muktimliberation
layamabsorption
vratamreligious vow

Even devotion. “I don’t even know bhakti” is the most devoted line in the hymn.

Even without bhakti.

Verse 5

“You alone are my refuge”
kukarmī kusaṅgī kubuddhiḥ kudāsaḥkulācārahīnaḥ kadācāralīnaḥkudṛṣṭiḥ kuvākyaprabandhaḥ sadāhaṃgatis tvaṃ gatis tvaṃ tvam ekā bhavāni
I do wrong, keep bad company, think badly, serve badly; I have abandoned my family’s ways and sunk into bad habits; my sight is wrong and my speech is harsh, always. You alone are my refuge, Bhavānī.
WordMeaning
ku-bad, wretched (prefix)
kusaṅgīin bad company
kubuddhiḥof bad thinking
kula-ācārafamily custom
kuvākyaharsh words

Honest confession. Nine “ku-” words pile up. Admitting faults openly is the first step out of them.

Come as you are.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 6

“You alone are my refuge”
prajeśaṃ rameśaṃ maheśaṃ sureśaṃdineśaṃ niśītheśvaraṃ vā kadācitna jānāmi cānyat sadāhaṃ śaraṇyegatis tvaṃ gatis tvaṃ tvam ekā bhavāni
Brahmā the lord of creatures, Viṣṇu the lord of Ramā, Śiva the great lord, Indra the lord of gods, the Sun lord of day, the Moon lord of night: I have never known any other, O refuge. You alone are my refuge, Bhavānī.
WordMeaning
prajā-īśamBrahmā
ramā-īśamViṣṇu, lord of Lakṣmī
mahā-īśamŚiva
sura-īśamIndra
dina-īśamthe Sun
niśītha-īśvaramthe Moon

Not rejection. This is the language of single-minded love (ananya bhakti), not a judgement on other gods. Śaṅkara wrote hymns to all of them.

One to turn to.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 7

“You alone are my refuge”
vivāde viṣāde pramāde pravāsejale cānale parvate śatrumadhyearaṇye śaraṇye sadā māṃ prapāhigatis tvaṃ gatis tvaṃ tvam ekā bhavāni
In quarrel, in grief, in carelessness, far from home; in water, in fire, on a mountain, among enemies, in the forest: O refuge, always protect me. You alone are my refuge, Bhavānī.
WordMeaning
vivādein a quarrel
viṣādein despair
pravāseabroad, travelling
analein fire
śatru-madhyeamong enemies
prapāhiprotect

A traveller’s prayer. Many recite this verse alone before journeys or in danger.

Protect me everywhere.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 8

“You alone are my refuge”
anātho daridro jarārogayuktomahākṣīṇadīnaḥ sadā jāḍyavaktraḥvipattau praviṣṭaḥ pranaṣṭaḥ sadāhaṃgatis tvaṃ gatis tvaṃ tvam ekā bhavāni
Orphaned, poor, struck by old age and illness, utterly weak and wretched, always dull of speech, fallen into misfortune, ruined, always: you are my refuge, you are my refuge, you alone, Bhavānī.
WordMeaning
anāthaḥwithout a protector
daridraḥpoor
jarā-rogaold age and disease
jāḍya-vaktraḥslow of speech
vipattauin calamity

The last word. At the lowest point, with nothing left, the singer is exactly where the Mother’s grace begins. (Interpretation.)

The Mother lifts us.

WHAT IT TEACHES

The power of surrender

Śaraṇāgati. Surrender means stopping the effort to save oneself and resting in God. The Gītā ends on the same note (18.66).

Honesty. The hymn never pretends. It names weakness and fault plainly; that honesty is itself freeing.

A hymn for hard days. Many people recite it in illness, grief or failure, when other hymns feel too grand.

Not passivity. Surrender is an inner attitude; traditions still expect us to act and do our duty. (Interpretation.)
