SATĪ · PĀRVATĪ · DURGĀ · KĀLĪ · ONE MOTHER

Pārvatī and Durgā

The Mountain’s Daughter and the Invincible Mother · explained and illustrated

The mountain’s daughter, the invincible

HOW THIS WORKS: Who the Devī is in her two best-loved faces: Pārvatī, the mountain’s daughter, and Durgā, the invincible. Satī, the penance, the battle with Mahiṣa, Kālī, her forms, worship and temples.

WHERE WE ARE

She is the gentle wife on Kailāsa with Gaṇeśa on her knee, and she is the ten-armed warrior on a lion. Hindus see in Pārvatī and Durgā two faces of one Devī, the Mother of the universe.

This guide follows her from Satī’s sacrifice to Pārvatī’s penance and marriage, then to Durgā’s great battle and Kālī’s fierce dance, and closes with her many forms, her worship and her seats across India.

The battles in full are in our Devī Māhātmya · 19 parts series; the nine nights in Navarātri · 16 articles.

WHO IS THE DEVĪ?

Śakti, the power of the world

Śakti. For Hindus the Devī is the power (śakti) by which God creates, sustains and dissolves. Without her, says a famous verse, Śiva cannot even stir.

One and many. Pārvatī, Durgā, Kālī, Lakṣmī, Sarasvatī: Śākta traditions see them all as forms of one Mahādevī.

Pārvatī. The name means “daughter of the mountain”: she is the child of Himavān.

Durgā. Usually explained as “hard to approach, invincible”: the fortress no evil can take.

ZAPPY: Without her Śiva can’t move?

PROF. BOLT: Śiva without Śakti is śava, a corpse, the verse says, Zappy.

She is power itself.

STORY 1 · SATĪ

Dakṣa’s sacrifice

The story

From the Purāṇas.

Satī, daughter of Dakṣa, married Śiva against her father’s wishes.
Dakṣa held a great sacrifice and invited everyone except Śiva.
Satī went anyway; Dakṣa insulted her husband before all. Unable to bear it, she gave up her body in the sacred fire.
Śiva’s fury destroyed the sacrifice; then he wandered the world carrying her body in grief.
Where parts of her body fell, the Śakti Pīṭhas arose; read our 18 Śakti Pīṭhas · 20 articles series.

ZAPPY: Her body became temples?

PROF. BOLT: Her love became places of power, Zappy.

Love that would not bow.

STORY 2 · PĀRVATĪ’S PENANCE

Aparṇā, who ate not even a leaf

The story

From the Purāṇas and Kālidāsa’s Kumārasambhava.

Satī was born again as Pārvatī, daughter of Himavān and Menā.
From childhood she set her heart on Śiva, but he was lost in meditation; even Kāma’s arrow failed and was burnt.
Pārvatī went into the forest for fierce penance, at last eating not even a leaf: so she is Aparṇā.
Śiva came as an old ascetic and mocked Śiva to test her. She defended him and turned to leave.
Śiva revealed himself and asked for her hand. Their wedding is celebrated across India.

ZAPPY: She won Śiva by penance?

PROF. BOLT: She matched the greatest yogī at his own game, Zappy.

Won by penance.

Pārvatī as wife and mother

STORY 3 · DURGĀ IS BORN

The light of all the gods

The story

From the Devī Māhātmya, chapter 2.

The buffalo-demon Mahiṣa defeated the gods and took heaven; a boon made him safe from men and gods.
The gods went to Viṣṇu and Śiva. In their anger a great light poured out of every god.
The lights merged into one radiant woman: the Devī.
Each god gave her his weapon: Śiva the trident, Viṣṇu the discus, Varuṇa the conch, Indra the thunderbolt; Himavān gave the lion.
She laughed a great laugh, and the worlds shook.

ZAPPY: Everyone gave her their best?

PROF. BOLT: And together it became one power, Zappy.

All powers in one.

STORY 4 · MAHIṢĀSURA

The buffalo slain

The story

From the Devī Māhātmya, chapters 2–4.

The Devī rode her lion into Mahiṣa’s armies and destroyed his generals.
Mahiṣa himself charged, changing from buffalo to lion to man with sword to elephant, and back to buffalo.
She drank the divine drink, laughed, and leapt on him.
As he half-emerged from the buffalo’s mouth, she struck him down: she is Mahiṣāsuramardinī.
The gods sang her praise; read our Mahiṣāsuramardinī Stotram.

ZAPPY: He kept changing shape!

PROF. BOLT: Like ego, Zappy. She sees through every disguise.

She sees through disguise.

KĀLĪ AND CĀMUṆḌĀ

The fierce mothers

Kālī. In the Devī Māhātmya she springs from the Devī’s frowning brow to destroy demons; she drinks the blood of Raktabīja so no new demons can rise.

Cāmuṇḍā. She kills the generals Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa and takes their names; read our Cāmuṇḍeśvarī guide.

Fierce for us. Devotees call Kālī “Mother”: her fierceness is aimed at what harms her children.

More. Kālī heads the Daśa Mahāvidyā · 10 guides + overview.

ZAPPY: She’s scary!

PROF. BOLT: To demons, Zappy. To devotees she is Mā.

Fierce for us.

HOW SHE IS WORSHIPPED

Navarātri, Durgā Pūjā and Gaurī

Navarātri. Nine nights of the Devī, ending in Vijayadaśamī; read our Navarātri series · 16 articles.

Durgā Pūjā. In Bengal, Odisha and Assam, great clay images of Durgā with her children are worshipped and immersed.

Mysuru Dasarā. Cāmuṇḍeśvarī on the golden howdah; read our Mysuru Dasarā guide.

Gaurī. Women keep Gaurī vratas for the family’s welfare; in Karnataka Gaurī comes home the day before Gaṇeśa Caturthī.

The text. The Devī Māhātmya (Durgā Saptaśatī) is recited in her worship.

ZAPPY: Gaurī comes before Gaṇeśa?

PROF. BOLT: Mother first, then son, Zappy.

Nine nights of the Mother.

WHAT THE DEVĪ TEACHES

Gentleness and strength

Strength is feminine too. Durgā is made when all the gods’ power becomes one woman. (Interpretation.)

Persistence. Pārvatī wins by fierce effort, not luck.

Gentle and fierce. Love protects; it can be terrible when its children are in danger.

The inner battle. Many read Mahiṣa as ignorance and ego, which change shape until truth strikes. (Interpretation.)

ZAPPY: So the battle is inside us?

PROF. BOLT: That’s one reading, Zappy. And she fights beside us.
