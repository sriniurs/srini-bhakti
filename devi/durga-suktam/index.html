Durgā Sūktam

Across Every Difficulty · explained and illustrated

Across every difficulty

HOW THIS WORKS: The Durgā Sūktam, a short Vedic hymn from the Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad, verse by verse: Agni as the ferryman who carries us over troubles “as a boat crosses a river”, Durgā as the flaming refuge, and the closing Durgā Gāyatrī.

WHERE WE ARE

Many South Indian families chant a short Vedic hymn before travel, before an operation, in Navarātri, or whenever life feels like a river too wide to cross. It is the Durgā Sūktam: seven verses, most of them prayers to Agni, the sacred fire, asking to be carried “across all difficulties”.

The key word is durga: as an adjective, “hard to pass”; as a noun, a difficulty or a fortress; and as a name, the Goddess who takes us across. This guide reads the hymn verse by verse.

THE WORD “DURGA”

A fortress, a difficulty, a goddess

Hard to go. Dur-ga literally means “hard to go through”. A durga is a fortress (hard to enter) or any difficulty (hard to cross).

Durgāṇi. The hymn’s refrain prays to be carried ati durgāṇi viśvā, “beyond all difficulties”. The word is plural and general.

The Goddess. In verse 2, Durgā appears by name as a fiery goddess who is the refuge. Later tradition reads the whole hymn as hers: she is the power in the fire that carries us over.

One idea. She who is hard to reach is also the one who takes us past what is hard. See Pārvatī and Durgā.

ZAPPY: So Durgā means “the one who gets you through”?

PROF. BOLT: That’s a fair reading, Zappy.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 1

Ṛgveda 1.99.1
jātavedase sunavāma somamarātīyato nidahāti vedaḥsa naḥ parṣad ati durgāṇi viśvānāveva sindhuṃ duritā’tyagniḥ
Let us press out soma for Jātavedas, the knower of all beings; may he burn up the wealth of those who are hostile. May Agni carry us across all difficulties, across all wrongs, as a boat across a river.
WordMeaning
jātavedaseto Agni, “knower of all that is born”
sunavāmalet us press (soma)
arātīyataḥof the hostile
parṣatmay he carry across
nāvā ivalike a boat
sindhumriver
duritātroubles, wrongs

The shortest Ṛgveda hymn. This single verse is a complete hymn of the Ṛgveda (1.99). Its picture, a boat over a river, sets the tone for the whole Sūktam.

Ṛgveda 1.99.1; Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.
Carry us across.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 2

Durgā by name
tām agnivarṇāṃ tapasā jvalantīṃvairocanīṃ karmaphaleṣu juṣṭāmdurgāṃ devīṃ śaraṇam ahaṃ prapadyesutarasi tarase namaḥ
I take refuge in her, the goddess Durgā, fire-coloured, blazing with tapas, the daughter of the radiant sun, who is sought for the fruits of action. O you who carry us across so well: salutations to you, the crossing itself.
WordMeaning
agni-varṇāmfire-coloured
tapasā jvalantīmblazing with austerity
vairocanīmradiant, of the sun
karma-phaleṣuin the fruits of action
śaraṇam prapadyeI take refuge
sutarasiO good ferry

The heart of the hymn. The only verse naming Durgā. Notice the surrender formula śaraṇam ahaṃ prapadye, “I take refuge”: the attitude of the whole prayer.

Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10 (Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad).
Take refuge.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 3

A new boat
agne tvaṃ pārayā navyo asmānsvastibhir ati durgāṇi viśvāpūś ca pṛthvī bahulā na urvībhavā tokāya tanayāya śaṃyoḥ
O Agni, ever new, carry us safely across all difficulties. Make our town and our land wide and plentiful, and be a blessing to our children and grandchildren.
WordMeaning
pārayacarry across
navyaḥnew, praiseworthy
svastibhiḥwith well-being
pūḥtown, settlement
pṛthvīland
tokāya tanayāyafor children and grandchildren

Not only for oneself. The prayer widens to the whole town and to the next generations.

Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.
Pray for others too.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 4

Protector of our bodies
viśvāni no durgahā jātavedaḥsindhuṃ na nāvā duritā’tiparṣiagne atrivan manasā gṛṇāno’smākaṃ bodhy avitā tanūnām
O Jātavedas, destroyer of difficulties, you carry us over all troubles as a boat over a river. O Agni, praised in the heart as Atri praised you, be the guardian of our bodies.
WordMeaning
durgahādestroyer of difficulties
atrivatlike the sage Atri
manasā gṛṇānaḥpraised with the mind
avitāprotector
tanūnāmof our bodies

For health. This is why many families chant the Sūktam during illness or before surgery: it asks for the body’s protection.

Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.
Guard our bodies.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 5

The victorious one
pṛtanājitaṃ sahamānam ugramagniṃ huvema paramāt sadhasthātsa naḥ parṣad ati durgāṇi viśvākṣāmad devo ati duritā’tyagniḥ
We call on fierce Agni, who wins battles and overpowers, from the highest seat. May he carry us across all difficulties; may the god Agni carry us past every wrong and fault.
WordMeaning
pṛtanā-jitamwinner of battles
sahamānamoverpowering
ugramfierce
huvemawe call
paramāt sadhasthātfrom the highest seat
kṣāmatmay he pass over

The refrain returns. “Sa naḥ parṣad ati durgāṇi viśvā” from verse 1 returns here, binding the hymn together.

Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.
Call on strength.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 6

The priest of the sacrifice
pratnoṣi kam īḍyo adhvareṣusanāc ca hotā navyaś ca satsisvāṃ cāgne tanuvaṃ piprayasvaasmabhyaṃ ca saubhagam āyajasva
You increase joy, you who are praised in the rites; you sit as the ancient and the new priest. O Agni, nourish your own body, and bring good fortune to us by worship.
WordMeaning
īḍyaḥto be praised
adhvareṣuin sacrifices
hotāthe priest who calls
sanāt navyaḥancient and new
piprayasvanourish, please
saubhagamgood fortune

Ancient and new. Agni is the oldest priest and also new in every fire lit today: tradition renewed each time.

Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10.
Old fire, new flame.

IN THE VERSE

Verse 7

Following Indra and Viṣṇu
gobhir juṣṭam ayujo niṣiktaṃtavendra viṣṇor anusañcaremanākasya pṛṣṭham abhi saṃvasānovaiṣṇavīṃ loka iha mādayantām
May we follow you, Indra, and you, Viṣṇu, in that which is rich in cattle and poured out without measure. May those who dwell at the summit of heaven delight in this Vaiṣṇavī world here.
WordMeaning
gobhiḥ juṣṭamrich in cows, blessed
anusañcaremamay we follow
nākasya pṛṣṭhamthe top of heaven
vaiṣṇavīmof Viṣṇu; also a name of the Goddess
mādayantāmmay they rejoice

A difficult verse. Translators differ on its exact sense; we give a plain reading. Tradition notes the word vaiṣṇavī, also a name of Durgā.

Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 10. Translations of this verse vary.
Follow the gods’ path.

IN THE VERSE

Durgā Gāyatrī

The closing mantra
kātyāyanāya vidmahe kanyakumāri dhīmahitanno durgiḥ pracodayāt
May we know Kātyāyanī; may we meditate on the Kanyākumārī, the virgin goddess; may Durgā inspire our understanding.
WordMeaning
vidmahemay we know
dhīmahimay we meditate
tatthat
pracodayātmay she inspire

The Gāyatrī form. Follows the pattern of the Gāyatrī mantra. Kātyāyanī is also the sixth form of Navarātri.

Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad; recited at the end of the Durgā Sūktam.
Inspire our mind.

CHANTING IT

When and how

Protection. Before journeys, examinations, operations and in times of danger.

Navarātri and homa. Recited in Devī worship, and as part of the Caṇḍī homa with the Devī Māhātmya.

Vedic chanting. As a Vedic text it has svaras (pitch accents); it is best learnt by listening to a teacher or a good recording.

Simple use. Those who cannot chant can read it slowly with its meaning, or repeat the refrain: carry us across all difficulties.
