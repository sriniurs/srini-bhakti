Lakṣmī

Śrī, Goddess of Fortune and Grace · explained and illustrated

Śrī, the radiant

HOW THIS WORKS: Who Lakṣmī is: Śrī of the Veda, born from the churning of the ocean, her lotus and elephants, her life beside Viṣṇu, her eight forms, her stories, and how homes welcome her.

WHERE WE ARE

Every shop that opens with a prayer, every home that lights lamps at Dīpāvalī, every bride welcomed as “Lakṣmī come home”: Lakṣmī is the goddess of good fortune, beauty and abundance, and the constant companion of Viṣṇu.

This guide follows her from Śrī of the Veda to the goddess who rose from the ocean of milk, reads her lotus and elephants, tells her stories and shows how she is welcomed in Karnataka homes.

Her eight forms are in our Aṣṭa Lakṣmī · 8 forms + overview guide.

ŚRĪ IN THE VEDA

From a quality to a goddess

Śrī. In the Ṛgveda śrī means radiance, beauty, prosperity: a quality. The word occurs often, and over time it became a goddess.

The Śrī Sūkta. A hymn appended (khila) to the Ṛgveda, one of the earliest dedicated to her, praising the golden goddess seated on a lotus, delighted by elephants.

Lakṣmī. The name is linked to roots meaning “mark, sign, goal”. Her name is a reminder of what we aim at. (Interpretation.)

Śrī as a title. That is why “Śrī” is placed before holy names and on every auspicious beginning.

ZAPPY: Śrī is a goddess’s name?

PROF. BOLT: Every time you write Śrī, Zappy, you invoke her.

Śrī becomes a goddess.

STORY 1 · BORN OF THE OCEAN

The churning of the milk ocean

The story

From the Purāṇas.

Gods and demons churned the ocean of milk for the nectar of immortality.
First came poison, which Śiva drank; then treasures: the wish-cow, the white elephant, the moon and more.
Then rose Lakṣmī, seated on a lotus, radiant, holding a lotus.
Everyone wanted her. She looked at all the gods and chose Viṣṇu, placing her garland on him.
She made her home on his chest. Without her, the gods’ fortune had fled; with her, it returned.

ZAPPY: She chose him herself?

PROF. BOLT: The goddess of fortune chooses, Zappy. Always.

She chooses.

HER IMAGE

Lotus, gold and elephants

🪷 The lotus

She sits and stands on a lotus and holds lotuses: beauty that rises clean from muddy water.

🪙 Gold

A stream of coins from her hand: wealth flowing outward, not hoarded.

🐘 Elephants

Gajalakṣmī: elephants pour water over her, rain and royal plenty.

✋ Four arms

Often read as the four aims of life: dharma, artha, kāma and mokṣa.

🦉 The owl

Her mount in some regions, especially in the east.

🔴 Red and gold

Her colours, the colours of a bride and of good fortune.

ZAPPY: Why an owl?

PROF. BOLT: It sees in the dark, Zappy. Some say wealth needs wisdom to see where it goes.

Lotus and gold.

BESIDE VIṢṆU

Śrī on his chest

Śrīvatsa. The mark on Viṣṇu’s chest is her seat: she never leaves him.

With every avatāra. She comes as Sītā with Rāma and as Rukmiṇī with Kṛṣṇa; at Tirumala as Padmāvatī.

Śrī Vaiṣṇavism. In Rāmānuja’s tradition the devotee approaches Viṣṇu through her: she pleads for the soul.

Śrīdevī and Bhūdevī. In the south Viṣṇu stands between Śrī and Bhū, fortune and the earth.

ZAPPY: She speaks up for us?

PROF. BOLT: Like a mother asking father to forgive, Zappy.

Mother who pleads.

STORY 2 · THE SHOWER OF GOLD

Śaṅkara and the poor woman

The story

Traditional account of the Kanakadhārā Stotram.

Young Śaṅkara, a boy monk, came begging alms at a poor woman’s door.
She had nothing but a single dried gooseberry, and gave it to him with shame and love.
Moved, Śaṅkara sang a hymn to Lakṣmī on her behalf.
The goddess was pleased: golden gooseberries rained on the house.
The hymn is the Kanakadhārā Stotram, “the stream of gold”.

ZAPPY: For one gooseberry?

PROF. BOLT: For the love it was given with, Zappy.

Love brings gold.

STORY 3 · WHEN ŚRĪ LEFT

Durvāsā’s garland

The story

From the Viṣṇu Purāṇa and others.

The sage Durvāsā gave Indra a garland of heavenly flowers.
Indra, proud, placed it on his elephant, which flung it down and trampled it.
Durvāsā cursed him: the fortune of the gods would leave them.
Śrī left heaven; the gods grew weak and the demons won.
So began the churning of the ocean, by which she returned. Pride, the story says, drives fortune away.

ZAPPY: Because of a garland?

PROF. BOLT: Because of pride, Zappy.

Pride loses fortune.

HOW SHE IS WORSHIPPED

Fridays, Varalakṣmī and Dīpāvalī

Fridays. Her day: homes are cleaned, lamps lit and her hymns sung; read our Mahālakṣmī Aṣṭakam.

Varamahalakṣmī. In Karnataka women dress a kalaśa as the goddess on the Friday before Śrāvaṇa Pūrṇimā; read our Varamahalakṣmī guide.

Dīpāvalī. Lakṣmī Pūjā on the new-moon night, with every door lit; read our Dīpāvalī guide.

Kojāgarī Pūrṇimā. On the autumn full moon she is said to ask “who is awake?” and bless those who are.

ZAPPY: Clean houses for Lakṣmī?

PROF. BOLT: She won’t enter a dirty one, Zappy, says every grandmother.

Lamps at every door.

HER TEMPLES

Kolhāpur and beyond

Kolhāpur. Mahālakṣmī (Ambābāī) is a great Śakti Pīṭha; read our Kolhāpur guide.

With Viṣṇu. In every Viṣṇu temple she has her own shrine: Padmāvatī at Tiruchanūr near Tirumala, Raṅganāyakī at Śrīraṅgam.

Karnataka. Kolhāpur Lakṣmī is the family goddess of many Karnataka families, and Lakṣmī shrines stand beside Viṣṇu at Mēlukōṭe and elsewhere.

Beyond India. Buddhists and Jains honour her too; in Japan she is Kichijōten.

ZAPPY: Lakṣmī in Japan?

PROF. BOLT: Fortune travels far, Zappy.

Fortune everywhere.

WHAT LAKṢMĪ TEACHES

Wealth with grace

Abundance is sacred. Prosperity is not opposed to spirit; it is a goddess, to be honoured. (Interpretation.)

Clean wealth. The lotus grows from mud but stays clean: earn and use wealth rightly.

Flowing wealth. Coins flow from her hand; wealth is meant to move, to feed and to help.

Fickle and faithful. She leaves the proud and stays with the faithful, as she stays with Viṣṇu.

ZAPPY: So wealth is good?

PROF. BOLT: Wealth with dharma is Lakṣmī, Zappy. Without it, it isn’t.

Wealth with dharma.
