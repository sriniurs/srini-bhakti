Sarasvatī

Goddess of the Word, Learning and Music · explained and illustrated

She who flows

HOW THIS WORKS: Who Sarasvatī is: the river of the Veda who became Vāc, the goddess of speech, her vīṇā, book, swan and white, her stories, vidyārambha and Sarasvatī Pūjā, and Śāradā of Śṛṅgeri.

WHERE WE ARE

Before an exam, a recital or a child’s first letter, Hindus bow to Sarasvatī: the goddess of knowledge, speech, music and the arts, dressed in white and playing the vīṇā.

This guide follows her from a mighty river in the Ṛgveda to the goddess of the word, reads her image, tells her stories, and shows how she is worshipped, from Vasant Pañcamī to Sarasvatī Pūjā in Navarātri and the first letter written in rice.

Her worship in the nine nights is in our Sarasvatī Pūjā and Durgāṣṭamī guide.

THE RIVER OF THE VEDA

Sarasvatī who flows

A river first. In the Ṛgveda Sarasvatī is a mighty river, “best of mothers, best of rivers, best of goddesses”, on whose banks the Vedic people sacrificed.

The name. Usually explained as “she who has pools” or “she who flows”.

A lost river. Later texts say the river vanished into the desert; tradition holds she flows underground and meets the Gaṅgā and Yamunā at Prayāga.

Purifier. As river she purifies and makes fertile; as goddess she purifies speech and mind.

ZAPPY: A river became a goddess of books?

PROF. BOLT: Rivers flow, Zappy, and so do words and ideas.

A river of words.

SHE BECOMES VĀC

The goddess of speech

Vāc. Later Vedic texts merge Sarasvatī with Vāc, sacred speech, who in a famous Ṛgveda hymn (10.125) declares that she moves with all the gods and supports the world.

The word is power. In Vedic thought the right word, rightly spoken, makes things happen: so speech is a goddess.

Bhāratī. She is also Bhāratī, “eloquence”, and Vāgdevī, “goddess of speech”.

Mahāsarasvatī. In the Devī Māhātmya she is the goddess of its third episode; read our Devī Māhātmya · 19 parts.

ZAPPY: Words have power?

PROF. BOLT: Think of a promise or a lie, Zappy. Words move the world.

Speech is sacred.

STORY 1 · BRAHMĀ AND SARASVATĪ

The creator and the word

The story

From the Purāṇas; tellings differ.

Brahmā, on the lotus from Viṣṇu’s navel, wished to create but did not know how.
From him (or before him, by his prayer) arose Sarasvatī, knowledge and speech.
With knowledge, Brahmā could plan; with the word, he could name, and naming is making.
So she is called his consort, or in some tellings his daughter or his power.
Wherever creation needs a plan and a name, Sarasvatī is there. (Interpretation.)

ZAPPY: You can’t create without knowing how?

PROF. BOLT: Not even the creator, Zappy.

Creation needs knowledge.

STORY 2 · THE SLIP OF THE TONGUE

Kumbhakarṇa’s boon

The story

A widely told Rāmāyaṇa tradition; tellings differ.

The giant Kumbhakarṇa, Rāvaṇa’s brother, did great penance and Brahmā came to grant a boon.
The gods feared he would ask for Indra’s throne, indrāsana.
They prayed to Sarasvatī, who sat on his tongue.
He asked instead for nidrāsana, a bed of sleep.
So he slept for months at a time. Sarasvatī, the story says, decides what our words really say.

ZAPPY: She changed his words!

PROF. BOLT: One letter, Zappy. That’s the power of speech.

Words matter.

THE FIRST LETTER

Vidyārambha and Akṣarābhyāsa

The first letter. Children begin learning by writing their first letters in rice or sand, guiding a finger, before Sarasvatī.

Vijayadaśamī. In Karnataka and Kerala the day after Sarasvatī Pūjā is the great day for this; at Kollūr crowds of children are initiated.

Vasant Pañcamī. In the north the spring fifth, her own festival, is the day to begin learning.

Books and tools. On Sarasvatī Pūjā books and instruments are placed before her and not used until the next day.

PROF. BOLT: Your first letters, offered to the goddess, Zappy.

Learning begins with her.

HER FESTIVALS

Spring and autumn

Vasant Pañcamī. The fifth day of the bright half of Māgha, in early spring: yellow clothes, yellow flowers and her worship, especially by students and in Bengal.

Sarasvatī Pūjā in Navarātri. In the south the last days of Navarātri are hers, with books and instruments worshipped; read our guide.

Śāradā Navarātri. At Śṛṅgeri the whole of Navarātri is her festival.

Guru Pūrṇimā. Honouring teachers honours her; read our Guru Pūrṇimā · 3 articles guides.

ZAPPY: Yellow for spring?

PROF. BOLT: Mustard fields in flower, Zappy.

Spring and autumn.

ŚĀRADĀ OF ŚṚṄGERI

The goddess of the teachers

Śāradā. Another name of Sarasvatī, “of the autumn”, the goddess of learning.

Śṛṅgeri. On the Tuṅgā, Ādi Śaṅkara founded his first maṭha with Śāradā as its goddess; read our Śṛṅgeri Śāradā guide.

Kashmir. The ancient Śāradā Pīṭha of Kashmir was among the great seats of learning; Kashmir was called Śāradā-deśa.

Tantric forms. Nīla Sarasvatī, the blue Sarasvatī; read our Nīla Sarasvatī guide.

ZAPPY: Śṛṅgeri is in Karnataka!

PROF. BOLT: Our own, Zappy, on the Tuṅgā.

Karnataka’s Śāradā.

BEYOND HINDUISM

Jains, Buddhists and Japan

Jains. Sarasvatī is honoured as the goddess of the teachings of the Tīrthaṅkaras, and fine Jain Sarasvatī images survive.

Buddhists. She appears in Mahāyāna texts; in Japan she became Benzaiten, goddess of water, music and eloquence, and in China Biàncáitiān.

Artists. Musicians and dancers across India begin a performance with a prayer to her.

ZAPPY: She’s in Japan?

PROF. BOLT: With her lute, Zappy. Music travels.

Learning has no borders.

WHAT SARASVATĪ TEACHES

Clear, flowing knowledge

Knowledge flows. Like a river it must keep moving: shared, taught, renewed. (Interpretation.)

Purity. White and simple: learning needs a clear mind, not decoration.

Discrimination. The swan’s gift: telling true from false.

Speech. Words are sacred; use them carefully.
