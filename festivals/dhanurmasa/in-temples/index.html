Dhanurmāsa in Temples

Tirumala, Śrīraṅgam and the Triraṅga · explained and illustrated

Temples at dawn

HOW THIS WORKS: Tirumala, where Āṇḍāḷ wakes the Lord, Śrīraṅgam’s festival of recitation, Karnataka’s Triraṅga, and Āṇḍāḷ’s wedding.

WHERE WE ARE

In Dhanurmāsa the great Viṣṇu temples of the south change their rhythm. At Tirumala the Tiruppāvai replaces the Suprabhātam at dawn; at Śrīraṅgam the long Adhyayana Utsavam recites the Āḻvārs’ hymns and opens the Vaikuṇṭha gate; and in Karnataka the Triraṅga temples keep the month with dawn worship.

This part visits them. It gives no timings; temples publish their own each year.

With the blessings of the Lord,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: The Suprabhātam stops?

PROF. BOLT: At Tirumala, in this month, Āṇḍāḷ’s verses wake the Lord instead. A remarkable honour for a girl’s song.

TIRUMALA

When Āṇḍāḷ wakes the Lord

No Suprabhātam. Every other morning of the year the Venkaṭeśa Suprabhātam · 5 parts wakes the Lord at Tirumala. In Dhanurmāsa it is replaced by Āṇḍāḷ’s Tiruppāvai.

Why. In the Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition the Tamil hymns of the Āḻvārs are counted equal to the Veda, and in Mārgaḻi Āṇḍāḷ’s waking songs are the right ones to wake the Lord.

Tulasī and Bilva. Special offerings and arrangements mark the month at the temple.

Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī. The Vaikuṇṭha dvāra at Tirumala opens for the great Ekādaśī within the month; see our Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī article.

ZAPPY: A girl’s song instead of a Sanskrit one?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly. That’s how highly the tradition holds her.

At Tirumala, Āṇḍāḷ wakes the Lord.

ŚRĪRAṄGAM

The festival of recitation

Adhyayana Utsavam. At Śrīraṅgam, Dhanurmāsa brings the Adhyayana Utsavam, about twenty days of reciting the Āḻvārs’ 4,000 verses (the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham): ten days before Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī (pagal pattu) and ten after (rā pattu).

The gate. On Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī the Paramapada vāsal, the northern gate, opens and Namperumāḷ passes through it.

Āṇḍāḷ’s groom. Raṅganātha is the Lord Āṇḍāḷ married (Part 2).

Our series. See our Temples of Tamil Nadu · 11 articles and Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī.

ZAPPY: Twenty days of recitation?

PROF. BOLT: 4,000 Tamil verses, sung in procession before the Lord. It is one of the great festivals of the Vaiṣṇava world.

Śrīraṅgam: twenty days of the Āḻvārs’ verses.

THE TRIRAṄGA IN DHANURMĀSA

Karnataka’s Raṅganāthas

Three Raṅganāthas. Along the Kāverī lie the three great Raṅganātha shrines: Ādi Raṅga at Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa, Madhya Raṅga at Śivanasamudra and Antya Raṅga at Śrīraṅgam. Our Triraṅga series · 4 articles visits each.

Dawn worship. Through Dhanurmāsa, Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa and other Karnataka Vaiṣṇava temples hold Dhanurmāsa pūjā before sunrise, with pongal offered and shared.

Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī. The northern gate (Vaikuṇṭha dvāra) opens at Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa and other temples on the great Ekādaśī.

Melukōṭe and more. Śrīvaiṣṇava centres in Karnataka, such as Melukōṭe, keep the month with the Tiruppāvai; see our Melukōṭe article.

ZAPPY: So Mysuru has its own Dhanurmāsa?

PROF. BOLT: At Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa, twenty kilometres away. Raṅganātha is the Lord of the month.

Karnataka’s Raṅganāthas: dawn worship and the northern gate.

ĀṆḌĀḶ’S WEDDING

Bhogi

On Bhogi. On the last day of Dhanurmāsa, Bhogi, the eve of Makara Saṅkrānti, many Vaiṣṇava temples celebrate Āṇḍāḷ Kalyāṇam, Āṇḍāḷ’s wedding to Raṅganātha.

Her own hymn. Her Nācciyār Tirumoḻi includes a famous dream of her own wedding (Vāraṇamāyiram), sung at Śrīvaiṣṇava weddings to this day.

The month’s end. The wedding completes the month that her Tiruppāvai began.

Then Saṅkrānti. The next day the sun enters Makara; see our Saṅkrānti article.

ZAPPY: The girl gets her wish at the end of the month?

PROF. BOLT: Every year. The month is her love story, ending in a wedding.

On Bhogi, Āṇḍāḷ weds Raṅganātha.

MELUKŌṬE

Rāmānuja’s Karnataka

Rāmānuja’s home. Melukōṭe near Mysuru, where Rāmānuja lived for years, is Karnataka’s great Śrīvaiṣṇava centre.

Dhanurmāsa. The Tiruppāvai is sung there through the month.

Our article. See Melukōṭe.

Tamil in Karnataka. Āṇḍāḷ’s Tamil verses are sung in a Karnataka temple: the tradition crosses languages.

ZAPPY: Tamil songs in Karnataka?

PROF. BOLT: The Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition carries them everywhere. Melukōṭe sings them beautifully.

Melukōṭe: Āṇḍāḷ sung near Mysuru.

PAGAL PATTU AND RĀ PATTU

Twenty days at Śrīraṅgam

Day ten. Pagal pattu, the ten daytime days before Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī, recite the first 2,000 verses of the Prabandham.

Night ten. Rā pattu, the ten nights after, recite the rest.

The deity listens. Namperumāḷ, the festival image, sits in the hall and “listens” to the Āḻvārs’ hymns.

Araiyars. Hereditary singers (araiyars) perform the verses with gesture.

ZAPPY: The Lord listens to the hymns?

PROF. BOLT: That’s the beautiful idea: the saints sing, and he comes out to hear.

Twenty days of the Lord listening to his saints.

TEMPLES AT SAṄKRĀNTI

The month’s end in temples

Bhogi. Āṇḍāḷ’s wedding on Bhogi closes the month.

Saṅkrānti. Temples decorate with sugarcane and offer pongal on Saṅkrānti.

Cows. In Tamil Nadu, Kaṇṇum/Māṭṭu Pongal honours cattle the next day.

Our article. See Makara Saṅkrānti.

ZAPPY: The month ends with a wedding and a harvest?

PROF. BOLT: A perfect ending: Āṇḍāḷ’s joy, then the harvest’s.

A wedding, then the harvest.

VISITING IN THE MONTH

Practical notes without timings

Early. Dawn worship means arriving in the dark; bring a shawl.

Crowds. Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī brings very large crowds; temples publish arrangements.

Prasāda. Hot pongal/huggi is often distributed after the dawn pūjā.

Check. Each temple announces its own schedule; we give none here.

ZAPPY: Bring a shawl to the temple?

PROF. BOLT: December dawns are cold, even in the south!

Come early, come warm.
