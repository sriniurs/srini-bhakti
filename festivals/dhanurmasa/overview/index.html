Dhanurmāsa

The Month of Dawn Worship · explained and illustrated

The month of dawn worship

HOW THIS WORKS: Why this month is the gods’ dawn, the daily practice, and why it is a month without weddings.

WHERE WE ARE

When the sun enters the sign of Dhanus, the archer, in mid-December, begins Dhanurmāsa, the month before Makara Saṅkrānti. In the south it is a month of dawn worship: temples open before sunrise, the Tiruppāvai is sung, rangolis cover the streets, and hot pongal (huggi) is offered to Viṣṇu.

This 4-part series covers the month, Āṇḍāḷ and her Tiruppāvai, the month in temples (Tirumala, Śrīraṅgam and our Triraṅga), and at home.

ZAPPY: A whole month of getting up early?

PROF. BOLT: In the coldest month too. It is said to be the gods’ dawn, so devotees rise with them.

THE GODS’ DAWN

Why this month

A day of the gods. Tradition says a human year is one day of the gods: the sun’s northern course (uttarāyaṇa, from Makara Saṅkrānti) is their day, the southern course their night.

Their dawn. Dhanurmāsa, the month just before Saṅkrānti, is thus the gods’ brāhma muhūrta, the sacred hour before dawn. Worship in it is especially praised.

Mārgaśīrṣa. It overlaps with Mārgaśīrṣa, the month Kṛṣṇa calls himself among months (Gītā 10.35).

So we rise early. Worship is done in the cold dark before sunrise, every day of the month.

ZAPPY: So the whole month is the gods’ early morning?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly. And what’s more sacred than early-morning prayer?

Dhanurmāsa: the gods’ hour before dawn.

THE DAILY PRACTICE

Before sunrise, every day

Early pūjā. Temples hold special Dhanurmāsa worship before sunrise, and households who keep it do the same at home.

The offering. Hot pongal or huggi (rice and lentils cooked with ghee, pepper and cumin, or sweet with jaggery) is offered to Viṣṇu and shared.

Songs. In Vaiṣṇava homes and temples the Tiruppāvai is sung, one verse a day (Part 2).

Rangoli. Women draw large rangolis (kolam) at the doorstep before dawn (Part 4).

ZAPPY: Pongal at dawn in the cold?

PROF. BOLT: Hot ghee pongal on a December morning. Some devotees will tell you that is half the reason they come!

Up before dawn: pūjā, pongal, song.

A MONTH WITHOUT WEDDINGS

Śūnya māsa

No ceremonies. Dhanurmāsa is counted a śūnya māsa, an “empty month”, for family ceremonies: weddings, house-warmings and the like are generally not held.

Why. The month is given over entirely to God; the gods themselves are in their pre-dawn worship.

Not inauspicious for worship. For devotion, it is one of the best months of the year.

After Saṅkrānti. Ceremonies resume when the sun enters Makara.

ZAPPY: Empty for weddings, full for God?

PROF. BOLT: That is exactly the idea.

Empty for ceremonies, full for God.

MĀRGAŚĪRṢA, THE BEST MONTH

“Among months, I am Mārgaśīrṣa”

The Gītā. In Gītā 10.35 Kṛṣṇa says, “Among months I am Mārgaśīrṣa.”

Overlap. Dhanurmāsa overlaps Mārgaśīrṣa and Pauṣa in the lunar calendar.

Why best. Commentators note the harvest, the pleasant weather, and its position as the gods’ dawn.

Gītā Jayantī. The Gītā is said to have been taught in Mārgaśīrṣa, on Mokṣadā Ekādaśī.

ZAPPY: Kṛṣṇa picked a favourite month?

PROF. BOLT: He named it as his own glory. Devotees took the hint.

“Among months, I am Mārgaśīrṣa.”

A SOLAR MONTH

Dhanus and the sun

Solar, not lunar. Unlike most festivals, Dhanurmāsa follows the sun: it lasts while the sun is in Dhanus (Sagittarius).

Dates. It therefore falls at about the same time every year, roughly mid-December to mid-January.

Tamil Mārgaḻi. The Tamil month Mārgaḻi is this same solar month.

Ends at Saṅkrānti. The sun enters Makara on Saṅkrānti, ending the month.

ZAPPY: So it’s on the same dates each year?

PROF. BOLT: Nearly. That’s the sun for you, steady.

A solar month, mid-December to mid-January.

VAIKUṆṬHA EKĀDAŚĪ IN THE MONTH

The great day within

Usually inside. Vaikuṇṭha (Mokṣadā) Ekādaśī usually falls within Dhanurmāsa.

The gate. The Vaikuṇṭha dvāra opens in Viṣṇu temples.

Our article. See Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī and Mokṣadā.

The peak. For many it is the high point of Dhanurmāsa.

ZAPPY: The best Ekādaśī in the best month?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly why the crowds are so huge.

Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī: the month’s peak.

DHANURMĀSA IN KARNATAKA

Dawn worship across the state

Temples. Vaiṣṇava and many other temples across Karnataka hold Dhanurmāsa pūjā before sunrise.

Huggi. Huggi is offered and distributed (Part 4).

Bhajans. Groups go round streets singing before dawn in some places.

Mysuru. The Triraṅga and Mysuru temples keep the month; see Part 3.

ZAPPY: Does Mysuru keep it?

PROF. BOLT: Very much. Ask for Dhanurmāsa huggi at any Viṣṇu temple at dawn.

Karnataka at dawn: pūjā and huggi.

WHY RISE IN THE DARK

The meaning of the month

Before the world wakes. The hour before dawn is quiet; prayer then is undistracted.

The gods’ dawn. Rising early in this month mirrors the gods’ own pre-dawn.

Discipline. Cold mornings make it a discipline.

Joy. And yet the month is joyful: songs, rangolis, warm food.

ZAPPY: Cold and joyful at once?

PROF. BOLT: That’s Dhanurmāsa. Ask anyone who grew up with it.

Rise in the dark, meet the dawn with God.
