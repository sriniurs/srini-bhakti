The Ekādaśīs of Phālguna

Vijayā and Āmalakī · explained and illustrated

Two lamps for Phālguna

HOW THIS WORKS: The month, then Vijayā and Āmalakī: each story told in order, then what it means and how it is kept.

WHERE WE ARE

Month by month through the year. This part covers Phālguna, the season of Śivarātri and Holī, and its two Ekādaśīs: Vijayā in the dark fortnight and Āmalakī in the bright fortnight.

Each is told as its māhātmya tells it (from the Padma and Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇas, where Kṛṣṇa tells Yudhiṣṭhira), with what it means and how it is kept. Tellings differ in details between editions. In Karnataka’s new-moon-ending (amānta) calendar, the dark-fortnight Ekādaśī here falls under the previous month’s name.

VIJAYĀ: THE STORY

The dark fortnight Ekādaśī

The story

Vijayā is the Ekādaśī Rāma kept before Laṅkā.

With the vānara army Rāma reached the ocean shore, with no way across to Laṅkā.
Lakṣmaṇa took him to the hermitage of the sage Bakadālbhya.
The sage told Rāma to keep the Vijayā Ekādaśī, with a kalaśa worship, to win the victory.
Rāma kept it with his army.
The ocean was crossed, the bridge built, and Rāvaṇa defeated: Vijayā, “complete victory”.

ZAPPY: Even Rāma kept Ekādaśī?

PROF. BOLT: So this story says. A god in human form shows humans the way: even the strongest begin with a vow.

Rāma’s fast before the crossing.

VIJAYĀ: THE MEANING

“complete victory”

The name. Vijayā, complete victory.

Before a hard task. Kept by those facing a great challenge, as Rāma faced the ocean.

Near Śivarātri. It falls in dark Phālguna (northern reckoning), close to Mahā Śivarātri; see our Śivarātri article.

Keeping it. As with every Ekādaśī: prepare on Daśamī, fast at your level, remember Viṣṇu, and break the fast on Dvādaśī (Part 2).

ĀMALAKĪ: THE STORY

The bright fortnight Ekādaśī

The story

Āmalakī is the Ekādaśī of a sacred tree.

In the city of King Caitraratha everyone kept Āmalakī Ekādaśī, worshipping Viṣṇu at an āmalakī tree through the night.
A hungry hunter came to the temple to find food, and sat watching the worship all night, hearing the stories.
He went home and ate only on Dvādaśī; without meaning to, he had fasted and kept vigil.
He was reborn as King Vasuratha, brave and generous.
When enemies attacked him in the forest, a radiant goddess sprang from his body and destroyed them: the power of that one Āmalakī night.

ZAPPY: Watching other people worship counted?

PROF. BOLT: Sitting all night with the Lord’s name in your ears does something. The story says so, generously.

One night at the gooseberry tree, a lifetime’s protection.

ĀMALAKĪ: THE MEANING

“of the āmalakī (Indian gooseberry) tree”

The name. Āmalakī, the Indian gooseberry (amla) tree, said to be dear to Viṣṇu.

Tree worship. Devotees water and circle the āmalakī tree and worship Viṣṇu beneath it.

Before Holī. It falls in bright Phālguna, just before Holī; see our Holī article.

Keeping it. As with every Ekādaśī: prepare on Daśamī, fast at your level, remember Viṣṇu, and break the fast on Dvādaśī (Part 2).

THE TWO TOGETHER

What Phālguna’s Ekādaśīs teach

Vijayā. Rāma’s fast before the crossing.. So this story says. A god in human form shows humans the way: even the strongest begin with a vow.

Āmalakī. One night at the gooseberry tree, a lifetime’s protection.. Sitting all night with the Lord’s name in your ears does something. The story says so, generously.

The season. Phālguna is the season of Śivarātri and Holī; many families tie the two fasts to the month’s other festivals.

The pattern. Like all Ekādaśī stories, both end in release and a fresh start (Part 1).

Vijayā and Āmalakī: two doors in Phālguna.
