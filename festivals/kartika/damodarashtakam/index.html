Bound by his mother, bound by love

HOW THIS WORKS: First the story of Yaśodā and the rope, then every verse of Satyavrata’s hymn with its meaning, and how it is sung in Kārtika.

WHERE WE ARE

The great hymn of Kārtika is the Dāmodarāṣṭakam, a prayer to the child Kṛṣṇa tied with a rope (dāma) round his belly (udara). The Padma Purāṇa gives it to the sage Satyavrata, who sang it while worshipping Dāmodara in Kārtika at Vṛndāvana.

Vaiṣṇavas, especially in the Gauḍīya tradition, sing it every evening of the month while offering a lamp. Here is the story behind it and every verse, with its meaning.

THE STORY: YAŚODĀ BINDS KṚṢṆA

Bhāgavata Purāṇa 10.9–10

The story

This story is the reason for the name Dāmodara.

One morning Yaśodā left the churning to tend boiling milk. Little Kṛṣṇa, angry at being put down, broke the butter pot and ran off to feed butter to the monkeys.
Yaśodā chased him with a stick. He ran in fear, but she caught him.
She decided to tie him to the wooden mortar. Every rope was two fingers too short, however many she joined.
Seeing his mother tired and sweating, Kṛṣṇa let himself be tied. So he is Dāmodara, “the one with a rope round his belly”.
Crawling, he dragged the mortar between two arjuna trees and pulled them down; from them stepped Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva, Kubera’s sons, freed from a curse.

ZAPPY: Why was the rope always too short?

PROF. BOLT: You can’t tie up the infinite. He let himself be tied when he saw her love. That is the theology in one picture.

Only love could tie the Lord: Dāmodara.

IN THE VERSE

VERSE 1

THE LORD WHO RAN FROM HIS MOTHER
namāmīśvaraṃ saccidānandarūpaṃ lasatkuṇḍalaṃ gokule bhrājamānam .yaśodābhiyolūkhale dhāvamānaṃ parāmṛṣṭamatyantato dūtagopyā .. 5..
I bow to the Lord whose form is being, awareness and bliss, his earrings swinging, shining in Gokula; who ran from the mortar in fear of Yaśodā, and was caught from behind by the cowherd woman, running faster still.
WordMeaning
īśvaramthe Lord
sac-cid-ānanda-rūpamwhose form is being, awareness and bliss
lasat-kuṇḍalamwith swinging earrings
ulūkhalefrom the mortar
parāmṛṣṭamcaught

God, caught. The very first line calls him the Lord of existence and bliss; the next lines show him running from his mother and caught. That contrast is the whole hymn.

Text: sanskritdocuments.org, Satyavratokta Dāmodarastotram (Padma Purāṇa), hymn verses 5–11 numbered here 1–7.
The Lord, caught by love.

IN THE VERSE

VERSE 2

TEARS OF A CHILD
rudantaṃ muhurnetrayugmaṃ mṛjantaṃ karāmbhojayugmena sātaṅkanetram .muhuḥśvāsakaṃ patrirekhāṅkakaṇṭhaṃ sthitaṃ naumi dāmodaraṃ bhaktavandyam .. 6..
He cries again and again, rubbing his two eyes with his lotus hands, eyes full of fear; sobbing, the lines of the necklace on his throat trembling, bound by love. I praise Dāmodara, worshipped by his devotees.
WordMeaning
rudantamcrying
mṛjantamrubbing
sātaṅka-netramwith frightened eyes
muhuḥ-śvāsakamsobbing again and again
bhakta-vandyamworshipped by devotees

A real child. The verse lingers on tiny details: hands rubbing eyes, catching breath. The poet wants us to see God as a frightened little boy, and love him.

Text: sanskritdocuments.org, Satyavratokta Dāmodarastotram (Padma Purāṇa), hymn verses 5–11 numbered here 1–7.
Tears of a child; God within reach.

IN THE VERSE

VERSE 3

NOT EVEN LIBERATION
varaṃ deva dehīśa mokṣāvadhiṃ vā na cānyaṃ vṛṇe’haṃ vareśādapīha .idaṃ te vapurnātha gopālabālaṃ sadā me manasyāvirāstāṃ kimanyaiḥ .. 7..
O Lord, giver of boons, I do not ask you for liberation, or for the highest goal, or any other boon. May this form of yours, the cowherd child, always appear in my mind. What use are other things?
WordMeaning
varama boon
mokṣa-avadhimliberation, the final goal
na anyam vṛṇeI choose nothing else
gopāla-bālamthe cowherd boy
āvirāstāmmay it appear

The famous refusal. The devotee turns down even liberation. All he wants is to keep seeing the child Kṛṣṇa in his heart: love for its own sake.

Text: sanskritdocuments.org, Satyavratokta Dāmodarastotram (Padma Purāṇa), hymn verses 5–11 numbered here 1–7. Many popular editions add a further verse, “itīdṛk svalīlābhir…”; this edition does not include it.
Not even liberation: only this child in my heart.

IN THE VERSE

VERSE 4

THE FACE KISSED BY HIS MOTHER
idaṃ te mukhāmbhojamatyantanīlairvṛtaṃ kuntalaiḥ snigdhavaktraiśca gopyā .muhuścumbitaṃ bimbaraktādharaṃ me manasyāvirāstāmalaṃ lakṣalābhaiḥ .. 8..
May this lotus face of yours, framed by dark, glossy curls, its lips red as the bimba fruit, kissed again and again by the cowherd mother, appear in my mind. Enough of a hundred thousand other gains!
WordMeaning
mukha-ambhojamlotus face
kuntalaiḥwith curls
cumbitamkissed
bimba-rakta-adharamlips red as the bimba fruit
alam lakṣa-lābhaiḥenough of countless gains

Through her eyes. We see Kṛṣṇa as Yaśodā sees him: a face to kiss. The devotee asks to love him the way a mother does.

Text: sanskritdocuments.org, Satyavratokta Dāmodarastotram (Padma Purāṇa), hymn verses 5–11 numbered here 1–7.
Love him as his mother does.

IN THE VERSE

VERSE 5

A PLEA FOR GRACE
namo deva dāmodarānanta viṣṇo prasīda prabho duḥkhajālābdhimagnam .kṛpādṛṣṭivṛṣṭyā’tidīnaṃ ca rakṣa gṛhāṇeśa māmajñamevākṣidṛśyam .. 9..
Salutations, O Lord Dāmodara, Ananta, Viṣṇu! Be gracious, Master. With the rain of your merciful glance, protect me, drowning in an ocean of sorrows, wretched and ignorant; become visible to my eyes.
WordMeaning
prasīdabe gracious
duḥkha-jāla-abdhi-magnamdrowning in the sea of sorrows
kṛpā-dṛṣṭi-vṛṣṭyāby the rain of your merciful glance
rakṣaprotect
akṣi-dṛśyamvisible to the eyes

From wonder to need. After the tender pictures, the devotee speaks plainly of his own sorrow and asks only to be looked upon with mercy.

Text: sanskritdocuments.org, Satyavratokta Dāmodarastotram (Padma Purāṇa), hymn verses 5–11 numbered here 1–7.
Be gracious; look on me.

IN THE VERSE

VERSE 6

THE TWO TREES
kuberātmajau vṛkṣamūrtī ca yadvatvayā mocitau bhaktibhājau kṛtau ca .tathā premabhaktiṃ svakāṃ me prayaccha na mokṣe’graho me’sti dāmodareha .. 10..
Just as you freed Kubera’s two sons, who had become trees, and made them your devotees, so grant me your own loving devotion. I have no craving for liberation, Dāmodara.
WordMeaning
kubera-ātmajauthe two sons of Kubera
vṛkṣa-mūrtīin the form of trees
mocitaufreed
prema-bhaktimloving devotion
na mokṣe āgrahaḥno insistence on liberation

The story behind the hymn. Tied to the mortar, little Kṛṣṇa crawled between two arjuna trees and pulled them down, freeing Kubera’s cursed sons, Nalakūbara and Maṇigrīva (Bhāgavatam 10.10). The devotee asks to be freed into love the same way.

Text: sanskritdocuments.org, Satyavratokta Dāmodarastotram (Padma Purāṇa), hymn verses 5–11 numbered here 1–7.
Free me into love, as you freed the trees.

IN THE VERSE

VERSE 7

THE ROPE OF LOVE
namaste sudāmne sphuraddīptadhāmne tathorasthaviśvasya dhāmne namaste .namo rādhikāyai tvadīyapriyāyai namo’nantalīlāya devāya tubhyam .. 11..
Salutations to the rope (dāma) round your belly, shining bright; salutations to your belly, home of the whole universe; salutations to Rādhikā, dear to you; salutations to you, the God of endless play.
WordMeaning
sudāmneto the beautiful rope
sphurad-dīpta-dhāmneshining with light
udara-stha-viśvasya dhāmneto the belly that holds the universe
rādhikāyaito Rādhikā
ananta-līlāyawhose play is endless

The rope and the universe. The same belly that Yaśodā bound holds the whole universe. Only love can bind the infinite: that is the meaning of the name Dāmodara.

Text: sanskritdocuments.org, Satyavratokta Dāmodarastotram (Padma Purāṇa), hymn verses 5–11 numbered here 1–7.
A rope of love binds the infinite.

SINGING IT IN KĀRTIKA

The evening lamp

Every evening. In the Vaiṣṇava tradition the Dāmodarāṣṭakam is sung each evening of Kārtika while offering a small ghee lamp to Kṛṣṇa as Dāmodara.

Everyone offers. In temples and homes each person, including children, takes a turn offering the lamp, often in a small clay dish.

Simple worship. No elaborate ritual is needed: a lamp, the hymn and love.

Read with meaning. Our IAST guide (How to read IAST) helps with pronunciation.

ZAPPY: Can children offer the lamp?

PROF. BOLT: Especially children. It is a hymn about a child.

A lamp and the hymn, every evening of Kārtika.
