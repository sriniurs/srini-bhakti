Śiva’s Mondays and the Lakṣa Dīpotsava · explained and illustrated

A hundred thousand lamps

HOW THIS WORKS: Śiva’s Kārtika Mondays, the Lakṣa Dīpotsava of Karnataka’s temples, and why light is offered to Śiva.

WHERE WE ARE

Kārtika belongs to Śiva too. Its Mondays, the Kārtika Somavāras, are kept with fasting and worship in Śiva temples, and through the month temples across Karnataka light thousands of lamps in dīpotsavas, lamp festivals.

The greatest of them is the Lakṣa Dīpotsava, the festival of a hundred thousand lamps, held at Dharmasthaḷa and at many other temples.

ŚIVA’S MONDAYS

Kārtika Somavāra

Moon’s day. Monday (Somavāra) is named after Soma, the moon, whom Śiva wears in his hair.

The vow. On the Mondays of Kārtika devotees fast till evening, bathe the liṅga with water or milk (abhiṣeka), offer bilva leaves and light lamps.

Popular story. The Skanda Purāṇa’s Kārtika praise includes stories of devotees blessed for keeping these Mondays; families remember them as they fast.

In temples. Kārtika Mondays see the biggest crowds in Śiva temples across the south.

ZAPPY: Can I just light a lamp?

PROF. BOLT: A lamp at a Śiva shrine on a Kārtika Monday is already the vow.

Kārtika Mondays: water, bilva and lamps for Śiva.

THE LAKṢA DĪPOTSAVA

A hundred thousand lamps

The name. Lakṣa is a hundred thousand: lamps beyond counting set out on walls, steps and stands, lit together at dusk.

Dharmasthaḷa. The Lakṣa Dīpotsava at Dharmasthaḷa, home of Śrī Mañjunātha, is among Karnataka’s best known, with a procession of the deity and a gathering of scholars. Our Tulu Nadu collection · 28 articles visits the temple.

Everywhere. Many temples, large and small, hold their own dīpotsava in Kārtika, often on a Monday or the full moon.

Taking part. Devotees bring oil and wicks, or sponsor a set of lamps, as their offering.

ZAPPY: Who lights them all?

PROF. BOLT: Everyone. That’s the beauty: a whole town lighting lamps together.

Lamps beyond counting: the Lakṣa Dīpotsava.

WHY LIGHT FOR ŚIVA

The column of fire

The endless flame. The Purāṇas tell how Brahmā and Viṣṇu once argued over who was greater, and a column of fire appeared between them. Brahmā flew up as a swan and Viṣṇu dug down as a boar, but neither found its end. Śiva appeared from it: Liṅgodbhava.

Light as Śiva. The lamps of Kārtika echo this endless light, most of all in the Kārttikai Dīpam at Tiruvaṇṇāmalai (Part 4).

Inner light. Many read the lamp as the light of awareness, which is Śiva.

Our series. See also our Pañcabhūta Sthalas · 6 articles, where Tiruvaṇṇāmalai is the temple of fire.

ZAPPY: Even Brahmā and Viṣṇu couldn’t find the end?

PROF. BOLT: That is the point: the light is infinite. Our little lamps are bows to it.

Kārtika’s lamps echo the endless column of fire.
