Siddhidātrī

Night 9 · Giver of Perfection · explained and illustrated

Worshipped by gods and demons alike

HOW THIS WORKS: Her name, the eight siddhis, the story of Ardhanārīśvara, her form, her verse word by word, Mahānavamī, and how she closes the nine nights.

WHERE WE ARE

We reach the last of the nine Durgās. After the shining Mahāgaurī comes Siddhidātrī, “the giver of perfections”, seated on a lotus and worshipped by gods, sages, celestials and even demons.

Her night is Mahānavamī, the great ninth, the last of the nine nights before the victory of the tenth day.

ZAPPY: What’s a siddhi?

PROF. BOLT: A perfection or power: becoming tiny, becoming vast, getting whatever you wish. She gives them all, and teaches that the greatest is to need none.

THE GIVER OF PERFECTIONS

Siddhi-dātrī: who she is

Her name. Siddhidātrī is “the giver (dātrī) of siddhis”, the perfections or attainments that tradition says come to the accomplished.

The eight siddhis. The classic list: aṇimā (becoming tiny), mahimā (becoming vast), garimā (becoming heavy), laghimā (becoming light), prāpti (reaching anything), prākāmya (fulfilling any wish), īśitva (lordship) and vaśitva (control). Lists vary slightly.

Worshipped by all. Her verse says she is served by siddhas, gandharvas, yakṣas, asuras and gods alike.

The ninth night. She completes the nine: the seeker who began on the mountain with Śailaputrī arrives at fulfilment.

ZAPPY: Can she make me fly?

PROF. BOLT: The tradition says siddhis come, and the wise don’t chase them. The real siddhi is the peace that doesn’t want to fly.

Siddhidātrī: giver of every perfection, the completion of the nine nights.

HALF ŚIVA, HALF ŚAKTI

Ardhanārīśvara, a popular Navarātri telling

The story

This telling is widespread in Navarātri literature; the image of Ardhanārīśvara itself is ancient.

At the beginning of creation, Śiva worshipped the Supreme Goddess, the primal power (Ādi Parāśakti), who had no form.
Pleased, she appeared as Siddhidātrī and granted him all the siddhis.
By her grace, half of Śiva’s body became hers: he became Ardhanārīśvara, “the Lord who is half woman”, right side Śiva, left side Pārvatī.
Since then Śiva and Śakti are understood as one, never apart: consciousness and power, word and meaning.
So the ninth Durgā, giver of perfection, is also the one who makes the Lord whole.

ZAPPY: Half man, half woman?

PROF. BOLT: One being, both at once. The image says: power and consciousness can’t be separated. Kālidāsa compared them to a word and its meaning.

Through her grace Śiva became Ardhanārīśvara: Śiva and Śakti, one.

HER FORM AND SYMBOLS

What the image teaches

🎡 Discus and conch

Viṣṇu’s weapons: she holds the powers of all the gods, as she did when they gave her their weapons in Part 2.

🔨 Mace

Strength to defend what has been attained.

🪷 Lotus seat

She sits on a great lotus, fulfilled and at ease: the end of the journey.

🙏 All bow to her

Gods, sages, celestials and asuras: her gifts are open to everyone.

ZAPPY: Why Viṣṇu’s weapons on a form of Pārvatī?

PROF. BOLT: By the ninth night we see she is the power behind every god, not one god’s wife. She holds them all.

Discus, conch, mace, lotus: every power, held at peace.

IN THE VERSE

NAVADURGĀ STOTRA 9

HER MEDITATION VERSE (DHYĀNA)
siddhagandharvayakṣādyairasurairamarairapi .sevyamānā sadā bhūyāt siddhidā siddhidāyinī ..
Served always by siddhas, gandharvas, yakṣas and others, by demons and by gods too, may she, the giver of perfection, be the bestower of perfection (to us).
WordMeaning
siddha-gandharva-yakṣa-ādyaiḥby siddhas, gandharvas, yakṣas and others
asuraiḥ amaraiḥ apiby demons and by gods too
sevyamānābeing served, worshipped
sadā bhūyātmay she always be
siddhidā siddhidāyinīgiver of siddhi, bestower of siddhi

No one left out. The last verse of the nine draws the widest circle: every kind of being, even the demons the earlier nights fought, bows to her. It ends by naming her twice: giver, bestower.

Text as in the Navadurgā Stotra (shlokam.org transliteration).
Gods and demons alike bow: may the giver of perfection give it to us.

WORSHIP ON HER NIGHT

How devotees keep night 9

Mahānavamī. The ninth day is Mahānavamī: in Karnataka it is Āyudha Pūjā, when tools and vehicles are worshipped; many homes do Kanyā Pūjā today rather than on Aṣṭamī (Part 15).

Completing the vow. Those who read the Devī Māhātmya through the nights finish it today, and many perform a homa (fire offering) to close the reading.

Thanks. Siddhidātrī is thanked for carrying the devotee through all nine nights.

Offerings and colours. A widely followed North Indian custom offers sesame on this night; lists differ between families and regions. Many cities also publish a “colour of the day” for each night, but that colour is set each year by the weekday, so we do not fix one here.

ZAPPY: What if I missed some nights?

PROF. BOLT: Then tonight, thank her for the ones you kept. She is the giver of completion.

Night nine: complete the reading, give thanks.

THE INNER READING

What many teachers see in her

Completion. The ninth night is read as arrival: the seeker who started on the rock of the first night is now whole.

Beyond powers. Teachers warn that siddhis are distractions; the true gift of Siddhidātrī is the perfection that no longer needs them.

One with Śiva. Ardhanārīśvara shows the goal: power and consciousness, action and stillness, joined.

The yogic map. A modern yogic reading links each night to a centre of the subtle body; for Siddhidātrī it is the crown and beyond (lists differ). This mapping is popular but recent, and teachers differ; take it as a meditation aid, not scripture.

ZAPPY: So the nine nights are one long journey?

PROF. BOLT: From the mountain to the lotus. That is how many teachers read the nine Durgās: nine steps of one climb.

Siddhidātrī gives completion.
