Onam

The Good King Comes Home · explained and illustrated

The good king comes home

HOW THIS WORKS: Onam, Kerala’s great festival, and Vāmana Jayantī: the story of the generous king Mahābali and the dwarf Vāmana, the ten days from Attam to Tiruvōṇam, the pookalam flower carpet, the Onasadya feast, snake-boat races, tiger dancers and the Thrikkakara temple.

WHERE WE ARE

Once a year, Kerala believes, a beloved king comes back to visit his people. Houses are cleaned, flower carpets bloom on every doorstep, the best feast of the year is laid on banana leaves, and boats race on the rivers. The king is Mahābali, and the festival is Onam.

The story behind it is that of Vāmana, Viṣṇu’s dwarf avatāra, whose birthday, Vāmana Jayantī, falls in the same season. This guide tells both, and how Onam is celebrated. For Vāmana in the ten avatāras, see Vāmana.

THE KING AND THE DWARF

Three steps

The story

Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Book 8, with Kerala’s Onam tradition.

Mahābali, grandson of Prahlāda, ruled so well that the asuras won heaven itself. His land knew no poverty, no lies, no theft: a golden age.
The gods, displaced, prayed to Viṣṇu. He was born as Vāmana, a dwarf brahmin boy, son of Aditi and Kaśyapa.
At Mahābali’s great sacrifice, Vāmana asked for just three steps of land. The guru Śukrācārya warned the king; Mahābali would not break his word.
Vāmana grew immense. One step covered the earth, the second the heavens. “Where shall I put the third?” Mahābali bowed his head.
Pleased by his honesty, Viṣṇu sent him to rule Sutala in the underworld, promised to guard his gate, and granted him leave to visit his people once a year: Onam.

ZAPPY: He lost his kingdom and still bowed?

PROF. BOLT: He kept his word, Zappy. That’s why Kerala loves him.

Keep your word.

TWO READINGS OF ONE STORY

Generosity and humility

In the Purāṇas. Vāmana restores order to the cosmos, and Mahābali’s pride in his gifts is humbled; but he is also honoured as a great devotee, one of the twelve mahājanas.

In Kerala. Mahābali is the hero: the just king of a land where “all were equal”. Onam celebrates his rule and his return.

Other readings. Historians have linked the festival to harvest and to old Kerala kingship, and read the story partly as an allegory of the sun and seasons. (Scholarly views.)

Both true for devotees. Many in Kerala worship Vāmana at Thrikkakara and welcome Mahābali at home on the same day.

ZAPPY: So who is the good guy?

PROF. BOLT: Both, Zappy. That’s what makes the story rich.

Generous king, humble god.

TEN DAYS

Attam to Tiruvōṇam

🌼 Attam

Day 1: the first small pookalam, yellow flowers only; the flag rises at Thrikkakara and the Atthachamayam procession leaves Thrippunithura.

📈 Growing

Each day the flower carpet grows by a ring and a colour.

🛍️ Uthrādam

Day 9, “first Onam”: last-minute shopping and preparations.

👑 Tiruvōṇam

Day 10, the main day: Mahābali visits, the great feast is served. Celebrations continue for a few days after.

ZAPPY: The days are named after stars?

PROF. BOLT: Malayalam nakṣatra names, Zappy. See Nakṣatras and Rāśis.

A carpet that grows.

THE POOKALAM

A carpet of flowers

Flowers, not flour. Kerala’s Onam carpet is made of fresh petals: marigold, chrysanthemum, thumba (a small white flower linked with Mahābali), and many others.

Grows daily. It begins small on Attam and widens each day until Tiruvōṇam.

Welcome. Like the kolam, it welcomes a guest, here the returning king.

Onathappan. Pyramid-shaped clay figures of Vāmana (Thrikkakara Appan) are set at the door and worshipped; they are immersed a few days later.

ZAPPY: Why the little clay pyramids?

PROF. BOLT: They stand for Vāmana, Zappy, the god of Thrikkakara.

Petals for the king.

THE ONASADYA

A feast on a leaf

On a banana leaf. Rice with many curries, chutneys, pickles and sweets, served in a fixed order on a leaf, eaten by hand.

Many dishes. Traditionally nine courses, often two dozen dishes or more: sambar, avial, olan, thoran, kaalan, banana chips, pappadam.

Payasam last. Several kinds of sweet payasam end the meal.

Sharing. “Kaanam vittum Onam unnanam”, a Malayalam saying: sell even your land, but eat the Onam feast. (Proverb.)

ZAPPY: Two dozen dishes on one leaf!

PROF. BOLT: And it all has a set place on the leaf, Zappy.

Feast together.

BOATS, TIGERS AND MORE

Kerala at play

🚣 Vallamkali

Snake-boat races with dozens of oarsmen, on the Pampā and backwaters; the Aranmula Uthrattadhi race is linked to the temple there; see Āranmuḷa.

🐯 Pulikali

In Thrissur, men painted as tigers dance through the streets to drums.

👗 Onakkodi

New clothes, the cream-and-gold kasavu sari and mundu.

🎭 More

Kaikottikali (women’s clapping dance), swings, games and Onam songs.

ZAPPY: Painted tigers!

PROF. BOLT: Thrissur’s favourite, Zappy. Bellies painted with tiger faces too.

Kerala celebrates.

THRIKKAKARA

Vāmana’s temple

The centre of Onam. The Thrikkakara Vāmanamūrti temple near Kochi is held to be where Mahābali ruled and where Vāmana pressed him down; Vāmana is the presiding deity.

Flag on Attam. The festival flag goes up here on the first day; a great public feast is held on Tiruvōṇam.

From Thrippunithura. The Atthachamayam procession, once of the Kochi royal family, still opens the season.

Elsewhere. Vāmana is worshipped as Trivikrama at Tirukkōvalūr (Tamil Nadu) and in many Viṣṇu temples.

ZAPPY: Vāmana has his own temple?

PROF. BOLT: A famous one, Zappy, though he is rarer than Rāma or Kṛṣṇa.

Where the story lives.

VĀMANA JAYANTĪ

The dwarf’s birthday

When. On Bhādrapada Śukla Dvādaśī (Aug–Sept), the day after Parivartinī Ekādaśī, when he is said to have been born at noon on the Śravaṇa star.

Worship. Viṣṇu as Vāmana is worshipped, the story of Mahābali read, and curd-rice and gifts given, especially an umbrella and sandals, Vāmana’s own things.

Same star. Śravaṇa (Tiruvōṇam) is both Vāmana’s birth star and Onam’s day.

Trivikrama. The giant Vāmana with one foot raised to the sky, Trivikrama, is carved at Bādāmi, Mahābalipuram and Ellora.

ZAPPY: Gift an umbrella on his birthday?

PROF. BOLT: He carried one as a boy-priest, Zappy. A nice touch.

The boy who measured the world.
