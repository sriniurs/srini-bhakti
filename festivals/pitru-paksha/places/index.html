The Places of Śrāddha

Gayā, Kāśī, Prayāga and Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa · explained and illustrated

Holy places of offering

HOW THIS WORKS: Gayā and the story of Gayāsura, the Gayā circuit, Kāśī and Prayāga, and Karnataka’s Paścimavāhinī.

WHERE WE ARE

Śrāddha can be done anywhere, but certain places are held to make it especially effective. The greatest is Gayā in Bihar; others include Kāśī, Prayāga, and in Karnataka the Paścimavāhinī at Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa, where the Kāverī flows west.

This part visits them, with their stories. It gives no travel details: these are timeless guides.

ZAPPY: Why go so far for a rite?

PROF. BOLT: Pilgrimage adds devotion. And many families go once in a lifetime to Gayā for their parents.

GAYĀ

The demon whose body became holy ground

The story

The Gayā Māhātmya (in the Vāyu Purāṇa) tells this story.

Gayāsura was a pious demon whose body became so holy, through penance, that anyone who saw or touched him went to heaven.
The gods, worried that heaven would overflow, asked him for his body as a place for a sacrifice. He agreed and lay down.
They placed a great rock on him, but he kept stirring.
Viṣṇu set his foot on the rock, and Gayāsura became still, asking only that the place be holy for all who offer śrāddha there.
The footprint is worshipped at the Viṣṇupada temple; piṇḍas offered at Gayā are said to free the ancestors.

ZAPPY: The demon asked for the place to be holy?

PROF. BOLT: A demon who wanted everyone saved. Gayā remembers him in its very name.

Gayāsura’s body, Viṣṇu’s foot: Gayā.

THE RITES AT GAYĀ

Viṣṇupada, Phalgu and the banyan

Viṣṇupada. The temple enshrines the footprint of Viṣṇu in rock; piṇḍas are offered there.

The Phalgu. The river, often a dry bed of sand with water beneath, is where tarpaṇa is offered; a story links its dryness to a curse by Sītā, who offered piṇḍa there for Daśaratha.

The undying banyan. The akṣayavaṭa, “undying banyan”, is the final place of offering on the Gayā circuit.

Once in a lifetime. Many families travel to Gayā once for their parents, often during Pitṛ Pakṣa.

ZAPPY: Sītā did śrāddha too?

PROF. BOLT: The local tradition says so, in Rāma’s absence, with sand piṇḍas. The Phalgu, the cow, the ketakī and the brahmin who lied about it were cursed; the banyan who told the truth was blessed.

Viṣṇupada, Phalgu, akṣayavaṭa: the Gayā circuit.

KĀŚĪ AND PRAYĀGA

The Gaṅgā’s holy places

Kāśī. Vārāṇasī, Śiva’s city, is believed to grant liberation to those who die there. Śrāddha on its ghāṭs, especially at Piśācamocana and Maṇikarṇikā, is held to be highly effective.

Prayāga. At the Triveṇī Saṅgama, where the Gaṅgā, Yamunā and the hidden Sarasvatī meet, pilgrims shave their heads and offer piṇḍas for their ancestors.

The three cities. Many families make a pilgrimage of all three (Kāśī, Prayāga, Gayā), the tristhalī.

Our pages. See our Saptapurī · 8 articles and Jyotirliṅga · 13 articles articles.

ZAPPY: The three-city pilgrimage?

PROF. BOLT: Tristhalī yātrā: Kāśī for the Lord, Prayāga for the rivers, Gayā for the ancestors.

Kāśī, Prayāga, Gayā: the tristhalī.

ŚRĪRAṄGAPAṬṆA’S PAŚCIMAVĀHINĪ

Karnataka’s place of offering

Flowing west. At Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa the Kāverī briefly flows westward (paścima-vāhinī), unusual for a river that runs east to the sea. Such places are considered especially holy.

Rites for the departed. Families from across old Mysuru come here to perform śrāddha, immerse ashes and offer tarpaṇa, especially in Pitṛ Pakṣa and on Mahālaya Amāvāsyā.

Raṅganātha. Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa is also the first of the Triraṅga temples; see our Triraṅga series · 4 articles.

Elsewhere in Karnataka. Gokarṇa on the coast is another place of śrāddha; see our Gokarṇa article.

ZAPPY: A river flowing the “wrong” way is holy?

PROF. BOLT: Rivers turning west or north are counted special in many places. At Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa it is Mysuru’s own Gayā.

Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa: Mysuru’s place of offering.

BRAHMA KAPĀLA AT BADRĪNĀTH

The Himalayan place of offering

On the Alakanandā. At Badrīnāth, the Brahma Kapāla ghāṭ on the Alakanandā river is a famous place for śrāddha.

Final offering. Some traditions say offering piṇḍa here frees the ancestors completely.

The story. The name links to Brahmā’s skull, which fell here from Śiva’s hand.

Char Dhām. See our Char Dhām series · 8 articles.

ZAPPY: Śiva had Brahmā’s skull in his hand?

PROF. BOLT: A Purāṇic story: it stuck to his hand until it fell at this holy place.

Badrīnāth’s Brahma Kapāla.

RĀMEŚVARAM AND THE SEA

Tīrtha baths and offerings

Rāma’s place. Rāmeśvaram, where Rāma worshipped Śiva, is a major place of ancestral rites, at the sea (Agni Tīrtham).

Twenty-two wells. Pilgrims bathe in the temple’s sacred wells before worship.

A pair. Many families combine Kāśī and Rāmeśvaram in one lifetime’s pilgrimage.

Our series. See our Jyotirliṅga series · 13 articles.

ZAPPY: Kāśī and Rāmeśvaram together?

PROF. BOLT: North and south: a traditional pairing. Pilgrims carry Gaṅgā water to Rāmeśvaram.

Rāmeśvaram: offerings at the sea.

GOKARṆA

Karnataka’s coast

Mahābaleśvara. Gokarṇa’s Ātmaliṅga temple on the coast is a major Karnataka pilgrimage.

Ancestral rites. Families come for śrāddha and the immersion of ashes at the sea.

Kāśī of the south. Gokarṇa is sometimes called Dakṣiṇa Kāśī.

Our article. See Gokarṇa.

ZAPPY: Another “Kāśī of the south”?

PROF. BOLT: Several places claim the title. Gokarṇa’s claim is ancient.

Gokarṇa: the coast’s Kāśī.

ANYWHERE WITH FAITH

When you cannot travel

Faith first. The texts say the power of śrāddha is in śraddhā, faith. The rite at home is complete.

Facing south. At home, face south, the ancestors’ direction.

Local temples. Many temples offer śrāddha arrangements for families.

Pilgrimage when possible. The holy places add devotion; they are not required.

ZAPPY: So Gayā is not compulsory?

PROF. BOLT: It is highly praised, not compulsory. Love at home reaches the ancestors too.

Any place, with faith.
