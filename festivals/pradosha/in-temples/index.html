Nandi, the Crescent Path and the Procession · explained and illustrated

Between Nandi’s horns

HOW THIS WORKS: Nandi’s abhiṣeka, seeing Śiva between the horns, the crescent-shaped circuit, the procession, and Pradoṣa in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu.

WHERE WE ARE

Pradoṣa is most alive in temples. At twilight on Trayodaśī, Śiva temples across South India are packed: Nandi is bathed first, the liṅga receives a long abhiṣeka, devotees walk an unusual back-and-forth circuit, and the festival image of Śiva and Pārvatī rides out on Nandi.

This part explains what you will see, and why.

ZAPPY: Why is it so crowded?

PROF. BOLT: Because everyone wants to see Nandi’s abhiṣeka and Śiva between his horns at that one hour.

NANDI’S ABHIṢEKA

The first and greatest

At twilight. As Pradoṣa begins, priests bathe the Nandi statue facing the sanctum with milk, curd, honey, turmeric, sandal, tender coconut and holy water, one after another.

Decoration. Nandi is then dressed with flowers, garlands and often a silver kavaca (cover), and lamps are waved.

Why first. Nandi carried the poison and was dazed by it; Śiva danced between his horns (Part 2). Honouring Nandi first honours that story.

Offerings to Nandi. Devotees bring green grass, rice and jaggery, and bilva for him.

ZAPPY: The bull gets a bigger bath than God?

PROF. BOLT: At Pradoṣa, Nandi’s bath is the famous part. Then the Lord’s.

At twilight, Nandi is bathed first.

ŚIVA BETWEEN THE HORNS

The view from Nandi

The view. Devotees stand behind Nandi and look at the liṅga through the space between his horns: seeing Śiva as Nandi sees him, at the hour of the dance.

A gesture. Some touch Nandi’s horns with thumb and forefinger and look through.

Whispering. A popular custom is to whisper one’s prayer into Nandi’s ear, trusting him to carry it to Śiva.

Not in all regions. These gestures are strongest in Tamil and Karnataka temples.

ZAPPY: Whisper in a statue’s ear?

PROF. BOLT: In Nandi’s ear. He is Śiva’s gatekeeper; devotees ask him to pass it on.

Śiva seen between Nandi’s horns.

THE CRESCENT PATH

Somasūtra pradakṣiṇa

Not a full circle. Normally devotees circle a shrine clockwise. At Pradoṣa many South Indian temples follow the somasūtra pradakṣiṇa, whose path is shaped like a crescent moon (soma).

The outlet. The water from Śiva’s abhiṣeka drains through a spout (gomukhī or somasūtra) on the north side. Devotees do not step across it.

The pattern. Starting at Nandi, one goes round to the outlet, turns back without crossing it, returns past Nandi to the other side, and so on, going back and forth, often three times. Temples teach their own exact sequence.

Why. Tradition says the abhiṣeka water is too sacred to cross; the back-and-forth is also linked to the gods’ panicked running to and fro around Kailāsa when the poison rose.

ZAPPY: So you go back and forth instead of round?

PROF. BOLT: Like the gods running to and fro in their fear, until they found Śiva. Ask the temple priest for their sequence.

A crescent, never crossing the sacred water.

THE PROCESSION

Śiva and Pārvatī on Nandi

Pradoṣa mūrti. In many Tamil and Karnataka temples the festival bronzes of Śiva and Pārvatī, seated together on Nandi (Riṣabhārūḍha), are carried around the inner corridor after the abhiṣeka.

Music. Nāgasvaram and drums accompany them; devotees follow.

Seen at twilight. The procession recreates the scene on Kailāsa: Śiva and Gaurī, with all beings gathered (Part 4).

Then the kathā. Temples may read or explain the Pradoṣa story afterwards.

ZAPPY: They ride Nandi around the temple?

PROF. BOLT: In bronze, on a decorated Nandi vāhana. It’s the high point of the evening.

Śiva and Pārvatī ride Nandi round the temple.

IN KARNATAKA

Where to keep Pradoṣa

Everywhere. Almost every Śiva temple in Karnataka marks Pradoṣa, from village shrines to the great kṣetras.

Nañjanagūḍu. The Śrīkaṇṭheśvara temple of Nañjuṇḍa, “the one who swallowed the poison”, near Mysuru, is a natural place for Pradoṣa; see our article.

On the coast. Gokarṇa and Murudeśvara draw crowds on Pradoṣa evenings.

In Bengaluru and Mysuru. City Śiva temples hold Nandi abhiṣeka at Pradoṣa; many publish their times.

ZAPPY: Nañjanagūḍu is the poison temple?

PROF. BOLT: Nañjuṇḍa means “one who ate the poison”: the very story of Pradoṣa, in Mysuru’s backyard.

Karnataka’s Pradoṣa: Nañjuṇḍa above all.

IN TAMIL NADU

Where Pradoṣam is a festival

Pradōṣam. In Tamil Nadu, Pradōṣam is one of the busiest regular days in Śiva temples, with special Nandi worship, somasūtra circuits and processions.

Great temples. Tiruvaṇṇāmalai, Chidambaram, Madurai, Thanjavur’s great Nandi and many more draw large crowds.

Tanjore Nandi. The huge monolithic Nandi of the Bṛhadīśvara temple at Thanjavur receives a spectacular Pradoṣa abhiṣeka; see our Thanjavur article.

The model. Much of what Karnataka temples do at Pradoṣa follows this Tamil Śaiva pattern.

ZAPPY: Thanjavur’s Nandi is huge, isn’t it?

PROF. BOLT: One of the largest in India, carved from a single stone. Its Pradoṣa bath is a sight.

Tamil Pradōṣam: the great Nandis bathed.

AT THE TEMPLE

A few customs

Arrive early. Pradoṣa is crowded; come before sunset to see Nandi’s abhiṣeka.

Do not stand between. By custom, devotees avoid standing directly between Nandi and the liṅga, so as not to block Nandi’s gaze.

Offerings. Bring milk, bilva, flowers, or simply a lamp’s oil. Temples often prefer sealed milk packets for abhiṣeka.

Follow the temple. Each temple has its own order; watch, ask, and follow the priests.

ZAPPY: Why not stand between them?

PROF. BOLT: Nandi is always gazing at Śiva. Don’t block his view.

Come early, don’t block Nandi’s gaze.

PRADOṢA AT HOME

When you cannot reach a temple

A small shrine. A picture of Śiva or a small liṅga, a lamp, water, and bilva or any white flower is enough.

The same order. Remember Nandi (a picture or simply a bow), offer water or milk to Śiva, flowers, the lamp, and chant.

The story. Read the vrata kathā aloud with the family.

Online darśan. Many temples stream their Pradoṣa abhiṣeka; watching with attention at the hour is a devotion too.

ZAPPY: Is home worship as good as the temple?

PROF. BOLT: The texts praise the temple, but they praise sincerity more. Śiva is the same everywhere.

A lamp and water at twilight: Pradoṣa at home.
