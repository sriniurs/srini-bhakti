RATHA SAPTAMĪ

The sun’s chariot turns north

FESTIVALS · A REFERENCE GUIDE

FOREWORD

On the seventh day of the bright half of Māgha, the sun god turns his seven-horsed chariot towards the north. India greets him with a dawn bath under seven arka leaves, water offered to the rising sun, and a chariot drawn in rangoli at the door.

This guide covers the stories, the bath, Tirumala’s one-day Brahmotsava, and the sun temples from Konark to Melukōṭe.

Oṃ sūryāya namaḥ,

AT A GLANCE

The sun’s birthday

2027Saturday 13 February
Also calledSūrya Jayantī, Māgha Saptamī
MeaningThe sun turns his chariot north; the god of light is honoured
BathAt dawn, with seven arka leaves

KarnatakaProcessions at Melukōṭe and Śrīraṅgapaṭṭaṇa

ZAPPY: Why seven?

PROF. BOLT: Seven horses pull the sun’s chariot, Zappy. Seventh day, seven leaves, seven vāhanas.

Ratha Saptamī falls on the seventh day of the bright half of Māgha, in late January or February; in 2027 on Saturday 13 February. It is kept as Sūrya Jayantī, the birthday of the sun god, and as the day his seven-horsed chariot turns towards the north.

People bathe at dawn with arka leaves and offer water to the rising sun. Tirumala celebrates a one-day Brahmotsava, with Veṅkaṭeśvara riding seven vāhanas between sunrise and night.

THE SUN’S CHARIOT

One wheel, seven horses
CharioteerAruṇa, the dawn, Garuḍa’s brother
HorsesSeven, said to be the seven Vedic metres
WheelOne, by the Purāṇas: the year
CourseTurned northward after Saṅkrānti
Ratha“Chariot”: hence Ratha Saptamī
KonarkThe great sun temple built as a stone chariot

ZAPPY: A chariot with one wheel?

PROF. BOLT: It’s the wheel of time, Zappy. Spokes are the seasons; the horses are the hymns that carry him.

In the Purāṇas, Sūrya rides a chariot with one wheel, driven by Aruṇa, the dawn, and pulled by seven horses, often identified with the seven Vedic metres. The one wheel is read as the year, its spokes as the seasons and months.

Ratha means chariot. The greatest image of it in stone is the 13th-century Sun Temple at Konark in Odisha, built as a giant chariot with carved wheels and horses.

SŪRYA JAYANTĪ

Birth of the sun
ParentsKaśyapa and Aditi, in Purāṇic genealogy
NameĀditya: son of Aditi
DayRatha Saptamī kept as his birthday
SaptaSeventh tithi sacred to Sūrya every month
SinsWorship said to remove seven kinds of sin
Next dayBhīṣmāṣṭamī, the day of Bhīṣma’s passing

ZAPPY: Seven kinds of sin?

PROF. BOLT: Knowing and unknowing, by word, body and mind, in this life and past ones. A neat list, from the almanacs.

Purāṇic genealogy makes Sūrya a son of the sage Kaśyapa and Aditi, hence his name Āditya. Ratha Saptamī is kept as his birthday, and the seventh tithi of every fortnight is sacred to him.

Almanacs say worship on this day removes seven kinds of sin: committed knowingly or unknowingly, by word, body or mind, in this birth or earlier ones. The next day, Māgha śukla aṣṭamī, is Bhīṣmāṣṭamī, remembered as the day Bhīṣma left his body in uttarāyaṇa.

THE DAWN BATH

Arka leaves on head and limbs
TimeAruṇodaya, before sunrise
LeavesArka (ekka in Kannada, erukku in Tamil, jilleḍu in Telugu)
PlacementOne on the head, two on the shoulders, two on the knees, two on the feet
Some addAkṣata (rice) and a pinch of turmeric
ThenArghya (water offered to the rising sun)
RiversMany bathe in rivers or the sea

ZAPPY: What is arka?

PROF. BOLT: A tough wild shrub with big grey-green leaves. It’s the sun’s plant, as tulasī is Viṣṇu’s.

The central rite is a bath at aruṇodaya, before sunrise, with seven leaves of arka, Calotropis gigantea: one on the head, two on the shoulders, two on the knees and two on the feet. Some families add rice grains or turmeric.

Arka is called ekka in Kannada, erukku in Tamil and jilleḍu in Telugu; its name is also a name of the sun. After the bath, people offer arghya, water from cupped hands, to the rising sun.

AT HOME

Rangoli and offerings
RangoliA chariot with seven horses drawn at the door
NaivedyaMilk boiled over, sweet pongal, sometimes on a cow-dung fire in the sun
HymnsĀditya Hṛdayam; Sūrya namaskāra
LampIn some places a lamp carried on the head for the bath
FastingSome keep a light fast
ColourRed flowers, red sandal

ZAPPY: What is the Āditya Hṛdayam?

PROF. BOLT: A hymn to the sun in the Rāmāyaṇa, taught to Rāma before his battle. A good one for a future guide.

At home, a rangoli of the sun’s chariot with seven horses is drawn at the door. In some regions, milk is boiled until it overflows, sometimes on a fire of cow-dung cakes in the open sun, and sweet pongal is offered.

Many recite the Āditya Hṛdayam from the Rāmāyaṇa, or perform Sūrya namaskāra. Customs vary: in some places a small lamp is carried on the head during the bath.

TIRUMALA

The one-day Brahmotsava
DeityMalayappasvāmī with Śrīdevī and Bhūdevī
SevenSūryaprabhā, Chinna Śeṣa, Garuḍa, Hanumān, Kalpavṛkṣa, Sarvabhūpāla, Candraprabhā
FirstSūryaprabhā at sunrise: the sun’s halo
LastCandraprabhā at night: the moon’s halo
AlsoCakrasnāna, the bath of the discus, at midday
AgeKept since the 1560s, temple records say

ZAPPY: All seven in one day?

PROF. BOLT: Dawn to night. In the big Brahmotsava he rides only one or two a day.

At Tirumala, the utsava image of Veṅkaṭeśvara, Malayappasvāmī, with Śrīdevī and Bhūdevī, is taken round the four Māḍa streets on seven vāhanas in one day: Sūryaprabhā at sunrise, then Chinna Śeṣa, Garuḍa, Hanumān, Kalpavṛkṣa and Sarvabhūpāla, ending on Candraprabhā at night. A cakrasnāna, the ritual bath of the discus, takes place at midday.

Because so many vāhanas are crowded into one day, it is called the one-day Brahmotsava. Reports cite temple records of its observance from the 1560s.

ZAPPY: Any near Mysuru?

PROF. BOLT: Melukōṭe and Śrīraṅgapaṭṭaṇa both keep it grandly. An hour’s drive.

Sun temples celebrate it grandly. At Arasavalli near Srikakulam, the Sūryanārāyaṇa temple’s festival was declared a three-day state festival by Andhra Pradesh in December 2024. Konark in Odisha, Modhera in Gujarat and Sūryanār Kōvil near Kumbakōṇam are other great sun shrines.

Near Mysuru, Melukōṭe holds a procession of the Sūryamaṇḍala, and the Raṅganātha temple at Śrīraṅgapaṭṭaṇa keeps a Ratha Saptamī festival.

THE MEANING

Light, health and time
SeasonStart of spring warmth; end of winter
HealthSunlight and the early bath, for strength
TimeThe sun as the maker of days, months and years
GratitudeLife depends on him; the bath and arghya say thanks
GāyatrīThe Vedic Gāyatrī is itself a prayer to Savitṛ, the sun
SimpleNo idol needed: just face the rising sun

ZAPPY: Can I just say thanks to the sun?

PROF. BOLT: That’s the whole festival in one sentence, Zappy.

Ratha Saptamī comes as winter gives way to the first warmth of spring. It thanks the sun as the giver of light, health and time, the maker of days and years.

It needs nothing elaborate: an early bath and water offered to the rising sun. The Vedic Gāyatrī itself is addressed to Savitṛ, the sun as inspirer.
