MUKKOṬI · THE NORTHERN GATE · GĪTĀ JAYANTĪ

Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī

The Day the Northern Gate Opens · explained and illustrated

The gate opens before dawn

HOW THIS WORKS: What Ekādaśī is and why this one is the greatest, the northern gate and its story, Śrīraṅgam’s songs and Tirumala, the fast and vigil, and three verses for the day.

WHERE WE ARE

Of the twenty-four Ekādaśīs of the year, one is the greatest in the South: Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī, the bright eleventh of Mārgaśīrṣa, in the month of Dhanus. Before dawn, in every Viṣṇu temple, a gate on the northern side that stays shut all year is thrown open: the Vaikuṇṭha dvāra, the door of Viṣṇu’s heaven. Millions queue through the night at Tirumala, Śrīraṅgam and their own town temples to pass through it.

The same day is Mokṣadā Ekādaśī in the north and Gītā Jayantī, the day the Gītā was spoken. This article explains the Ekādaśī itself and its goddess, the best of months, the gate and why it opens, the twenty-one-day festival of Śrīraṅgam with the songs of the Āḻvārs, Tirumala, the fast and the vigil, and gives three verses for the day.

WHAT IS EKĀDAŚĪ?

The eleventh day and its fast

Twice a month. Ekādaśī, the eleventh lunar day of each fortnight, comes about twenty-four times a year. Each is sacred to Viṣṇu and has its own name and story; read our Ekādaśī series · 28 articles for all of them.

The fast. Grains, rice above all, are given up; many fast fully, some without water. The night is kept in vigil, and the fast is broken on Dvādaśī, the twelfth day, within a fixed morning window (pāraṇa).

Who keeps it. Ekādaśī is the most widely kept fast in India: Vaiṣṇavas above all, but also Śaiva and Smārta homes, and many who keep no other fast. Karnataka’s Haridāsas sang its praise; Maharashtra’s Vārkarīs walk to Paṇḍharpur for it.

The greatest of them. In the South the Ekādaśī of the bright half of Mārgaśīrṣa, in Dhanurmāsa, is the one when Vaikuṇṭha’s gate opens. It is said to carry the merit of all the others.

ZAPPY: Why does this one count most?

PROF. BOLT: Of twenty-four Ekādaśīs a year, this is the one when the gate is opened, Zappy. The rest are steps; this is the door.

Twenty-four steps, one door.

THE GODDESS EKĀDAŚĪ

Why the eleventh day is holy

The story

Padma Purāṇa (Uttara Khaṇḍa), the Ekādaśī māhātmya; told in full in our Utpannā Ekādaśī article.

The demon Mura had driven the gods from heaven. Viṣṇu fought him for ages, then, weary, withdrew to rest in a cave called Haimavatī.
Mura followed to kill the sleeping Lord. At that moment a radiant goddess sprang from Viṣṇu’s body, armed and blazing, and challenged the demon.
With a single sound of “huṃ” she reduced Mura to ashes.
Viṣṇu woke and asked who she was. Because she had appeared on the eleventh lunar day, he named her Ekādaśī.
He promised that whoever fasts on her day would be freed from sin and reach his abode. So every Ekādaśī is her day, and Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī the greatest of them.

ZAPPY: A goddess called Ekādaśī?

PROF. BOLT: Born from Viṣṇu’s body while he slept, Zappy. She saved him, and he made her day holy.

The day is a goddess.

The best of months. In the Gītā’s tenth chapter Kṛṣṇa names the best of each kind and calls himself Mārgaśīrṣa among months. The solar month of Dhanus, which overlaps it, is held to be the gods’ dawn: the six months of the sun’s southward course are their night. So the month is given to dawn worship (read our Dhanurmāsa · 4 articles series), and its Ekādaśī opens the gate.

THE NORTHERN GATE

The Vaikuṇṭha dvāra

What it is. On the north side of the temple a door or passage is kept shut all year. Before dawn on Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī it is opened, and devotees enter or leave the sanctum’s circuit through it: the Vaikuṇṭha dvāra, called Paramapada vāsal at Śrīraṅgam, Svargavāsal in Tamil.

What it means. A Viṣṇu temple is built as an image of Vaikuṇṭha, and the northern gate stands for the passage from this world to his abode. Passing through it is a foretaste of liberation, staged for the living.

Everywhere. Temples that have no permanent north gate set up a decorated one for the day, so that devotees in every town can walk through. In Karnataka the Veṅkaṭeśvara temples of Bengaluru, Mēlukōṭe, Śrīraṅgapaṭṇa and countless village temples do so.

The welcome. Śrīvaiṣṇavas recall Nammāḻvār’s hymn of the soul’s arrival in Vaikuṇṭha, where the gods line the way and the doors are thrown open. The temple rite enacts that welcome.

ZAPPY: Why the north?

PROF. BOLT: North is the direction of the gods and of the sun’s bright path, Zappy. Read our Saṅkrānti article for why.

The door that opens once a year.

WHY THE GATE OPENS

The story told in temples

The story

A South Indian temple tradition; the Purāṇic support is indirect.

During Viṣṇu’s cosmic sleep two demons, Madhu and Kaiṭabha, were born from the wax of his ear and set out to destroy Brahmā on the lotus.
Viṣṇu woke and fought them for five thousand years, the Purāṇas say, and finally slew them on his thighs. (The Devī Māhātmya tells the same battle; read our Devī Māhātmya · 19 parts.)
The temple telling continues: touched by the Lord, the demons repented and asked for one thing: that a gate of Vaikuṇṭha be opened, and that whoever saw Viṣṇu coming through it on this day should reach his abode.
Viṣṇu granted it. So the northern gate opens on Mārgaśīrṣa Śukla Ekādaśī.
The name Mukkoṭi, “three crores”, says that the thirty-three crore gods throng to Vaikuṇṭha to see Viṣṇu this day; devotees on earth join them.

ZAPPY: The demons asked for heaven’s gate for everyone?

PROF. BOLT: That’s the beauty of the telling, Zappy. Even the defeated get a door, and they hold it open for us.

Even demons hold the door.

ŚRĪRAṄGAM

The twenty-one days of song

The Adhyayana Utsava. At Śrīraṅgam the day sits in the middle of a twenty-one-day festival: ten days of Pagal Pattu (day) and ten of Rā Pattu (night), in which the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham, the four thousand Tamil verses of the Āḻvārs, is recited in order before Raṅganātha.

Mohinī. On the day before, the festival image, Namperumāḷ, appears as Mohinī, the enchantress who won the nectar for the gods.

The gate. Before dawn on the Ekādaśī, Namperumāḷ, in a dazzling armour of jewels, is carried through the Paramapada vāsal, and the crowds follow. The Rā Pattu nights then carry Nammāḻvār’s Tiruvāymoḻi.

Nammāḻvār’s mokṣa. The festival ends with the enactment of the poet-saint Nammāḻvār reaching Vaikuṇṭha, a moving rite in which his image is carried to the Lord’s feet and covered with tulasī. Tradition credits the tenth-century ācārya Nāthamuni with recovering the hymns and starting the festival. See our Triraṅga · 4 articles series.

ZAPPY: Twenty-one days for one day?

PROF. BOLT: The day is the peak, Zappy. The festival is the mountain.

Four thousand verses, one gate.

THE ARAIYAR AND THE ĀḺVĀRS

Song, dance and the poets of Viṣṇu

🎭 Araiyar sēvai

A hereditary art of singing and enacting the Āḻvārs’ hymns with gesture and commentary, wearing tall caps, before the deity. It survives in only a few temples: Śrīraṅgam, Śrīvilliputtūr, Āḻvār Tirunagari.

📜 The Āḻvārs

Twelve Tamil poet-saints (sixth to ninth centuries by common dating) whose hymns to Viṣṇu were gathered as the Divya Prabandham; Āṇḍāḷ of the Tiruppāvai is one of them.

🙏 Nammāḻvār

The greatest of them, author of the Tiruvāymoḻi, called the “Tamil Veda” in Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition.

🎶 In Karnataka

Mēlukōṭe, where Rāmānujācārya lived for years, keeps the Śrīvaiṣṇava calendar with the Prabandham recited before Celuvanārāyaṇa.

ZAPPY: Dance as worship?

PROF. BOLT: The Āḻvārs sang for God, Zappy; the Araiyars still sing them back to him, with their hands too.

Poetry sung back to God.

TIRUMALA AND THE TELUGU COUNTRY

Mukkoṭi Ekādaśī

The passage. At Tirumala the Vaikuṇṭha dvāram is a passage around the sanctum, normally closed. The temple authority now keeps it open for ten days from Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī, with tokens issued in advance, so that more pilgrims can pass through.

Dvādaśī. The next morning the Sudarśana cakra is bathed in the Svāmi Puṣkariṇī tank (cakrasnāna), and pilgrims bathe with it.

Bhadrācalam. In Telaṅgāṇa the Rāma temple of Bhadrācalam holds a famous gate-opening, the uttara dvāra darśana, with Rāma seen through the northern gate.

The crowds. Tirumala’s Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī crowds are among the largest single-day gatherings at any temple; Karnataka pilgrims travel by the busload.

ZAPPY: Ten days instead of one?

PROF. BOLT: So everyone gets a turn, Zappy. The gate is generous now.

The seven hills open the door.

The vigil verse. Through the Ekādaśī night devotees keep awake with Viṣṇu’s names. This dhyāna verse, recited before the Viṣṇu Sahasranāma, gives the image to hold: Viṣṇu at peace on Śeṣa, the one who removes the fear of the round of births, which is exactly what the gate promises.

THE FAST AND THE VIGIL

How the day is kept

Daśamī. The evening before, a single light meal, no grains after.

Ekādaśī. A complete fast for the strong, fruit and milk for others; no rice in any case. Before dawn the gate darśan; through the day Viṣṇu’s names, the Sahasranāma, the Gītā.

Jāgaraṇa. The night is kept awake with bhajans and readings, at home or in the temple. In Karnataka the Haridāsa songs of Purandara Dāsa and Kanaka Dāsa fill the night.

Dvādaśī pāraṇa. The fast is broken next morning in the time window the almanac gives, often with a simple meal of rice, greens and āmalaka, after feeding others first. Many Mādhva homes make a special Dvādaśī meal with agase (flax) and greens.

ZAPPY: Can children fast?

PROF. BOLT: A small fast, Zappy: no rice, one meal, a few names. The vigil matters more than the hunger.

Fast, wake, and break it well.

Gītā Jayantī. The same Mārgaśīrṣa Ekādaśī is kept as the day Kṛṣṇa spoke the Gītā on the field of Kurukṣetra, and many read the whole Gītā on it. This verse, which Śrīvaiṣṇavas call the carama śloka, the final verse, is the Gītā’s last promise, and it matches the gate: mokṣayiṣyāmi, I will free you.

WHAT THE GATE MEANS

Liberation made visible

A picture of mokṣa. The Vaikuṇṭha dvāra turns the most abstract idea of Hindu thought, liberation, into a step you can take. Walking through it does not by itself free anyone; it reminds us what the whole of devotion is for. (Interpretation.)

For everyone. In the temple story even the demons Madhu and Kaiṭabha ask for the gate to open for all. The queue is the most democratic sight in India: rich and poor, old and young, in one line through one door.

Grace. The Gītā’s carama śloka and the gate say the same: the door is opened from the other side. Śrīvaiṣṇava teachers make this the heart of their theology. (Interpretation.)

Dawn. The gate opens before sunrise, in the gods’ dawn month: the whole day is about waking up.

ZAPPY: If I could do one thing for Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī?

PROF. BOLT: Skip the rice, stay awake for one hour of Viṣṇu’s names, and walk through any temple’s north gate at dawn, Zappy, with your whole attention.

The door opened from the other side.
