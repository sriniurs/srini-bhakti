Aṅgārakī Caturthī

When Saṅkaṣṭī Falls on a Tuesday · explained and illustrated

When Saṅkaṣṭī falls on a Tuesday

HOW THIS WORKS: Who Aṅgāraka is, the story of his boon, why the day is prized, the temple crowds, and how it is kept.

WHERE WE ARE

When Saṅkaṣṭī falls on a Tuesday it is called Aṅgārakī, after Aṅgāraka, the planet Mars, lord of Tuesday. It is the most prized Saṅkaṣṭī of all, and Gaṇeśa temples are crowded from dawn.

This part tells who Aṅgāraka is, how he won the day for his name, and how Aṅgārakī is kept.

ZAPPY: Mars and Gaṇeśa? Odd pair.

PROF. BOLT: Both red, both connected with Earth and Śiva in the stories. And Mars was a devotee.

WHO IS AṄGĀRAKA

Mars, son of the Earth

The name. Aṅgāraka means “glowing coal”, for the planet’s red colour. He is also Maṅgala (“auspicious”) and Bhauma (“son of Bhūmi”, the Earth).

Birth. Tellings differ. Many make him the son of the Earth goddess; one has him born from a drop of Śiva’s sweat that fell to earth; the Aṅgārakī story makes the sage Bharadvāja his father and the Earth his foster-mother.

A navagraha. One of the nine planetary deities, lord of Tuesday (Maṅgalavāra).

Red. Like Gaṇeśa’s favourite flowers and sindūra.

Mars: the glowing coal.

THE STORY OF AṄGĀRAKĪ

How Mars won the day

The story

As commonly told from the Gaṇeśa Purāṇa; details vary.

A red child, the son of the sage Bharadvāja, was raised by the Earth goddess until he was seven, then returned to his father.
Bharadvāja taught him the Vedas and initiated him into Gaṇeśa’s mantra; the boy went to the banks of the Narmadā to do penance.
After long penance Gaṇeśa appeared to him, on the dark fourth of Māgha, which was a Tuesday.
Asked for a boon, the boy wished only that his name be joined to Gaṇeśa’s forever.
Gaṇeśa granted it: a caturthī falling on a Tuesday would be called Aṅgārakī, and its keepers would have their wishes fulfilled. The boy became the planet Maṅgala.

ZAPPY: He just asked for his name next to Gaṇeśa’s?

PROF. BOLT: The humblest wish in the story, and it gave him the most famous day of the Gaṇeśa calendar.

A boy’s wish: my name beside yours.

WHY IT IS PRIZED

The merit of a Tuesday Saṅkaṣṭī

The boon. Gaṇeśa’s promise to Aṅgāraka is the root of its fame.

Rarity. A Saṅkaṣṭī falls on a Tuesday only a few times in some years and not at all in others, which makes each one an occasion.

A popular claim. Many say one Aṅgārakī equals keeping all the Saṅkaṣṭīs of a year (or of many years). This is devotional praise; we mark it as such.

Maṅgala-doṣa. Some keep it to ease what astrologers call Mars affliction in a horoscope; that is a separate, astrological belief.

Rare, and richly promised.

MARS AND GAṆEŚA

Why they belong together

Colour. Both are red; red flowers and sindūra are offered to both.

Earth. Mars is the Earth’s son; Gaṇeśa’s mouse burrows in the earth, and his body was shaped from earth by Pārvatī in one telling.

Tuesday. Already a day of Gaṇeśa and Hanumān worship in many regions.

Obstacles. Mars is feared as a planet of conflict; Gaṇeśa removes obstacles. The pairing reads naturally. (Interpretation.)

Two red ones, one Tuesday.

AṄGĀRAKĪ AT THE TEMPLES

The great crowds

Maharashtra. Siddhivināyaka in Mumbai and the eight Aṣṭavināyaka shrines draw vast crowds, many queuing from the night before. See our Aṣṭavināyaka guide.

Karnataka. Gaṇapati temples hold special abhiṣeka and pūjā; see Idagunji.

Tamil Nadu. Piḷḷaiyār temples, such as Piḷḷaiyārpaṭṭi, mark the day.

Plan. If you go, go early, carry water, and be patient; the darśana is often brief.

Queues from before dawn.

KEEPING AṄGĀRAKĪ

The same vow, with care

The vow. Kept exactly like any Saṅkaṣṭī: fast, evening pūjā, the month’s story, moonrise and arghya. See Part 2.

Extra. Red flowers and dūrvā in abundance; many recite the Gaṇapati Atharvaśīrṣa or the Saṅkaṭanāśana Stotra.

Which story. The month’s own kathā, and the Aṅgāraka story above.

Even a little. Those who cannot fast often offer a few blades of dūrvā at a temple; that too is counted.

Same vow, more red.

THE BRIGHT-FORTNIGHT AṄGĀRAKĪ

A Tuesday Vināyaka Caturthī

Also called Aṅgārakī. A Vināyaka Caturthī (bright fourth) that falls on a Tuesday is also called Aṅgārakī in some calendars, especially in Maharashtra.

The difference. On it there is no moonrise vow, and the moon is not specially sought.

Less common usage. When people say “Aṅgārakī” without qualification, they usually mean the Saṅkaṣṭī.

Check. Your pañcāṅga will say which is which.

Usually means the dark-fortnight one.

TELLING CHILDREN

Mars’s wish

The story. A red boy, raised by Mother Earth, prayed to Gaṇeśa by a river for years.

Ask them. If Gaṇeśa offered you anything, what would you ask?

The answer. The boy asked only to have his name beside Gaṇeśa’s. And he got the most famous Tuesday of all.

The point. Humble wishes, generously granted.

Ask humbly, receive richly.
