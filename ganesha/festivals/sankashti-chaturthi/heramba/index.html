WHERE WE ARE

This article is about Heramba Saṅkaṣṭī, the Saṅkaṣṭī of Śrāvaṇa (in the northern calendar, Bhādrapada). Gaṇeśa is worshipped this month as Heramba Mahāgaṇapati, seated on the Gaṇapati pīṭha. The name means “protector of the helpless”, as tradition explains the name; in images, five-headed and riding a lion.

Here is the form and its name, the seat, who tells the story, the story itself, its characters, what it teaches and promises, the offering, and how the day is kept. Before it comes Gajānana; after it, Vighnarāja.

THE NAME HERAMBA

“protector of the helpless”, as tradition explains the name; in images, five-headed and riding a lion

Heramba. The name means “protector of the helpless”, as tradition explains the name; in images, five-headed and riding a lion.

The full form. The monthly lists call him Heramba Mahāgaṇapati this month.

A traditional gloss. “Heramba” is explained as protector (ramba) of the helpless (he); a pious etymology, but a fitting one here.

In the stotra. This name is not among the twelve of the Saṅkaṭanāśana Stotra; the monthly list and the hymn overlap only in part.

Heramba: “protector of the helpless”.

THE SEAT OF THE MONTH

The pīṭha

The Gaṇapati pīṭha. Each month Gaṇeśa is invoked on a different seat (pīṭha) in the pūjā; this month’s is the Gaṇapati pīṭha.

What a pīṭha is. In formal worship the deity is first given a seat, often visualised as a lotus with petals, before being invoked into the image.

The thirteen seats. They range from Gaṇeśa’s own seats to those of Viṣṇu, Śiva, Durgā, the Sun and Bhuvaneśvarī: Gaṇeśa received in the home of each deity in turn. (Interpretation.)

At home. Families without a priest simply name the seat in the saṅkalpa or invocation; the list is from the common monthly tables.

This month: Gaṇapati pīṭha.

WHO TELLS THE STORY

The frame

The teller. Pārvatī asks Gaṇeśa which form is worshipped this month; Gaṇeśa answers by retelling what Kṛṣṇa told Yudhiṣṭhira during the Pāṇḍavas’ exile.

The pattern. The vrata kathās are framed as questions and answers, so each carries the authority of the one who tells it.

The sources. These stories circulate in printed Saṅkaṣṭī vrata books; the common editions do not name a Purāṇa.

Read at the vow. The story is read or heard after the evening pūjā, before moonrise.

A story within a story.

THE STORY OF HERAMBA

The vrata kathā in order

The story

The great love story of Nala and Damayantī becomes, in the vrata book, a Saṅkaṣṭī story.

King Nala and his queen Damayantī lived happily until a curse fell on Nala.
Thieves took his elephants and horses, bandits looted his treasury, fire burned the rest; ministers deserted him and he lost the kingdom at dice.
The family was scattered: Nala tending horses in one town, Damayantī in another, their son elsewhere, all poor and sick.
In the forest Damayantī met the sage Śarabhaṅga, who told her to keep this Saṅkaṣṭī and worship Heramba.
She kept it each month; within three months she was reunited with Nala and their son, and the kingdom was restored.

ZAPPY: Isn’t Nala’s story in the Mahābhārata?

PROF. BOLT: Yes, told to Yudhiṣṭhira in exile. The vrata book adds the Saṅkaṣṭī thread; the epic itself doesn’t mention the vow.

THE OFFERING

What the vrata book names

This month. The vrata book names no special dish for this month.

Every month. Dūrvā (twenty-one blades), red flowers, a lamp, modakas or laḍḍus, and arghya to the moon at moonrise.

If you can’t. Simple offerings with devotion are enough; the stories themselves praise a widow’s cow-dung Gaṇeśa and laḍḍus made from alms.

Sharing. Many kathās ask that part of the offering go to a brahmin or guest before the devotee eats.

The month’s special offering.

WHAT IT PROMISES

Phala-śruti

The promise. Reunion of the separated and the return of what was lost.

Kṛṣṇa’s word. Most months close with Kṛṣṇa telling Yudhiṣṭhira that by this vow he will defeat his enemies and regain his kingdom.

Read with sense. The promises encourage; teachers say the real fruit is a mind that turns to Gaṇeśa in trouble rather than despair.

Hearing counts. The kathās say that even hearing the story with faith brings part of the merit.

The promise, and the real one.

KEEPING HERAMBA SAṄKAṢṬĪ

What is special this month

Special to this month. In the north this day is also Bahulā Caturthī, when cows and calves are honoured; families pray for their children.

The usual vow. Fast from sunrise at your level; evening pūjā with dūrvā and red flowers; read this story; wait for the moon; offer arghya; break the fast. Details are in Part 2.

On a Tuesday. If it falls on a Tuesday it is also Aṅgārakī; see Part 4.

Care. The sick, the old, pregnant women and children are traditionally excused from strict fasting.

Keep Heramba with its own touch.

WHEN IT FALLS

Two calendars

Karnataka. In the new-moon-ending (amānta) calendar used in Karnataka, Maharashtra and the south, it is the Saṅkaṣṭī of Śrāvaṇa.

The north. In the full-moon-ending (pūrṇimānta) calendar, the same day falls in Bhādrapada, because the dark fortnight there belongs to the next month.

Same day. Only the month name differs; the date is the same.

Your almanac. The pañcāṅga for your place gives the date and the moonrise.

Śrāvaṇa in Karnataka, Bhādrapada in the north.
