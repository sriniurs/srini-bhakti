ZAPPY: Eight more faces of Gaṇeśa?

PROF. BOLT: Eight more, Zappy: Vighna, Kṣipra, Heramba, Lakṣmī, Mahā, Vijaya, Nṛtya and Ūrdhva.

This guide describes forms 9 to 16 of the thirty-two forms of Gaṇapati: The lord of obstacles, the quick one, five-headed Heramba, Lakṣmī, the great Mahā, the victor, the dancer and Ūrdhva.

For each, it gives the meaning of the name, the colour, the arms, what the hands hold, any special features and the mount or seat, following the Śrītattvanidhi made for the Mysuru court.

Our drawings are simple sketches to help recognition; the hands and objects are listed in full in each table.

9. VIGHNA GAṆAPATI

Lord of obstacles
Name meansLord of obstacles
ColourGolden
Arms8
HoldsTusk, discus, flower-arrow, axe, conch, sugarcane, noose and goad
SpecialHis trunk holds a cluster of flowers
Mount or seatNot specified in the verse

ZAPPY: Isn’t every form lord of obstacles?

PROF. BOLT: Yes, but this one is named for it, with eight arms to deal with them.

Vighna Gaṇapati, “lord of obstacles”, is golden with eight arms, holding his tusk, a discus, a flower-arrow, an axe, a conch, sugarcane, a noose and a goad. His trunk holds a cluster of flowers.

The discus and conch are Viṣṇu’s; the flower-arrow and sugarcane recall Kāma and Lalitā. The form gathers powers from many gods.

10. KṢIPRA GAṆAPATI

The quick
Name meansThe quick
ColourRed
Arms4
HoldsTusk, goad, a wish-granting creeper and noose
SpecialHis trunk holds a pot of jewels
Mount or seatNot specified in the verse

ZAPPY: Quick at what?

PROF. BOLT: Quick to be pleased, and quick to grant. A god for people in a hurry.

Kṣipra Gaṇapati, “the quick”, is red and four-armed, holding his tusk, a goad, a sprig of the wish-granting creeper and a noose, with a pot of jewels in his trunk.

His name means he is easily pleased and quick to answer prayers.

11. HERAMBA GAṆAPATI

Protector of the weak; mother’s beloved
Name meansProtector of the weak; mother’s beloved
ColourFair (dark in some descriptions)
Arms10; 5 heads
HoldsRosary, citron, club, goad, noose, axe, rice-cake and tusk; one hand blesses, one grants fearlessness
SpecialFive heads; rides a lion
Mount or seatA lion

ZAPPY: Five heads and a lion!

PROF. BOLT: The grandest of them. Heramba is especially loved in Nepal.

Heramba Gaṇapati has five heads and ten arms and rides a lion. He holds a rosary, citron, club, goad, noose, axe, rice-cake and his tusk, and his remaining hands bless and grant fearlessness.

His colour is given as fair, though the description elsewhere calls him dark. The five-headed Heramba is especially popular in Nepal.

12. LAKṢMĪ GAṆAPATI

The fortunate
Name meansThe fortunate
ColourFair
Arms8
HoldsPomegranate, sword, wish-creeper, goad, parrot, noose and a pot of jewels; one hand grants boons
SpecialWith Siddhi and Buddhi
Mount or seatNot specified in the verse

ZAPPY: Lakṣmī is a goddess. Why is Gaṇeśa called that?

PROF. BOLT: Because this form gives prosperity. And he has both his consorts with him.

Lakṣmī Gaṇapati is fair, with eight arms, embracing Siddhi and Buddhi. He holds a pomegranate, a sword, a wish-creeper, a goad, a parrot, a noose and a pot of jewels, and his last hand grants boons.

He is the Gaṇeśa of prosperity, often worshipped together with Lakṣmī at Dīpāvali.

13. MAHĀ GAṆAPATI

The great
Name meansThe great
ColourRed
Arms10
HoldsTusk, pomegranate, club, sugarcane bow, discus, conch, noose, blue lotus, ear of paddy and pot of jewels
SpecialMoon on his head, three eyes; with his śakti holding a lotus
Mount or seatNot specified in the verse

ZAPPY: Is this the Mahāgaṇapati of Śrīvidyā?

PROF. BOLT: The same form. He’s worshipped first in the Śrīcakra worship, and there was a whole Gāṇapatya branch devoted to him.

Mahā Gaṇapati, “the great”, is red with ten arms, three eyes and the crescent moon on his head, with his śakti beside him holding a lotus. His hands hold the tusk, pomegranate, club, sugarcane bow, discus, conch, noose, blue lotus, paddy and a pot of jewels.

Ramachandra Rao notes that he is especially widely worshipped, with a tantric following of his own. In Śrīvidyā he is worshipped at the start of the Śrīcakra worship; our Śrīvidyā guide describes him.

14. VIJAYA GAṆAPATI

The victorious
Name meansThe victorious
ColourRed
Arms4
HoldsTusk, goad, noose and mango
SpecialRides a swift mouse
Mount or seatA mouse

ZAPPY: Victory on a mouse?

PROF. BOLT: A fast-trotting one, the verse says. Victory doesn’t always arrive on an elephant.

Vijaya Gaṇapati, “the victorious”, is red and four-armed, holding his tusk, a goad, a noose and a mango, and riding a briskly trotting mouse.

He is worshipped for success in undertakings.

15. NṚTYA GAṆAPATI

The dancer
Name meansThe dancer
ColourGolden
Arms4
HoldsTusk, goad, noose and axe; sometimes a sweet cake
SpecialDances under the wish-granting tree
Mount or seatNot specified in the verse

ZAPPY: Gaṇeśa dances?

PROF. BOLT: Like his father. Under the wish-granting tree, with a sweet in hand.

Nṛtya Gaṇapati is golden and four-armed, dancing beneath the wish-granting tree, holding his tusk, a goad, a noose and an axe, and sometimes a sweet cake.

The dancing Gaṇeśa is a favourite of sculptors and dancers, who invoke him before performing.

16. ŪRDHVA GAṆAPATI

The raised
Name meansThe raised
ColourGolden
Arms8
HoldsTusk, flower-arrow, lotus, water-lily, sugarcane bow, ear of paddy and club
SpecialWith his śakti on his lap
Mount or seatNot specified in the verse

ZAPPY: What’s ‘raised’ about him?

PROF. BOLT: Scholars aren’t agreed. It may mean exalted, or refer to his upright posture. He’s the last of the popular sixteen.

Ūrdhva Gaṇapati is golden with eight arms, holding his tusk, a flower-arrow, a lotus, a water-lily, a sugarcane bow, paddy and a club, with his śakti seated on his left thigh.

He is the sixteenth form; the first sixteen together are worshipped as the Ṣoḍaśa Gaṇapati.

ZAPPY: Which is my favourite?

PROF. BOLT: Read them again and see which one you remember tomorrow. That’s your answer.

Side by side, the eight forms show how a few elements, colour, arms, heads, consort and mount, combine to make distinct images.

At Nanjangūḍu these forms are carved on the outer walls of the Śrīkaṇṭheśvara temple, where they can be compared in stone.

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: The Śrītattvanidhi (Śivanidhi section), as summarised in Wikipedia, “Thirty-two forms of Ganesha” (with Martin-Dubost and Ramachandra Rao), whose meditation verses are attributed to the Mudgala Purāṇa; Alice Getty, Gaṇeśa (1936).

Sources differ / unverified: We paraphrase the meditation verses; we do not quote them. Colours in the verses and the paintings sometimes differ. Our drawings are simple sketches, not copies of the Mysuru paintings, and do not show every arm or object. Tantric forms are described, not explained in practice; seed mantras are named, not given.

What we leave out: This series explains names, stories, forms, history, philosophy, hymns, temples and festivals. It does not reproduce, assemble or decode initiatory mantras (such as the Mahāgaṇapati mūla-mantra), nor any nyāsa, dīkṣā or pūjā procedure: those are received from a teacher.

Meanings are our own plain-English renderings. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
