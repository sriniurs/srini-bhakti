108 NAMES OF GAṆEŚA

Oṃ gajānanāya namaḥ · twelve groups of nine

ŚRĪ GAṆEŚA AṢṬOTTARAŚATA-NĀMĀVALI

HOW THIS WORKS: All 108 names in one go for chanting, then every group of nine on two pages: the names with their meanings, then a deep dive into the stories behind them.

WHERE WE ARE

A nāmāvali is a garland of names, each offered with “oṃ … namaḥ”, often with a flower or a pinch of rice at every name. This one, of 108 names, is the Gaṇeśa nāmāvali most chanted in South Indian temples and homes.

Its exact textual source is uncertain, and many versions exist; we give it as commonly printed, with obvious typing errors corrected. The names are in the dative case, “to the one who…”; the meanings give the plain sense.

We group the names in nines, as they fall, and give each group a theme.

NAMES 1–9 · DEEP DIVE

HIS CHIEF NAMES

🔎 In context

The list opens with his best-known titles, gajānana, gaṇādhyakṣa, vighnarāja, vināyaka, and then some puzzles. Dvaimātura, “of two mothers”, recalls the birth stories in which another mother, Gaṅgā or the elephant-headed Mālinī, shares in his birth; dvimukha, “two-faced”, is explained in different ways, as facing both the world and the Absolute, or as uniting the human and the elephant.

📜 Looking deeper

This nāmāvali is the one most chanted in South Indian temples and homes, each name in the dative case framed by oṃ … namaḥ, “Om, salutation to…”. Its textual source is not certain; we give it as commonly printed. Kṛtin, “one who has done (well)”, means the skilful, the accomplished: a good name to end the first group.

🌱 For today

A name is a handle. Pick the one that fits your need today.

NAMES 10–18 · DEEP DIVE

LORD OF THE GODS

🔎 In context

The second group lifts him above the gods: chief of the gods (surādhyakṣa), slayer of their enemies (surārighna), great lord of the gaṇas (mahāgaṇapati), honoured by all (mānya). Mahākāla, “great Time”, is usually a name of Śiva; here it passes to his son.

📜 Looking deeper

Heramba is an old name of uncertain meaning, used especially for the five-headed form of Gaṇeśa who rides a lion, worshipped in Nepal and Bengal; our Forms guides describe him. Supradīpa, “shining brightly”, and sukhanidhi, “treasure of happiness”, keep the tone warm: power, yes, but power that gives light and joy.

🌱 For today

Strength used to protect is the kind worth praising.

NAMES 19–27 · DEEP DIVE

HIS BODY AND HIS MIGHT

🔎 In context

Group three is affectionate about his shape: hanging belly (lambajaṭhara), short neck (hrasvagrīva), great belly (mahodara). Then it turns to strength and wisdom: exuberant (madotkaṭa), great hero (mahāvīra), counsellor (mantrin), sweet-voiced (maṅgalasvara).

📜 Looking deeper

Madotkaṭa is literally “overflowing with mada”, the ichor of an elephant in its prime, read devotionally as overflowing joy or energy; it is also the name of a Gaṇeśa avatāra in the Gaṇeśa Purāṇa. Pramatha refers to Śiva’s wild troop, the pramathas, whose lord he is. Prathama, “the first”, closes the group: before every rite and every god, he comes first.

🌱 For today

Your shape is not your worth. The pot-bellied god is also the great hero.

NAMES 28–36 · DEEP DIVE

LORD OF SPEECH

🔎 In context

Group four pairs opposites: vighnakartṛ, maker of obstacles, and vighnahantṛ, slayer of obstacles. He places them before the proud and clears them for the humble. Then come cosmic titles: eye of the universe, lord of the cosmic form (virāṭ), lord of prosperity (śrīpati) and lord of speech (vākpati).

📜 Looking deeper

Vākpati, lord of speech, links him with learning and writing, and with Bṛhaspati, the Vedic lord of prayer whose title gaṇapati he inherited. Śṛṅgārin, adorned or graceful, is a reminder that he is handsome in his own way. Āśritavatsala, loving as a cow to her calf to those who take refuge, ends the group on tenderness.

🌱 For today

The same power can block or clear the way. Approach with humility.

NAMES 37–45 · DEEP DIVE

SON OF ŚIVA

🔎 In context

Group five names him as Śiva’s beloved son: dear to Śiva (śivapriya), son of Bhava (bhavātmaja). In between are eternal (śāśvata), strength (bala), swift in action (śīghrakārin) and the ancient person (purāṇapuruṣa).

📜 Looking deeper

Pūṣan, the nourisher, is the name of a Vedic god of roads and herds; here Gaṇeśa takes that role too. Puṣkarotkṣiptavārin is a charming picture: puṣkara is the tip of an elephant’s trunk, and he “sprays water from” it, as an elephant does when bathing or blessing. Indian temple elephants still bless with their trunks.

🌱 For today

Be quick to help. Swiftness is a form of kindness.

NAMES 46–54 · DEEP DIVE

FIRST OF ALL

🔎 In context

Group six is all about being first: first to be counted (agragaṇya), first to be worshipped (agrapūjya), first to go (agragāmin). This is the heart of his role in Hindu worship: every rite, from the Vedic to the household, begins with him.

📜 Looking deeper

Then the circle widens to “all”: sarva, the all; sarvopāsya, worshipped by all; sarvakartṛ, maker of all; sarvanetṛ, leader of all. Mantrakṛt, “maker of mantras”, fits a lord of speech and sound. Cāmīkaraprabha, bright as gold, is one of several colour words for him; elsewhere he is red, white, or smoke-coloured.

🌱 For today

Begin well and the rest follows. That is why he comes first.

NAMES 55–63 · DEEP DIVE

GIVER OF SUCCESS

🔎 In context

Group seven begins with success: giver of every siddhi, and siddhi itself. Then come family names: five-handed (pañcahasta, counting the trunk), Pārvatī’s delight (pārvatīnandana), teacher of Kumāra (kumāraguru), and breaker of the elephant-demon (kuñjarāsurabhañjana).

📜 Looking deeper

Kumāraguru, teacher of Kumāra (Skanda), assumes Gaṇeśa is the elder brother, as in South India; northern tradition often makes Skanda the elder. The elephant-demon is Gajāsura or another in different stories. Siddhi is also the name of one of his consorts in the western tradition, so “siddhi itself” can be read on two levels.

🌱 For today

Success is a gift as much as an achievement. Be grateful for it.

NAMES 64–72 · DEEP DIVE

LOVER OF SWEETS

🔎 In context

Group eight includes the name every child loves: modakapriya, fond of modakas. He is also fond of wood-apple and jackfruit (kapitthapanasapriya), radiant, steadfast and desirable.

📜 Looking deeper

Then, surprisingly, brahmacārin, the celibate student. In much of South India Gaṇeśa is unmarried, while in the west and north he has two wives, Riddhi and Siddhi, or Buddhi and Siddhi; the nāmāvali here follows the southern view. Brahmarūpin, “whose form is Brahman”, and brahmavidyādidānabhū, “source of the gift of sacred knowledge”, connect the sweet-loving boy with the highest teaching, as the Atharvaśīrṣa does.

🌱 For today

Enjoy the sweet things. Then remember the deepest ones.

NAMES 73–81 · DEEP DIVE

SON OF GAṄGĀ

🔎 In context

Group nine includes the striking name gaṅgāsuta, son of Gaṅgā, linked to the birth stories in which the river helps bring him to life; it matches dvaimātura, “two-mothered”, in the first group. He is dear to Viṣṇu, life of his devotees, conqueror of Kāma, the god of desire.

📜 Looking deeper

Jitamanmatha, conqueror of Manmatha (Kāma), fits the celibate brahmacārin of the last group. Aiśvaryakāraṇa, cause of sovereignty, and jyāyas, the eldest or greatest, speak of rank. Yakṣas and kinnaras, semi-divine beings of the mountains, serve him. Gambhīraninada, deep-voiced, closes the group with a rumble like an elephant’s.

🌱 For today

Mastery of desire is a quieter kind of victory.

THE BOY AND THE LIGHT

🔎 In context

Group ten begins with vaṭu, the young student or boy, the form in which Gaṇeśa appears in many stories, from Gokarṇa to the Rock Fort. Then he is the giver of boons, light itself (jyotis), treasure of devotees and giver of good fortune.

📜 Looking deeper

Bhāvagamya, “reached through bhāva”, feeling or devotion, is a key idea: he is not reached by learning alone. Avyakta, the unmanifest, and aprākṛtaparākrama, of more than natural valour, point beyond the visible god. Satyadharmin, whose dharma is truth, recalls the Atharvaśīrṣa’s “ṛtaṃ vacmi, satyaṃ vacmi”.

🌱 For today

Some things are reached by the heart before the head.

↑ NAMES 82–90 · A boy, and light itself.

NAMES 91–99 · DEEP DIVE

THE FRIEND

🔎 In context

Group eleven begins with the simplest name of all: sakhi, friend. Then the ocean of delight, the great lord, of divine limbs, with a jingling girdle of jewelled bells, the embodiment of all the gods.

📜 Looking deeper

Sahiṣṇu, patient or forbearing, and satatotthita, ever-risen or ever-alert, describe a god who does not tire of devotees. Vighātakārin, “one who strikes down”, is read as destroyer of the wicked or of obstacles. The bells, maṇikiṅkiṇī, on his girdle are a familiar detail of bronze images, and a reminder that he dances too.

🌱 For today

God as friend is one of the oldest and best ideas in bhakti.

↑ NAMES 91–99 · The great lord is a friend.

NAMES 100–108 · DEEP DIVE

THE ALL-SEEING PROTECTOR

🔎 In context

The last group gathers the largest names: all-seeing (viśvagdṛś), protector of the world, teacher of the good, unconquered, support of all the worlds, giver of all prosperity, lord who pervades mind and matter, and finally śrī vighneśvara, the lord of obstacles.

📜 Looking deeper

Unmattaveṣa, “wild in dress”, borrows a trait of Śiva, the wandering ascetic, and gives it to his son. Ākrāntacidacitprabhu is the most philosophical name: he is the lord who pervades both cit, the conscious, and acit, the non-conscious, the whole of reality. Ending with vighneśvara brings the list back to where every prayer to him starts: the lord of obstacles.

🌱 For today

The god of small beginnings holds up the whole world. Start small; trust the support.

↑ NAMES 100–108 · Lord of obstacles, support of the worlds.
