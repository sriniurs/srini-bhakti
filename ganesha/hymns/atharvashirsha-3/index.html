The fruits of recitation

GAṆAPATI ATHARVAŚĪRṢA · PART 3 OF 3 · SECTIONS 11–14 AND CLOSING

HOW THIS WORKS: Sections 11–14 and the closing chant in one go, then every section on two pages: the text, a picture, its meaning and key words; then a deep dive.

WHERE WE ARE

This is the last part of the Gaṇapati Atharvaśīrṣa. Part 1 called Gaṇeśa Brahman; Part 2 gave his Gāyatrī and his form.

Part 3 is the phalaśruti, the fruits of recitation: when to chant, what to offer, what it brings. Much of today’s Gaṇeśa worship, the dūrvā grass, the modakas, the caturthī fast, the thousandfold chanting, can be traced to these lines.

Promises of this kind are a convention of Indian hymns: they praise the text and encourage its use. We translate them as they stand.

SECTION 11

WHOEVER STUDIES THIS
etadatharvaśīrṣaṃ yo'dhītesa brahmabhūyāya kalpatesa sarvataḥ sukhamedhatesa sarvavighnairna bādhyatesa pañcamahāpāpātpramucyatesāyamadhīyāno divasakṛtaṃ pāpaṃ nāśayatiprātaradhīyāno rātrikṛtaṃ pāpaṃ nāśayatisāyamprātaḥ prayuñjāno apāpo bhavatisarvatrādhīyāno'pavighno bhavatidharmārthakāmamokṣaṃ ca vindatiidamatharvaśīrṣamaśiṣyāya na deyamyo yadi mohāddāsyati sa pāpīyān bhavatisahasrāvartanādyaṃ yaṃ kāmamadhīte taṃ tamanena sādhayet ‖11‖
Who studies this becomes fit for Brahman, thrives, meets no obstacle, and is freed from the five great sins. Studied at evening it ends the day’s sins, at morning the night’s; at both, one is sinless. Studied everywhere, it removes obstacles and gives dharma, wealth, desire and liberation. It is not to be given to a non-disciple; who does so from delusion is the worse for it. Repeated a thousand times, it fulfils whatever wish it is studied for.

SECTION 11 · DEEP DIVE

WHOEVER STUDIES THIS

🔎 In context

With section 11 the text turns to its phalaśruti, the “hearing of fruits”: what recitation brings. It promises the highest goal first, becoming Brahman, then freedom from obstacles, as befits Gaṇeśa, and freedom from the five great sins of the law-books. Evening and morning recitation wipe out the faults of day and night.

📜 Looking deeper

Two lines stand out. One says the text should not be given to someone who is not a disciple: a common formula in Upaniṣads, from a time when texts were taught only by a teacher to a pupil. Today the Atharvaśīrṣa is printed in booklets and chanted aloud in temples, homes and festival pandals by thousands together; the line is best read as a call for respect, not secrecy. The other promises fulfilment of wishes through a thousand recitations, the origin of the practice of sahasrāvartana, a thousandfold chanting, which is a well-known Gaṇeśa Caturthī event in Pune and elsewhere.

🌱 For today

Small daily practice, morning and evening, does more than a great effort once.

↑ SECTION 11 · Morning and evening, free of obstacles.

SECTION 12

ELOQUENCE AND LEARNING
anena gaṇapatimabhiṣiñcati sa vāgmī bhavaticaturthyāmanaśnan japati sa vidyāvān bhavati sa yaśovān bhavatiityatharvaṇavākyambrahmādyāvaraṇaṃ vidyāt na bibheti kadācaneti ‖12‖
One who bathes Gaṇapati with this becomes eloquent. One who recites it on the fourth lunar day without eating becomes learned, and becomes famous. This is the word of Atharvaṇa. One who knows the enclosure beginning with Brahmā never fears.

SECTION 12 · DEEP DIVE

ELOQUENCE AND LEARNING

🔎 In context

Section 12 links the text to two practices still common today: abhiṣeka, bathing Gaṇeśa’s image while chanting the Atharvaśīrṣa, and fasting on caturthī, the fourth day of the lunar fortnight sacred to him. The promised fruits are eloquence, learning and fame, gifts of the lord of speech.

📜 Looking deeper

The phrase “ity atharvaṇavākyam”, this is the word of Atharvaṇa, claims the sage of the Atharvaveda as its source. The last line is obscure. Brahmādyāvaraṇa, “the covering beginning with Brahmā”, is read by some commentators as knowing Gaṇeśa as the one who encloses Brahmā and all the gods, by others as knowing the gods as his surrounding retinue; the meaning is debated, and we leave it open. The promise is clear: such a person never fears.

🌱 For today

Fasting and bathing the god are outward acts. The fruit the text names is inward: clear speech, and no fear.

SECTION 13

WHAT TO OFFER
yo dūrvāṅkurairyajati sa vaiśravaṇopamo bhavatiyo lājairyajati sa yaśovān bhavatisa medhāvān bhavatiyo modakasahasreṇa yajati sa vāñchitaphalamavāpnotiyaḥ sājyasamidbhiryajati sa sarvaṃ labhate sa sarvaṃ labhate ‖13‖
Whoever worships with sprouts of dūrvā grass becomes like Vaiśravaṇa, lord of wealth. Whoever worships with puffed rice becomes famous, and becomes wise. Whoever worships with a thousand modakas obtains the fruit he desires. Whoever worships with sticks of firewood and ghee gains everything, gains everything.

vaiśravaṇopamalike Vaiśravaṇa (Kubera)

SECTION 13 · DEEP DIVE

WHAT TO OFFER

🔎 In context

Section 13 lists offerings and their fruits, the list behind much of today’s Gaṇeśa worship. Dūrvā, a hardy grass offered in bunches of three or five blades, is the most characteristic; modakas, his favourite sweet; puffed rice; and firewood dipped in ghee for a fire offering.

📜 Looking deeper

Vaiśravaṇa is Kubera, the god of wealth, son of Viśravas; dūrvā makes the worshipper as rich as he. Each offering is simple and cheap: a grass that grows anywhere, puffed rice, sweets made at home. The point of the list is generosity of spirit rather than expense. The repeated “sa sarvaṃ labhate”, he gains everything, marks the end of the list. On the story of why Gaṇeśa loves dūrvā, see our Stories and Festivals guides.

🌱 For today

The humblest offering, given with love, counts for most.

↑ SECTION 13 · Grass, rice, sweets, ghee: he gains everything.

SECTION 14

THUS THE UPANIṢAD
aṣṭau brāhmaṇān samyaggrāhayitvā sūryavarcasvī bhavatisūryagrahe mahānadyāṃ pratimāsannidhau vā japtvā siddhamantro bhavatimahāvighnātpramucyatemahādoṣātpramucyatemahāpāpātpramucyatesa sarvavidbhavati sa sarvavidbhavatiya evaṃ veda ityupaniṣat ‖14‖
Having taught it properly to eight brāhmaṇas, one shines like the sun. Reciting it during a solar eclipse, at a great river or near an image of the god, one gains the mantra’s full power. One is freed from great obstacles. One is freed from great faults. One is freed from great sins. He knows everything, he knows everything, who knows this. Thus the Upaniṣad.

SECTION 14 · DEEP DIVE

THUS THE UPANIṢAD

🔎 In context

The last section of the Upaniṣad proper names times and places of special power: a solar eclipse, the bank of a great river, the presence of the deity’s image. It praises teaching the text to others, and ends with three freedoms, from great obstacles, faults and sins.

📜 Looking deeper

“Ya evaṃ veda”, who knows thus, is one of the oldest closing formulas of the Vedic texts, used in the Brāhmaṇas and early Upaniṣads; “ity upaniṣat”, thus the Upaniṣad, names the text as such. Teaching it is praised alongside reciting it: a text lives only if it is passed on. “Sa sarvavid bhavati”, he becomes all-knowing, repeated twice, is the final promise, returning to the claim of Part 1: to know Gaṇeśa is to know Brahman, and to know Brahman is to know all.

🌱 For today

Pass on what you have learned. A teaching kept to oneself dies with one.

↑ SECTION 14 · Who knows this knows everything.

CLOSING ŚĀNTI

MAY WE BE PROTECTED TOGETHER
oṃ saha nāvavatusaha nau bhunaktusaha vīryaṃ karavāvahaitejasvi nāvadhītamastu mā vidviṣāvahaioṃ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ śāntiḥ ‖śānti‖
Om. May he protect us both together. May he nourish us both together. May we work together with vigour. May our study be full of light. May we never quarrel. Om, peace, peace, peace.
WordMeans
saha nāv avatumay he protect us both together
bhunaktumay he nourish
vīryaṃ karavāvahaimay we work with vigour
tejasvi nāv adhītam astumay our study be radiant
mā vidviṣāvahaimay we not quarrel
Together, without quarrel: peace, peace, peace.

CLOSING ŚĀNTI · DEEP DIVE

MAY WE BE PROTECTED TOGETHER

🔎 In context

The text closes with one of the best-known peace-chants, “saha nāv avatu”, from the Taittirīya and Kaṭha Upaniṣads. It is a prayer of teacher and pupil together, in the dual number: “us two”. Some editions, including ours, follow it by repeating the opening “bhadraṃ karṇebhiḥ”; we give it once, in Part 1.

📜 Looking deeper

Every word is shared: protect us both, nourish us both, let us both work. The last wish, “may we not quarrel”, is famously practical; even sages knew that study can breed rivalry. Śānti is said three times, by one common explanation for peace from three kinds of trouble: from oneself, from other beings, and from the gods or nature. With this, the Atharvaśīrṣa ends as it began: in the teacher’s house, asking for peace.

🌱 For today

Learn together, without rivalry. The friendship is part of the learning.

↑ CLOSING ŚĀNTI · Together, without quarrel: peace, peace, peace.

THE HYMN ON ONE PAGE

Part 3 in five lines
11. Recite it morning and evening: free of obstacles and sins; a thousand times for any wish.
12. Bathe his image with it for eloquence; fast on caturthī for learning; never fear.
13. Dūrvā for wealth, puffed rice for fame and wisdom, modakas for wishes, ghee for all.
14. Teach it, chant it at an eclipse or a river: who knows this knows everything.
Closing. May we be protected and nourished together, and never quarrel. Peace.
