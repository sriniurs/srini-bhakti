AṢṬAVINĀYAKA 6 OF 8 · THE CAVE · 283 STEPS

Leṇyādri

Gaṇeśa in a Buddhist cave · explained and illustrated

Son of the mountain’s daughter

HOW THIS WORKS: About thirty Buddhist caves, Cave 7 where Gaṇeśa sits, Pārvatī’s penance, the image, and the 283 steps.

WHERE WE ARE

High on a hill near Junnar, among about thirty caves cut by Buddhist monks nearly two thousand years ago, sits Girijātmaja, “son of Girijā”, the mountain’s daughter Pārvatī. It is the only Aṣṭavināyaka in a cave, and the only one on a mountain.

Tradition says Pārvatī did penance here for a son and that Gaṇeśa spent his childhood here. This guide covers the caves, Cave 7, the legend, the image and the climb. See the Aṣṭavināyaka overview.

WHERE IT IS

The place and the road

Junnar. Leṇyādri lies near the old town of Junnar, about 95 km north of Pune, on the Kukaḍī river side of the hills.

The hill. The caves face south across the valley; Shivneri fort, birthplace of Śivājī, stands nearby.

Ojhar next. Ojhar is close by, so many pilgrims visit both on one day.

ZAPPY: Śivājī’s fort is nearby?

PROF. BOLT: Shivneri, Zappy. One valley, many stories.

Caves above Junnar.

PĀRVATĪ’S PENANCE

Where Gaṇeśa grew up

The story

Gaṇeśa Purāṇa (Tretā age) and temple tradition.

In the Tretā age, Pārvatī wished for a son and did penance on this hill for twelve years.
Gaṇeśa was born to her here, in the Gaṇeśa Purāṇa’s telling of his Tretā-age form.
He spent his childhood here, about fifteen years, the stories say.
Grown, he rode out on a peacock to destroy the demon Sindhu at Morgaon.
So Leṇyādri and Morgaon tell two halves of one story: the birth and the victory.

ZAPPY: Gaṇeśa grew up in a cave?

PROF. BOLT: In this telling, Zappy. A mountain childhood for a mountain-mother’s son.

Two halves of one story.

THE BUDDHIST CAVES

Monks before Gaṇeśa

About thirty caves. Cut by Buddhist monks between the 1st and 3rd centuries CE, facing south over the valley.

Chapels. Caves 6 and 14 are caityas with stūpas; the rest are vihāras, dwellings for monks, with rock-cut water cisterns.

Inscriptions. Several record gifts by individuals and guilds; Cave 6’s dates to the 2nd century.

Protected. The Archaeological Survey of India looks after the site.

ZAPPY: Who cut them?

PROF. BOLT: Buddhist monks, Zappy, nearly two thousand years ago.

Monks first.

CAVE 7

Gaṇeśa’s shrine in a monks’ hall

A vihāra. Cave 7 was a monks’ hall, the largest excavation near Junnar: about 17 by 15 metres with twenty cells.

The shrine. Two central cells were joined to hold Gaṇeśa.

Pillars. The veranda has octagonal pillars topped with animal figures; inside, the hall is open, without pillars.

Daylight. No electric lamps: the cave is lit by the sun.

ZAPPY: No electric lights?

PROF. BOLT: Just daylight and oil lamps, Zappy. As the monks had it.

A monks’ hall, now Gaṇeśa’s.

THE IMAGE AND THE STEPS

Girijātmaja and the climb

The image. Facing north, trunk to the left; sources say devotees see it from the rear, and that it is less finely carved than the other seven.

Priests. Worship is by Yajurvedī Brahmins.

283 steps. In eight flights up the hillside. Take water and take your time; arrangements and fees are set by the authorities.

ZAPPY: 283 steps!

PROF. BOLT: In eight flights, Zappy. Count them as japa.

283 steps.

A MOTHER’S PATIENCE

What Leṇyādri teaches

Twelve years. Pārvatī waited and worked twelve years for her child. Many read Leṇyādri as about patience in prayer. (Interpretation.)

Shared rock. A Buddhist monastery that became a Hindu shrine: the hill holds both histories.

The climb. The effort of the steps is part of the visit: you come up to Gaṇeśa, he does not come down.

ZAPPY: Did the monks mind?

PROF. BOLT: They had left long before, Zappy. The rock remembers them still.

Patience in prayer.

THE JUNNAR VALLEY

Caves, forts and Gaṇeśa

Shivneri. The hill fort where Śivājī was born stands above Junnar.

More caves. Junnar has several other groups of Buddhist caves, among the largest clusters in India.

Two Gaṇeśas. Leṇyādri and Ojhar are close; most pilgrims visit both in one day.

ZAPPY: So much in one valley!

PROF. BOLT: Monks, kings and Gaṇeśa, Zappy. Junnar has them all.

A valley of stories.

WORDS TO KNOW

The temple vocabulary of this guide

🛕 Svayambhū

Self-manifested: an image held to have appeared by itself.

🟠 Sindūr

The orange-red paste that coats many Gaṇeśa images.

🪔 Dīpamāḷā

A lamp tower of stone, lit with many lamps at festivals.

🌿 Dūrvā

The three-bladed grass dear to Gaṇeśa.

🙏 Āratī

Waving of lamps, with the song “Sukhakartā Duḥkhahartā”.

🌗 Caturthī · Saṅkaṣṭī

The bright and dark fourth days, sacred to Gaṇeśa.

🕳️ Vihāra · Caitya

A monks’ dwelling cave; a prayer hall with a stūpa.
