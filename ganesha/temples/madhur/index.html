GAṆEŚA TEMPLES · KASARAGOD · THE GAṆEŚA WHO GREW

Madhūr

The Gaṇeśa a boy drew on a wall · explained and illustrated

The drawing that grew

HOW THIS WORKS: A boy’s drawing that became a god, Boḍḍajja, the Tipu story, the Mūḍappa sevā of appams, and the Kerala-style temple.

WHERE WE ARE

On the Madhuvāhinī river near Kasaragod in north Kerala stands the Madhūr temple. Its presiding deity is Śiva, but pilgrims come above all for the Gaṇeśa on the sanctum wall.

Legend says a young boy drew him there in play, and the figure kept growing. This guide covers the legend, the Tipu story, the famous appam offering, the building and visiting.

WHERE IT IS

The place and the road

North Kerala. Kasaragod lies on the Karnataka border; Kannada, Tulu and Malayalam all meet here.

The river. The temple sits on the bank of the Madhuvāhinī, also called the Mogral.

Mangaluru. About 50 km north.

PROF. BOLT: And one Gaṇeśa, Zappy.

On the Madhuvāhinī.

THE LEGEND

A drawing that grew

The story

Temple tradition.

A young boy served at the Śiva temple.
In play, he drew a picture of Gaṇeśa on the southern wall of the sanctum.
Day by day the figure grew larger, until it became a living image.
The boy called him “Boḍḍajja”: Bodda Gaṇeśa, the big one.
Gaṇeśa’s fame spread far; Śiva as Madanantēśvara still presides.

ZAPPY: A child made a god?

PROF. BOLT: A child’s love did, Zappy.

A child’s drawing.

SIDDHIVINĀYAKA

The Gaṇeśa on the wall

Position. On the southern wall of the sanctum, facing south.

Form. A large figure, not carved from stone but said to be of a special material that grew.

Darśana. Pilgrims see him beside the Śiva sanctum.

ZAPPY: Is it stone?

PROF. BOLT: Tradition says no, Zappy. Not an ordinary image.

Not ordinary.

THE TIPU STORY

A mark on the well

Lore. During his 18th-century Malabar campaign, Tipu Sultan is said to have come to destroy the temple.

The water. After drinking from the temple well, he changed his mind.

The mark. A sword or dagger cut on the well-house is still shown. (Local lore.)

ZAPPY: The water changed his mind?

PROF. BOLT: So the story goes, Zappy.

The well’s story.

MŪḌAPPA SEVĀ

Covered in appams

What. The whole great image is covered with appams of rice and ghee.

Rare. Held only occasionally because of the vast cost; Kerala Tourism records one in 1992, others report later ones.

Daily. Simpler offerings; the appam is always his favourite.

ZAPPY: The whole Gaṇeśa covered in appams?

PROF. BOLT: Every inch, Zappy.

Mountains of appams.

THE BUILDING

Three-tiered and turtle-backed

Gajapṛṣṭha. The sanctum has a rounded “elephant-back” form, with a three-tiered roof plated in copper.

Kerala style. Wooden carvings of stories from the epics adorn the eaves.

Tuḷunāḍu. Counted among the six Gaṇeśa temples of the old Tulu country.

ZAPPY: An elephant-back roof!

PROF. BOLT: Fitting, Zappy.

An elephant-back roof.

ŚIVA WHO PRESIDES

Madanantēśvara

Madanantēśvara. Śiva is the presiding deity of the temple, its oldest worship.

Father and son. Gaṇeśa beside his father on the same sanctum.

Worship. Pilgrims honour both.

ZAPPY: Gaṇeśa is more famous than his father here?

PROF. BOLT: Śiva won’t mind, Zappy.

Father and son.

VISITING

Kasaragod and the coast

From Kasaragod. About 8 km by road.

Busy days. Gaṇeśa Caturthī and Vināyaka Caturthī months; Tuesdays and Saṅkaṣṭī.

Practical. Dress codes and sevās follow Kerala temple rules; check locally.

ZAPPY: Dress code?

PROF. BOLT: Traditional dress, Zappy. Kerala temples are strict.

Dress traditionally.

WORDS TO KNOW

The temple vocabulary of this guide

🛕 Garbhagṛha

The sanctum, the “womb-house” of the image.

🪨 Monolith

An image carved from a single stone.

🌀 Valampuri

A right-turned trunk, rare and especially revered.

🍯 Prasāda

Food offered to the god and shared as blessing.

🙏 Sevā

A ritual service offered by a devotee.

💧 Tīrtha

A sacred tank or pond.
