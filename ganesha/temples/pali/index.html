AṢṬAVINĀYAKA 3 OF 8 · THE CHILD DEVOTEE · THE ŚRĪ-SHAPED TEMPLE

Pālī

The Gaṇeśa named after a child · explained and illustrated

Ballāḷa’s lord

HOW THIS WORKS: The boy Ballāḷa, the thrown stone Ḍhuṇḍhi, the image dressed as a Brahmin, the temple shaped like Śrī, and the Portuguese bell.

WHERE WE ARE

Below the cliffs of Sarasgaḍ fort, by the Ambā river in Raigad district, stands the only Gaṇeśa in India named after a child devotee: Ballāḷeśvara, “Ballāḷa’s lord”.

A boy who loved Gaṇeśa was beaten and tied to a tree by his own father. Gaṇeśa came, freed him, and stayed for ever under his name. This guide covers the story, the stone that was thrown away, the image, the temple shaped like a letter, and a bell captured in war. See the Aṣṭavināyaka overview.

With the blessings of the Lord,

WHERE IT IS

The place and the road

Konkan. Pālī lies below the Sahyādri in Raigad district, between the fort of Sarasgaḍ and the Ambā river.

The road. From Pune it is about 111 km, down the ghats by Lonavla and Khopoli.

The mountain. The image is said to echo the shape of the mountain behind the temple.

ZAPPY: Down the ghats?

PROF. BOLT: Pālī and Mahaḍ are the two coastal-side temples, Zappy. The air changes.

Below the fort.

BALLĀḶA AND HIS FATHER

The boy Gaṇeśa would not abandon

The story

Gaṇeśa Purāṇa (Upāsanā Khaṇḍa) and temple tradition.

In Pallīpur, old Pālī, lived the merchant Kalyāṇ Śeṭh and his wife Indumatī. Their son Ballāḷa loved Gaṇeśa more than anything.
He and his friends played at worship in the forest, with a stone as Gaṇeśa, and stayed out for hours. The other parents blamed Ballāḷa.
His father, furious, broke up the worship, threw away the stone, beat the boy and tied him to a tree.
Ballāḷa kept calling on Gaṇeśa. Gaṇeśa came as a Brahmin, freed him and healed his wounds.
The boy asked one boon: “Stay here for ever.” Gaṇeśa agreed, and took the boy’s name: Ballāḷeśvara.

ZAPPY: His own father?

PROF. BOLT: In the story, Zappy. And Gaṇeśa chose the child. That’s why the god has his name.

The god with a child’s name.

ḌHUṆḌHI VINĀYAKA

The thrown stone

The stone. The stone Kalyāṇ threw away is worshipped as Ḍhuṇḍhi Vināyaka, in a shrine behind the main temple.

Facing west. Rare for a Gaṇeśa; it is also held to be svayambhū.

First. By custom pilgrims greet Ḍhuṇḍhi Vināyaka before Ballāḷeśvara. He has his own five-day festival.

ZAPPY: The thrown stone is worshipped first?

PROF. BOLT: Thrown away by anger, honoured first ever since, Zappy.

The rejected stone, honoured first.

THE IMAGE

Ballāḷeśvara dressed as a Brahmin

Form. Facing east, trunk to the left, with diamonds in the eyes and navel and a mouse in front holding a modaka.

Dress. The image is dressed as a Brahmin, the form in which Gaṇeśa came to free Ballāḷa.

Prasāda. Unusually, it is besan laḍḍu rather than modaka.

ZAPPY: Why diamonds in the eyes?

PROF. BOLT: They catch the lamplight, Zappy. Many Aṣṭavināyaka images have them.

Dressed as a Brahmin.

THE TEMPLE OF ŚRĪ

A plan shaped like a letter

Rebuilt. The old wooden temple was rebuilt in stone in 1760 under the Phaḍnavīs family, with lead mixed into the mortar, by one account.

Śrī. Its plan is shaped like the Devanāgarī letter Śrī, the syllable of auspiciousness.

The sun. The temple faces east; after the winter solstice the rising sun falls on the image.

Water. Two lakes adjoin it, one kept for the deity’s worship.

ZAPPY: A temple shaped like a letter?

PROF. BOLT: Śrī, Zappy. You’d see it best from the sky.

A temple shaped like Śrī.

THE BELL FROM VASAI

A trophy of war

1739. Chimājī Appā, brother of Peshwa Bājīrāo I, captured Vasai (Bassein) from the Portuguese.

The bells. Bells from the Portuguese churches were brought back and offered at Aṣṭavināyaka and other temples. Pālī’s hangs here still.

A word. The bell is a war trophy; today it simply calls devotees to āratī.

ZAPPY: A church bell in a Gaṇeśa temple?

PROF. BOLT: History hangs there, Zappy. Now it just rings for āratī.

A bell with a history.

THE CHILD’S FAITH

What Pālī teaches

Play is worship. Ballāḷa worshipped a stone in play, and Gaṇeśa accepted it. Many read this as: sincerity matters more than ceremony. (Interpretation.)

The weak protected. The god came to the bound child, not to the powerful father.

The rejected honoured. The thrown stone is greeted first. What was cast away is made precious.

ZAPPY: So my play-pūjā counts?

PROF. BOLT: If your heart is in it, Zappy. Ask Ballāḷa.

Sincerity first.

WORDS TO KNOW

The temple vocabulary of this guide

🛕 Svayambhū

Self-manifested: an image held to have appeared by itself.

🟠 Sindūr

The orange-red paste that coats many Gaṇeśa images.

🪔 Dīpamāḷā

A lamp tower of stone, lit with many lamps at festivals.

🌿 Dūrvā

The three-bladed grass dear to Gaṇeśa.

🙏 Āratī

Waving of lamps, with the song “Sukhakartā Duḥkhahartā”.

🌗 Caturthī · Saṅkaṣṭī

The bright and dark fourth days, sacred to Gaṇeśa.
