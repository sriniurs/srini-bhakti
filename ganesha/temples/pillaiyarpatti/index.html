FOREWORD

Near Karaikudi in Chettinad, Karpaka Vināyakar of Pillaiyārpaṭṭi is a six-foot Gaṇeśa carved straight into a cave wall in about the 7th century.

This guide describes the rare two-armed, right-trunked image, the sculptor’s signature, the Nagarattār connection and the festivals.

ZAPPY: Two arms and a right trunk?

PROF. BOLT: Rare on both counts, Zappy. An early form of Gaṇeśa, before four arms became usual.

Pillaiyārpaṭṭi, near Karaikudi in Sivaganga district, is home to Karpaka Vināyakar, a six-foot Gaṇeśa carved in relief directly from the rock of a cave. Its rock-cut core dates to about the 7th century.

The image has only two arms and a right-turned trunk, both rare. It is one of the nine clan temples of the Nagarattār (Nattukottai Chettiar) community, and the only one of the nine with Gaṇeśa as presiding deity.

Karpaka refers to the wish-granting tree.

ZAPPY: Why is two-armed special?

PROF. BOLT: The earliest Gaṇeśas had two arms. This one keeps that old form.

Karpaka Vināyakar is a six-foot relief carved directly from the rock of the cave, seated, facing north, with two arms and a right-turned trunk. Some accounts describe him holding a liṅga.

His name refers to the karpaka, the wish-granting tree of heaven.

ZAPPY: The sculptor signed it?

PROF. BOLT: Ekkāṭṭūr Kōṉ Perumparaṇaṉ. Artists’ signatures that old are rare.

The rock-cut cave, dated to the 7th century, holds the Gaṇeśa, a Śiva-liṅga called Tiruvīsar, and a figure variously identified as Ardhanārīśvara, Harihara or the royal patron.

An inscription preserves the sculptor’s name, Ekkāṭṭūr Kōṉ Perumparaṇaṉ, in an archaic script with Tamil-Brahmi features. Of more than fifteen inscriptions, one names “Desi Vināyakar”; later Pandya grants date from 1091 to 1238.

ZAPPY: Chettinad, like the mansions and the food?

PROF. BOLT: The same community. Their temple to Gaṇeśa is the jewel of the region.

Pillaiyārpaṭṭi is one of the nine clan temples of the Nagarattār, or Nattukottai Chettiar, community of Chettinad, and the only one of the nine where Gaṇeśa is the presiding deity.

From the 13th century the community added the vimāna, gateway tower and halls round the cave, with a large tank to the north.

ZAPPY: A 27-kilo modaka?

PROF. BOLT: At least that. Gaṇeśa’s appetite is legendary; Chettinad’s cooks oblige.

Vināyakar Caturthī is celebrated for ten days in the Tamil month of Āvaṇi (August–September), culminating in the offering of a giant modakam weighing more than 27 kg.

The temple is easily combined with a visit to Karaikudi and the Chettinad mansions.
