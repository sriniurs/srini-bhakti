Rāñjaṇgāv

The great Gaṇapati whom Śiva worshipped · explained and illustrated

The great Gaṇapati

HOW THIS WORKS: How Śiva worshipped Gaṇeśa before destroying Tripura, the image and its hidden twin, the temple and the sun, and closing the circle.

WHERE WE ARE

On the Pune–Ahilyanagar road, about 50 km from Pune, stands Mahāgaṇapati, the great Gaṇapati, the eighth and last of the circuit. Here, the story goes, even Śiva stopped to worship his son before his greatest battle.

This guide covers the legend of Tripura, the image, the temple, its festivals and the return to Morgaon to close the circle. See the Aṣṭavināyaka overview.

WHERE IT IS

The place and the road

On the highway. Rāñjaṇgāv lies on the Pune–Ahilyanagar road in Shirur taluka.

Old name. The town was once called Maṇipur.

Last stop. From here pilgrims return to Morgaon to complete the circuit.

ZAPPY: Easy to reach?

PROF. BOLT: Right on the highway, Zappy.

On the highway.

ŚIVA AND TRIPURA

Even Śiva begins with Gaṇeśa

The story

Temple tradition; the Tripura story is told in many Purāṇas.

The demon Tripurāsura ruled three flying cities of gold, silver and iron; they could be destroyed only by one arrow when they lined up.
Śiva set out to destroy them, but did not first honour Gaṇeśa.
In one telling, the axle of Śiva’s chariot broke. Śiva understood the cause.
He worshipped Gaṇeśa here, at Maṇipur.
Then, with one arrow, he burned the three cities. Read our Kārtika Pūrṇimā guide for Tripurāri.

ZAPPY: The axle broke?

PROF. BOLT: A reminder, Zappy. Gaṇeśa first, then the arrow.

Gaṇeśa first, then the arrow.

THE IMAGE

Mahāgaṇapati

Form. Seated cross-legged, facing east, with a broad forehead and the trunk to the left, flanked by Riddhi and Siddhi.

The hidden image. Local belief holds that the true image, with ten trunks and twenty arms, lies in a vault below. This is unproven.

Name. Mahāgaṇapati, the great Gaṇapati: the form Śiva worshipped.

ZAPPY: Ten trunks?

PROF. BOLT: A story no one has proved, Zappy. A good one, though.

The great Gaṇapati.

THE TEMPLE AND THE SUN

Built for the light

Facing east. The gate is guarded by Jaya and Vijaya.

The sun. The temple is aligned so that the sun’s rays fall on the image during the sun’s southward course.

Peshwa work. Peshwa Mādhavrāo built the stone sanctum; in 1790 Anyābā Dev was authorised to worship the image.

Age. Some say the architecture recalls the 9th–10th centuries; this is unverified.

ZAPPY: Sunlight on Gaṇeśa?

PROF. BOLT: On certain days, Zappy. Planned centuries ago.

Built for the sun.

Bhādrapada at Rāñjaṇgāv

Gaṇeśa Caturthī in Bhādrapada draws great crowds.

Other days. Saṅkaṣṭī, Aṅgārakī and Gaṇeśa Jayantī.

Arrangements are set by the temple trust; check locally.

ZAPPY: Busy?

PROF. BOLT: Very, on Caturthī, Zappy.

CLOSING THE CIRCLE

Back to Morgaon

Return. After Rāñjaṇgāv, pilgrims go back to Morgaon to finish where they began.

Why. A circle closed means a vow fulfilled. The journey ends with thanks to Mayūreśvara.

The whole circuit. Eight temples, eight stories, one Gaṇeśa: see the Aṣṭavināyaka overview.

ZAPPY: Back to the start?

PROF. BOLT: That’s what makes it a circle, Zappy.

The circle closes.

GAṆEŚA FIRST

What Rāñjaṇgāv teaches

Even Śiva. The destroyer of Tripura could not begin without honouring Gaṇeśa. Many read this as the humility every great task needs. (Interpretation.)

The broken axle. An obstacle is sometimes a reminder, not an enemy.

The last temple. The circuit ends with the god whom even Śiva worshipped: Gaṇeśa is the first and the greatest.

ZAPPY: So obstacles can be reminders?

PROF. BOLT: Sometimes, Zappy. Ask Śiva’s chariot.

Humility before the task.

WORDS TO KNOW

The temple vocabulary of this guide

🛕 Svayambhū

Self-manifested: an image held to have appeared by itself.

🟠 Sindūr

The orange-red paste that coats many Gaṇeśa images.

🪔 Dīpamāḷā

A lamp tower of stone, lit with many lamps at festivals.

🌿 Dūrvā

The three-bladed grass dear to Gaṇeśa.

🙏 Āratī

Waving of lamps, with the song “Sukhakartā Duḥkhahartā”.

🌗 Caturthī · Saṅkaṣṭī

The bright and dark fourth days, sacred to Gaṇeśa.

🏹 Tripura

The three flying cities Śiva destroyed with one arrow.
