UCCHI PILLAIYĀR

Gaṇeśa at the top of the rock · explained and illustrated

GAṆEŚA · A REFERENCE GUIDE

HOW THIS WORKS: Ucchi Pillaiyār at the summit of the Rock Fort, Tiruchirāppaḷḷi: the 7th-century rock-cut temple, Vibhīṣaṇa and Raṅganātha, the mark on the forehead, the climb past Tāyumānavar, and the view of Śrīraṅgam.

FOREWORD

At the very top of Tiruchi’s Rock Fort sits Ucchi Pillaiyār, “Pillaiyār of the summit”, once the greatest temple of the Gāṇapatya tradition.

This guide covers the legend of Vibhīṣaṇa, the climb, the temples on the way and the view.

ZAPPY: The same trick as Gokarṇa?

PROF. BOLT: The same. Different demon-king’s brother, same boy, same dent.

Ucchi Pillaiyār stands at the summit of the Rock Fort in Tiruchirāppaḷḷi; ucchi means “summit” in Tamil. It is a rock-cut temple usually dated to about the 7th century, and was once the greatest shrine of the Gāṇapatya tradition.

Its legend tells how Gaṇeśa, as a boy, set Vibhīṣaṇa’s Raṅganātha down at Śrīraṅgam and fled up this rock, where Vibhīṣaṇa caught him and struck his forehead.

The climb passes the Māṇikka Vināyakar and Tāyumānavar Śiva temples.

ZAPPY: Where’s the full story?

PROF. BOLT: In Part B: ‘The Kāverī and the Rock Fort’.

See: The Kāverī and the Rock Fort

Rāma gave Vibhīṣaṇa an image of Raṅganātha that could not be moved once set down. At the Kāverī, Gaṇeśa, as a boy, held it while Vibhīṣaṇa bathed, and set it down at what became Śrīraṅgam.

Vibhīṣaṇa chased him up this rock and struck his forehead; Gaṇeśa revealed himself and explained that Raṅganātha was meant to stay. The full story is in our Stories guide.

ZAPPY: Śiva who became a mother?

PROF. BOLT: Tāyumānavar: he came as a mother to help a devotee’s daughter in labour. A lovely story for another day.

Pilgrims climb hundreds of steps cut into the rock, beginning with the Māṇikka Vināyakar shrine at the foot and passing the Tāyumānavar Śiva temple halfway up, before reaching Ucchi Pillaiyār at the top.

The climb takes about half an hour.

ZAPPY: Why so small, if it was the greatest?

PROF. BOLT: Greatest in importance. On a summit, space is precious.

See: The Gāṇapatya tradition

The shrine at the summit is small. Its image bears a mark on the forehead, said to be Vibhīṣaṇa’s blow. The rock-cut temples of the Rock Fort are usually dated to the Pallava period, about the 7th century.

Wikipedia notes that it was once the largest temple of the Gāṇapatya tradition.

ZAPPY: Can you really see Śrīraṅgam?

PROF. BOLT: On a clear day. The whole legend in one view.

See: Antya Raṅga, Śrīraṅgam

From the summit, Śrīraṅgam’s towers rise across the Kāverī: the very place where, in the legend, Gaṇeśa set Raṅganātha down.

Śrīraṅgam and Tiruvānaikkāval (with our Śrīvidyā guide to Akhilāṇḍeśvarī) make a natural day with the Rock Fort.
