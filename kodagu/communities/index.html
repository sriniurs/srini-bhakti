Kodagu’s Many Communities

Gowdas, Airis, Meda and Others · explained and illustrated

Many peoples, one hill country

HOW THIS WORKS: Kodagu’s communities: the languages of the district, the twenty Kodava-speaking groups, the Amma Kodavas, Airis and Meda, the Kodagu Gowdas, the forest peoples, the Kodava Maaple and newer neighbours.

WHERE WE ARE

Kodagu is not only the Kodavas. The district’s half-million people speak Kannaḍa, Malayāḷam, Kodava, Tuḷu, Are, Yerava and more, and around twenty communities besides the Kodavas speak Kodava takk at home, keep ainmanes, wear the kupya and dance at the same festivals. The festivals in this series belong to all of them in different ways.

This last part introduces the main communities: the Kodava-speaking groups such as the Amma Kodavas, Airis, Kembaṭṭis and Meda; the Kodagu Gowdas who speak Are bhāṣe; the forest peoples; the Kodava Maaple Muslims; and the newer neighbours.

PROF. BOLT: Both. The Kodava people, and the Kodava language that many communities speak.

PROF. BOLT: Most communities do, each in its own way.

Many peoples, one hill country.

LANGUAGES

Who speaks what

Mixed. At the 2011 census about 31% gave Kannaḍa as first language, 21% Malayāḷam, 15% Kodava, 9% Tuḷu, 6% Are, 5% Yerava and 4% Tamiḻ.

Kodava takk. Spoken not only by the Kodavas but by many other communities.

Are bhāṣe. The speech of the Kodagu Gowdas, close to Kannaḍa.

Malayāḷam. Many families have Kerala roots, old and new.

Kannaḍa. The official language and the common tongue.

ZAPPY: Which language for the festivals?

PROF. BOLT: Kodava songs, Kannaḍa notices, Malayāḷam at the tea stall.

Half a dozen tongues in one district.

KODAVA-SPEAKING COMMUNITIES

About twenty groups

Many. Besides the Kodavas, around 18 to 22 communities speak Kodava takk; lists differ.

Names. They include the Amma Kodava, Heggaḍe (Peggaḍe), Airi, Koyava, Koleya, Golla, Kodava Nāyar, Banna, Madivāḷa, Hajāma (Nainda), Kaniya, Panika, Māliya, Marangi, Kembaṭṭi, Meda and the Kodava Maaple.

Ainmanes. Most have ancestral houses.

Culture. They follow Kodava culture in dress, festivals and rites, each with its own customs.

Academy. The Karnataka Kodava Sahitya Academy has documented them.

ZAPPY: Are they Kodavas?

PROF. BOLT: They are Kodava-speaking. “Kodava” as a community name usually means the dominant group.

One language, many houses.

AMMA KODAVAS

The people of the mother

Small. The Amma Kodavas are a small community.

Priests. They are believed to have once been principal priests (arcakas) of Kodagu.

Name. One of their spokesmen says the name comes from Amma, Mother Kāverī, by whose mercy their ancestors were restored: they call themselves Ammaṅga.

Origins. Several origin stories are told; the community itself disputes some of them.

Customs. They follow Kodava dress and many rites, with their own differences.

ZAPPY: Which story is right?

PROF. BOLT: The community’s own account deserves first hearing.

Named for the mother.

HANDS OF KODAGU

Smiths, carpenters, weavers, drummers

Traditional occupations are named here for history; today members of every community work in every field.

🔨 Airi

Carpenters and village smiths; makers of Kodava ornaments, weapons and the wooden chest (sandūka). Airira is a common family name.

🧺 Meda

Basket and mat weavers, and drummers of the vālaga.

🌾 Kembaṭṭi

Household workers and labourers of old estates, with their own festival at Pannaṅgālatamme.

🎭 Banna, Maleya

Performers of kōla rites at village temples.

✂️ Hajāma, Madivāḷa

Barbers and washermen with ritual roles.

🌿 Māliya

Known for herbal medicine.

The crafts behind the festivals.

KODAGU GOWDAS

Speakers of Are bhāṣe

Who. The Arebhāṣe Gowdas, or Kodagu Gowdas, are a community of Kodagu and Dakṣiṇa Kannaḍa.

Language. They speak Are bhāṣe.

Shared. Their traditional dress is close to the Kodava; many revere Igguthappa and keep Puttari and Kakkaḍa customs.

Gowda Samāja. Gowda Samājas hold their own Puttari celebrations.

1837. Guḍḍemane Appaiah Gowda led, with men of many communities, the armed revolt of 1837 against the British across Kodagu and Dakṣiṇa Kannaḍa.

ZAPPY: Rivals or neighbours?

PROF. BOLT: Neighbours. Like all neighbours, they sometimes disagree, and they share the same festivals.

Neighbours in dress and faith.

FOREST PEOPLES

Yerava, Kuruba and others

Yerava. The Yerava speak their own language; many worked on estates.

Kuruba. Forest communities such as the Jēnu and Beṭṭa Kurubas live near Nāgarahoḷe and the southern forests.

Knowledge. They hold deep knowledge of forest, honey, herbs and animals.

Challenges. Land rights, wages and displacement from forests are long-running issues.

Festivals. They keep their own festivals and gods, and also take part in district ones.

ZAPPY: Are they part of Kodava culture?

PROF. BOLT: They are part of Kodagu’s culture, with their own traditions.

The forest’s own people.

KODAVA MAAPLE

Muslims with clan names

Who. The Kodava Maaple, also called Jamma Maaple, are a Muslim community of Kodagu.

Language. They speak Kodava takk.

Clans. Many keep ancestral clan names, like the Kodavas.

Dress. Traditionally the men wore Kodava dress.

Others. Kodagu’s other Muslims include Bearys, Mappiḷas, Syeds and Sheikhs; together about 87,000 at the 2011 census.

ZAPPY: Muslims who follow Kodava custom?

PROF. BOLT: In dress, language and clan life, yes. Faith is Islam; culture is Kodagu.

Kodava in speech, Muslim in faith.

NEWER NEIGHBOURS

Tibetans, migrants, estate workers

Bylakuppe. Tibetan settlements near Kuśālanagar, founded from the 1960s, are among the largest in India.

Estate workers. Workers from other districts and states keep the coffee estates going.

Kerala ties. Malayāḷi families have lived in Kodagu for generations.

Tuḷu speakers. Billavas, Mogaveeras, Bunts and others came from the coast.

Change. Kodagu’s mix keeps changing; the festivals adapt.

ZAPPY: Do Tibetans celebrate Puttari?

PROF. BOLT: Not as their own festival, but Kodagu is their home now too.

A district that keeps changing.
