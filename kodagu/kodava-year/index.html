The Kodava Year and Its Food

Months, Kakkaḍa Padinett and Festival Dishes · explained and illustrated

Sun, paddy and pork

HOW THIS WORKS: The Kodava year: the solar months from Edmyār to Mīnyār, New Year with the plough, Kakkaḍa Padinett and its purple herb, and the food of Kodagu from pandi curry to kaḍambuṭṭ.

WHERE WE ARE

The Kodava year is a solar year of farmers. It begins in mid-April with the plough, passes through the drenching month of Kakkaḍa when a purple herb is cooked into sweets, and climbs to the three great festivals: arms in Chingyār, the river in Tholyār, the rice in Birchyār. And at every step there is food: rice balls, pork curry soured with kachampuli, bamboo shoots, mushrooms and rice-flour sweets.

This part sets out the months, the smaller observances of the year and the main Kodava dishes, with what is eaten when.

The three great festivals have their own parts: Kailpoḷd, Kāverī Saṅkramaṇa, Puttari. Compare the Tuḷu year. Next: communities.

THE MONTHS

Names of the Kodava months

Order. Edmyār (Mēṣa, the ram), Kadyār (bull), Āḍare (twins), Kakkaḍa (crab), Chingyār (lion), Kanyār (maiden), Tholyār (scales), Birchyār (scorpion), Dalmyār (archer), Malyār, and two more before the year turns, Mīnyār the last.

Solar. Each month begins when the sun enters the sign.

Farm cycle. Each month matches a stage of paddy and, today, coffee.

Like neighbours. Kakkaḍa is Āṣāḍha of the Kannaḍigas, Āṭi of the Tuḷuvas, Āḍi of the Tamils.

Spellings. Names are written differently by different writers.

ZAPPY: Why can’t you list all twelve?

PROF. BOLT: Two names are spelt so variously that we would rather not guess.

Twelve suns, one paddy year.

EDMYĀR OND

New Year and the plough

Edmyār Ond. The first day of Edmyār, also called Bisu Caṅgrāndi or Pudhiyāṇḍ, is the Kodava New Year.

The plough. The agricultural cycle starts: families offer puja to the plough and Mother Earth and plough the first furrow.

When. Usually 14 or 15 April.

Revival. Kodava groups have worked to popularise it again in recent years.

Shared. It coincides with Viṣu in Kerala and Bisu in Tulu Nadu.

ZAPPY: No fireworks?

PROF. BOLT: A plough and a puja. Very Kodava.

The year begins with a furrow.

KAKKAḌA PADINETT

The herb of the eighteenth day

Kakkaḍa. The monsoon month, mid-July to mid-August: rain, cold and little work outside.

Padinett. On its 18th day, usually 2 or 3 August, Kodavas cook with madd tōppu (Justicia wynaadensis).

The herb. Tradition holds that the herb reaches its full medicinal strength on this day.

Dishes. Madd pāyasa, madd puṭṭ and madd kūḷ, coloured deep purple by the herb’s extract.

Others too. Other communities of Kodagu make their own madd dishes in Kakkaḍa.

ZAPPY: Does it really work as medicine?

PROF. BOLT: Tradition says so. We report the belief, not a cure.

A purple sweet for the rainy month.

PANDI CURRY

The pork of Kodagu

Pandi. Pandi curry, pork in a dark, spicy gravy, is Kodagu’s signature dish.

Kachampuli. It is soured with kachampuli, a black vinegar made from the Garcinia fruit.

Spices. Roasted spices give the dark colour; recipes are family secrets.

Festive. It is the centre of the Kailpoḷd and wedding meals.

With. Eaten with kaḍambuṭṭ or rice.

ZAPPY: Must it be pork?

PROF. BOLT: For pandi, yes. Chicken and mutton curries are cooked the same way.

Dark, sour and festive.

FESTIVAL MENUS

What is eaten when

Kodava festivals are known by their food as much as by their rites.

🟣 Kakkaḍa Padinett

Madd pāyasa, madd puṭṭ.

🍛 Kailpoḷd

Kaḍambuṭṭ and pork curry, offered first to the weapons.

🥞 Kāverī Saṅkramaṇa

Vegetarian dosa and pumpkin curry.

🍌 Puttari

Tambuṭṭ, new-rice dishes, then pork in the days after.

💍 Weddings

Pork, rice, kaḍambuṭṭ for hundreds.

🙏 Ancestors

Family food at the kaimaḍa.
