Domes and waterfalls

HOW THIS WORKS: The temples of Kodagu: Omkāreśvara in Madikeri with its domes, Bhagaṇḍēśvara at the Bhāgamaṇḍala confluence, the Talakāverī source, Iruppu by its falls, and more.

WHERE WE ARE

Kodagu’s faith lives mostly in mands, groves and ancestral houses. But the district also has temples worth knowing: a domed Śiva temple built by a remorseful king in Madikeri, the Kerala-style shrine at the confluence of Bhāgamaṇḍala, the source temple at Talakāverī, the forest falls of Iruppu, and more.

This part is a short guide to these temples: who built them, what is worshipped, how they look, their festivals and their stories, with notes for visiting.

Pāḍi Igguthappa has its own part; Talakāverī is covered in Part 4; village shrines in Part 9. For the rest of Karnataka see Temples of Karnataka.

Domes, roofs and waterfalls.

OMKĀREŚVARA

A king’s temple in Madikeri

Built. Work began in 1817; the liṅga was consecrated on 26 March 1820, under the Hāleri king Liṅgarājendra II.

Style. A central dome with four corner turrets, recalling Islamic and Gothic forms, joined with Kerala-style tiled roofs.

Liṅga. The Śiva liṅga is said to have been brought from Kāśī.

Tank. A tank full of fish lies in front, with a small pavilion.

Where. In Madikeri town near the fort, about 1 km from the bus stand.

ZAPPY: Why the domes?

PROF. BOLT: Fashions of the age, and a king who wanted something different. Nobody else in Karnataka built a Śiva temple quite like it.

A Śiva temple under a dome.

THE KING’S REMORSE

Why Omkāreśvara was built

The story

A popular legend told at the temple; historians tell the king’s story differently.

The story says the king had a Brahmin put to death, an innocent man who had opposed him.
The dead man’s spirit troubled him; his advisers told him to build a Śiva temple and bring a liṅga from Kāśī.
He built Omkāreśvara in atonement. (Legend; the king died later in 1820.)

BHAGAṆḌĒŚVARA

The temple at the confluence

Where. At Bhāgamaṇḍala, where the Kāverī meets the Kannike and the hidden Sujyōti, about 8 km below Talakāverī.

God. Śiva as Bhagaṇḍēśvara, named after the sage Bhagaṇḍa said to have done penance here.

Style. Kerala-style, with layered sloping roofs and a walled courtyard.

Other shrines. Shrines to Viṣṇu, Subrahmaṇya and Gaṇapati within the complex. (As described.)

Pilgrims. Pilgrims bathe at the confluence and worship here before Talakāverī.

ZAPPY: Bhagaṇḍa?

PROF. BOLT: The sage after whom the place is named: Bhagaṇḍa’s maṇḍala.

A sage’s temple where waters meet.

TALAKĀVERĪ TEMPLE

At the source

Kuṇḍike. The Brahma Kuṇḍike, source of the river, with a bathing tank before it.

Shrines. To Agastyēśvara (the liṅga of Agastya) and Gaṇapati.

Festival. Kāverī Saṅkramaṇa and the tīrthōdbhava in mid-October.

Hilltop. Steps lead up Brahmagiri for a wide view.

Read. Full story in Part 4.

ZAPPY: Can I bathe there?

PROF. BOLT: In the tank, yes; most pilgrims do on the festival.

The river’s first temple.

IRUPPU

Rāmēśvara and the Lakṣmaṇa Tīrtha

Where. In south Kodagu near Kuṭṭa, on the edge of the Brahmagiri forests.

Temple. A Rāmēśvara (Śiva) temple stands by the Lakṣmaṇa Tīrtha river.

Legend. Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa passed here; Lakṣmaṇa shot an arrow into the hill and the stream sprang out. (Legend.)

Śivarātri. On Mahā Śivarātri thousands bathe in the falls, believing it washes away sins.

Falls. The Iruppu Falls are a popular forest walk.

ZAPPY: An arrow made the river?

PROF. BOLT: So the story goes. The falls are real and cold.

A river from an arrow.

PANNAṄGĀLATAMME

The sister goddess

Sister. Pannaṅgālatamme is the one sister among the seven divine siblings of the Igguthappa legend.

Shrine. Her main temple is near Kakkabe, not far from Pāḍi.

Kembaṭṭi festival. Every year members of the Kembaṭṭi community gather here for a two-day festival.

Old record. The legend of Igguthappa, Pannaṅgālatamme and their brothers appears in 19th-century sources, including the Hāleri texts.

Visit both. Pāḍi and Pannaṅgālatamme are often visited together.

ZAPPY: Why a separate temple?

PROF. BOLT: Each sibling chose a home. She chose hers.

The sister near her brother.

VISITING

Notes for the pilgrim

Route. Madikeri → Bhāgamaṇḍala (about 40 km) → Talakāverī (about 8 km more) is the classic day.

South. Pāḍi from Kakkabe; Iruppu from Goṇikoppa or Kuṭṭa.

Dress. Many Kodagu temples follow dress codes; men may be asked to remove shirts in some.

Weather. Heavy rain June to September; leeches in forest walks.

Festivals. Crowds at Saṅkramaṇa and Śivarātri; plan early.

ZAPPY: Can I do all in one day?

PROF. BOLT: Madikeri, Bhāgamaṇḍala and Talakāverī, yes. Pāḍi and Iruppu need another.

A district of short, steep drives.
