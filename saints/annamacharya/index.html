SAINTS · THE SINGER OF THE SEVEN HILLS

Annamācārya

Singer of the Seven Hills · explained and illustrated

Thirty-two thousand songs for one God

HOW THIS WORKS: Tāḷḷapāka Annamācārya (Annamayya), the 15th-century Telugu saint who sang to Veṅkaṭeśvara of Tirumala every day: his life, the legend of 32,000 songs, the copper plates that saved them, songs of devotion and of love, his message of equality, his family of poets, and the modern revival of his music.

WHERE WE ARE

For more than five hundred years a family of poets in Andhra sang a song every day for the god of Tirumala. The first and greatest of them, Annamācārya, is said to have composed 32,000 songs. Most were lost; about 12,000 survive, because they were engraved on copper plates and hidden in the temple.

Today “Brahmam okkaṭe”, “Adivo alladivo” and “Muddugāre Yaśodā” are sung across South India. This guide tells his story. For Tirumala itself, see Tirumala; for its morning hymn, Veṅkaṭeśa Suprabhātam.

THE BOY WHO CLIMBED THE HILLS

Annamayya’s life

The story

Biography by his grandson Cinnanna (Annamācārya Caritramu), with legend.

Annamayya was born at Tāḷḷapāka in a family of scholars. As a boy, tradition says, he slipped away to join pilgrims going to Tirumala.
Climbing the hills with his sandals on, he collapsed from hunger. The goddess Alamelumaṅga appeared, told him to remove his sandals on the sacred hill, and fed him.
At the temple he began to sing, and dedicated his life to Veṅkaṭeśvara. He studied, married, and became a Vaiṣṇava teacher.
For a time he lived at the court of a local ruler; one story says he refused to sing the king’s praises, saying his songs belonged to God alone, and was imprisoned until released by grace.
He lived about 95 years, singing, by tradition, at least one song a day.

ZAPPY: He refused to sing for a king?

PROF. BOLT: Only for Veṅkaṭeśvara, Zappy. So the story goes.

Songs for God alone.

IN THE VERSE

Brahmam okkaṭe

His most famous song (refrain)
tandanāna āhi tandanāna purētandanāna bhaḷā tandanānabrahmam okkaṭe parabrahmam okkaṭeparabrahmam okkaṭe
(Tandanāna: a folk rhythm.) Brahman is one, the supreme Brahman is one.
WordMeaning
tandanānafolk refrain syllables
bhaḷābravo, well done
brahmamthe supreme reality
okkaṭeis one (Telugu)
parasupreme

Equality. The verses go on to say the king and the servant sleep the same sleep; the earth the elephant walks on and the earth the dog walks on is the same; the high-born and the outcaste share one Lord. Sung in a lively folk rhythm.

Annamācārya, saṅkīrtana “Brahmam okkaṭe” (Telugu); refrain only quoted, the rest paraphrased.
One God, one humanity.

HIS SONGS

Devotion and love

🎵 Saṅkīrtana

A song with a refrain (pallavi) and verses (caraṇas), easy for groups to sing: he made it the classic Telugu devotional form.

🙏 Adhyātma

Spiritual songs on the soul, the world, dharma and surrender, like “Brahmam okkaṭe” and “Nānāṭi bratuku nāṭakamu” (life is a play).

💞 Śṛṅgāra

Love songs to Veṅkaṭeśvara and Alamelumaṅga, the soul as a woman longing for God.

👶 Famous

“Adivo alladivo” (there, there is the hill of Hari), “Muddugāre Yaśodā” (Kṛṣṇa as Yaśodā’s jewel), “Kondalalo nelakonna”.

PROF. BOLT: Like Rādhā’s, Zappy. See Rādhā.

Devotion in two moods.

SAVED IN COPPER

Lost and found

Engraved by his family. His son and grandsons had his songs engraved on copper plates, a rare care for a poet’s work.

Hidden. The plates were stored in a small room in the Tirumala temple and largely forgotten for centuries.

Found. Mentioned in 1849, they were later rediscovered and studied; Tirumala Tirupati Devasthānams (TTD) has worked to publish and preserve them.

About 12,000. That is what survives of the traditional 32,000, plus a treatise on song-writing, the Saṅkīrtana Lakṣaṇamu.

ZAPPY: Lost for centuries!

PROF. BOLT: Thank goodness for copper, Zappy.

Copper kept the songs.

A SAINT FOR EVERYONE

The social message

Against pride of birth. Several songs say plainly that devotion, not caste, matters to God.

Plain Telugu. He sang in the language of ordinary people, not only Sanskrit.

Daily life. Songs mention farmers, cowherds, mothers and children, bringing God into ordinary scenes.

Part of a movement. Like the Haridāsas of Karnataka, such as Purandara Dāsa who is said to have met him, he made bhakti a people’s song. (The meeting is tradition.)

ZAPPY: Did he meet Purandara Dāsa?

PROF. BOLT: Tradition says so, Zappy. It would be a wonderful meeting.

God for everyone.

A FAMILY OF POETS

The Tāḷḷapāka line

Timmakka. His wife wrote the Subhadrā Kalyāṇam; she is counted among the earliest women poets in Telugu.

Pedda Tirumalācārya. His son continued the songs and the copper plates.

Cinnanna. A grandson wrote Annamācārya’s life story.

The temple link. The Tāḷḷapāka family held a hereditary role at Tirumala, singing before the god.

ZAPPY: His wife was a poet too!

PROF. BOLT: One of the first women writers in Telugu, Zappy.

A household of song.

THE REVIVAL

Annamayya today

Musicians. M. S. Subbulakshmi made songs like “Bhāvamulona” and “Nānāṭi bratuku” famous; Dr M. Balamuralikrishna set music to over 800 of them; Nedunuri Krishnamurthy and others composed many tunes.

Film. The 1997 Telugu film Annamayya brought his story to millions.

At Tirumala. TTD runs an Annamācārya project; his songs are sung in the temple and at his birthplace on his jayantī and ārādhana.

Carnatic music. Scholars see him as an influence on the later Carnatic tradition.

ZAPPY: His tunes are mostly modern?

PROF. BOLT: The original tunes were lost, Zappy; the words survived, and musicians set them again.

Old words, new tunes.
