SAINTS · THE TEACHER OF DIFFERENCE

Madhvācārya

The Teacher of Udupi · explained and illustrated

Hari is supreme

HOW THIS WORKS: Madhvācārya of Udupi, founder of Dvaita Vedānta: his boyhood at Pājaka, the image of Kṛṣṇa from a shipwreck that he installed at Udupi, the eight maṭhas and the paryāya, his teaching of five differences and the supremacy of Hari, his works, his journeys to Badarī, and the Haridāsas who sang his message in Kannada.

WHERE WE ARE

On the Tuḷu coast, tradition says, a ship was caught in a storm near Udupi. A young monk on the shore waved his upper cloth and the ship came safely in. The grateful captain offered him anything; the monk asked only for a block of white clay in the hold. Inside it was an image of the child Kṛṣṇa. The monk was Madhvācārya, and that image is worshipped at Udupi to this day.

Madhva founded the Dvaita school of Vedānta and the Mādhva tradition, which gave Karnataka the Haridāsas, Rāghavendra Svāmi and Purandara Dāsa. This guide tells his life and teaching.

THE BOY FROM PĀJAKA

Early signs

The story

Madhvavijaya of Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍitācārya (14th c.); legend.

Vāsudeva was born at Pājaka, near Udupi, to a Śivaḷḷi Brahmin family. Stories tell of a strong, fearless boy who once killed a demon in serpent form with his toe.
Still in his teens, he took sannyāsa under Acyutaprekṣa, who taught Advaita, and received the name Pūrṇaprajña.
He disagreed with Advaita’s reading of the scriptures and argued with his teacher; tradition says Acyutaprekṣa came to accept his view.
For his learning he was named Ānandatīrtha, head of the Vedānta school. He travelled south and north, debating scholars.
His biography, the Madhvavijaya, was written by the son of one of his disciples.

ZAPPY: He argued with his own guru?

PROF. BOLT: And won him over, says the tradition, Zappy.

Fearless from the start.

KṚṢṆA FROM THE SEA

The Udupi image

The story

Udupi temple tradition.

A ship caught in a storm off Malpe was brought safely to shore, tradition says, when Madhva waved his upper cloth.
The captain offered gifts. Madhva asked only for a heavy block of gopīcandana (white clay) used as ballast.
Inside was an image of the child Kṛṣṇa holding a churning rod, said to have been worshipped by Rukmiṇī at Dvārakā.
Singing the twelve hymns of the Dvādaśa Stotra, Madhva carried it to Udupi, washed it in the Madhva Sarovara and installed it.
He appointed eight young monks to worship it in turn: the beginning of Udupi’s Aṣṭa Maṭhas. See Udupi Śrī Kṛṣṇa.

ZAPPY: The clay was just ballast!

PROF. BOLT: The treasure in plain sight, Zappy.

Treasure in plain clay.

THE EIGHT MAṬHAS

Udupi’s rotation

8️⃣ Aṣṭa Maṭhas

Pejāvara, Palimāru, Adamāru, Puttige, Sodhe, Kaṇiyūru, Śirūru and Kṛṣṇāpura: each founded by one of Madhva’s eight disciples.

🔄 Paryāya

The worship of Kṛṣṇa rotates among the eight; today each maṭha’s head serves for two years.

🎉 Paryāya festival

The handover in January every second year is one of Udupi’s biggest events; see Udupi festivals.

🏛️ Other maṭhas

Uttarādi Maṭha, Rāghavendra Maṭha (Mantrālaya), Vyāsarāja Maṭha and others trace their lineage to him.

ZAPPY: Two years each, eight maṭhas!

PROF. BOLT: Sixteen years round the cycle, Zappy.

Worship in turn.

DVAITA: FIVE DIFFERENCES

What he taught

Difference is real. Against Advaita, Madhva taught that God, souls and matter are eternally distinct; he called his view Tattvavāda, “the doctrine of reality”.

Five differences (pañca-bheda). Between God and soul, God and matter, soul and soul, soul and matter, and matter and matter.

God is independent. Viṣṇu alone is svatantra, fully independent; everything else is dependent on him.

Grace. Liberation comes by God’s grace, won through knowledge and devotion; it is a state of joy in God’s presence, not merging.

ZAPPY: So I’m never God?

PROF. BOLT: In Dvaita, Zappy: you are God’s, forever. That’s the joy.

You are God’s.

HARI IS SUPREME

The Mādhva motto

🔝 Harisarvottama

“Hari (Viṣṇu) is supreme over all”: the first half of the Mādhva motto.

💨 Vāyujīvottama

“Vāyu is the highest of souls”: Mukhyaprāṇa, the life-breath, as the chief devotee.

🪜 Tāratamya

Souls differ in nature and capacity, forming a gradation from the highest to the lowest. (This doctrine is distinctive and debated by other schools.)

🐒 Three avatāras

Mādhvas hold that Vāyu came as Hanumān, Bhīma and Madhva himself; Madhva also presents himself so in his writings.

ZAPPY: Is that why Mādhva temples have Hanumān?

PROF. BOLT: Mukhyaprāṇa, Zappy. Look for him beside Kṛṣṇa.

Hari above, Vāyu nearest.

BADARĪ AND THE END

Meeting Vyāsa

To the Himalayas. Madhva travelled to Badarī more than once; tradition says he met the sage Vyāsa, author of the Brahma Sūtra, in the high Himalayas and learned from him.

Criticism. Some modern scholars say certain texts he quotes cannot be traced and may be his own; Mādhva scholars reject this.

The end. Tradition says that while teaching the Aitareya Upaniṣad at Udupi he disappeared in a shower of flowers and went to Badarī, where he lives with Vyāsa. The day is kept as Madhva Navamī.

Madhva Jayantī. His birth is celebrated on Vijayadaśamī.

ZAPPY: He didn’t die?

PROF. BOLT: Mādhvas say he went to Vyāsa, Zappy. The place at Udupi is marked.

Still with Vyāsa.

HIS LEGACY

Karnataka’s singing tradition

The Haridāsas. Śrīpādarāja, Vyāsatīrtha, Purandara Dāsa and Kanaka Dāsa sang his ideas in simple Kannada songs that reached every home.

Great teachers. Jayatīrtha, Vyāsatīrtha and Rāghavendra Tīrtha wrote and taught in his line.

Debate. Madhva challenged Advaita most strongly and also disagreed with Rāmānuja; the arguments shaped Vedānta for centuries.

Udupi. The Kṛṣṇa temple, its maṭhas and its food tradition carry his presence daily.

ZAPPY: So Udupi’s songs come from him!

PROF. BOLT: Through the Haridāsas, Zappy. Karnataka sings his teaching.

A teaching that sings.
