SAINTS · THE TEACHER OF SURRENDER

Rāmānuja

The Teacher of Surrender · explained and illustrated

Grace for everyone

HOW THIS WORKS: Rāmānujācārya, the great teacher of Viśiṣṭādvaita and Śrīvaiṣṇavism: his life from Śrīperumbudūr to Śrīraṅgam, the secret mantra shouted from a temple tower, the years in Karnataka at Mēlukōṭe, his works, his teaching of surrender open to all, and his legacy.

WHERE WE ARE

A teacher climbs a temple tower and shouts a sacred mantra to the crowd below, a mantra he had been told to keep secret on pain of hell. “If they are saved,” he says, “I will gladly go to hell.” That is the most famous story of Rāmānujācārya.

Rāmānuja gave Śrīvaiṣṇavism its philosophy, organised worship at Śrīraṅgam, and spent years in Karnataka at Mēlukōṭe. This guide tells his life and explains his teaching.

A YOUNG SEEKER

Teachers and a turn

The story

Śrīvaiṣṇava hagiographies (Guruparamparā); legend.

Rāmānuja was born at Śrīperumbudūr and studied Vedānta at Kāñcī under Yādavaprakāśa, an Advaita teacher. He kept disagreeing over the meaning of Upaniṣad passages.
Tradition says jealous Yādavaprakāśa plotted to drown him on pilgrimage; warned, Rāmānuja escaped through a forest, guided by a hunter couple he later saw as Viṣṇu and Lakṣmī of Kāñcī.
Yāmunācārya, the head of the Śrīvaiṣṇavas at Śrīraṅgam, heard of him and sent for him. Rāmānuja arrived to find him dead, three fingers folded.
He understood: three unfulfilled wishes. He vowed to write a commentary on the Brahma Sūtra, honour the sage Parāśara, and spread the Tamil hymns of Nammāḻvār. The fingers opened.
Rāmānuja later became a monk at Kāñcī and the leader at Śrīraṅgam.

ZAPPY: A dead teacher’s fingers opened!

PROF. BOLT: The tradition’s way of saying he took up the work, Zappy.

THE MANTRA FROM THE TOWER

Grace for all

The story

Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition; legend.

Rāmānuja went to Tirukkōṣṭiyūr Nambi to learn the meaning of the sacred eight-syllable mantra. Tradition says he was turned away seventeen times before being taught on the eighteenth.
The teacher warned: “Keep it secret. Whoever learns it from a worthy teacher is saved; if you reveal it carelessly you will go to hell.”
Rāmānuja climbed the temple tower and called the people. He shared the mantra and its meaning with everyone gathered.
His teacher was furious. Rāmānuja said: “If so many are saved, I will happily go to hell alone.”
Nambi embraced him and called him Emberumānār, “our lord”.

ZAPPY: He risked hell for strangers!

PROF. BOLT: That’s why people call him compassion itself, Zappy.

Share what saves.

AT ŚRĪRAṄGAM

Temple and community

Organising worship. He reorganised the rituals and administration of the Raṅganātha temple at Śrīraṅgam, much of which survives.

Tamil and Sanskrit. He placed the Tamil hymns of the Āḻvārs beside the Sanskrit Vedas: the “ubhaya Vedānta”, two-fold Veda.

Opening doors. He gave non-Brahmin devotees roles in temple service and worship.

Disciples. Kūrattāḻvān, Mudaliyāṇḍān, Embār and many others; his circle wrote down his teaching.

ZAPPY: Tamil songs equal to the Vedas?

PROF. BOLT: In Śrīvaiṣṇava eyes, Zappy, yes. A big step.

Two Vedas, one temple.

THE KARNATAKA YEARS

Mēlukōṭe and the Hoysaḷas

Exile. Hagiographies say persecution by a Chōḻa king drove him from Śrīraṅgam to Karnataka for about fourteen years. His disciple Kūrattāḻvān, who stayed, was blinded.

Viṣṇuvardhana. At the Hoysaḷa court, tradition says, the Jain king Biṭṭideva became his follower and took the name Viṣṇuvardhana. (Historians debate the details.)

Mēlukōṭe. Rāmānuja is linked with restoring the temple of Tirunārāyaṇa at Mēlukōṭe and with its festival image, Cheluvanārāyaṇa.

Open to all. At Mēlukōṭe he is said to have admitted communities then excluded from temples to worship on festival days.

ZAPPY: So Mysuru district is his home too!

PROF. BOLT: His second home, Zappy. Mēlukōṭe still celebrates him.

A home in Karnataka.

IN THE VERSE

The verse he lived by

Gītā 18.66
sarvadharmān parityajyamām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vrajaahaṃ tvā sarvapāpebhyomokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ
Giving up all other means, take refuge in me alone. I will free you from all sins; do not grieve.
WordMeaning
sarva-dharmānall (other) means
parityajyagiving up
śaraṇam vrajatake refuge
mokṣayiṣyāmiI will free
mā śucaḥdo not grieve

The “final verse”. Śrīvaiṣṇavas call it the carama śloka: Kṛṣṇa’s last word in the Gītā. For Rāmānuja’s tradition it is the charter of surrender (prapatti).

Bhagavad Gītā 18.66; see Gītā chapter 18.
Do not grieve.

QUALIFIED NON-DUALISM

Viśiṣṭādvaita

One, with qualities. There is one reality, Brahman, who is Nārāyaṇa (Viṣṇu), full of countless good qualities, not a featureless absolute.

The body of God. Souls and the world are real and are God’s body; he is their inner self. One God, “qualified” by them: viśiṣṭa-advaita.

Liberation. The freed soul does not dissolve into God but serves and enjoys him forever in Vaikuṇṭha.

Bhakti and prapatti. Loving meditation (bhakti) leads to God; for those who cannot, complete surrender (prapatti) is enough, and it is open to everyone. See Sampradāyas.

PROF. BOLT: For Śaṅkara the many dissolve into one, Zappy; for Rāmānuja they remain, held in God.

Nine books

📘 Gītā Bhāṣya

Commentary on the Bhagavad Gītā.

🙏 Gadya Traya

PROF. BOLT: Gadya means prose, Zappy. Unusual and beautiful.

Philosophy and prayer.

HIS LEGACY

A thousand years on

A long life. Tradition gives him 120 years. His preserved form (tirumeni) is venerated at Śrīraṅgam, by tradition.

Two schools. After him the tradition divided into the Vaḍakalai and Teṅkalai schools, differing on how grace and effort combine.

Bhakti movement. His ideas spread north; Rāmānanda and later North Indian devotion trace links to him.

Statue of Equality. A 216-foot statue of Rāmānuja, opened near Hyderabad in 2022, honours his openness to all.

ZAPPY: A statue for equality!

PROF. BOLT: For opening the temple doors, Zappy.
