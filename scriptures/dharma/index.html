Dharma

And the Four Aims of Life · explained and illustrated

What a life is for

HOW THIS WORKS: Dharma, the right way that holds everything together, and the four puruṣārthas, the aims of life: dharma, artha (prosperity), kāma (love and enjoyment) and mokṣa (freedom). Where the ideas come from, how they fit together, the four stages of life, universal virtues, how to know what is right, and the debates.

WHERE WE ARE

What is a good life for? Hindu tradition gives a surprisingly balanced answer: four aims, the puruṣārthas. Live rightly (dharma), earn and prosper (artha), love and enjoy (kāma), and finally become free (mokṣa).

Holding them together is dharma, a word with no single English equivalent: duty, righteousness, law, the natural order, the way things should be. This guide explains both. For action and its results, see Karma, rebirth and mokṣa.

WHAT IS DHARMA?

That which holds

To hold. Dharma comes from the root dhṛ, “to hold, support”. Dharma is whatever upholds life: the person, the family, society, the cosmos.

Ṛta. In the Ṛgveda the cosmic order is ṛta; dharma grows from that idea of a right order.

Many meanings. Duty, law, virtue, religion, nature: fire’s dharma is to burn, a teacher’s is to teach, a ruler’s is to protect.

Subtle. The Mahābhārata repeatedly says dharma is sūkṣma, subtle, hard to know in hard cases. Its heroes struggle with real dilemmas.

ZAPPY: Fire has a dharma?

PROF. BOLT: Its nature, Zappy: to burn and give light.

What holds us up.

KINDS OF DHARMA

Universal and personal

🌍 Sādhāraṇa dharma

Duties for everyone: non-violence (ahiṃsā), truth (satya), not stealing, purity, self-control. Lists vary.

👤 Svadharma

One’s own duty, by stage of life, role and nature: student, parent, doctor, ruler. “Better one’s own dharma, imperfectly done, than another’s done well” (Gītā 3.35).

⚠️ Āpad dharma

Rules for emergencies, when normal duty is impossible.

⏳ Yuga dharma

What fits a particular age; e.g. the teaching that in Kali yuga, chanting God’s name is the chief practice.

ZAPPY: My dharma isn’t the same as yours?

PROF. BOLT: Your role differs, Zappy; truth and kindness are for both of us.

Shared and personal.

HOW TO KNOW WHAT IS RIGHT

Four guides

Scripture (śruti). The Vedas, the first source.

Tradition (smṛti). Texts like the Dharmaśāstras and the epics, read with a teacher.

Good people (sadācāra). How the wise and good actually behave.

Conscience (ātmatuṣṭi). What satisfies one’s own clear inner sense. Manusmṛti (2.6, 2.12) names these four; the last is the guide where others are silent.

ZAPPY: My conscience counts?

PROF. BOLT: As the fourth guide, Zappy, when it’s clear and honest.

Four guides.

THE FOUR AIMS

Puruṣārthas

⚖️ Dharma

Right conduct: duty, ethics, justice.

💰 Artha

Prosperity and means: work, wealth, security, good government. Kauṭilya’s Arthaśāstra calls it the base of the others.

❤️ Kāma

Pleasure, love, beauty and the arts. The Kāmasūtra treats it as a proper goal, within dharma.

🕊️ Mokṣa

Liberation, the highest aim for most traditions; see Karma.

ZAPPY: A whole book on artha!

PROF. BOLT: And on kāma, Zappy. The tradition took ordinary life seriously.

All four count.

KEEPING THE BALANCE

Dharma first

Dharma leads. When wealth or pleasure conflicts with right conduct, dharma comes first: earn honestly, love faithfully.

No extremes. The Arthaśāstra warns that chasing any one aim excessively harms all three; the epics show kings ruined by greed or lust.

Tirukkuṟaḷ. The great Tamil classic is arranged in three books: aṟam (dharma), poruḷ (artha) and iṉpam (kāma), implying that living them well leads to the fourth.

A full life. The ideal is not to suppress artha and kāma but to make them serve dharma.

ZAPPY: Money isn’t bad?

PROF. BOLT: Not when it’s earned rightly and used well, Zappy.

Earn, love, within dharma.

THE FOUR STAGES OF LIFE

Āśramas

Brahmacarya. The student: learning and discipline.

Gṛhastha. The householder: work, family, giving. Texts praise it as the support of all others.

Vānaprastha. Retirement: stepping back, handing over, more time for study and worship.

Sannyāsa. Renunciation, devoted wholly to mokṣa. Some scholars link the four aims to these stages; others say the fit is loose.

ZAPPY: Which stage am I?

PROF. BOLT: Student, Zappy. The best one for learning all this.

A time for each.

IN THE VERSE

Dharma protects

Manusmṛti 8.15
dharma eva hato hantidharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥtasmād dharmo na hantavyomā no dharmo hato’vadhīt
Dharma, when destroyed, destroys; dharma, when protected, protects. Therefore dharma must not be destroyed, lest dharma destroyed destroy us.
WordMeaning
hataḥdestroyed, violated
hantidestroys
rakṣatiprotects
rakṣitaḥwhen protected
na hantavyaḥmust not be violated

A famous line. The middle phrase, “dharmo rakṣati rakṣitaḥ”, is a motto of many Indian institutions. The idea: uphold what is right and it will uphold you.

QUESTIONS AND DEBATES

Living tradition

World or renunciation? Thinkers long debated pravṛtti (active life) versus nivṛtti (withdrawal). The Gītā’s answer: act, but without attachment.

Birth-based duty. Old texts tie duties to varṇa and gender; many modern Hindus and reformers reject birth-based hierarchy while keeping svadharma as personal calling.

Changing rules. Dharmaśāstras themselves say custom and time change what is right; dharma has always been interpreted.

Hard cases. The Mahābhārata, with Yudhiṣṭhira’s and Arjuna’s dilemmas, is the great textbook of dharma’s difficulty.

ZAPPY: So Hindus disagree about dharma?

PROF. BOLT: Always have, Zappy. Arguing about what’s right is part of dharma.

Dharma is thought through.
