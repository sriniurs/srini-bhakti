Four Yogas

Four Paths, One Summit · explained and illustrated

Four paths, one summit

HOW THIS WORKS: The four yogas, the classic paths to God: karma yoga (selfless action), bhakti yoga (love and devotion), jñāna yoga (knowledge) and rāja yoga (meditation, Patañjali’s eight limbs). Where the idea comes from in the Gītā, who made it famous, how to find your path, and why most people walk all four.

WHERE WE ARE

Today “yoga” usually means postures on a mat. In Hindu tradition the word is much bigger: yoga means “joining”, any discipline that unites us with God or the true Self.

Tradition describes several such paths. The best-known scheme names four: work, love, knowledge and meditation. This guide explains each, with its key verses, and how they fit together. For karma itself, see Karma, rebirth and mokṣa.

WHERE THE FOUR COME FROM

Gītā and Vivekananda

The Gītā’s yogas. The Bhagavad Gītā teaches karma yoga, bhakti yoga, jñāna yoga and dhyāna (meditation) yoga in different chapters, and its chapters are each called a “yoga”.

Patañjali. The Yoga Sūtras (early centuries CE) systematise meditation: yoga is “the stilling of the movements of the mind” (1.2).

The four-fold scheme. Swami Vivekananda made the four paths famous in lectures and books in the 1890s, such as Rāja Yoga (1896), presenting them for a worldwide audience.

Older and newer. The paths are ancient; the neat “four” is a modern summary. Traditions also speak of other yogas (e.g. mantra, haṭha).

ZAPPY: So the four-path list is modern?

PROF. BOLT: The summary is, Zappy. The paths are very old.

IN THE VERSE

Karma yoga

Bhagavad Gītā 2.47
karmaṇy evādhikāras temā phaleṣu kadācanamā karmaphalahetur bhūrmā te saṅgo’stv akarmaṇi
Your right is to the action alone, never to its fruits. Do not let the fruit of action be your motive, and do not be attached to inaction.
WordMeaning

saṅgaḥattachment

The heart of karma yoga. Act fully and well; offer the result. Not doing nothing (that is also attachment), but doing without grasping. The whole of Gītā chapter 3 explains this path.

THE PATH OF WORK

Karma yoga in life

For whom. Active people, who find God through service. Most people live in action; this path transforms it.

How. Do your duty well; do it for others; offer the results to God; stay even in success and failure.

Seva. Service to the poor, the sick and the community, done without wanting credit, is karma yoga in practice.

Effect. Work done this way purifies the mind of selfishness, preparing it for knowledge and love.

THE PATH OF LOVE

Bhakti yoga

For whom. People of feeling: those who are moved by love, music and the personal God. Many teachers call it the easiest path for our age.

The Gītā. Chapter 12 praises the devotee who is friendly to all, free of hate, steady in joy and sorrow: “that devotee is dear to me”.

Nine forms. The Bhāgavata lists nine ways: hearing, singing, remembering, serving God’s feet, worship, salutation, service, friendship and self-surrender.

Saints. The Āḻvārs, Nāyanmārs, Haridāsas, Annamācārya and Rādhā are its models.

ZAPPY: Singing counts as yoga?

PROF. BOLT: Kīrtana is one of the nine forms, Zappy.

Love as the way.

THE PATH OF KNOWLEDGE

Jñāna yoga

For whom. Thinkers: those driven to ask “what is real?” and “who am I?”

Discrimination. Separating the real from the passing (viveka), and turning away from what passes (vairāgya).

Three steps. Hearing the teaching (śravaṇa), reflecting on it (manana), and meditating on it (nididhyāsana), as taught in the Upaniṣads.

Goal. Direct knowledge of the Self; for Śaṅkara this alone gives liberation.

ZAPPY: Thinking your way to God?

PROF. BOLT: Thinking until thinking falls quiet, Zappy.

Know the knower.

THE PATH OF MEDITATION

Rāja yoga: the eight limbs

🤝 Yama and niyama

Ethics first: non-violence, truth, not stealing, restraint, non-greed; and purity, contentment, discipline, study, devotion to God.

🧘 Āsana and prāṇāyāma

A steady, comfortable posture and control of the breath: the part most people know.

🙈 Pratyāhāra

Withdrawing the senses from their objects.

🎯 Dhāraṇā, dhyāna, samādhi

Concentration, meditation and absorption, which Patañjali calls together saṃyama.

ZAPPY: Postures are only step three?

PROF. BOLT: Of eight, Zappy. Ethics come first.

Eight steps inward.

WHICH PATH IS MINE?

Temperament and balance

By nature. Teachers often match the path to temperament: active, emotional, intellectual or meditative.

Combine them. Most traditions and teachers say the paths support each other. Work purifies, love softens, knowledge clarifies, meditation steadies.

Schools differ. Advaita stresses jñāna; Śrīvaiṣṇava and Mādhva traditions stress bhakti and grace; all value karma yoga and meditation.

Start where you are. One small daily practice in each: do one task as an offering, sing one bhajan, read one verse, sit quietly for five minutes.
