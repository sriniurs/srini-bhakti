Hayagrīva

Viṣṇu’s horse-headed form and Agastya’s teacher.

Viṣṇu as the teacher

Hayagrīva is the horse-headed form of Viṣṇu who teaches Agastya in the Lalitopākhyāna. The name combines haya, “horse,” and grīva, “neck.” In this narrative his distinguishing role is to transmit knowledge of Lalitā.

The opening frame of the Lalitopākhyāna places Agastya at Kāñcī. Troubled by the suffering of beings, the sage asks how they may be helped. Hayagrīva answers by unfolding the account of Lalitā. When Agastya asks how Bhaṇḍa arose, how the Goddess appeared, and how she defeated him, those questions set the course of the story.

From the story to the two hymns

The Lalitā Sahasranāma · 10 articles is framed as a dialogue in which Hayagrīva transmits the thousand names to Agastya. The hymn credits Vaśinī and the other speech goddesses with composition. That distinction helps explain why both Hayagrīva and the speech goddesses appear in accounts of the hymn.

The Lalitā Triśatī is the separate hymn of three hundred names, likewise presented in a Hayagrīva–Agastya dialogue. Reading the narrative and the hymns together helps identify the people, places, and images compressed into the names.
