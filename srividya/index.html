    Lalitopākhyāna Part 1: Kāma burned, Bhaṇḍa born. Tāraka and the gods, Kāma’s arrows and Śiva’s third eye, Citrakarmā shaping a man from the ash, the Śatarudrīya and Śiva’s boons, the name Bhaṇḍa, the city Śūnyaka and his brothers.

  • Lalitopākhyāna 2: Lalitā rises from the fire

    Lalitopākhyāna Part 2: Lalitā rises from the fire. Bhaṇḍa’s reign, Viṣṇu’s Māyā, the gods’ penance and great sacrifice, the Goddess appearing in a wheel of light, the Lalitā Stavarāja, Kāmeśvara, the wedding and its gifts, the coronation as Kāmākṣī, and the birth of her army.

  • Lalitopākhyāna 3: Śrīpura, the city of the Goddess

    Lalitopākhyāna Part 3: Śrīpura, the city of the Goddess. On the central peak of Meru, built by Viśvakarmā and Maya; walls of metal from black iron to gold and of gems from topaz to pearl; heavenly groves; the officers’ quarters; the Cintāmaṇi palace; nine Sahasranāma names; Maṇidvīpa.

  • Lalitopākhyāna 4: The war: the first day

    Lalitopākhyāna Part 4: the war, first day. The march and the three chariots, panic in Śūnyaka, Sampatkarī and Aśvārūḍhā kill Durmada and Kuraṇḍa, Nakulī defeats Sarpiṇī, Tiraskariṇī destroys the seven brothers, Viṣaṅga’s night attack and the fifteen Nityās.

  • Lalitopākhyāna 5: The war: Bālā and Gaṇeśa

    Lalitopākhyāna Part 5: the war, days two and three. The officers’ apology, Jvālāmālinī’s wall of fire and the camp, Bālā on her swan palanquin kills Bhaṇḍa’s thirty sons, Viśukra’s Jayavighna yantra, Gaṇeśa born from Lalitā’s laugh, Gajāsura, the thirst weapon and the ocean, and the fall of the brothers.

  • Lalitopākhyāna 6: Bhaṇḍa falls, Kāma returns

    Lalitopākhyāna Part 6: Bhaṇḍa falls, Kāma returns. Lalitā rides out on the fourth day; Bhaṇḍa’s weapons and her counters; Durgā; the avatāras from her fingernails; the Nārāyaṇa, Pāśupata and Mahākāmeśvara missiles; Rati’s grief; Kāma revived; Śiva and Pārvatī united; Skanda born.

Deities of Śrīvidyā

  • Aśvārūḍhā

    Aśvārūḍhā, the horse-riding commander of Lalitā’s cavalry: her name, her origin from Lalitā’s noose, her form, her place in the war and the Sahasranāma, horses in Indian thought, the Kaṭha Upaniṣad’s chariot of the senses, and her meaning.

    Read

  • Bālā Tripurasundarī

    Bālā Tripurasundarī, the Goddess as a young girl: her form with book and rosary, her victory over the thirty sons of Bhaṇḍa, her name in the Sahasranāma, the three ages of the Goddess, patron of learning, kanyā and kumārī pūjā, families, Nemili and her meaning.

    Read

  • Kāmeśvara

    Kāmeśvara, Śiva as the Lord of Desire and consort of Lalitā: the burning of Kāma and Śiva’s transformation, his form as her mirror, the wedding, his names in the Sahasranāma, the Kāmeśvara missile, Gaṇeśa born from a glance, the bindu of the Śrīcakra, and his temple forms.

    Read

  • Mahāgaṇapati

    Mahāgaṇapati with Siddhilakṣmī, the form of Gaṇeśa in Śrīvidyā: born from Lalitā’s glance at Kāmeśvara, his ten hands, his consort, the shattering of the Jayavighna obstacle-yantra, his Sahasranāma names, first in worship, the 32 forms of the Mysore Śrītattvanidhi, and his temples.

    Read

  • Nīla Sarasvatī

    Nīla Sarasvatī, the blue goddess of speech: her identity as a form of Tārā, her fierce form with sword and scissors, the shared Hindu and Buddhist imagery, the gift of speech, legends of poets, the Śrīkula and Kālīkula, her place in some Śrīvidyā lineages, and her meaning.

    Read

  • Pratyaṅgirā

    Pratyaṅgirā, the lion-faced goddess of protection: her name (harm turned back; the sages Aṅgiras and Pratyaṅgiras), her form, the Śaiva and Śākta telling of Narasiṃha’s fury, Indrajit and Nikumbhilā, the Atharvaveda link, her temples at Ayyāvāḍi and elsewhere. Story, form, temples and meaning only.

  • Rājaśyāmalā

    Rājaśyāmalā, also Mātaṅgī and Mantriṇī, Lalitā’s prime minister and goddess of music and speech: her names and green form with vīṇā and parrot, the Geyacakra chariot and the slaying of Viṣaṅga, Mātaṅgī among the Mahāvidyās, Kālidāsa’s Śyāmalā Daṇḍaka, Madurai and Māgha Navarātri.

  • Sampatkarī

    Sampatkarī, the elephant-riding commander of Lalitā’s army and maker of prosperity: her name, her origin from Lalitā’s goad, her form, her place in the war and the Sahasranāma, elephants in Indian thought, anger mastered, prosperity and stability, and the Mysuru Dasarā parallel.

    Read

  • Tiraskariṇī

    Tiraskariṇī, the veiling goddess of Lalitā’s army: her name (veil, curtain, the hidden goddess), her dark form with the bow Mohana, her chariot Tamoliptā, her victory over the seven Balāhaka brothers in Lalitopākhyāna chapter 24, her place under Daṇḍanāthā, and the protective power of veiling.

    Read

  • Ucchiṣṭa Gaṇapati

    Ucchiṣṭa Gaṇapati, the Gaṇeśa of the remnant: the meaning of ucchiṣṭa, the Atharvaveda hymn to the remnant, his blue form with consort and pomegranate, the Ucchiṣṭa Gāṇapatya school, Ucchiṣṭa Mātaṅgī, the Tirunelveli temple, and stories of sacred leftovers. Story, form, temples and meaning only.

  • Vāñchākalpalatā Gaṇapati

    Vāñchākalpalatā Gaṇapati, the “creeper that fulfils every wish”, a form of Gaṇapati honoured within Śrīvidyā lineages: the meaning of his name, the wish-fulfilling tree and creeper, his relation to Mahāgaṇapati and other joined forms, what is open and what is guarded, and what wish-fulfilment means in Śrīvidyā.

  • Vārāhī

    Vārāhī, the boar-faced commander of Lalitā’s army: her twelve names, her form with plough and pestle, her life as one of the seven Mothers, Varāha and the earth, the Kiricakra chariot and the killing of Viśukra, Āṣāḍha Navarātri, and her temples at Chaurasi, Varanasi and Thanjavur.
