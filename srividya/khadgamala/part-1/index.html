ŚRĪVIDYĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE

THE OPENING AND THE SIX LIMBS

Sets 1–2 · calling the Mother, and her limbs

KHAḌGAMĀLĀ · 1 OF 13

FOREWORD

The Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā, “garland of the sword”, is one long chain of names. It calls, one after another, every devī who lives in the Śrīcakra, from the gates of the outer square to Lalitā at the centre: 182 names in the edition we follow. Chanting it in order is called a Navāvaraṇa worship done with the voice and the mind alone.

This series follows the sets of that edition, one part for each, with a diagram showing where every devī sits. Part 1 covers the first two sets: the opening salutation, and the six devīs of the Mother’s limbs.

Śrī Lalitāyai namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Prof, why is a list of names called a sword?

PROF. BOLT: Tradition says this garland gives a sword of protection, Zappy. And the sword is wisdom too: it cuts the knot that makes us feel separate from her.

WHAT IS THE KHAḌGAMĀLĀ?

The garland of the sword
NameKhaḍga = sword; mālā = garland
Also calledŚuddhaśaktimālā: the garland of pure Śakti
SourceTraditionally the Vāmakeśvara Tantra
FormA single chain of names, almost all in the vocative: “O …!”
Count182 names in the edition we follow, in 15 sets
OrderFrom the outer square of the Śrīcakra inward to the bindu
PromiseKhaḍga-siddhi: a protecting sword that wins every battle, outer and inner

ZAPPY: So it’s like a guided walk through the Śrīcakra?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly. Every name is a step, every set an enclosure. You arrive at the centre by naming everyone on the way.

The hymn is made of names in the vocative case, the form used to call someone: Kāmeśvari! Nityaklinne! A few, ending in -mayī, mean “you who are present as…”. It is meant to be said aloud, at a steady pace, with attention resting on each devī in turn.

Its tradition places it in the Vāmakeśvara Tantra, one of the root texts of Śrīvidyā, and it is widely recited in South India, including by householders who do not perform the full Navāvaraṇa worship. Many treat a single recitation as a complete worship of the Śrīcakra.

THE JOURNEY OF THE GARLAND

Five stages
1Sambodhanam: calling the Mother
2Nyāsa, Nityās, gurus: her limbs, her moon days, her lineage
3Āvaraṇas 1–9: from the outer square to the bindu
4The nine Navacakreśvarīs: the queens of the nine enclosures
5Namaskāra: nine great salutations
DirectionFrom outside to inside: from the world of the senses to the bliss at the centre

ZAPPY: Thirteen parts for one hymn?

PROF. BOLT: One for each stage of the walk, so every devī gets her place and her meaning. Your diagram shows where to find her.

The journey mirrors the Śrīcakra itself. The guides to the nine enclosures in our Śrīcakra series describe each layer, its presiding Tripurā and its Dīkṣitar song; this series follows the hymn’s own order, name by name, and shows where each devī sits.

The garland begins with preparation (the limbs, the moon days, the teachers), then enters the city at its gates and walks inward, and ends at the bindu with salutation. Outside to inside is the movement of every Śrīvidyā practice.

THE ŚRĪCAKRA AS A MAP

How to read the diagrams
OrientationEast at the top; you sit at the west, facing it
MovementThe worship moves from the outer square inward to the bindu
Within a setDevīs are placed from the west, in front of you, moving anticlockwise
TrianglesNine interlocking triangles, four of Śiva and five of Śakti, make 43 small triangles
Each partShows its set on the diagram, numbered in chanting order

ZAPPY: Why anticlockwise?

PROF. BOLT: That’s the common order in this worship. If your own teacher places them differently, follow your teacher.

The diagrams in this series are from Srini’s Khaḍgamālā booklet. Each one shows the Śrīcakra or the part of it for that set, with east at the top and the worshipper at the west, and numbers the devīs in the order they are chanted.

The Śrīcakra’s layers, from outside: the square with four gates, three circles, sixteen petals, eight petals, fourteen triangles, two rings of ten, eight triangles, the central triangle, and the bindu. Each Khaḍgamālā set belongs to one of them.

SET 1 · CALLING THE MOTHER

Śrī Devī Sambodhanam
WhoŚrī Lalitā Tripurasundarī
WhereThe whole Śrīcakra, which is her body
NamesOne salutation
The words we givenamas tripurasundarī: “salutation to Tripurasundarī”
Before itSeed-syllables, given by a teacher; not reproduced here
MeaningThe garland opens by calling the Mother directly, as one calls someone beloved

ZAPPY: Why start with just one name?

PROF. BOLT: Because she’s the whole thing. Every name after this is one of her own powers. The first call is to her, all of her.

THE OPENING LINE (SEED-SYLLABLES OMITTED)

… namas tripurasundarī

In printed editions the opening line begins with a row of seed-syllables before the words “namas tripurasundarī”. Those syllables belong to initiation, and in this series we do not reproduce them. What remains is a single salutation, and it is enough to show what the whole hymn is: one long bow.

Tripurasundarī means the beauty of the three cities, three worlds or three states: waking, dream and sleep. Every devī named after this is one of her powers, sitting somewhere in her city.

Nyāsāṅga devatāḥ
WhereAround the bindu, inside the central triangle
PlacesThe four corners, the centre, and the four sides for the weapon
Names6
hṛdayadevīGoddess of the heart · SE corner
śirodevīGoddess of the head · NE corner
śikhādevīGoddess of the crown-lock · SW corner
kavacadevīGoddess of the armour · NW corner
netradevīGoddess of the eyes · centre
astradevīGoddess of the weapon · the four sides

ZAPPY: What’s nyāsa?

PROF. BOLT: Placing. In worship these six are placed on your own body, so you become her temple. We name them; the placing is learned from a teacher.

Each limb devī has a place on the yantra as well as on the body: Hṛdayadevī in the south-east corner, Śirodevī in the north-east, Śikhādevī in the south-west, Kavacadevī in the north-west, Netradevī at the centre, and Astradevī on all four sides, as the booklet’s diagram shows.

The same six limbs appear in the worship of many deities, not only the Goddess. They are a standard Tantric preparation: the worshipper takes on, for the time of worship, the form of what is worshipped.

WHY SIX LIMBS?

Becoming her temple
HeartFeeling: where she is loved
HeadThought: where she is known
Crown-lockSpirit: the point that rises upward
ArmourProtection: the shoulders and the body held safe
EyesSight: seeing her everywhere
WeaponGuarding the space on every side
The ideaBefore approaching the Goddess, the worshipper is made fit to stand before her

ZAPPY: So the first six names are about getting ready?

PROF. BOLT: Yes. You can’t walk into a palace in muddy clothes. The six limbs prepare the worshipper.

CHANT THIS SET

hṛdayadevī, śirodevī, śikhādevī, kavacadevī, netradevī, astradevī

A practical note from the booklet: as each name is said, the worshipper may touch or feel each place, heart, forehead, crown, shoulders and eyes, and then circle the hands for the weapon. We mention it as the booklet does; the full nyāsa is learned in person.

The six also stand for the qualities of the Goddess: heart for all-knowing love, head for fullness, crown for freedom, armour for power, eyes for awareness, weapon for protection. Commentators give several such readings.

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: Names, devī positions and diagrams from Srini’s own Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā booklet. Readings of the enclosures in the body follow the Bhāvanopaniṣad as summarised in modern studies; the Lalitā Sahasranāma; the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Lalitopākhyāna (G. V. Tagare’s translation); Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar’s Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa kṛtis (described).

Sources differ / unverified: Editions of the Khaḍgamālā differ slightly in their names and their order; we follow the booklet. Lists of the siddhis usually have ten and leave out Garimā; this edition has eleven. Placement of the devīs within each enclosure follows the common anticlockwise order from the west, as the booklet says; where a teacher’s tradition differs, follow the teacher. The gems linked with each enclosure are the booklet’s scheme; other traditions give other correspondences. The booklet also gives seed-syllables and practice instructions for each set; we leave those out.

What we leave out: This series explains names, stories, philosophy, the Śrīcakra’s geometry, deities, temples and music. It does not reproduce, assemble or decode any initiatory mantra (Bālā, Pañcadaśī, Ṣoḍaśī or others), nor any nyāsa, dīkṣā or pūjā procedure: those are received from a teacher.

Meanings are our own plain-English renderings. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
