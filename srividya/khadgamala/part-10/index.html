ŚRĪVIDYĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE

ĀVARAṆA 7 · SARVAROGAHARA

Set 11 · the circuit that removes all disease

KHAḌGAMĀLĀ · 10 OF 13

FOREWORD

The seventh enclosure is the ring of eight triangles around the centre, Sarvarogahara, “the circuit that removes all disease”. Its devīs are the eight Vāgdevīs, goddesses of speech.

In the Lalitopākhyāna they composed the Lalitā Sahasranāma at the Goddess’s command. They rule cold and heat, pleasure and pain, desire and the three guṇas, the booklet says, and so remove the deepest “disease”: duality.

Śrī Lalitāyai namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Prof, the Sahasranāma’s authors are in the Śrīcakra?

PROF. BOLT: Right here, Zappy: eight goddesses of speech, one in each triangle around the centre.

ZAPPY: Disease of what?

PROF. BOLT: Of the body, and in the deeper reading, the ‘disease’ of seeing everything as two.

The seventh enclosure is the eight-cornered figure around the central triangle. Its presiding form of the Goddess is Tripurāsiddhā, and its yoginī is rahasya, secret. The walk is now just outside the innermost triangle.

Its eight devīs are among the best known in Śrīvidyā. Every time the Lalitā Sahasranāma is recited, their words are on the reciter’s lips.

ZAPPY: Only the central triangle is left after this?

PROF. BOLT: The central triangle and the bindu. The Vāgdevīs stand right around them.

On the booklet’s diagram the eight triangles are numbered from the west, anticlockwise, in a tight ring around the central triangle.

Their closeness to the centre fits their role: speech is the nearest thing to thought, and thought the nearest thing to awareness.

ZAPPY: Kāmeśvarī again?

PROF. BOLT: Same name as the first Nityā, different devī: here she’s a goddess of speech, speech as will.

The names describe powers of speech. Vaśinī controls; Kāmeśvarī is the lady of desire, speech as will; Modinī delights; Vimalā is stainless; Aruṇā is red, Lalitā’s colour; Jayinī is victorious; Sarveśvarī is sovereign of all; Kaulinī belongs to the kula, the Kaula way.

In the Lalitopākhyāna, when the war is over, Lalitā asks these eight to compose a hymn of her names. That hymn is the Lalitā Sahasranāma. The guide to Āvaraṇa 7 in the Śrīcakra series tells the story.

ZAPPY: So chanting the Sahasranāma links to this ring?

PROF. BOLT: Directly. These eight wrote it. To chant it is to borrow their words.

The Bhāvanopaniṣad reads the eight as cold and heat, pleasure and pain, desire, and the three guṇas: the pairs and qualities that keep the mind swinging. The “disease” this enclosure removes is that swinging.

Dīkṣitar’s song for this enclosure is Śrī Kamalāmbikāyām in rāga Śahānā. The Āvaraṇa 7 guide in the Śrīcakra series describes it.

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: Names, devī positions and diagrams from Srini’s own Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā booklet. Readings of the enclosures in the body follow the Bhāvanopaniṣad as summarised in modern studies; the Lalitā Sahasranāma; the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Lalitopākhyāna (G. V. Tagare’s translation); Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar’s Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa kṛtis (described). See also the guide to Āvaraṇa 7 in the Śrīcakra series.

Sources differ / unverified: Editions of the Khaḍgamālā differ slightly in their names and their order; we follow the booklet. Lists of the siddhis usually have ten and leave out Garimā; this edition has eleven. Placement of the devīs within each enclosure follows the common anticlockwise order from the west, as the booklet says; where a teacher’s tradition differs, follow the teacher. The gems linked with each enclosure are the booklet’s scheme; other traditions give other correspondences. The booklet also gives seed-syllables and practice instructions for each set; we leave those out.

What we leave out: This series explains names, stories, philosophy, the Śrīcakra’s geometry, deities, temples and music. It does not reproduce, assemble or decode any initiatory mantra (Bālā, Pañcadaśī, Ṣoḍaśī or others), nor any nyāsa, dīkṣā or pūjā procedure: those are received from a teacher.

Meanings are our own plain-English renderings. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
