ŚRĪVIDYĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE

ĀVARAṆA 8 · SARVASIDDHIPRADA

Set 12 · the circuit that grants every perfection

KHAḌGAMĀLĀ · 11 OF 13

FOREWORD

The eighth enclosure is the central triangle, Sarvasiddhiprada, “the circuit that grants every perfection”. Around it stand the Mother’s four weapons as devīs: the arrows, the bow, the noose and the goad. At its three corners sit Mahākāmeśvarī, Mahāvajreśvarī and Mahābhagamālinī.

The booklet reads the weapons as the five senses (arrows), the mind (bow), attachment (noose) and anger (goad), and the corners as the Śaktis of Rudra, Viṣṇu and Brahmā: will, action and knowledge.

Śrī Lalitāyai namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Every perfection, at last?

PROF. BOLT: This is the last ring before the bindu. What’s granted here is perfection itself.

The eighth enclosure is the downward-pointing triangle at the heart of the Śrīcakra. Its presiding form of the Goddess is Tripurāmbā, and its yoginī is atirahasya, most secret. The Nityās, from Part 2, also line its sides.

The four weapon devīs carry the four objects Lalitā holds in her hands. In the Lalitopākhyāna, these weapons are wedding gifts and the instruments of her war against Bhaṇḍa.

ZAPPY: Why are the weapons outside the triangle?

PROF. BOLT: They guard it. Inside is the centre; outside, her weapons keep watch.

On the booklet’s diagram the four weapon devīs are numbered outside the triangle, and the three great devīs at its corners, in the order the hymn calls them.

The three corners are also where the Nityā row turns: the first, sixth and second Nityās have their “great” forms here.

ZAPPY: Mahākāmeśvarī is the first Nityā’s ‘great’ form?

PROF. BOLT: Yes, and Mahāvajreśvarī the sixth’s, Mahābhagamālinī the second’s. The Nityās return at the corners.

The weapon devīs are named simply after what they hold: Bāṇinī, she of the arrows; Cāpinī, of the bow; Pāśinī, of the noose; Aṅkuśinī, of the goad. Then the three great corner devīs, whose names repeat the first, sixth and second Nityās with the prefix mahā-, great.

The booklet links the corners with the Śaktis of Rudra, Viṣṇu and Brahmā, and with will, action and knowledge, the three powers through which the Goddess creates.

The queen of this enclosure is the cakrasvāminī of Sarvasiddhiprada, and its yoginī is Atirahasya. The walk now stands at the edge of the bindu.

Weapons and corners

ZAPPY: So her weapons are my own mind and senses?

PROF. BOLT: In this reading, yes. In her hands they become instruments of grace.

The Bhāvanopaniṣad gives a similar reading of the weapons: the five subtle elements as arrows, the mind as the bow, attachment as the noose, aversion as the goad. In the Goddess’s hands, each binding force becomes a means of drawing the devotee to her.

Dīkṣitar’s song for this enclosure is Śrī Kamalāmbike in rāga Ghaṇṭā. The Āvaraṇa 8 guide in the Śrīcakra series describes it.

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: Names, devī positions and diagrams from Srini’s own Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā booklet. Readings of the enclosures in the body follow the Bhāvanopaniṣad as summarised in modern studies; the Lalitā Sahasranāma; the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Lalitopākhyāna (G. V. Tagare’s translation); Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar’s Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa kṛtis (described). See also the guide to Āvaraṇa 8 in the Śrīcakra series.

Sources differ / unverified: Editions of the Khaḍgamālā differ slightly in their names and their order; we follow the booklet. Lists of the siddhis usually have ten and leave out Garimā; this edition has eleven. Placement of the devīs within each enclosure follows the common anticlockwise order from the west, as the booklet says; where a teacher’s tradition differs, follow the teacher. The gems linked with each enclosure are the booklet’s scheme; other traditions give other correspondences. The booklet also gives seed-syllables and practice instructions for each set; we leave those out.

What we leave out: This series explains names, stories, philosophy, the Śrīcakra’s geometry, deities, temples and music. It does not reproduce, assemble or decode any initiatory mantra (Bālā, Pañcadaśī, Ṣoḍaśī or others), nor any nyāsa, dīkṣā or pūjā procedure: those are received from a teacher.

Meanings are our own plain-English renderings. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
