ĀVARAṆA 9 · SARVĀNANDAMAYA

Set 13 · the circuit made of pure bliss

KHAḌGAMĀLĀ · 12 OF 13

FOREWORD

The garland reaches the centre. The ninth enclosure is the bindu, the point at the heart of the Śrīcakra: Sarvānandamaya, “the circuit made of pure bliss”. Here sits Śrī Lalitā Mahābhaṭṭārikā, the great queen, in union with Kāmeśvara.

Only three names are said here: the great queen herself, the queen of this enclosure, and its yoginī, the supreme secret. There is no more outside and inside, the booklet says: every devī of every enclosure returns to her, and the seeker rests in bliss in everything.

Śrī Lalitāyai namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Prof, only three names at the centre?

PROF. BOLT: After a hundred and sixty steps, Zappy, the centre needs almost no words. Just her.

ZAPPY: Made of pure bliss?

PROF. BOLT: Sarvānanda: bliss in everything. That’s where the walk ends.

The ninth enclosure is the bindu, the point at the very centre of the Śrīcakra. Its presiding form is Mahātripurasundarī, and its yoginī parāpararahasya, the supreme secret. It is the home of the whole yantra.

In every guide in this series the walk has moved inward. Here it stops. The names are few because the centre is simple: the Goddess and her Lord, as one.

ZAPPY: Is the bindu really just a dot?

PROF. BOLT: On paper. In the tradition it holds everything: the whole Śrīcakra unfolds from it and returns to it.

The booklet’s diagram shows the full Śrīcakra with the bindu marked at its centre. All the lines, petals and triangles that earlier parts traced lead here.

The tradition says the Śrīcakra is drawn outward from the bindu and worshipped inward towards it: creation and return in one diagram.

THE DEVĪS

The great queen
śrī śrī mahābhaṭṭārikeO most revered great queen, twice auspicious · the bindu

ZAPPY: Why ‘śrī śrī’ twice?

PROF. BOLT: An honour: twice auspicious. And mahābhaṭṭārikā means the most revered great queen.

In the Lalitā Sahasranāma she is called Śrīmahārājñī, the great queen, and Śrīmatsiṃhāsaneśvarī, sovereign of the lion throne, at the very start of the hymn. The Khaḍgamālā’s single name at the centre gathers all of these titles into one.

The Lalitopākhyāna ends its war story with her wedding to Kāmeśvara and her enthronement in Śrīpura. The bindu is that throne, pictured as a point.

Śrī śrī mahābhaṭṭārike is the only devī-name of this enclosure. Bhaṭṭārikā is an old royal title, “most honoured lady”, and mahā makes it greater still. The double śrī is a mark of the highest respect.

She is Lalitā herself, the queen of the whole city, seated on the couch of Śiva in the palace of wish-gems, as the Saundaryalaharī and the Lalitopākhyāna both describe.

CHANT THIS SET

śrī śrī mahābhaṭṭārike, sarvānandamaya cakrasvāminī, parāpararahasyayoginī

The queen of this enclosure is the cakrasvāminī of Sarvānandamaya, and its yoginī is Parāpararahasya. With these three names the nine enclosures are complete.

ZAPPY: So the whole hymn is a road to this point?

PROF. BOLT: Yes. And the next part turns round and calls the nine queens, from the gate back to here.

The bindu is where the sixteenth Nityā, Mahānityā, sits, and where the guides of the Nityā series ended. It is also where the Śrīcakra series ended. All three series, the moon, the yantra and the hymn, meet at this point.

Dīkṣitar’s song for this enclosure is Śrī Kamalāmbā jayati in rāga Āhiri. The Āvaraṇa 9 guide in the Śrīcakra series describes it.

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: Names, devī positions and diagrams from Srini’s own Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā booklet. Readings of the enclosures in the body follow the Bhāvanopaniṣad as summarised in modern studies; the Lalitā Sahasranāma; the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Lalitopākhyāna (G. V. Tagare’s translation); Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar’s Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa kṛtis (described). See also the guide to Āvaraṇa 9 in the Śrīcakra series.

Sources differ / unverified: Editions of the Khaḍgamālā differ slightly in their names and their order; we follow the booklet. Lists of the siddhis usually have ten and leave out Garimā; this edition has eleven. Placement of the devīs within each enclosure follows the common anticlockwise order from the west, as the booklet says; where a teacher’s tradition differs, follow the teacher. The gems linked with each enclosure are the booklet’s scheme; other traditions give other correspondences. The booklet also gives seed-syllables and practice instructions for each set; we leave those out.

What we leave out: This series explains names, stories, philosophy, the Śrīcakra’s geometry, deities, temples and music. It does not reproduce, assemble or decode any initiatory mantra (Bālā, Pañcadaśī, Ṣoḍaśī or others), nor any nyāsa, dīkṣā or pūjā procedure: those are received from a teacher.

Meanings are our own plain-English renderings. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
