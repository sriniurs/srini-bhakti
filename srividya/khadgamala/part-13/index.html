THE NINE QUEENS AND THE SALUTATION

Sets 14–15 · Navacakreśvarīs and Namaskāra

KHAḌGAMĀLĀ · 13 OF 13

FOREWORD

The walk has reached the bindu. Now the garland turns and calls together the nine queens of the nine enclosures, each a form of Lalitā who rules one layer of her city: from Tripurā at the gates to Mahātripurasundarī at the centre.

Then it ends in pure praise. Nine times the Mother is called mahā-mahā, great beyond great, and the last words are a triple bow: namaste namaste namaste namaḥ.

Śrī Lalitāyai namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Prof, how does the garland end?

PROF. BOLT: With nothing but praise, Zappy. Nine ‘greats’, and three bows.

SET 14 ON THE ŚRĪCAKRA

The nine queens
WhereOne in each enclosure, numbered from outside in
Names9
WhoEach queen is a form of Lalitā who rules that enclosure

ZAPPY: Haven’t we met these queens already?

PROF. BOLT: Each enclosure named its queen by the enclosure’s title. Here they’re called by their own names, all together.

On the booklet’s diagram the nine queens are numbered from the outer square, where Tripurā rules, to the bindu, where Mahātripurasundarī sits. Calling them in order is a quick second walk through the whole Śrīcakra.

These queens are the same as the presiding Tripurās given in each guide of the Śrīcakra series. There they appear one per guide; here they come together as a single line of nine names.

ZAPPY: All named Tripurā something?

PROF. BOLT: Tripurā, Tripureśī, Tripurasundarī and on: nine faces of the Lady of the three cities, ending in the great one.

The names grow from Tripurā, the plain name, through Tripureśī (the lady), Tripurasundarī (the beauty), Tripuravāsinī (the indweller), Tripurāśrī (the splendour), Tripuramālinī (the garlanded), Tripurāsiddhā (the perfected) and Tripurāmbā (the mother), to Mahātripurasundarī, the great beauty, at the centre.

Read as a sequence, they describe the Goddess revealing herself more fully at each step inward: first named, then sovereign, beautiful, dwelling within, radiant, adorned, perfected, mothering, and finally whole.

ZAPPY: Why say ‘great’ twice each time?

PROF. BOLT: Because once isn’t enough. Mahā-mahā: great beyond great.

THE CLOSING LINE

mahāmaheśvarī, mahāmahārājñī, mahāmahāśakte, mahāmahāgupte, mahāmahājñapte, mahāmahānande, mahāmahāskandhe, mahāmahāśaye, mahāmahāśrīcakranagarasāmrājñī, namaste namaste namaste namaḥ

The nine salutations are called the namaskāra navākṣarī. Each doubles mahā, great: great-great Goddess, Queen, Power, Hidden one, Knowing one, Bliss, Support, Abode, and last, great-great Empress of the city of the Śrīcakra. The city the whole garland has walked through is named at the end.

Then come the closing words: namaste namaste namaste namaḥ. Three salutations and a bow. After 182 names, the garland ends with the simplest act of worship there is.

ZAPPY: And after the last ‘namaḥ’?

PROF. BOLT: Sit quietly, the booklet says. Feel the whole Śrīcakra, every devī in her place, alive inside you, with Lalitā at the bindu of your heart.

This completes the Khaḍgamālā in thirteen parts. Readers can go back to any part from the series index, and to the guides of the Śrīcakra and the Tithi Nityās for more on each enclosure and each moon day.

The booklet’s closing advice is worth repeating: after the last namaḥ, sit quietly. The point of naming every devī in her place is to feel the whole Śrīcakra alive inside, with Lalitā at its centre.

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: Names, devī positions and diagrams from Srini’s own Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā booklet. Readings of the enclosures in the body follow the Bhāvanopaniṣad as summarised in modern studies; the Lalitā Sahasranāma; the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Lalitopākhyāna (G. V. Tagare’s translation); Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar’s Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa kṛtis (described).

Sources differ / unverified: Editions of the Khaḍgamālā differ slightly in their names and their order; we follow the booklet. Lists of the siddhis usually have ten and leave out Garimā; this edition has eleven. Placement of the devīs within each enclosure follows the common anticlockwise order from the west, as the booklet says; where a teacher’s tradition differs, follow the teacher. The gems linked with each enclosure are the booklet’s scheme; other traditions give other correspondences. The booklet also gives seed-syllables and practice instructions for each set; we leave those out.

What we leave out: This series explains names, stories, philosophy, the Śrīcakra’s geometry, deities, temples and music. It does not reproduce, assemble or decode any initiatory mantra (Bālā, Pañcadaśī, Ṣoḍaśī or others), nor any nyāsa, dīkṣā or pūjā procedure: those are received from a teacher.

Meanings are our own plain-English renderings. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
