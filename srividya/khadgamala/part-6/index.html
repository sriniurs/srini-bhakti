ŚRĪVIDYĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE

ĀVARAṆA 3 · SARVASAṄKṢOBHAṆA

Set 7 · the circuit that stirs everything

KHAḌGAMĀLĀ · 6 OF 13

FOREWORD

The third enclosure is the eight-petalled lotus, Sarvasaṅkṣobhaṇa, “the circuit that stirs everything”. Its eight devīs all bear the name Anaṅga, “the bodiless one”: Kāma, the god of love, after Śiva burned his body and Lalitā restored him.

They are his powers, now in the Goddess’s service. In the booklet’s words, they awaken and purify the powers of action, and the heart begins to be stirred towards the Mother.

Śrī Lalitāyai namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Prof, Kāma is in the Śrīcakra?

PROF. BOLT: His powers are, Zappy, all eight of them, serving her. Desire isn’t thrown out; it’s turned towards her.

ZAPPY: Why ‘stirs everything’?

PROF. BOLT: Because desire stirs. Here it’s stirred towards the Goddess instead of away.

This enclosure is the second lotus ring, inside the sixteen petals. Its presiding form of the Goddess is Tripurasundarī, the name the whole Śrīcakra bears, and its yoginī is guptatara, more hidden than the last.

Its theme is the Lalitopākhyāna’s great story: Kāma burned to ash by Śiva’s third eye, the demon Bhaṇḍa born from that ash, and Kāma revived by Lalitā’s glance. Here his powers have found their place, around her.

ZAPPY: Inner lotus now?

PROF. BOLT: Yes: inside the sixteen petals is a ring of eight. One devī to a petal.

The booklet’s diagram numbers the eight petals from the west, anticlockwise. Each Anaṅga devī glows in her petal.

The eight petals face the eight directions, so the eight devīs also stand for the eight quarters of space.

ZAPPY: All named ‘Anaṅga’?

PROF. BOLT: Flower, girdle, intoxication, longing, line, speed, goad, garland: eight faces of love, all bodiless.

Each name pairs Anaṅga with a quality of love: kusuma, the flower; mekhalā, the girdle, the embrace; madanā, intoxication; madanāturā, the one made love-sick; rekhā, the line; veginī, the swift; aṅkuśā, the goad; mālinī, the garlanded. Together they are the whole course of love.

In the hymn they are called in the vocative: O flower of the bodiless one! The meanings above follow the words; the deep dive gives the reading.

The queen of this enclosure is the cakrasvāminī of Sarvasaṅkṣobhaṇa, and its yoginī is Guptatara. Three enclosures in, the walk has passed the powers, the Mothers, the attractors and the bodiless ones.

ZAPPY: So desire becomes devotion here?

PROF. BOLT: That’s the reading. The same force, aimed at her.

The Bhāvanopaniṣad reads the eight as speech, grasping, walking, excretion and pleasure, the five organs of action, together with three attitudes of mind: rejecting, accepting and remaining indifferent. To worship them is to see every act as hers.

Dīkṣitar’s song for this enclosure is Śrī Kamalāmbikayā in rāga Śaṅkarābharaṇam. The Āvaraṇa 3 guide in the Śrīcakra series says more.

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: Names, devī positions and diagrams from Srini’s own Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā booklet. Readings of the enclosures in the body follow the Bhāvanopaniṣad as summarised in modern studies; the Lalitā Sahasranāma; the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Lalitopākhyāna (G. V. Tagare’s translation); Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar’s Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa kṛtis (described). See also the guide to Āvaraṇa 3 in the Śrīcakra series.

Sources differ / unverified: Editions of the Khaḍgamālā differ slightly in their names and their order; we follow the booklet. Lists of the siddhis usually have ten and leave out Garimā; this edition has eleven. Placement of the devīs within each enclosure follows the common anticlockwise order from the west, as the booklet says; where a teacher’s tradition differs, follow the teacher. The gems linked with each enclosure are the booklet’s scheme; other traditions give other correspondences. The booklet also gives seed-syllables and practice instructions for each set; we leave those out.

What we leave out: This series explains names, stories, philosophy, the Śrīcakra’s geometry, deities, temples and music. It does not reproduce, assemble or decode any initiatory mantra (Bālā, Pañcadaśī, Ṣoḍaśī or others), nor any nyāsa, dīkṣā or pūjā procedure: those are received from a teacher.

Meanings are our own plain-English renderings. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
