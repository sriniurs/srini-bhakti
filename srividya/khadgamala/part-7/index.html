ŚRĪVIDYĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE

ĀVARAṆA 4 · SARVASAUBHĀGYADĀYAKA

Set 8 · the circuit that grants all good fortune

KHAḌGAMĀLĀ · 7 OF 13

FOREWORD

The fourth enclosure is the ring of fourteen triangles, Sarvasaubhāgyadāyaka, “the circuit that grants all good fortune”. Here the walk leaves the lotuses and enters the triangles of the Śrīcakra’s heart.

The fourteen are read as the fourteen worlds and as the fourteen main channels (nāḍīs) of the body. Their devīs carry noose, goad, mirror and a cup of nectar, the booklet says, and give every kind of good fortune: health, beauty, love, prosperity and grace.

Śrī Lalitāyai namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Prof, are these the same names as the mudrā devīs?

PROF. BOLT: Several are, Zappy. The same powers come back at every level, deeper each time.

ZAPPY: Good fortune of what kind?

PROF. BOLT: Health, beauty, love, prosperity, grace, and at the end, freedom from the pull of opposites.

The fourth enclosure is the first ring of triangles, formed where the nine interlocking triangles of the Śrīcakra meet. Its presiding form of the Goddess is Tripuravāsinī, and its yoginī is called sampradāya, of the tradition.

The names here are a reprise. Sarvasaṅkṣobhiṇī, Sarvavidrāviṇī, Sarvākarṣiṇī and others were the mudrā devīs of the outer square. Commentators read the repetition as the same graces working at a deeper level.

Where the devīs of Āvaraṇa 4 sit
WhereThe fourteen outer triangles (caturdaśāra)
OrderTriangle 1 at the west, then anticlockwise
Names14 + queen and yoginī
Read asThe fourteen worlds; the fourteen main nāḍīs

ZAPPY: Fourteen triangles round the edge?

PROF. BOLT: The outer ring of the triangle pattern, made by the nine interlocking triangles.

On the booklet’s diagram the fourteen triangles are numbered from the west, anticlockwise, round the outer edge of the triangle pattern. Each devī sits in her own point.

The fourteen points touch the circle that holds the triangles, so this ring is the boundary between the lotuses outside and the triangles within.

ZAPPY: Stirring, melting, attracting again?

PROF. BOLT: The first ones repeat the mudrā devīs of the gate. Now they act deeper, in the channels of the body.

The first seven describe how grace takes hold: it stirs, melts, attracts, delights, enchants, stills and opens. Sarvastambhinī, “she who makes all still”, and Sarvajṛmbhiṇī, “she who makes all open out”, are a pair: stillness and expansion.

ZAPPY: The last one ends opposites?

PROF. BOLT: Heat and cold, praise and blame, pleasure and pain. Good fortune at its deepest is peace.

The second seven describe what grace gives: control, delight, ecstasy, every aim, every wealth, every mantra, and finally the end of all opposites. That last name is the deepest gift of the enclosure.

Sarvamantramayī, “she who is all mantras”, is worth noticing: in Śrīvidyā the Goddess is the sacred sound itself, not only its object.

The queen of this enclosure is the cakrasvāminī of Sarvasaubhāgyadāyaka, and its yoginī is Sampradāya. The yoginīs now take names of tradition and secrecy rather than of visibility.

READING THE FOURTEEN

Worlds and channels
Fourteen worldsThe seven above and seven below of Purāṇic cosmology
Fourteen nāḍīsThe Bhāvanopaniṣad reads them as the main channels of the subtle body
Their giftsHealth, beauty, love, prosperity, grace
The last nameSarvadvandvakṣayaṅkarī: she who ends all pairs of opposites
MoreThe Āvaraṇa 4 guide gives Tripuravāsinī and Dīkṣitar’s Kamalāmbikāyai

ZAPPY: So the whole universe is in this ring?

PROF. BOLT: Its fourteen worlds, and the fourteen channels of your own body. Outer and inner, same pattern.

The Bhāvanopaniṣad names the fourteen channels of the subtle body, among them Iḍā, Piṅgalā and Suṣumnā, and assigns them to these fourteen triangles. The worship of this enclosure is the purification of those channels, in its reading.

Dīkṣitar’s song for this enclosure is Kamalāmbikāyai in rāga Kāmbhoji. The Āvaraṇa 4 guide in the Śrīcakra series describes it.

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: Names, devī positions and diagrams from Srini’s own Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā booklet. Readings of the enclosures in the body follow the Bhāvanopaniṣad as summarised in modern studies; the Lalitā Sahasranāma; the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Lalitopākhyāna (G. V. Tagare’s translation); Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar’s Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa kṛtis (described). See also the guide to Āvaraṇa 4 in the Śrīcakra series.

Sources differ / unverified: Editions of the Khaḍgamālā differ slightly in their names and their order; we follow the booklet. Lists of the siddhis usually have ten and leave out Garimā; this edition has eleven. Placement of the devīs within each enclosure follows the common anticlockwise order from the west, as the booklet says; where a teacher’s tradition differs, follow the teacher. The gems linked with each enclosure are the booklet’s scheme; other traditions give other correspondences. The booklet also gives seed-syllables and practice instructions for each set; we leave those out.

What we leave out: This series explains names, stories, philosophy, the Śrīcakra’s geometry, deities, temples and music. It does not reproduce, assemble or decode any initiatory mantra (Bālā, Pañcadaśī, Ṣoḍaśī or others), nor any nyāsa, dīkṣā or pūjā procedure: those are received from a teacher.

Meanings are our own plain-English renderings. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
