ŚRĪVIDYĀ · A REFERENCE GUIDE

ĀVARAṆA 5 · SARVĀRTHASĀDHAKA

Set 9 · the circuit that accomplishes every aim

KHAḌGAMĀLĀ · 8 OF 13

FOREWORD

The fifth enclosure is the outer ring of ten triangles, Sarvārthasādhaka, “the circuit that accomplishes every aim”. Its ten devīs shine like red hibiscus, the booklet says, and grant success in every undertaking.

They are read as the ten prāṇas, the vital airs that move the body. Their names are a list of gifts: attainment, wealth, love, auspiciousness, desire fulfilled, freedom from sorrow, death and obstacles, beauty and good fortune.

Śrī Lalitāyai namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Prof, this one sounds like a list of blessings!

PROF. BOLT: It is, Zappy. Ten blessings, one in each triangle.

ZAPPY: Every aim means what?

PROF. BOLT: The four aims of life: dharma, wealth, pleasure and freedom. This ring accomplishes them all.

The fifth enclosure is the outer of two rings of ten triangles. Its presiding form of the Goddess is Tripurāśrī, and its yoginī is kulottīrṇa, “beyond the clan”: the walk is now past every family boundary.

This enclosure is where the hymn turns most practical. Its names ask for what everyone asks for: success, wealth, love, safety, health and beauty. The tradition does not look down on such prayers.

ZAPPY: Another ring of triangles?

PROF. BOLT: The next one in. Fourteen, then ten, then ten again, then eight: the rings get smaller towards the centre.

On the booklet’s diagram the ten triangles are numbered from the west, anticlockwise. They sit just inside the ring of fourteen.

There are two rings of ten, outer and inner. The inner ring is the next enclosure, and the two are often described as a pair.

ZAPPY: ‘Who calms every death’?

PROF. BOLT: Sarvamṛtyupraśamanī: she who pacifies death. Life’s breath is guarded here.

Read the names as a single prayer: give every attainment and every wealth; make all things dear; do every auspicious thing; give every desire; free from every sorrow; calm every death; ward off every obstacle; be beautiful in every limb; give every good fortune.

Sarvamṛtyupraśamanī, “she who calms every death”, may refer to untimely death, as many commentators take it. Sarvavighnanivāriṇī, “she who wards off every obstacle”, echoes Gaṇeśa’s role; Śrīvidyā always begins with him.

ZAPPY: So my breathing is in the Śrīcakra?

PROF. BOLT: In this reading, yes: the ten airs that keep you alive are ten devīs in her city.

The Bhāvanopaniṣad reads the ten triangles as the ten vital airs: the five main ones, prāṇa, apāna, vyāna, udāna and samāna, and the five lesser ones. Their worship is a recognition that breath itself belongs to the Goddess.

Dīkṣitar’s song for this enclosure is Śrī Kamalāmbikāyāḥ param in rāga Bhairavī. The Āvaraṇa 5 guide in the Śrīcakra series describes it.

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: Names, devī positions and diagrams from Srini’s own Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā booklet. Readings of the enclosures in the body follow the Bhāvanopaniṣad as summarised in modern studies; the Lalitā Sahasranāma; the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Lalitopākhyāna (G. V. Tagare’s translation); Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar’s Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa kṛtis (described). See also the guide to Āvaraṇa 5 in the Śrīcakra series.

Sources differ / unverified: Editions of the Khaḍgamālā differ slightly in their names and their order; we follow the booklet. Lists of the siddhis usually have ten and leave out Garimā; this edition has eleven. Placement of the devīs within each enclosure follows the common anticlockwise order from the west, as the booklet says; where a teacher’s tradition differs, follow the teacher. The gems linked with each enclosure are the booklet’s scheme; other traditions give other correspondences. The booklet also gives seed-syllables and practice instructions for each set; we leave those out.

What we leave out: This series explains names, stories, philosophy, the Śrīcakra’s geometry, deities, temples and music. It does not reproduce, assemble or decode any initiatory mantra (Bālā, Pañcadaśī, Ṣoḍaśī or others), nor any nyāsa, dīkṣā or pūjā procedure: those are received from a teacher.

Meanings are our own plain-English renderings. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
