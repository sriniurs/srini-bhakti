ĀVARAṆA 6 · SARVARAKṢĀKARA

Set 10 · the circuit that protects completely

KHAḌGAMĀLĀ · 9 OF 13

FOREWORD

The sixth enclosure is the inner ring of ten triangles, Sarvarakṣākara, “the circuit that protects completely”. Its devīs are bright as a thousand rising suns, the booklet says; they are all-knowing and all-powerful, destroy disease and sin, and become perfect protection.

They are read as the ten fires of the body that digest, transform and nourish. Their names describe the Goddess’s own nature more than her gifts: all-knowing, all-powerful, the support of all, made of bliss.

Śrī Lalitāyai namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Prof, protection from what?

PROF. BOLT: From disease, from sin, from fear, Zappy. And the names say how: she knows all and can do all.

ZAPPY: Why is protection so near the centre?

PROF. BOLT: Because the closer you come to her, the more completely you’re held.

The sixth enclosure is the inner ring of ten triangles. Its presiding form of the Goddess is Tripuramālinī, and its yoginī is nigarbha, the inmost. From the outside, the walk has passed six of nine layers.

The tone of the names changes here. Earlier rings asked for gifts; this one names the Goddess’s own qualities. Protection, the enclosure’s theme, comes from who she is.

ZAPPY: Smaller than the last ring?

PROF. BOLT: Yes, the inner ten. After this, only the eight triangles and the centre remain.

On the booklet’s diagram the inner ten triangles are numbered from the west, anticlockwise, inside the outer ten. They are smaller and brighter, closer to the centre.

The two rings of ten together hold twenty devīs: ten of accomplishment outside, ten of protection inside.

ZAPPY: All-knowing, all-powerful… are these her own names?

PROF. BOLT: Yes. Here the devīs describe the Goddess herself: her knowledge, power, support and bliss.

The ten names make a short creed: you are all-knowing and all-powerful; you give every sovereignty; you are made of knowledge; you destroy disease; you are the support of all; you remove sin; you are made of bliss; your form is protection; you give every desired fruit.

Sarvavyādhivināśinī, “destroyer of every disease”, has made this enclosure especially dear to those praying for health. The next enclosure, Sarvarogahara, “remover of all illness”, carries the same theme further.

PROF. BOLT: Nigarbha: the one in the very womb, the inmost. We’re close to the centre now.

The queen of this enclosure is the cakrasvāminī of Sarvarakṣākara, and its yoginī is Nigarbha. Only three enclosures remain: the eight triangles, the central triangle and the bindu.

ZAPPY: Fires that protect?

PROF. BOLT: The fire that digests food keeps you alive. Transformation is protection.

The Bhāvanopaniṣad reads the ten triangles as ten fires in the body: those that cause elimination, digestion, drying, burning, flooding and so on. Fire transforms; transformation sustains life; and so the fires are, in this reading, the Goddess’s protection.

Dīkṣitar’s song for this enclosure is Kamalāmbikāyās tava in rāga Punnāgavarāḷi. The Āvaraṇa 6 guide in the Śrīcakra series describes it.

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: Names, devī positions and diagrams from Srini’s own Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā booklet. Readings of the enclosures in the body follow the Bhāvanopaniṣad as summarised in modern studies; the Lalitā Sahasranāma; the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa, Lalitopākhyāna (G. V. Tagare’s translation); Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar’s Kamalāmbā Navāvaraṇa kṛtis (described). See also the guide to Āvaraṇa 6 in the Śrīcakra series.

Sources differ / unverified: Editions of the Khaḍgamālā differ slightly in their names and their order; we follow the booklet. Lists of the siddhis usually have ten and leave out Garimā; this edition has eleven. Placement of the devīs within each enclosure follows the common anticlockwise order from the west, as the booklet says; where a teacher’s tradition differs, follow the teacher. The gems linked with each enclosure are the booklet’s scheme; other traditions give other correspondences. The booklet also gives seed-syllables and practice instructions for each set; we leave those out.

What we leave out: This series explains names, stories, philosophy, the Śrīcakra’s geometry, deities, temples and music. It does not reproduce, assemble or decode any initiatory mantra (Bālā, Pañcadaśī, Ṣoḍaśī or others), nor any nyāsa, dīkṣā or pūjā procedure: those are received from a teacher.

Meanings are our own plain-English renderings. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
