The thousand names · to Lalitāmbikā

HYMNS OF THE GODDESS

HOW THIS WORKS: All the ślokas in one go for chanting, then every śloka on two pages: the śloka, a picture, its meaning and every name in the word table; then a deep dive.

WHERE WE ARE

Part 10 completes the hymn: ślokas 168–183, names 906–1000. It returns to “That, you”, names her the remover of every danger, worshipped with awareness as water and flowers, and the Goddess for the learned and the simple alike.

Then Viśālākṣī, the couch of the five gods, the queen of the elements, Bālā the girl and the married woman, the ten mudrā goddesses, and name 992, the most loved of all: compassion without any reason.

It ends where the tradition lives: known even to children and cowherds, dwelling in the Śrīcakra, Śrīmat Tripurasundarī, the oneness of Śiva and Śakti, and name 1000, Lalitāmbikā.

With the blessings of the Lord,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: A thousand names, and the last one is…?

PROF. BOLT: Lalitāmbikā. Mother Lalitā. After all of it, simply Mother.

ZAPPY: What counts as a tattva?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya says only the thirty-six principles that last until the dissolution. Pots and bodies, which perish sooner, do not count.

ŚLOKA 168 · DEEP DIVE

THE MEANING OF “THAT”

🔎 In context

She is beyond the tattvas, the principles of reality, and made of them. Name 908 returns to tat tvam asi, “That you are”, from Part 5: she is the meaning of both “That” (Brahman) and “you” (the self), and so of their oneness.

The thirty-six tattvas of Śaiva thought run from earth up to Śiva. She is the ladder and above it.

📖 What the commentary adds

Tattvamayī: following the Jñānārṇava, she is both kinds of samādhi, with and without support. He describes their marks: in one, laughter, tears, hair standing on end, trembling; in the other, fixed eyes and a still body. Both are reached by meditating on a light, which must be learned from a teacher.

Tattvamarthasvarūpiṇī: tat is Śiva, tvam the souls, as in the great sayings; she is both. Saumyā: fit for the soma sacrifice; or sa-umā; or pleasing like the moon or camphor. Sadāśivakuṭumbinī: may refer to Śyāmalā, Śuddhavidyā, Aśvārūḍhā and the rest of her household.

📜 Looking deeper

The Sāmaveda is the sung Veda, the root of Indian music; she loves it. Then the hymn comes home: she is Sadāśiva’s kuṭumbinī, the lady of his household.

Name 908 reads tat and tvam together as one meaning: the goal of the Upaniṣadic teaching that the self and the absolute are one.

🌱 For today

The highest truth also keeps a home.

ZAPPY: Right path and left path?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya gives several readings: three paths of the sun across the sky; the gods’ path of light and the ancestors’ path of smoke; right- and left-hand worship; and the iḍā, piṅgalā and suṣumnā channels.

🔗 ALSO SEE: Svabhāvamadhurā, “sweet by nature”, he also reads as Mīnākṣī of Madurai, the Hālāsya kṣetra.
She wards off every danger.

ŚLOKA 169 · DEEP DIVE

REMOVER OF EVERY DANGER

🔎 In context

Savya and apasavya, right and left, are read as the two great ways of worship, Vaidika and Tāntrika, or right-hand and left-hand practice. She is present on both. The hymn takes no side; neither do we.

📖 What the commentary adds

On name 912 he cites the Vāyu and Viṣṇu Purāṇas on the sun’s paths and the pole star, and the Kālikā Purāṇa on right- and left-hand worship: the right follows the five sacrifices, and Mahāmāyā, Śāradā and Pārvatī are to be worshipped by the right path alone. Finally Śiva Sūtra 3.45: the three channels of breath.

Sarvāpadvinivāriṇī: he cites the Chāndogya, the Harivaṃśa and the Varāha Purāṇa on refuge in her. Svasthā: abiding in oneself. Dhīrā: dhī, knowledge, plus ra, giving; he cites the Avadhūta Gītā on non-duality.

📜 Looking deeper

Sarvāpadvinivāriṇī, remover of every danger, is one of the most used names in prayer. Svasthā means both self-abiding and healthy: in Āyurveda svastha is the word for health. Svabhāvamadhurā: sweet by her very nature.

Svastha, “established in oneself”, is Āyurveda’s word for health: the Suśruta Saṃhitā defines the healthy person as one whose body, senses and mind are in balance.

🌱 For today

Sweetness that is natural cannot be faked.

ŚLOKA 170 · DEEP DIVE

WORSHIPPED WITH CONSCIOUSNESS

🔎 In context

In outer worship one offers water (arghya) and flowers. Names 918–919 say the true offering is consciousness itself: awareness offered as water, awareness offered as a flower.

📖 What the commentary adds

Caitanyārghyasamārādhyā: consciousness as the Self (Śiva Sūtra), offered as water. Caitanyakusumapriyā: consciousness as a flower, with eight inner flowers listed.

Sadoditā: sat as the good: she always appears among good people. Sadātuṣṭā: ever content with them. Taruṇādityapāṭalā: her colour varies with the form meditated on: white, red, saffron, black, tawny, rosy.

📜 Looking deeper

Sadoditā, ever-risen: a sun that never sets. Taruṇādityapāṭalā, rosy as the rising sun, returns to the dawn-red colour of the first dhyāna verse in Part 1.

The Bhāvanopaniṣad lists inner equivalents for every outer offering. Names 918–919 are the hymn’s own version: awareness is the water and the flower.

🌱 For today

Attention is the finest offering.

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya says the dakṣiṇa are the wise, or ritualists, and the adakṣiṇa the simple, or the knowers of Brahman. He cites Gītā 7.16 on the four kinds of devotees.

🔗 ALSO SEE: Kaivalya as a fifth liberation beyond the usual four: name 625.
For the learned and the simple.

ŚLOKA 171 · DEEP DIVE

GIVER OF THE PRICELESS

🔎 In context

Our text prints “dakṣiṇā, dakṣiṇārādhyā”; the standard count reads one name, dakṣiṇādakṣiṇārādhyā: worshipped by the skilled and the unskilled, the learned and the simple. Another reading: by both right-hand and left-hand ways. Either way, nobody is shut out.

📖 What the commentary adds

Darasmeramukhāmbujā: dara as conch: her face shines white and stays smiling even at the dissolution. Kaulinī kevalā: kevala as Īśvara-knowledge in Jaina usage, or free of attributes (Śiva Sūtra); or read kālinī kevalā, pure time.

Anarghyakaivalyapadadāyinī: kaivalya is the fifth state of liberation; some read anarghya as “everywhere”.

📜 Looking deeper

Kaulinī kevalā is counted as one name: she is of the kula and yet absolute. Kaivalya, liberation as aloneness, is priceless, anarghya: it cannot be bought.

Name 923 is one of the hymn’s most generous: worship done well and worship done clumsily both reach her.

🌱 For today

Nobody is too simple to worship.

ŚLOKA 172 · DEEP DIVE

LOVER OF HYMNS

🔎 In context

Stotrapriyā: she loves hymns, like this one, like the Saundaryalaharī and the Khaḍgamālā in our series. Stutimatī: she is also the praise, so the hymn praises her with herself.

📖 What the commentary adds

Stutimatī: praising her brings knowledge and sovereignty. Śrutisaṃstutavaibhavā: her glory in four forms (body, metres, scriptures, the Bahvṛca); the Kūrma Purāṇa’s four śaktis. Manasvinī: the mind depends on her.

Mānavatī: māna as elevated mind, regard, forgiveness, proof or measure. Maheśī: born from Mahādeva (Devī Purāṇa).

📜 Looking deeper

Mānavatī has a lovely double sense: full of dignity, and also, in poetry, a woman who sulks with her beloved. The commentators enjoy both. Maṅgalākṛti: her very form is auspicious.

Stotras are a major literature in Sanskrit: hymns of praise like the Saundaryalaharī, the Khaḍgamālā and this Sahasranāma. Name 927 says she loves them all.

🌱 For today

A hymn sung with love is never wasted.

ŚLOKA 173 · DEEP DIVE

VIŚĀLĀKṢĪ

🔎 In context

Viśālākṣī, wide-eyed, is the Goddess of Kāśī, worshipped beside Viśvanātha, and a Śakti Pīṭha. Her eyes are wide to see every devotee.

📖 What the commentary adds

Viśvamātā: Viśva read as Viṣṇu; a śruti calls her Viṣṇu’s mother. Jagaddhātrī: nurse or protector (Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Devī Purāṇa). Pragalbhā: bold in creating.

Paramodārā and parāmodā: great in space and time; or āmoda as fame or fragrance. Manomayī: Brahman is fixed in the pure mind; the Vāmana Purāṇa and Bṛhadāraṇyaka on mind as the instrument of self-knowledge.

📜 Looking deeper

Virāgiṇī, dispassionate, sits next to parāmodā, supreme joy: she is detached and full of joy together, as a free person is. Manomayī, made of mind, is the third of the five sheaths, from Part 5.

Viśālākṣī’s shrine in Vārāṇasī is counted among the Śakti Pīṭhas. Jagaddhātrī pūjā in Bengal, especially at Chandannagar, is a major autumn festival.

🌱 For today

Freedom from craving is not the end of joy.

ŚLOKA 174 · DEEP DIVE

THE COUCH OF FIVE

🔎 In context

Name 947 completes name 58 of Part 2: her couch is made of the five gods, Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra, Īśvara and Sadāśiva, who without her Śakti are lifeless (preta). Four are its legs and Sadāśiva its seat. The image says: without her, even the gods cannot act.

The five daily sacrifices (pañca-mahāyajña) are to gods, sages, ancestors, all beings, and guests: every household’s duty in the dharma texts.

📖 What the commentary adds

Vyomakeśī: space as her hair in the cosmic form; or wife of Vyomakeśa, Śiva. Vimānasthā: vimāna as chariot, scripture, or the fourteen branches of learning.

Vāmakeśvarī: name of a Tantra. Pañcayajñapriyā: five sacrifices by śruti, smṛti, Pāñcarātra, Kaula and other lists. Pañcapretamañcādhiśāyinī: Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra and Īśvara as legs, Sadāśiva as mattress (Bhairavayāmala; Saundaryalaharī).

📜 Looking deeper

The five daily sacrifices are to the gods, the sages, the ancestors, all beings and guests. Vāmakeśvarī links her to the Vāmakeśvara Tantra, an early text of Śrīvidyā. We name it; we do not teach from it.

Name 947 completes the image of name 58 and name 249: the five gods, inert without her, become her couch.

🌱 For today

Even the greatest powers need a living spark.

ŚLOKA 175 · DEEP DIVE

QUEEN OF THE ELEMENTS

🔎 In context

The fives continue: she is the fifth, the queen of the five elements, and worshipped with the fivefold offering: sandal, flower, incense, lamp and food, the simplest pūjā any home can do.

📖 What the commentary adds

Pañcamī: fifth in Brahmā’s order, Sadāśiva’s wife; or the fifth oblation, or the fifth state of liberation. Pañcasaṅkhyopacāriṇī: scent, flowers, incense, lamp and food.

Śāśvatī: worshipped continually (Kaṭha on eternal attainment). Śāśvataiśvaryā: read with the five-preta seat. Names 953–954 he leaves without comment.

📜 Looking deeper

Śambhumohinī, who enchants Śiva: the ascetic god is captivated by her. The Saundaryalaharī tells this again and again. She is eternal, and so is her sovereignty.

The five offerings, sandal, flower, incense, lamp and food, are the simplest complete worship. Anyone can offer them; the hymn says she is worshipped this way.

🌱 For today

Five simple things, offered daily, are enough.

ŚLOKA 176 · DEEP DIVE

BEYOND EVERYTHING

🔎 In context

First the Earth, firm and nourishing, and Pārvatī, daughter of the mountain that bears the earth. Then three steps beyond: beyond the worlds, beyond the guṇas, beyond everything.

📖 What the commentary adds

Dharā: the earth, or support of all; the letter la is her form (Jñānārṇava). Dhanyā: also a yoginī in astrology, and one of four final meditations. Dharmiṇī: possessor of bliss, eternity and the rest. Dharmavardhinī: the Vāmana Purāṇa on self-control and purity.

Sarvātītā: sarva as all words; the Jñānārṇava on Brahman beyond speech. Śamātmikā: she calms worldly activity.

📜 Looking deeper

The last name of the śloka is śamātmikā, whose nature is peace. After everything, peace. Dharmavardhinī, who increases dharma, is close to Dharmasaṃvardhinī, the name of the Goddess at Tiruvaiyāru.

Guṇātīta is the Gītā’s word (14.25) for one who has gone beyond the three guṇas. The hymn gives the ideal its source in her.

Beyond everything there is peace.

BĀLĀ, THE GIRL

🔎 In context

Bālā is the Goddess as a girl of nine, worshipped in her own right in Śrīvidyā as Bālā Tripurasundarī. We name her; her mantra is for initiates. Here she is simply the playful girl, delighting in play.

📖 What the commentary adds

Bālā: the girl; he cites the Śvetāśvatara and the Tripurāsiddhānta. Līlāvinodinī: the play of the universe, or Queen Līlā of the Yogavāsiṣṭha. Sumaṅgalī: su-maṅgala, Brahman (Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Atri Smṛti).

Suvāsinī: a married woman whose husband lives; the Goddess is not separate from such women.

📜 Looking deeper

Then the same Goddess as a married woman, sumaṅgalī and suvāsinī, richly dressed. Girl and bride: the hymn sees her at every stage of a woman’s life.

Bālā Tripurasundarī is often the first form of the Goddess a Śrīvidyā student is introduced to. Her mantra is given only by a teacher; we name her and no more.

TRIPURĀMBIKĀ

🔎 In context

Suvāsinī pūjā, the worship of married women as forms of the Goddess, is a common household rite in South India, especially on Navarātri and Fridays. Name 971 says she is pleased by it.

📖 What the commentary adds

Name 971: pleased by the worship of married women. Bindutarpaṇasantuṣṭā: the bindu is the Sarvānandamaya cakra; he notes offerings that differ by community, and also reads bindu as the wise.

Tripurāmbikā: deity of the eighth cakra (Yoginīhṛdaya); tri as the three states (Kauṣītaki).

📜 Looking deeper

Bindutarpaṇa, offering to the bindu, belongs to the inner ritual; we name it only. Tripurāmbikā is the presiding goddess of the eighth enclosure in our Khaḍgamālā and Āvaraṇa guides.

Suvāsinī pūjā honours women as living forms of the Goddess; Kanyā pūjā on Navarātri does the same for young girls.

Honouring the women of the house is worship.

THE TEN MUDRĀS

🔎 In context

The ten mudrās are also the ten mudrā goddesses of the Śrīcakra, Sarvasaṅkṣobhiṇī to Trikhaṇḍā, whom our Khaḍgamālā guides name. Their hand-gestures are learned from a teacher; we do not describe them.

Daśamudrāsamārādhyā: worshipped with the ten mudrās from Saṅkṣobhiṇī to Trikhaṇḍā (Nityāhṛdaya); the gestures belong to practice under a teacher. Tripurāśrīvaśaṅkarī: Tripurāśrī presides over the fifth enclosure.

Tripurāśrī presides over the fifth enclosure. Jñānamudrā, the knowledge-gesture of thumb and forefinger joined, is the one mudrā everyone knows: Dakṣiṇāmūrti shows it. Name 981 unites the knower’s knowledge and what is known.

THE WONDERFUL STORY

🔎 In context

Yonimudrā and Trikhaṇḍā are the ninth and tenth mudrā goddesses, named in our Khaḍgamālā guides. Our text prints triguṇāmbā and anaghādbhutacāritrā; the standard count splits each into two names.

📖 What the commentary adds

Yonimudrā and Trikhaṇḍeśī: the ninth and tenth mudrā goddesses; trikhaṇḍā is also the three sections of the mantra. Triguṇā: primal nature of the Sāṅkhyas (Vāyu, Viṣṇu, Devī Purāṇas).

Ambā: the Tantrarāja explains perfect contemplation of “That”. Trikoṇagā: the triangle is the yoni-cakra. Anaghā: agha is sin, pain, misery.

📜 Looking deeper

Ambā, Mother, appears simply, as Mātā did in Part 5. Adbhutacāritrā: her story, the Lalitopākhyāna, is wonderful. Vāñchitārthapradāyinī: she grants every wish, a name devotees end prayers with.

Our text prints triguṇāmbā and anaghādbhutacāritrā; the standard count splits each into two. That is how the hymn reaches exactly a thousand.

🌱 For today

Simple names, like “Mother”, carry the most.

ŚLOKA 181 · DEEP DIVE

COMPASSION WITHOUT REASON

🔎 In context

Abhyāsa, steady practice, is the Gītā’s and the Yoga Sūtra’s word: she is known through it. The six paths (adhvans) are the Śaiva map of reality, from letters to tattvas; she is beyond all six.

The six adhvans are the Śaiva map of reality: letters, words, mantras, tattvas, worlds and kalās. She is beyond all six.

📖 What the commentary adds

Abhyāsātiśayajñātā: known by constant meditation on the oneness of Brahman and Self; Brahma Sūtra 4.1.1 on repetition. Ṣaḍadhvātītarūpiṇī: beyond the six adhvans; or six kinds of devotion (Kulārṇava).

Avyājakaruṇāmūrti: he gives it simple praise, no proof-text: it needs none. Ajñānadhvāntadīpikā: Gītā 10.11, “I destroy the darkness born of ignorance with the shining lamp of knowledge”.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 992 is perhaps the most loved in the whole hymn: avyājakaruṇāmūrti, compassion without pretext. She does not need a reason to be kind. Name 993: the lamp in the darkness of not-knowing.

Avyāja means “without pretext”. Her compassion is not a reward for merit; it is her nature. Many devotees count this the most precious name of all.

ŚLOKA 182 · DEEP DIVE

ŚRĪ TRIPURASUNDARĪ

🔎 In context

Ābālagopaviditā: known to everyone, from children to cowherds. After all the philosophy and ritual, the hymn says plainly that she is not only for experts. A child who says “Amma” knows her.

📖 What the commentary adds

Ābālagopaviditā: he cites a Saṃhitā verse that every being knows the “I”, and that “I” is her. Sarvānullaṅghyaśāsanā: familiarity does not lessen her command; Saundaryalaharī 24.

Śrīcakrarājanilayā: the Śrīcakra is the body of Śiva and Devī, as the soul lives in the body. Śrīmattripurasundarī: wife of Tripurasundara, whose three bodies are Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra (Kālikā Purāṇa).

📜 Looking deeper

Then her home and her name: she dwells in the Śrīcakra, the king of cakras, which our Āvaraṇa and Khaḍgamālā guides walk through, and she is Śrīmat Tripurasundarī, the beautiful one of the three worlds.

After all the philosophy, the hymn insists she is known to the simplest. The Saundaryalaharī ends the same way: the poet offers back her own words like water from the Ganges to the Ganges.

Even a child knows her.

ŚLOKA 183 · DEEP DIVE

LALITĀMBIKĀ

🔎 In context

The last three names gather everything. She is Śivā, Śiva’s own; she is the oneness of Śiva and Śakti, the theme from the very first verse of the Saundaryalaharī; and she is Lalitāmbikā, the Mother who plays.

The Saundaryalaharī opens with Śiva and Śakti’s oneness; the Sahasranāma closes on it. The two great hymns of Śrīvidyā meet here.

📖 What the commentary adds

Śivaśaktyaikyarūpiṇī: absolute equality and non-difference (Vāyavīya and Saura Saṃhitās, Yogavāsiṣṭha); or the Śiva and Śakti triangles of the Śrīcakra; or the five śaktis of the five elements, citing the Virūpākṣapañcāśikā.

Lalitāmbikā: the five cosmic acts are hers, and Lalitā is her own name. He ends by explaining the Oṃ said at the start and close of the Sahasranāma.

📜 Looking deeper

The last line of the śloka is not a name: it says the goddesses of speech, Vaśinī and the others, sang these thousand names. The chapter closes as the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa’s Hayagrīva–Agastya dialogue. A thousand names, and the last one is simply “Mother”.

Lalitā means “she who plays”. The last name joins play and motherhood: the one who makes the worlds in play is the Mother who cares for each being in them.

🌱 For today

After a thousand names, the last is “Mother”.
