LALITĀ SAHASRANĀMA

The thousand names · the states of the self and the five acts

HYMNS OF THE GODDESS

HOW THIS WORKS: All the ślokas in one go for chanting, then every śloka on two pages: the śloka, a picture, its meaning and every name in the word table; then a deep dive.

WHERE WE ARE

Part 4 covers ślokas 61–80, names 249–365. It opens with her throne of the five Brahmās and her nature as consciousness and bliss, then walks through the four states of the self from the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad: waking, dream, deep sleep and the Fourth.

Next come the five acts, creation, protection, destruction, concealment and grace, each given to her; the thousand-headed cosmic form of the Puruṣa Sūkta; the Goddess as sound and beyond name and form; as the field and its knower of the Gītā; and finally as pure consciousness, the “That” of the Upaniṣads.

Two names (301, 322) refer to a seed-syllable and to kāmakalā. We give their meaning only. Names 330 and 333 speak of intoxicating drinks; we give the outer and inner readings the commentators give.

With the blessings of the Lord,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: This part sounds like the Upaniṣads!

PROF. BOLT: It is. The Vāgdevīs put the whole of Vedānta into her names: waking, dream, sleep, the Fourth, ‘That you are’.

ZAPPY: Why call the five gods “corpses”?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya quotes the Jñānārṇava: all activity belongs to Śakti. Without her the five gods cannot act, so they are pretas, lifeless, and become her seat.

ŚLOKA 61 · DEEP DIVE

ON THE FIVE THRONES

🔎 In context

Her throne again: four gods as its legs and Sadāśiva as its seat. Here they are called pretas, “corpses”, because without her power, Śakti, even the greatest gods are inert: the old play on śiva and śava from the Saundaryalaharī’s first verse.

Śiva without the “i” (Śakti) is śava, a corpse: the famous wordplay of Saundaryalaharī 1. The five-preta seat makes the same point with all five gods.

📖 What the commentary adds

The five Brahmas are Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra, Īśvara and Sadāśiva; or, in another list, Śiva’s five faces Īśāna, Tatpuruṣa, Aghora, Vāmadeva and Sadyojāta. He cites the Garuḍa Purāṇa and others for Śiva and Viṣṇu each becoming five through their śaktis.

Paramānandā: Chāndogya 7.23, “the infinite is happiness”. Vijñānaghana is “a single mass of consciousness” (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.4.12), or, in a second reading, the sum of all individual souls, Hiraṇyagarbha.

📜 Looking deeper

The names then go to the heart of the matter. She is cinmayī, made of consciousness; paramānandā, supreme bliss; vijñānaghana, a dense mass of knowing. Consciousness and bliss, cit and ānanda, are the classic marks of Brahman in Vedānta.

Name 253 echoes the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad’s vijñānaghana: consciousness so solid it has no gaps, like a lump of salt that is salt throughout.

🌱 For today

Power is what makes greatness move.

WAKING AND DREAM

ZAPPY: She is meditation, meditator and meditated?

PROF. BOLT: Yes. Bhāskararāya cites Yoga Sūtra 3.2: in deep contemplation the three merge, and that merged state is her.

🔗 ALSO SEE: The four states of waking, dream, sleep and the Fourth are the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad’s teaching.

ŚLOKA 62 · DEEP DIVE

WAKING AND DREAM

🔎 In context

Here begins a passage straight out of the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, which describes four states of the self: waking (Viśva), dream (Taijasa), deep sleep (Prājña), and the fourth, Turīya. The Sahasranāma names her in each.

Names 256–263 follow the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad’s four states of the self: waking (Viśva), dream (Taijasa), deep sleep (Prājña) and the Fourth (Turīya).

📖 What the commentary adds

Dharmādharmavivarjitā: he gathers definitions of dharma from the Matsya Purāṇa, Yājñavalkya and the Mīmāṃsā Sūtra (1.1.2), and says she is free of both merit and demerit because scriptural rules address only the ignorant.

With Viśvarūpā begins a run of nineteen names distinguishing the soul from Īśvara. Waking is outward action through the senses (Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya); the waking soul is Viśva in the gross body. Dream is mind-made vision from latent impressions; the dreamer is Taijasa in the subtle body.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 254 says she is meditation, the one who meditates, and the object meditated on, all three. In ordinary experience these are separate; in her they are one. It is the same teaching as the first dhyāna verse: “I meditate on her as myself”.

The meditation triad of 254 is the classic picture of samādhi: when knower, knowing and known merge, only she remains.

🌱 For today

The one who seeks and the one sought may be closer than you think.

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya says yes: a fifth, “beyond all states”, which comes from being firmly settled in the Fourth.

ŚLOKA 63 · DEEP DIVE

SLEEP AND THE FOURTH; THE THREE GODS

🔎 In context

Deep sleep, then the Fourth, Turīya, which underlies all three states, and then beyond all states. The Māṇḍūkya’s ladder is complete. Then the hymn turns to the five acts of the Goddess, beginning with creation and protection.

📖 What the commentary adds

Deep sleep is incapacity to discriminate (Śiva Sūtra 1.10); “I slept happily” shows ignorance, ego and happiness still present; the sleeper is Prājña. Turyā is where pure knowledge is tasted; Śiva Sūtra 1.7 says real enjoyment is only in the Fourth.

Name 263, beyond all states, he describes with Śiva Sūtras 3.27–29: for the liberated, physical life is worship, speech is japa, and giving is knowledge of the Self. Creation, preservation (264–266) are Īśvara’s acts under rajas and sattva.

📜 Looking deeper

Turīya, “the fourth”, is also how the Saundaryalaharī (verse 97) names her: beyond Sarasvatī, Lakṣmī and Pārvatī, she is “some fourth one”. Here she is the fourth state beyond waking, dream and sleep. Both say the same thing in different ways.

The Fourth is not a fourth state alongside the others; it is the awareness in which waking, dream and sleep appear. Name 263 adds: she is beyond even the idea of states.

🌱 For today

Under every state of mind something stays the same.

PROF. BOLT: One of the five. Bhāskararāya says tirodhāna and anugraha, concealment and grace, are bondage and release. She hides herself, then reveals herself.

ŚLOKA 64 · DEEP DIVE

THE FIVE ACTS

🔎 In context

Creation, protection, destruction, concealment and grace: the five acts of the divine in Śaiva and Śākta thought. The hymn gives each to the Goddess, in the forms of Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra, Īśvara and Sadāśiva, and then sums up: she is devoted to the five acts.

The five acts, creation, preservation, destruction, concealment and grace, are central to Śaiva and Śākta theology. The five gods each perform one, under her.

📖 What the commentary adds

Saṃhāriṇī: dissolution reduces the world to atoms, Īśvara’s act under tamas. Rudra: “who drives away sorrow”, or “who weeps”, since the rain at dissolution comes from his solar eye. Tirodhāna: total absorption into prakṛti, like a light going out; or the goddess Tiraskariṇī (Tripurāsiddhānta).

Īśvara he explains with the Virūpākṣapañcāśikā: lordship, activity, independence and consciousness. Anugraha is the re-forming of the world after dissolution. For the five acts he cites the Mṛgendra Saṃhitā, Saundaryalaharī 24 and the Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya.

📜 Looking deeper

The Saundaryalaharī (verse 24) says all five gods act at a flicker of her brow. The Sahasranāma goes further: she is each of them. The two hymns place the five acts in her hands in slightly different ways.

Concealment is listed as a divine act, not a failure: she hides herself so that the play of the world can happen, and grace is when she reveals herself again.

🌱 For today

Even concealment can be a kind of mercy, until the time for grace.

ŚLOKA 65 · DEEP DIVE

IN THE SUN’S DISC; VIṢṆU’S SISTER

🔎 In context

She is worshipped in the sun’s disc, as in the Gāyatrī tradition. Bhagamālinī is the second Tithi Nityā; our guide to her explains the double meaning of bhaga. And she is Viṣṇu’s sister.

In South Indian tradition Pārvatī is Viṣṇu’s sister: at Madurai, Viṣṇu gives Mīnākṣī in marriage to Sundareśvara. Name 280 records this.

📖 What the commentary adds

Bhānumaṇḍalamadhyasthā: meditate on her in the sun at sandhyā; he cites Brahma Sūtra 1.1.20 on the golden person in the sun. Or the sun’s disc is the heart lotus. Bhairavī: wife of Bhairava, Paramaśiva; or, he says, bhairava means all women.

Bhagamālinī: possessor of the six excellences, or the second Nityā. Padmāsanā: Brahmā’s consort; or the lotus of the tattvas; or slayer of a demon Padma. Bhagavatī: the six bhagas are sovereignty, dharma, fame, prosperity, wisdom and dispassion.

📜 Looking deeper

Padmanābhasahodarī: in South Indian tradition the Goddess Mīnākṣī of Madurai is Viṣṇu’s sister, given by him in marriage to Śiva as Sundareśvara. The idea that the Goddess and Viṣṇu are siblings runs through Śākta and temple traditions alike.

Meditating on the Goddess in the sun’s disc is linked with the Gāyatrī: the light worshipped at dawn is her light.

🌱 For today

Family ties can join traditions as well as people.

ŚLOKA 66 · DEEP DIVE

THE THOUSAND-HEADED

🔎 In context

Worlds rise when she opens her eyes and vanish when she closes them. The Saundaryalaharī (verse 55) uses the same image and adds that she never blinks, out of care for the world.

Names 282–284 are taken from the Puruṣa Sūkta (Ṛg Veda 10.90): sahasraśīrṣā puruṣaḥ sahasrākṣaḥ sahasrapāt. The Viṣṇu Sahasranāma uses the same words for Viṣṇu.

📖 What the commentary adds

On name 281 he cites the Ājñāvatāra, Kālidāsa and the Mahārthamañjarī of Gorakṣanātha (Maheśvarānanda): creation and dissolution are the opening and shutting of her eyes.

Sahasraśīrṣavadanā: “thousand” means unlimited, as in the Puruṣa Sūkta and Gītā 13.13. The thousand eyes and feet he cites from the Devī Bhāgavata (Book 3).

📜 Looking deeper

The thousand heads, eyes and feet are from the Puruṣa Sūkta of the Ṛgveda, the hymn of the cosmic Person: sahasraśīrṣā puruṣaḥ. The Sahasranāma gives the Vedic cosmic form to the Goddess.

Saundaryalaharī 55 says the same: the world dissolves when she shuts her eyes, so she never blinks. A blink of hers is the life of a universe.

🌱 For today

Everything that exists exists within one vast attention.

ŚLOKA 67 · DEEP DIVE

MOTHER OF ALL, GIVER OF THE VEDAS

🔎 In context

She is mother of all beings, from the creator to an insect. The Vedas are her own commands, and she gives the fruit of every action, good or bad.

📖 What the commentary adds

Ābrahmakīṭajananī: Brahmā here is Hiraṇyagarbha; kīṭa the smallest insect. Varṇāśramavidhāyinī: the Vedas are her commands; he cites the Kūrma Purāṇa on karma, devotion and wisdom.

Nijājñārūpanigamā: he cites the Mīmāṃsā Sūtra (1.1.22) and Kūrma Purāṇa, and notes that the 28 Śaiva Āgamas from the Kāmika onward follow the Veda, unlike Kāpālika texts. Puṇyāpuṇyaphalapradā: Kauṣītaki Upaniṣad 3.8 and the Brahma Sūtra on whether God is partial.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 286 refers to the traditional social and life order of the Dharmaśāstras. We give its meaning as the hymn states it. Many modern teachers read it as her establishing order and duty in the world, rather than as a fixed social hierarchy; readers will judge for themselves.

Name 286 reflects the social ideas of the text’s time. Readers today hold differing views on varṇa; we note the name as the hymn gives it.

🌱 For today

A mother cares for the smallest child as much as the greatest.

ŚLOKA 78 · DEEP DIVE

THE WISH-CREEPER

🔎 In context

The kalpalatā, a wish-fulfilling creeper, grants whatever is asked. For devotees, she is that creeper. Name 354 uses Śaiva terms: paśu, the bound soul; pāśa, its bonds. She frees the bound.

📖 What the commentary adds

Bhaktimatkalpalatikā: kalpa can mean imperfect, so she perfects the imperfect devotee; latā can also mean musk or jasmine (Rabhasa’s lexicon). Paśupāśavimocinī: paśus lack the sense of non-separation (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.10); he lists 52 bonds (Liṅga Purāṇa) and the three impurities, āṇava, māyīya, kārma.

Saṃhṛtāśeṣapāṣaṇḍā: heretics keep vows outside Veda and smṛti (Liṅga Purāṇa); he gives the fourteen seats of dharma (Brahmavaivarta). Sadācārapravartikā: the Kūrma Purāṇa has the Goddess say Vyāsa made the eighteen Purāṇas to establish dharma.

📜 Looking deeper

Pāṣaṇḍa means false doctrine or hypocrisy. Commentators read name 355 as her ending of false and harmful paths, and name 356 as her establishing good conduct in their place.

Paśu, pāśa and pati, soul, bond and Lord, are the three key terms of Śaiva Siddhānta. Name 354 places her as the one who cuts the bond.

🌱 For today

Freedom from bonds is the best wish granted.

ŚLOKA 79 · DEEP DIVE

MOONLIGHT FOR THE BURNING

🔎 In context

The three afflictions (tāpatraya) are suffering from one’s own body and mind, from other beings, and from nature and fate. To those scorched by them, she is cool moonlight.

The three sufferings are from oneself (ādhyātmika), from other beings (ādhibhautika) and from fate or nature (ādhidaivika). Peace prayers say “śāntiḥ” three times for them.

📖 What the commentary adds

The three miseries are of the body, of beings and of the gods (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.4.25). Tāpasārādhyā he splits as tāpa-sāra-dhyā: in a world of suffering, she is the essential object of meditation.

Tanumadhyā is a metre in Piṅgala’s Chandas Sūtra. Tamopahā: Īśa Upaniṣad 9 warns against worshipping ignorance; she removes it.

📜 Looking deeper

The image recalls the Saundaryalaharī’s moonstone image (verse 20): the Goddess cool and healing. She is the fiery red one of the dhyāna and the cool moonlight here; the hymn holds both.

Name 357 matches name 744 later: sunlight to old age. Moon for heat, sun for darkness: she is whichever cure is needed.

🌱 For today

Be moonlight to someone who is burning.

ŚLOKA 80 · DEEP DIVE

CONSCIOUSNESS, “THAT”

🔎 In context

Citi is pure consciousness. Name 363 refers to the great Upaniṣadic saying tat tvam asi, “That you are”: she is what “That” points to. Śrīvidyā here speaks the language of Advaita Vedānta.

The Taittirīya Upaniṣad measures happiness in rising steps, from a human’s to Brahmā’s, and says Brahman’s bliss is beyond all. Name 365 puts the Goddess at the top.

📖 What the commentary adds

Cit: wisdom opposed to avidyā; the Śakti Sūtra calls cit independent and the cause of the world. Tatpadalakṣyārthā: “that” directly means conditioned Brahman and indirectly the unconditioned, the two being one.

Cidekarasarūpiṇī: Padmapāda’s Pañcapādikā says bliss and the other attributes are one with consciousness though they seem distinct. Name 365: lava means both particle and play (Viśva lexicon).

📜 Looking deeper

Name 365 is breathtaking: all the bliss of Brahmā and every god and being is a tiny drop of her own bliss. That ends Part 4. Part 5 begins with the four levels of speech, and her names as Parā and Paśyantī.

Name 365 ends Part 4: every joy anyone has ever had, even a god’s, is a fragment of hers. To be happy at all is to taste a drop of the Goddess.

🌱 For today

Every joy you have known is a drop from the same ocean.
