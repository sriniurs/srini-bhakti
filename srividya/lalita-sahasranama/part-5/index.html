LALITĀ SAHASRANĀMA

The thousand names · speech, the Nityās, the Gāyatrī

HYMNS OF THE GODDESS

HOW THIS WORKS: All the ślokas in one go for chanting, then every śloka on two pages: the śloka, a picture, its meaning and every name in the word table; then a deep dive.

WHERE WE ARE

Part 5 covers ślokas 81–97, names 366–474. It opens with the four levels of speech, parā to vaikharī, and moves through Kāmeśvara’s life-breath, the sacred seats, the bindu, her name as the sixteen Nityās, the root of nature, the Gāyatrī, and the Upaniṣadic “That, you”.

It ends with names of the Mother: mother of Skanda and Gaṇeśa, contentment, nourishment, peace, delight; and mother of the perfected ones. Part 6 then begins the passage on the seven yoginīs of the body’s centres, names 475–534.

Two counting conventions matter here. Our text prints “tattvamayī” as one word; the standard count reads it as three names, tat, tvam, ayī (425–427). It prints “nityā, ṣoḍaśikārūpā” as two; the standard count takes it as one (391). We follow the standard numbering.

PROF. BOLT: That’s how the commentators reach exactly a thousand. And it gives a beautiful echo: ‘That, you… O Mother’.

ZAPPY: Where in the body do the four levels of speech sit?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya traces them rising on the breath: parā motionless at the root, paśyantī at the navel with the mind, madhyamā at the heart with the intellect, vaikharī at the mouth as sound.

ŚLOKA 81 · DEEP DIVE

THE FOUR LEVELS OF SPEECH

🔎 In context

Indian thought on language describes four levels of speech: parā, the silent source; paśyantī, the “seeing” stage where meaning is whole; madhyamā, speech formed in the mind; and vaikharī, the words we speak. The Goddess is all four.

Tantric maps place them in the body: parā at the base, paśyantī at the navel, madhyamā at the heart, vaikharī at the throat.

📖 What the commentary adds

Why these names at all? Bhāskararāya answers that spoken words express only Brahman’s gross form, Virāṭ; so the hymn names the higher levels of speech to point beyond it. He cites the Ṛg Veda (1.164.45), the Nitya Tantra and Pāṇini (1.4.14) on what a “word” is.

Paśyantī is also called uttīrṇā, “risen above”: the Saubhāgyasudhodaya says she sees everything within herself. Vaikharī is vi-khara, “very hard”, speech in physical form, or vai-kha, “in the ear-cavity”. Bhaktamānasahaṃsikā: as swans live on Lake Mānasa, she lives in the minds (manana) of devotees.

📜 Looking deeper

This fits the Sahasranāma’s own origin: composed by the goddesses of speech, it praises the Goddess as speech itself, from silence to sound. The last name is tender: she swims like a swan in the Mānasa lake of her devotees’ minds; the Saundaryalaharī (verse 38) has a pair of swans there.

🌱 For today

Before every word there is a silence it comes from.

ZAPPY: Why is she Kāmeśvara’s life-breath?

ŚLOKA 82 · DEEP DIVE

KĀMEŚVARA’S LIFE-BREATH

🔎 In context

She is Kāmeśvara’s prāṇanāḍī, his life-channel: without her he does not live. The Saundaryalaharī (verse 28) gives the same idea a story: her earrings keep Śiva alive. Kāma, the god of love, worships her; in Śrīvidyā he is the first worshipper of her vidyā.

📖 What the commentary adds

Kṛtajñā: kṛta is every action, good and bad, which nine witnesses observe; she rewards actions with knowledge. He also cites the dice-lore of Chāndogya 4 (kṛta is the winning throw). Kāmapūjitā: the Aruṇa Upaniṣad (Taittirīya Āraṇyaka 1.11) speaks of a bodiless son of Lakṣmī, Kāma, who received her mantra.

Śṛṅgārarasasampūrṇā: he reads it through the twelve-petalled heart lotus and the pīṭhas Kāmarūpa and Pūrṇagiri. Jayā: the Padma Purāṇa’s deity of Mount Varāha. Jālandharasthitā: the Padma Purāṇa names her Viṣṇumukhī at Jālandhara.

📜 Looking deeper

Jālandhara is one of the four great pīṭhas, sacred seats, of the tradition. The next śloka names a second, Oḍyāṇa. Our Śakti Pīṭha guides on this site describe the sacred seats of the Goddess across India.

The love between Kāmeśvara and Kāmeśvarī is the hymn’s picture of the relation between consciousness and its power. Neither exists without the other; their union is the world.

🌱 For today

Gratitude is a quality even of the highest.

↑ ŚLOKA 82 · Kāmeśvara’s life-breath.

OḌYĀṆA AND THE BINDU

Who dwells in the seat of Oḍyāṇa; who dwells in the circle of the bindu; worshipped by the order of the secret sacrifice; pleased by secret offerings.
WordMeans
(379) oḍyāṇa-pīṭha-nilayādwelling in the seat of Oḍyāṇa
(380) bindu-maṇḍala-vāsinīdwelling in the circle of the bindu
(381) raho-yāga-krama-ārādhyāworshipped by the order of the secret (inner) sacrifice
(382) rahas-tarpaṇa-tarpitāpleased by secret (inner) offerings

ZAPPY: What is the “secret worship” here?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya reads it through Saundaryalaharī 9: the secret union in the crown centre. It is worship inside, not outside.

🔗 ALSO SEE: Āpastamba’s Dharmasūtra, which he cites, praises a solitary, inward life as a path to freedom.
In the bindu, worshipped within.

ŚLOKA 83 · DEEP DIVE

OḌYĀṆA AND THE BINDU

🔎 In context

Oḍyāṇa is another of the great pīṭhas. Then she is named as the one in the bindu, the central point of the Śrīcakra, where our Āvaraṇa 9 and Khaḍgamālā guides end.

The four great pīṭhas, Kāmarūpa, Pūrṇagiri, Jālandhara and Oḍyāṇa, sit at the four corners of the central triangle of the Śrīcakra in some ritual maps. The hymn names two here and others elsewhere.

📖 What the commentary adds

Bindumaṇḍalavāsinī: the bindu circle is the Sarvānandamaya cakra, the centre of the Śrīcakra; or bindu as “white”, the brahmarandhra at the crown.

Rahoyāgakramārādhyā: he cites Saundaryalaharī 9 on the secret play in the sahasrāra, and Āpastamba on liberation through solitude. Rahastarpaṇatarpitā: the Aruṇa Upaniṣad’s solitary communion; the inner offering is the subject. We name it and do not teach it.

📜 Looking deeper

Names 381–382 speak of secret worship, raho-yāga. Commentators read “secret” as inner: worship performed within, in the heart, with no outer ritual. We describe the idea and do not teach the worship.

The movement of the śloka is inward: from outer sacred places, to the centre point of the yantra, to worship that happens only within. Each name takes one step further in.

🌱 For today

Some of the deepest worship is invisible.

ZAPPY: What are the six excellences?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya gives two lists. The Purāṇas: prosperity, dharma, fame, wealth, wisdom, dispassion. And, playfully, the Nītisāra’s six policies of a king: peace, war, march, halt, dividing forces, alliance.

🔗 ALSO SEE: The six limb-deities are the aṅga-devatās of our Khaḍgamālā Part 1.

ŚLOKA 84 · DEEP DIVE

WITNESS OF ALL

🔎 In context

Sadyaḥprasādinī: she is quick to grace, not slow to be pleased. She witnesses everything, and nothing witnesses her: consciousness sees but is not seen.

Witnessing is one of the oldest Upaniṣadic descriptions of the Self: the Śvetāśvatara calls it the sākṣī, the onlooker, alone and free of qualities. Here that witness is the Goddess.

📖 What the commentary adds

Sadyaḥprasādinī: she is pleased at once by the inner worship of 381–382. Sākṣivarjitā: as witness of all, she has no witness.

Ṣaḍaṅgadevatāyuktā: the six limbs are heart, head, tuft, eyes, armour and weapon (Jñānārṇava); or Śiva presiding over six qualities (Devī Bhāgavata); or the six Vedāṅgas. Ṣāḍguṇya gets both the Purāṇic six and the Nītisāra’s six royal policies.

📜 Looking deeper

The six limb-deities are the aṅga-devatās of our Khaḍgamālā Part 1: heart, head, crown, armour, eyes and weapon. The six excellences are the bhagas of our Bhagamālinī guide: sovereignty, valour, fame, prosperity, knowledge and detachment.

Name 384 and 385 together solve an old puzzle: if consciousness sees everything, what sees consciousness? Nothing does. It is self-evident, known by being it.

🌱 For today

Grace that comes at once is the best kind.

ŚLOKA 85 · DEEP DIVE

THE SIXTEEN NITYĀS

🔎 In context

Nityaklinnā is the third Tithi Nityā, and name 391 says she is all sixteen Nityās. Our Tithi Nityā series is built on exactly this name: the fifteen days of the moon and the sixteenth that never wanes.

The Nityās are also worshipped in the central triangle of the Śrīcakra, five on each side, with Lalitā at the centre. The Tantrarāja Tantra gives each a full chapter.

📖 What the commentary adds

Nityaklinnā is the deity of the third lunar day, described in the Garuḍa Purāṇa. Nirupamā: Śvetāśvatara, none is his equal.

Nityāṣoḍaśikārūpā: the Tantrarāja calls the fifteen Nityās the limbs of Lalitā, who is the sixteenth. Śrīkaṇṭhārdhaśarīriṇī: Śrīkaṇṭha is Śiva with the poisoned throat; he cites the Vāyu Purāṇa on the white and blue halves of the joined body.

📜 Looking deeper

Our text prints name 391 as two words, “nityā, ṣoḍaśikārūpā”; the standard count treats it as one name. Name 392 is Ardhanārīśvara: she is the half of Śiva’s body. The Saundaryalaharī (verse 23) jokes that she took the other half too.

Why moon-days? The moon waxes and wanes, but the sixteenth digit, the amṛtakalā, never fades. The Nityās show time itself as the Goddess: every day holy, and something in us that does not wane.

🌱 For today

Every day of the month belongs to her.

Manifest and unmanifest.

ŚLOKA 86 · DEEP DIVE

THE ROOT OF NATURE

🔎 In context

Mūlaprakṛti, the root of nature, and avyakta, the unmanifest, are terms from Sāṅkhya philosophy, the source from which the whole world unfolds. The Goddess is that source, and also the world unfolded from it.

📖 What the commentary adds

Prabhāvatī: surrounded by Aṇimā and the other radiant siddhis. Prabhārūpā shows that a quality and its possessor are one; Chāndogya, “mind itself, brightness itself”. Prasiddhā: known to all as the “I”.

Mūlaprakṛti: he discusses Sāṅkhya’s primal matter, the Mṛgendra Saṃhitā’s kuṇḍalinī, the Pāñcarātra, and the Viṣṇu Purāṇa’s six stages of creation. Avyaktā: Sāṅkhya Saptati and Pañcaśikha; the Brahma Sūtras and Liṅga Purāṇa identify the unmanifest with Brahman. On 399 he links the self-born, bāṇa and stone liṅgas to the manifest, unmanifest and both.

📜 Looking deeper

Prabhāvatī is also the name of a goddess among the attendants in some Śrīvidyā lists. Here it simply means the radiant one. The light of name 394 and the root of name 397 together say: she is both what shines and what everything grows from.

Sāṅkhya keeps nature and spirit separate. Śākta thought refuses that split: nature is the Goddess, and so is the spirit that knows it. This śloka quietly makes that move.

🌱 For today

The seen and the unseen come from one source.

ŚLOKA 87

KNOWLEDGE AND IGNORANCE

All-pervading; of many forms; whose form is both knowledge and ignorance; the moonlight that delights the night-lilies of the eyes of the great Kāmeśa.
WordMeans
(400) vyāpinīall-pervading
(401) vividha-ākārāof many forms
(402) vidyā-avidyā-svarūpiṇīwhose form is both knowledge and ignorance
(403) mahā-kāmeśa-nayana-kumuda-āhlāda-kaumudīthe moonlight delighting the night-lilies, the eyes of Kāmeśa

ZAPPY: What does kaumudī mean?

PROF. BOLT: Moonlight, and also the full moon of Kārttika, says Bhāskararāya. Kāmeśvara’s eyes are night-lilies that open in it.

🔗 ALSO SEE: Īśa Upaniṣad 9–11 on knowledge and ignorance, cited for name 402.
Knowledge and ignorance, both hers.

ŚLOKA 87 · DEEP DIVE

KNOWLEDGE AND IGNORANCE

🔎 In context

Name 402 is bold: she is both knowledge and ignorance. Ignorance, too, is her power, the veil that makes the play of the world possible, and knowledge is the same power lifting the veil. This matches the fourth of her five acts, concealment, in Part 4.

Name 402 is bold for a hymn of praise. The Devī Māhātmya says the same: it is the Goddess who throws even the wise into delusion, and the Goddess who frees them.

📖 What the commentary adds

Vyāpinī: pervading because she takes the forms of the three ego-born creations. Vividhākārā: the many-formed creation called Vaikṛta or Kaumāra.

Vidyāvidyāsvarūpiṇī: he cites the Īśa Upaniṣad, Nāradīya Purāṇa and Devī Bhāgavata, and the Liṅga Purāṇa’s three forms of Śiva. On 403 kumuda is glossed both as water-lily and, playfully, as ku-muda, people of low joy, whom she also gladdens.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 403 is pure poetry. Night-lilies open in moonlight; Kāmeśvara’s eyes are night-lilies; she is the moonlight that makes them open. The Saundaryalaharī delights in exactly such images of the love between them.

Name 403 is one of the hymn’s compound images. Kāmeśa’s eyes are lilies that open only at moonrise, and she is the moon. His seeing depends on her shining: Śiva knows only through Śakti.

🌱 For today

Even the veil is part of the play.

“THAT YOU ARE”

🔎 In context

Our text prints “tattvamayī” as one word, but the standard count, following Bhāskararāya, reads it as three names: tat, tvam, ayī. Together they echo the great saying tat tvam asi, “That you are”. The count of a thousand names depends on this reading.

The split into tat, tvam and ayī is not forced: without it the count falls short of a thousand. Bhāskararāya’s reading is the one most chanted lists follow.

📖 What the commentary adds

Tattvāsanā: the 36 tattvas are her seat; āsana is the yoga-seat. Tat signifies the movement of mind present in every intellect; tvam also signifies the Devī; ayī is a call.

Pañcakośāntarasthitā: he cites the Jñānārṇava, the Taittirīya, Śrīkaṇṭha’s commentary, the Brahma Gītā and Durvāsas: she is at the centre of the bliss-sheath. Nityayauvanā: untouched by age in past, present and future.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 428, dwelling within the five sheaths, is from the Taittirīya Upaniṣad: the body of food, breath, mind, knowledge and bliss. She is within all of them, the self at their core. And she is ever young.

The five sheaths are often pictured as nested shells, the Self at the centre unaffected by any of them. The Goddess is placed at that centre, the innermost, not the outermost.

85–87. Whose form is the sixteen Nityās; half of Śiva; root of nature; both knowledge and ignorance.
88–90. Sunlight in the devotee’s heart; Śivadūtī; beyond mind and speech; consciousness and matter; the Gāyatrī.
91–93. That, you, O Mother; within the five sheaths; ever young; rapture; Kurukullā.
94–97. Mother of Skanda and Gaṇeśa; contentment, peace, delight; the playful-eyed; subtle; mother of the perfected ones.
