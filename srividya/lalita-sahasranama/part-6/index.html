ŚRĪ LALITĀ SAHASRANĀMA · PART 6 OF 10 · NAMES 475–577

LALITĀ SAHASRANĀMA

The thousand names · the seven yoginīs of the body

HYMNS OF THE GODDESS

HOW THIS WORKS: All the ślokas in one go for chanting, then every śloka on two pages: the śloka, a picture, its meaning and every name in the word table; then a deep dive.

WHERE WE ARE

Part 6 covers ślokas 98–116, names 475–577. Its first sixty names (475–534) are the yoginī section: the Goddess as seven yoginīs who rule the centres of the subtle body and the seven tissues: Ḍākinī (throat, skin), Rākiṇī (heart, blood), Lākinī (navel, flesh), Kākinī (pelvis, fat), Sākinī (root, bone), Hākinī (brow, marrow) and Yākinī (crown, essence).

Each has a colour, a number of faces, weapons, attendants and a favourite dish: milk-rice, ghee-rice, jaggery-rice, curd-rice, green-gram rice, turmeric rice, every kind of rice. After them come names of offering, healing, friendship and the letters of the alphabet.

We describe the yoginīs as the hymn does. Meditation on them belongs to practice under a teacher.

ZAPPY: Each yoginī has a favourite food?

PROF. BOLT: Yes, and they’re all homely rice dishes: pāyasa, curd-rice, pongal. A household can offer every one of them.

ŚLOKA 98 · DEEP DIVE

ḌĀKINĪ: THE THROAT

🔎 In context

Names 475 to 534 are the yoginī section of the Sahasranāma: the Goddess as the seven yoginīs who rule the centres of the subtle body. The seven yoginīs of names 475–534 each rule one centre of the body and one bodily tissue, have their own colour, number of faces, weapons, attendants and favourite food. The order begins at the throat, goes down to the root, then up to the brow and the crown.

Viśuddhi means “purification”. It is the centre of the space element and of speech, which suits a hymn composed by the goddesses of speech.

📖 What the commentary adds

Bhāskararāya introduces names 475–534 as seven yoginīs tied to the centres, and gives each a dhyāna, a meditation picture, from which the names are drawn. Ḍākinī at the throat is rosy, three-eyed, one-faced.

Khaṭvāṅga means a bed’s leg, or a club topped with a skull; Ḍākinī holds a club, sword, trident and hide. Her one face, says the dhyāna, terrifies the ignorant.

📜 Looking deeper

The first yoginī, at the throat, is Ḍākinī, named in the next śloka. She has one face; each yoginī after her has one more face, up to the many-faced yoginī of the crown. The yoginīs here are the same names as the Mātṛkā-like goddesses that some yoga texts place in the centres, though the order differs.

The khaṭvāṅga is an ascetic’s weapon associated with Śiva and the cremation ground: a reminder of mortality held by the goddess of the skin, the body’s outer surface.

🌱 For today

The body is a temple with many shrines.

ŚLOKA 99 · DEEP DIVE

ḌĀKINĪ: SKIN AND MILK-RICE

🔎 In context

Ḍākinī rules the skin, the outermost tissue, and loves pāyasa, sweet rice cooked in milk, still a favourite temple offering. Amṛtā and fifteen other goddesses, one for each petal of the sixteen-petalled throat lotus, surround her.

📖 What the commentary adds

Ḍākinī loves pāyasa, sweet milk-rice, and presides over touch and the skin. The sixteen śaktis of the sixteen petals run from Amṛtākarṣiṇī, he says, citing the Svacchanda Tantra.

Ḍākinīśvarī (484) gathers all the attributes of 475–483 into one name. The pattern repeats for each yoginī: centre, colour, faces, weapons, attendants, tissue, food, and a closing name.

📜 Looking deeper

Each yoginī is paired with a tissue: skin, blood, flesh, fat, bone, marrow, and the essence. The scheme matches the seven dhātus of Āyurveda, which our guide to the tenth Nityā describes. The tradition gives each tissue a goddess.

The sixteen petal-goddesses of the throat lotus carry the sixteen vowels. Speech is built on vowels, and they surround the goddess of the speech centre.

🌱 For today

Even the skin has its goddess.

ŚLOKA 100 · DEEP DIVE

RĀKIṆĪ: THE HEART

🔎 In context

The second yoginī rules the heart centre, anāhata, and the blood. She is dark, with two faces and fierce tusks, holding a rosary. The heart and the blood belong together, as every reader of anatomy knows.

Anāhata, “unstruck”, refers to the inner sound heard in meditation without any two things striking. The heart is its seat.

📖 What the commentary adds

Rākiṇī lives in the twelve-petalled heart lotus; her dhyāna gives two faces, tusks, and a disc, trident, skull and drum. Akṣamālā here, he says, refers to that disc; akṣa can also mean a chariot wheel.

She presides over the blood, which the heart moves.

📜 Looking deeper

The fierce features of the yoginīs, tusks, skulls and weapons, mark them as protective powers of the body. They guard the tissues they rule. We describe the forms as the hymn gives them; meditation on them belongs to practice under a teacher.

The rosary in a fierce goddess’s hand joins devotion and protection. The heart centre is often given to bhakti in yoga texts.

🌱 For today

The heart keeps the blood moving; something keeps the heart.

ŚLOKA 101 · DEEP DIVE

RĀKIṆĪ: RICE WITH GHEE

🔎 In context

Rākiṇī’s attendants are Kālarātrī and eleven others, for the twelve petals of the heart lotus. Her food is snigdhaudana, rice made rich with ghee.

📖 What the commentary adds

Twelve śaktis, from Kālarātrī to Śaṅkarī, occupy the twelve petals of the heart lotus; he cites the Varāha Purāṇa on Kālarātrī.

Her food is rice rich with ghee, as her dhyāna says. Name 494 closes her section: the Goddess in Rākiṇī’s form.

📜 Looking deeper

The favourite foods of the yoginīs are all simple: milk-rice, ghee-rice, jaggery-rice, curd-rice, green-gram rice, turmeric rice, and finally every kind of rice. Commentators note that these are offerings any household can make.

Kālarātrī, “night of time”, is also one of the Navadurgās. In the heart lotus she leads the twelve petal-goddesses who carry the letters ka to ṭha.

🌱 For today

Simple food, offered with love, is enough.

ŚLOKA 102 · DEEP DIVE

LĀKINĪ: THE NAVEL

🔎 In context

The third yoginī rules the navel centre, maṇipūra. She has three faces, carries the thunderbolt, and is surrounded by ten attendants for the ten petals of this lotus.

📖 What the commentary adds

Lākinī’s dhyāna gives three faces and tusks; she holds a dart, the thunderbolt, a club, and the gesture of fearlessness.

Her ten attendants run from Ḍāmarī to Phaṭkāriṇī. Bhāskararāya records the ten-versus-eight discrepancy without forcing it.

📜 Looking deeper

The navel centre is traditionally the seat of the digestive fire. Lākinī, who rules it, is linked in the next śloka with the flesh, which that fire builds.

Maṇipūra is the fire centre. Lākinī’s thunderbolt is Indra’s weapon, fire from the sky.

🌱 For today

Fire within transforms food into strength.

ŚLOKA 103 · DEEP DIVE

LĀKINĪ: FLESH AND JAGGERY-RICE

🔎 In context

Lākinī is red, rules the flesh, and loves guḍānna, rice sweetened with jaggery, a favourite offering in Karnataka and Tamil Nadu. She gives happiness to all devotees.

📖 What the commentary adds

Lākinī is red, presides over the flesh, and is fond of sweets (guḍānna), by her dhyāna. Name 502, giver of happiness to all devotees, he takes as a general statement.

Name 500 closes the sixth of the commentary’s sections. Sastry keeps these divisions, which help readers find their place in a long work.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 502 widens the blessing: not only those who meditate on the navel centre but all devotees are made happy by her. The yoginī passage is never only technical; each yoginī is also a mother.

Each yoginī ends with “ambā”, mother: the hymn insists that even these fierce figures of the body are mothers to the devotee.

🌱 For today

Strength is meant to make others happy too.

ŚLOKA 104 · DEEP DIVE

KĀKINĪ: THE PELVIC CENTRE

🔎 In context

The fourth yoginī rules the svādhiṣṭhāna, the centre just above the root. She has four faces, is yellow, carries the trident, and is called very proud: a fierce, dignified figure.

📖 What the commentary adds

Kākinī lives in the six-petalled svādhiṣṭhāna. Her dhyāna gives four faces, and she holds a trident, noose, skull and the fearless gesture; she is yellow.

She presides over fat and is fond of honey and curd-rice.

📜 Looking deeper

Each yoginī has one more face than the last: one, two, three, four, and so on, up to the crown. The faces may be read as the widening of awareness as one goes through the centres.

Svādhiṣṭhāna is the water centre. Commentators note the yoginīs’ faces increase by one at each centre, a simple code for the growing breadth of awareness.

🌱 For today

Proper pride is dignity, not arrogance.

ŚLOKA 105 · DEEP DIVE

KĀKINĪ: FAT AND CURD-RICE

🔎 In context

Kākinī rules the fat, medas, loves honey, and her heart is set on dadhyanna, curd-rice, the dish South Indian meals end with. Six attendants, for the six petals of this lotus, are led by Bandinī.

📖 What the commentary adds

Medas, fat or lymph, is Kākinī’s tissue. Her six attendants run from Bandhinī to Lambauṣṭhī.

Name 513 closes her section in the usual way: the Goddess in Kākinī’s form.

📜 Looking deeper

Readers in the South will enjoy the detail: curd-rice offered to a yoginī of the body. The hymn is serious and homely at once.

Curd rice and honey, cool and sweet, suit the water centre. The foods follow a quiet logic of their own.

🌱 For today

The most ordinary food can be an offering.

106–110. Sākinī at the root, of the bones; Hākinī at the brow, of the marrow; Yākinī at the crown, facing everywhere; Svāhā, Svadhā, the Vedas.
111–113. To hear of her is holy; she releases bonds, calms every disease, wards off death; Kātyāyanī.
114–116. The friend, the treasure of devotees, reached by friendliness; the supreme Power; whose form is the alphabet.
