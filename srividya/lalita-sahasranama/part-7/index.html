LALITĀ SAHASRANĀMA

The thousand names · Śrīvidyā, Tripurā, Rājarājeśvarī

HYMNS OF THE GODDESS

HOW THIS WORKS: All the ślokas in one go for chanting, then every śloka on two pages: the śloka, a picture, its meaning and every name in the word table; then a deep dive.

WHERE WE ARE

Part 7 covers ślokas 117–134, names 578–688. It opens on the great Kailāsa, names her as Śrīvidyā itself, places her in the head, brow and heart, and tells of Satī, Dakṣa’s daughter.

Then come the Guru as her form, the fifteen lunar days, Lakṣmī and Sarasvatī as her attendants, Tripurā, Umā and Gaurī, Lopāmudrā’s worship, the three powers of will, knowledge and action, and pure non-duality.

It ends with Rājarājeśvarī, who seats her devotees on thrones. Names that refer to mantras (587, 588, 622, 630) are explained by meaning only.

ZAPPY: She’s called ‘easy to worship’?

PROF. BOLT: Sukhārādhyā, name 681. No harsh penance needed. Love is enough.

ZAPPY: Is Mahākailāsa the mountain in Tibet?

PROF. BOLT: No, says Bhāskararāya: it is the great Kailāsa beyond the physical mountain, or the Kailāsa form of the Śrīcakra, or the crown centre. He cites the Tripurāsāra.

ŚLOKA 117 · DEEP DIVE

ON THE GREAT KAILĀSA

🔎 In context

Mahākailāsa is not the earthly mountain but its archetype, the highest abode above all worlds, where Śiva and Śakti dwell as one. Her arms are as soft as lotus stalks: the hymn again sets the cosmic beside the tender.

📖 What the commentary adds

Mahākailāsanilayā: beyond the earthly peak; or the Kailāsa-prastāra of the Śrīcakra; or the sahasrāra. Names 579–582, soft arms, adorable, compassion embodied, ruler of the great empire, Bhāskararāya passes without comment.

His silence on dayāmūrti, compassion embodied, is not neglect: some names need no explanation.

📜 Looking deeper

Dayāmūrti, compassion in bodily form, is one of the simplest and most loved names. She rules a great empire, and yet her defining quality is compassion.

Mahākailāsa is distinguished from the earthly Kailāsa in Tibet: it is the archetype the mountain points to. Pilgrims to the earthly one often recite this hymn.

🌱 For today

Power and gentleness are not opposites.

ŚLOKA 118 · DEEP DIVE

ŚRĪVIDYĀ

🔎 In context

This śloka names the tradition our whole series is about: she is Śrīvidyā itself. Names 587–588 refer to the forms of her mantra, the sixteen-syllabled one and the three-part (trikūṭa) form. They name it; we do not give it. It is received from a teacher.

The three names 583–585 form a ladder: Self-knowledge, the great knowledge, and Śrīvidyā. The tradition says they are the same knowledge seen from three sides.

📖 What the commentary adds

Ātmavidyā may refer to an eight-syllabled mantra; Mahāvidyā is “great” because it removes sorrow, or names the Vanadurgā mantra; Śrīvidyā is the fifteen-syllabled mantra. He cites the Viṣṇu Purāṇa’s four sciences. Kāmasevitā: the Aruṇa Upaniṣad’s bodiless son of Lakṣmī, Kāma, who knew this jewel.

Trikūṭā: the three may be Brahmā and the others, the three states, or the three sections of the mantra. Kāmakoṭikā: Kāma is Paramaśiva, and the equality of Śiva and Devī is Brahman.

📜 Looking deeper

Kāmakoṭikā links her with Kāmakoṭi, the seat at Kāñcīpuram, where the Kāmākṣī temple stands. Kāma, the god of love, is again her first worshipper, as in Part 5.

Kāmakoṭi is the name of the Kāmākṣī shrine at Kāñcīpuram and of the pīṭha of the Kāñcī maṭha. The Saundaryalaharī tradition links Śaṅkara with this seat.

🌱 For today

Some knowledge is given, not taken.

ZAPPY: Where in the head is she?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya says in the brahmarandhra, in the form of the guru; a moon below it; and at the forehead, as a point of light.

🔗 ALSO SEE: The measures of subtle sound at the forehead come from the Nityāhṛdaya (Yoginīhṛdaya).

ŚLOKA 119 · DEEP DIVE

IN THE HEAD AND BROW

🔎 In context

One glance from her makes millions of Lakṣmīs, goddesses of wealth, into her servants. Wealth is her maid. The Saundaryalaharī (verse 15 onwards) uses similar images.

📖 What the commentary adds

Śiraḥsthitā: the brahmarandhra, where she dwells as guru. Candranibhā: a moon in the lower brahmarandhra. Phālasthā: at the forehead as a point (bindu).

Indradhanuḥprabhā: he gives the Nityāhṛdaya’s measures of the subtle stages of sound, the ardhamātrā at the forehead and the half-moon above it. Millions of Lakṣmīs served at her glance (590) he leaves without comment.

📜 Looking deeper

Then the hymn places her in the body for meditation: in the head like the moon, at the brow like a rainbow, and, in the next śloka, in the heart like the sun. Our text prints 593–594 as one phrase; the standard count makes two names.

Head, forehead and (next śloka) heart are the three places for meditating on her in some practice manuals. Colour changes with the place: moon-white, rainbow, sun-red.

🌱 For today

Wealth follows grace, not the other way round.

ZAPPY: Two Dakṣas lost their sacrifices?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya says yes: Prajāpati Dakṣa, and Dakṣa reborn as a man. Both sacrifices were destroyed with the Goddess as cause. He cites the Brahma and Vāyu Purāṇas.

Dakṣa’s daughter.

ŚLOKA 120 · DEEP DIVE

DĀKṢĀYAṆĪ

🔎 In context

In the heart she shines like the sun; in the central triangle of the Śrīcakra she is a lamp. Then the story: as Satī, Dakṣa’s daughter, she gave up her body when her father insulted Śiva, and his sacrifice was destroyed.

The triangle of 597 is the innermost triangle of the Śrīcakra and also the triangle at the root centre. In both she is a lamp.

📖 What the commentary adds

Hṛdayasthā: the heart, or the supreme seed, or the Parameśvarahṛdaya; he cites the Kalpasūtra and the Anuttaratriṃśikā. In the body, he says, a fire disc sits in the triangle at the root, a sun in the heart, a moon in the head.

Dākṣāyaṇī: Śiva’s wife; also Rohiṇī and the lunar mansions, Dakṣa’s daughters; and a Dākṣāyaṇa sacrifice repeating the new- and full-moon rites. Dakṣayajñavināśinī: the curse story of the two Dakṣas (Brahma and Vāyu Purāṇas).

📜 Looking deeper

Where Satī’s body fell, the Śakti Pīṭhas arose, the sacred seats our Śakti Pīṭha guides describe. Name 600 is the six-hundredth name, a story of a daughter’s loyalty to her husband against her own father.

The Dakṣa story is told in the Purāṇas: Satī went uninvited to her father’s sacrifice, heard Śiva insulted and gave up her body. Vīrabhadra destroyed the rite.

🌱 For today

Loyalty can cost a great deal.

ŚLOKA 121 · DEEP DIVE

THE GURU’S FORM

🔎 In context

Gurumūrti is a key name for Śrīvidyā: the Guru is a form of the Goddess. That is why the teacher is honoured so highly in the tradition, and why our teachers section tells the stories of Agastya, Lopāmudrā and others.

📖 What the commentary adds

Darāndolitadīrghākṣī: dara also means fear and āndolita “driven out”: her long glance drives away devotees’ fear. Gurumūrti: the Sundarī Tāpinī, the Śaktirahasya and the Nityāhṛdaya.

Guṇanidhi: Sāṅkhya’s guṇas, or nidhi as nine treasures; he tells a story from the Matsya and Kālikā Purāṇas. Gomātā: Surabhi, the wish-granting cow; go has many meanings (Viśva). Guhajanmabhūḥ: Guha is Skanda, or souls hidden by avidyā.

📜 Looking deeper

Gomātā, mother of cows, is read as Kāmadhenu, the wish-granting cow; go also means speech and light, and commentators enjoy both. She is the birthplace of Skanda, as in Part 5.

In Śrīvidyā the guru is worshipped as the Goddess herself, and the line of teachers (gurumaṇḍala, name 713) is part of daily worship.

🌱 For today

A good teacher is a gift of grace.

ŚLOKA 122 · DEEP DIVE

THE TITHIS

🔎 In context

Daṇḍanīti is the science of government and justice. She upholds it. Daharākāśa is the “small space” in the heart that the Chāndogya Upaniṣad says holds the whole universe.

The dahara-vidyā of the Chāndogya Upaniṣad (8.1) meditates on the “small space” in the heart, said to hold heaven and earth within it.

📖 What the commentary adds

Daharākāśarūpiṇī: the small space in the heart is Brahman (Brahma Sūtra 1.3.14; Chāndogya 8.1.1).

Name 610: she is worshipped through the fifteen lunar days; the Varāha Purāṇa gives each day’s deity, and the Kādimata counts the full and new moons differently.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 610 is the Tithi Nityās again: the fifteen lunar days, from pratipad to the full moon, each worship her. Our Tithi Nityā series is built on this, one guide for each day.

Name 610 is the scriptural root of the Tithi Nityā worship, one Nityā for each lunar day. Our Tithi Nityā series is built on it.

🌱 For today

A small space can hold everything.

ŚLOKA 123 · DEEP DIVE

LOVER OF POETRY

🔎 In context

Kalā means both a digit of the moon and an art. She is all the arts and all the moon’s phases. She loves poetry: the thousand names, composed by the goddesses of speech, are a poem offered to her.

📖 What the commentary adds

Kalātmikā: ten kalās of fire, twelve of the sun, sixteen of the moon, and the states of waking, dream, sleep and the Fourth. Kalānāthā: the moon’s disc, which is the Śrīcakra.

Kāvyālāpavinodinī: Vālmīki and later poets; he lists poetry’s eighteen marks. Lakṣmī and Sarasvatī with fly-whisks (614) he leaves without comment.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 614 gives a scene often painted: Lakṣmī on one side, Sarasvatī on the other, both waving cāmaras, yak-tail fans, for her. The two great goddesses serve her as attendants.

The cāmara, a yak-tail fan, is a royal emblem. Two great goddesses holding it for her shows her as queen of queens.

Delight in good words.

ŚLOKA 124 · DEEP DIVE

MOTHER OF MILLIONS OF UNIVERSES

🔎 In context

Ādiśakti, the primal power, before all things. Ātmā: she is the Self. And name 620 gives the cosmic scale: she is mother not of one universe but of countless millions.

📖 What the commentary adds

Ādiśakti: first, as cause of the world’s origin. Ātmā: the soul, the supreme Self, the body, or Brahman (Liṅga, Śiva Purāṇas). Paramā: para-mā, Brahman, or Para and Lakṣmī. Pāvanākṛti: ākṛti as action or knowledge; Yājñavalkya says austerity and knowledge purify.

Name 620: the countless universes each have their Virāṭ, Svarāṭ and Samrāṭ. Divyavigrahā: divya as heaven and vigraha as battle: Caṇḍikā fighting in the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa.

📜 Looking deeper

Purāṇic cosmology imagines innumerable universes, each a “Brahmā’s egg”, brahmāṇḍa, rising and falling like bubbles. All of them are her children.

Purāṇic cosmology imagines universes without number, each a brahmāṇḍa with its own Brahmā. All of them come from her.

ŚLOKA 125 · DEEP DIVE

TRIPURĀ

🔎 In context

Klīṃ is a bīja associated with Kāma and with attraction and love; she is its maker. We give the meaning only. Kevalā, the alone, and kaivalya, liberation as aloneness, belong together.

Tripurā has many triads: three cities, three bodies (gross, subtle, causal), three states, three gods, three powers. She is the one in whom every three becomes one.

📖 What the commentary adds

Klīṅkārī: maker of the Kāma-seed, or Śivakāma; we give the sense only. Kevalā: single, without attributes (Śiva Sūtra 3.35). Kaivalyapadadāyinī: the fifth state beyond sālokya, sārūpya, sāmīpya and sāyujya.

Tripurā: older than the three gods; he cites the Tripurā Rahasya, Gauḍapāda’s sūtras and the Kālikā Purāṇa. Trimūrti: a girl of three in the kumārī list; the Varāha Purāṇa gives three colours, white, red and black, for her three forms. Tridaśeśvarī: queen of the gods, or of the 33.

📜 Looking deeper

Tripurā is the name at the heart of the tradition: Tripurasundarī, beautiful in the three worlds, of the three states, the three gods. She is trimūrti: Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva together.

Kevalā and kaivalya come from the same root: alone. Liberation in this sense is being alone with nothing other than oneself.

Everything in threes comes back to one.

UMĀ, GAURĪ

🔎 In context

Tryakṣarī, of three syllables, refers to her three-part vidyā, or, in another reading, to the three letters of Umā (u, m, ā) mirroring Oṃ. We note the readings; we decode nothing.

In the Kena Upaniṣad Umā Haimavatī appears to Indra and teaches him about Brahman: the first appearance of the Goddess as teacher.

📖 What the commentary adds

Tryakṣarī: three seed-syllables, or other readings; we give no syllables. Divyagandhāḍhyā: he cites the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka and Yoga Sūtra 3.41. Sindūratilakāñcitā: sindūra can mean elephant; he cites the Bhāgavata and Rukmiṇī’s story.

📜 Looking deeper

Then her family names: Umā, Pārvatī as the mountain’s daughter, Gaurī the fair. The vermilion mark on her brow is the sign of the married woman, worn by countless women in her honour.

Umā is sometimes split as u-mā, “O, do not” (do penance), what her mother said when she began her austerities.

The mountain’s daughter is everyone’s mother.

WOMB OF THE WORLD

🔎 In context

Svarṇagarbha is the Vedic Hiraṇyagarbha, the golden womb from which creation emerges. She holds the universe the way a mother holds a child.

📖 What the commentary adds

LOPĀMUDRĀ’S GODDESS

🔎 In context

Lopāmudrā, Agastya’s wife, is one of the great teachers of Śrīvidyā; a form of the vidyā is named after her. Our Agastya and Lopāmudrā guide tells her story. Agastya is also the listener of this very hymn.

📜 Looking deeper

Līlā, play, is not frivolity: it means the world is made freely, out of fullness, not need. Nothing compels her; she creates as a child plays.

The world as play is a lighter way to hold it.

YOGINĪ

🔎 In context

Names 649–652 are pure Vedānta: she is the seer who cannot be seen, the knower with nothing outside her to know. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad asks: by what would one know the knower?

📖 What the commentary adds

📜 Looking deeper

Then yoga: she is the yoginī, she gives yoga, she is its bliss. Yugandharā has two readings, “who bears the ages” or “who holds the yoke”; both make her the one who keeps things moving.

The Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad (2.4.14) asks: “by what would one know the knower?” Names 649–652 answer that the knower cannot become an object.

ŚLOKA 130 · DEEP DIVE

WILL, KNOWLEDGE, ACTION

🔎 In context

Will, knowledge and action are the three powers of the Goddess in Śrīvidyā. To do anything we first want it, then know how, then act. She is all three. In the Śrīcakra the three corners of the inner triangle are often read as these three powers.

Will, knowledge and action are often placed at the three corners of the Śrīcakra’s central triangle, with the Goddess at the bindu.

📖 What the commentary adds

Name 658: will, knowledge and action; kriyā is fivefold (Śiva Saṃhitā). Sarvādhārā: support of the worlds; the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa says she is in all things.

Supratiṣṭhā: a twenty-syllable metre. Sadasadrūpadhāriṇī: being and non-being, with Taittirīya and Chāndogya passages and Yoga Sūtra 1.9 on vikalpa.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 658 is one of the longest single names, and one of the most quoted. Then she is the ground of all, firm, and both what is and what is not.

The order matters: will first, then knowledge, then action. Every act of creation, divine or human, goes in that order.

🌱 For today

Wanting, knowing and doing: all three are gifts.

ŚLOKA 131 · DEEP DIVE

BEYOND DUALITY

🔎 In context

Aṣṭamūrti: Śiva’s eight forms are the five elements, the sun, the moon and the sacrificer. She has them too. Ajā is “the unborn”, a name for primal nature and ignorance; she conquers it.

📖 What the commentary adds

Aṣṭamūrti: Matsya, Viṣṇu and Liṅga Purāṇas. Ajājaitrī: ajā is the unborn one of Śvetāśvatara 4.5, ignorance; she conquers it by wisdom. Lokayātrāvidhāyinī: the fourteen worlds and their protection or destruction.

Ekākinī: Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.2, alone without a second. Bhūmarūpā: bhūmā is Brahman (Brahma Sūtra 1.3.8); the Devī Purāṇa uses crystal, cloud and fire images. Nirdvaitā: Chāndogya 7.23, duality is impermanent.

📜 Looking deeper

Bhūmā, the vast, is from the Chāndogya Upaniṣad: “where one sees nothing else, that is the vast”. Ekākinī, nirdvaitā, dvaitavarjitā: she is alone, without a second, beyond two. The hymn says it three ways to be sure.

Bhūmā, “the vast”, is the Chāndogya Upaniṣad’s word (7.23): “the vast alone is happiness; there is no happiness in the small”.

Where there is no second, there is no fear.

She gives food first, then wealth: the hymn knows what people need. Annapūrṇā, the Goddess of food at Kāśī, is this aspect of her. Name 672 is the heart of Advaita: Brahman and the Self are one, and she is that oneness.
