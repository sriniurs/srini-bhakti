HYMNS OF THE GODDESS

HOW THIS WORKS: All the ślokas in one go for chanting, then every śloka on two pages: the śloka, a picture, its meaning and every name in the word table; then a deep dive.

WHERE WE ARE

Part 8 covers ślokas 135–151, names 689–796. It continues the royal names, reaches Saccidānanda at name 700, names her Sarasvatī, the circle of gurus and Dakṣiṇāmūrti, the silent teacher.

At its centre is a famous run of picture-names (742–749): nectar rain on the forest fire of life, a whirlwind to the cotton-fluff of bad luck, the thunderbolt to the mountain of disease and the axe to the tree of death.

Then the fierce Caṇḍikā, the three goals of life, Mount Meru, Mantriṇī who runs her kingdom, and the wish-granting cow.

ZAPPY: What is the fourfold army?

PROF. BOLT: Chariots, elephants, horse and foot; or four battle formations. Bhāskararāya adds that Vaiṣṇavas read the four as Vāsudeva and the other vyūhas, and Śaivas and Śāktas have their own fours.

The fortune of the kingdom.

ŚLOKA 135 · DEEP DIVE

FORTUNE OF THE KINGDOM

🔎 In context

The royal names continue from Part 7. The fourfold army, chariots, elephants, horse and foot, is the army of the Lalitopākhyāna. Kośa means treasury, but also the five sheaths of Part 5: she is mistress of both.

📖 What the commentary adds

Kośanāthā: kośa as the five sheaths, from the food-sheath upward. Caturaṅgabaleśvarī: he cites the Bahvṛca Upaniṣad’s four “persons”.

Sāmrājyadāyinī: a samrāj is a king who has performed the Rājasūya, a lord of the earth, a king of kings (Agni Purāṇa and the lexicons). Names 693–694 he leaves without comment.

📜 Looking deeper

Satyasandhā, true to her word, is a quality of the ideal ruler. Sāgaramekhalā, “whose girdle is the ocean”, is a classical name for the Earth: she is the Earth itself, with the seas as her belt.

Caturaṅga, the four-limbed army, gave its name to the ancestor of chess. Her army in the Lalitopākhyāna is exactly such a host.

🌱 For today

Keep your word; it is a royal quality.

ZAPPY: Dīkṣā means what, exactly?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya derives it from dhī, wisdom, and kṣi, to remove: what gives wisdom and removes sin. The Parānanda Tantra says she removes disciples’ sins through the mantra.

🔗 ALSO SEE: Name 700, saccidānanda, closes the eighth section of the commentary.
Being, consciousness, bliss.

ŚLOKA 136 · DEEP DIVE

SĀVITRĪ; SAT-CIT-ĀNANDA

🔎 In context

Dīkṣitā can mean that she is herself consecrated for the cosmic sacrifice, or that she gives dīkṣā, initiation. We note the meaning; initiation itself happens only with a teacher. Sāvitrī is the power of Savitṛ, the sun.

📖 What the commentary adds

Sarvārthadātrī: the four goals of life (Devī Purāṇa). Sāvitrī: the creator, linked with Śiva as Savitṛ; he cites the Devī and Devī Bhāgavata Purāṇas and the Padma Purāṇa’s worship at Puṣkara.

Name 700 gets no comment on itself: Bhāskararāya simply closes his eighth section there. Some names need no gloss.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 700 is Saccidānanda, the classic Vedānta definition of Brahman: being, consciousness, bliss. It falls exactly on the seven-hundredth name, a satisfying landmark.

Saccidānanda is the most quoted definition of Brahman in later Vedānta. That it lands on name 700 is one of the hymn’s neat coincidences.

🌱 For today

Being, knowing, joy: three words for one thing.

ŚLOKA 137

SARASVATĪ
deśakālā'paricchinnā, sarvagā, sarvamohinīsarasvatī, śāstramayī, guhāmbā, guhyarūpiṇī ‖137‖
Unbounded by place and time; all-pervading; who enchants all; Sarasvatī; whose form is the scriptures; mother of Guha; whose form is secret.
WordMeans
(701) deśa-kāla-aparicchinnāunbounded by place and time
(702) sarva-gāall-pervading
(703) sarva-mohinīwho enchants all
(704) sarasvatīSarasvatī
(705) śāstra-mayīwhose form is the scriptures
(706) guha-ambāmother of Guha (Skanda)

ZAPPY: How did she become all-pervading?

PROF. BOLT: The Varāha Purāṇa tells it: doing penance on Mount Śveta, she asked Brahmā for omnipresence, and received it.

🔗 ALSO SEE: Brahma Sūtra 1.1.3, “Brahman is known through scripture”, is his text for śāstramayī.
She is Sarasvatī.

ŚLOKA 137 · DEEP DIVE

SARASVATĪ

🔎 In context

She is not limited by space or time. Then she is Sarasvatī, goddess of learning, and all the scriptures are her body. In Part 7 Sarasvatī served her with a fly-whisk; here she is Sarasvatī herself. Both are true in the hymn’s logic.

📖 What the commentary adds

Deśakālāparicchinnā: Yoga Sūtra 1.26 and the Saura Saṃhitā. Sarvamohinī: she leads ordinary people to believe in duality (Kūrma Purāṇa on māyā). Sarasvatī: also a girl of two in the kumārī list, or “the stream of impressions”.

Śāstramayī: the Chāndogya and Brahma Purāṇa say she breathed out the Vedas. Guhāmbā: guhā is the cave of the heart (Kaṭha Upaniṣad), or Guha is Skanda. Guhyarūpiṇī: the secret non-dual truth (Kūrma Purāṇa).

📜 Looking deeper

Guhāmbā, mother of Guha, is Skanda’s mother again; the sound echoes guhya, “secret”, in the next name. The hymn loves such sound-play.

The tradition counts three ways a thing can be limited: by place, time and object. Name 701 removes two; nirupādhi and aparicchedyā elsewhere remove the third.

🌱 For today

Learning is a form of worship.

ZAPPY: Sādhvī or sādhu and ī?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya’s text reads sādhu, and he counts ī as a separate name. Sādhu he explains as her inherent quality, as burning is fire’s. That is how the count reaches a thousand.

🔗 ALSO SEE: The guru circle (713): “handed down from ear to ear”, says the Yoginīhṛdaya.
Her form is the circle of gurus.

ŚLOKA 138 · DEEP DIVE

THE GURU LINE

🔎 In context

Upādhis are the limiting adjuncts, body, mind, name, that make the one Self seem many; she is free of all of them. Then a homely name: she is Sadāśiva’s faithful wife. Our text prints sādhvī, “the virtuous”; the standard count, following Bhāskararāya, reads it as two names, sādhu and ī, which keeps the total at a thousand.

Bhāskararāya’s split of sādhvī into sādhu and ī keeps the total at exactly one thousand. Other versions repeat sādhvī here; sanskritdocuments notes both.

📖 What the commentary adds

Sarvopādhivinirmuktā: all limitations are false, like silver seen in a shell; he uses an eclipse-astronomy image for reconciling scriptures. Sampradāyeśvarī: the teaching handed down by teachers (Yoginīhṛdaya, Dattātreya Saṃhitā).

He reads ī as the kāmakalā and links it to Viṣṇu, so she shares Viṣṇu’s nature as his sister; we give only the sense. Gurumaṇḍalarūpiṇī: the secret must be learned from a teacher, not from books.

📜 Looking deeper

Names 710 and 713 matter for Śrīvidyā: she rules the sampradāya, the lineage of teaching, and her form is the guru-maṇḍala, the circle of teachers from the first to one’s own. Our teachers section tells the stories of some of them.

The guru circle is worshipped in three tiers in Śrīvidyā: divine, perfected and human teachers. The Goddess is all of them.

🌱 For today

A tradition is a chain of people who cared.

PROF. BOLT: The “honey doctrine” of the Chāndogya Upaniṣad, which sees the sun as honey of the gods. Bhāskararāya links madhumatī to it, and to a high stage of yoga.

Māyā, and beyond the kula.

ŚLOKA 139 · DEEP DIVE

BEYOND THE KULA

🔎 In context

In Part 2 she dwelt in the kula; here she is beyond it, as she is both in the body and beyond it. Bhaga has many meanings: the six excellences, the sun’s orb, the central triangle. Commentators differ; we leave it open.

📖 What the commentary adds

Kulottīrṇā: kula is the group of senses; she is beyond it; the next names, he says, stress the doctrine’s secrecy. Bhagārādhyā: the sun’s disc. Māyā: the Devī Purāṇa’s etymology, “marvellous results like illusion”, and the Śāṇḍilya Sūtra.

Mahī: a river name, and “she pervades all” (Devī Purāṇa). Gaṇāmbā: mother of Śiva’s hosts or of Gaṇeśa. Guhyakārādhyā: the guhyakas, or a secret place, the root centre. Gurupriyā: wife of Śiva, the world’s guru.

📜 Looking deeper

Māyā is her power of illusion, the veil she can draw and lift. Madhumatī, “honey-filled”, is the name of a high stage of yoga in the Yoga Sūtra commentaries. Gurupriyā closes the śloka: she loves the teacher.

Māyā, name 716, is not a defect in the world but her creative power. In Śākta thought it is Śakti herself, neither false nor ultimately other than her.

🌱 For today

The same power veils and unveils.

ŚLOKA 140 · DEEP DIVE

DAKṢIṆĀMŪRTI

🔎 In context

Dakṣiṇāmūrti is Śiva as the first teacher, seated under a banyan facing south, teaching four young sages, Sanaka and his brothers, through silence. The Goddess is that teacher too.

Śaṅkara’s Dakṣiṇāmūrti Stotra praises the silent teacher whose teaching is silence and whose students’ doubts vanish. The hymn makes that teacher a form of the Goddess.

📖 What the commentary adds

Svatantrā: she creates without instruments, and all Tantras are hers; or sva is Śiva and the Tantras depend on one another (Kālikā Purāṇa). Dakṣiṇāmūrtirūpiṇī: Śiva facing south, teacher of Brahmā and Nārāyaṇa.

Sanakādisamārādhyā: Sanaka and the other young sages worship her and stand in the guru line (Brahma Purāṇa). Śivajñānapradāyinī: Śiva is known through his vibrating energy (Yogavāsiṣṭha).

📜 Looking deeper

Svatantrā is a key word in Kashmir Śaiva philosophy: absolute freedom is the nature of consciousness. She is free, queen of every Tantra, and she gives the knowledge of Śiva. Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar, in our teachers list, composed a well-known kṛti to Dakṣiṇāmūrti.

Svātantrya, absolute freedom, is the defining mark of consciousness in Kashmir Śaivism. Name 723 says the Goddess is that freedom.

🌱 For today

Some teaching happens in silence.

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya does a calculation under name 732: thirty-six letters combined with the vowels give 20,737 possible names. She delights in all of them.

ŚLOKA 141 · DEEP DIVE

LOVER OF HER NAMES

🔎 In context

Citkalā and ānandakalikā: she is a spark of consciousness and a bud of bliss in each being. Then premarūpā, whose form is love, the simplest description of all.

📖 What the commentary adds

Citkalā: cit is Brahman, kalā a portion: the soul is a small part of consciousness (Gītā 15.7). Ānandakalikā: a portion of bliss, or the bud of the bliss-sheath (Taittirīya).

Nāmapārāyaṇaprītā: his letter arithmetic, and the Kādimata’s five ways of repeating names. Nandividyā: the vidyā worshipped by Nandi.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 732 is the hymn speaking about itself: she delights in the recitation of her names. Every reading of the Sahasranāma is what this name says she loves. Nandividyā is a form of her vidyā associated with Nandi, Śiva’s attendant; we name it only.

Name 732 explains why this hymn is recited: she delights in it. The phalaśruti adds that reciting the thousand names daily equals many pilgrimages.

🌱 For today

Saying a name with love is enough.

ŚLOKA 142 · DEEP DIVE

GROUND OF THE APPEARANCE

🔎 In context

Name 735 is Advaita: the world is mithyā, an appearance, and she is its ground, as a rope is the ground of the snake one mistakes it for. Commentators give this example.

📖 What the commentary adds

Mithyājagadadhiṣṭhānā: he cites Gauḍapāda and Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.4.19 but defends the reality of the world as Tantra holds it. Muktirūpiṇī: liberation is the Self, veiled by avidyā and revealed by knowledge; it is not a substance, quality or action (Saura Saṃhitā).

Layakarī: laya is a mental state said to equal ten meditations; it is also a pause in music. Rambhādivanditā: Urvaśī and the other apsarases worship her.

📜 Looking deeper

Lāsya is the graceful dance of Pārvatī, the counterpart to Śiva’s vigorous tāṇḍava. Layakarī: she causes laya, dissolution, and also keeps laya, rhythm, in the dance. Lajjā, modesty, is counted among her forms.

Lāsya and tāṇḍava are the two styles of Indian dance, gentle and vigorous, traditionally given to Pārvatī and Śiva. Lāsya is hers.

🌱 For today

The world is real enough to live in, and not the last word.

↑ ŚLOKA 142 · Ground of the appearance.

A rain of nectar on the forest fire of worldly life; a forest fire to the jungle of sin; a whirlwind to the cotton-fluff of ill fortune; sunlight to the darkness of old age.
WordMeans
(742) bhava-dāva-sudhā-vṛṣṭiḥa rain of nectar on the forest fire of worldly life
(743) pāpa-araṇya-dava-analāa forest fire to the jungle of sin
(744) daurbhāgya-tūla-vātūlāa whirlwind to the cotton-fluff of ill fortune

ZAPPY: Who gets rain on the forest fire?

PROF. BOLT: Those burning in worldly life. Bhāskararāya splits the name differently too, and cites the Rudrayāmala and Maṅgalarājastava on enjoyment and liberation together.

🔗 ALSO SEE: The Bṛhannāradīya Purāṇa, cited for name 743, calls the Gaṅgā a fire that burns sin.

ŚLOKA 143 · DEEP DIVE

RAIN ON THE FOREST FIRE

🔎 In context

Now comes a famous run of eight names, each a picture. Worldly life is a forest fire; she is nectar rain on it. Sin is a jungle; she is the fire that clears it. Bad luck is cotton-fluff; she is the whirlwind that scatters it.

Names 742–749 form a set of eight picture-names, each a metaphor of rescue. They are often recited on their own for protection.

📖 What the commentary adds

Pāpāraṇyadavānalā: the Bṛhannāradīya on Gaṅgā burning sin; the Brahma Purāṇa on remembering her feet as expiation. Daurbhāgyatūlavātūlā: the gale is good actions, which make misfortune light as cotton.

Name 745, sunlight on the darkness of age, he leaves without comment.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 745 is a gentle one for older readers: old age is a darkness, and she is sunlight on it. Note that she is both rain and fire, depending on what needs to be done.

Fire and rain both appear as her forms: to the burning she is rain, to the overgrown she is fire. Grace adapts to what is needed.

🌱 For today

Grace takes the form the moment needs.

ŚLOKA 144 · DEEP DIVE

AXE TO THE TREE OF DEATH

🔎 In context

The moon raises the tides; she raises the ocean of fortune. Peacocks dance when rain-clouds come; devotees’ hearts dance when she comes. These two images are pure Indian poetry.

📖 What the commentary adds

Name 746, moonlight on the ocean of fortune, has no comment. On 747 he reads ghana also as continual nourishing: she keeps devotees’ minds dancing like peacocks under rain.

Name 748, the thunderbolt to disease, has no comment. On 749 dāru is the 28 pains of Vararuci’s system, and death the “pickle” of Kaṭha 2.25.

📜 Looking deeper

Names 748–749 are among the most recited for health: disease is a mountain, she is Indra’s thunderbolt; death is a tree, she is the axe. The hymn does not think illness small.

Peacocks dancing at the first rain-clouds is the classic image of Indian monsoon poetry, from Kālidāsa on. The hymn turns it into joy at her coming.

🌱 For today

The heart dances when grace comes like rain.

ZAPPY: Which Mahākālī?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya links her to the Mahākāla shrine at Ujjain, or as Mahākāla’s wife.

ŚLOKA 145 · DEEP DIVE

CAṆḌIKĀ

🔎 In context

The gentle hymn turns fierce. Mahākālī, the great devourer and great eater, swallows the whole universe at the end of time. Caṇḍikā is the warrior Goddess of the Devī Māhātmya, whose Kālī form slew Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa and was named Cāmuṇḍā for it.

In the Devī Māhātmya (chapter 7) Kālī springs from the Goddess’s brow and kills Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa; she is named Cāmuṇḍā for it. Mysuru’s hill goddess Cāmuṇḍeśvarī carries this name.

📖 What the commentary adds

Mahāgrāsā: Kaṭha 2.25, where Brahmins and Kṣatriyas are “food” for the Lord. Mahāśanā: she consumes the moving and unmoving world. Aparṇā: from apa-ṛṇa, free of debt; and the Kālikā Purāṇa’s story of her giving up even leaves in penance. He quotes his own Devīstava.

Caṇḍikā: from caṇḍ, anger; a girl of seven in the kumārī list. The Cāmuṇḍā name: the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa on Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa, the Varāha Purāṇa on her cutting off Ruru’s head.

📜 Looking deeper

Between the fierce names sits Aparṇā, “without even a leaf”: Pārvatī, who fasted so completely in her penance for Śiva that she gave up eating even fallen leaves. The great eater and the one who ate nothing are the same Goddess.

The great eater and the one who ate nothing: the hymn pairs extremes to show she is beyond both.

🌱 For today

The fierce and the austere are one strength.

ŚLOKA 146 · DEEP DIVE

TRYAMBAKĀ

🔎 In context

Kṣara and akṣara, the perishable and the imperishable, are the Bhagavad Gītā’s terms (15.16) for the world and the unchanging. She is both. Readers of our Gītā series will know the passage.

📖 What the commentary adds

Subhagā: a girl of five in the kumārī list; he gives a long Viṣṇu Purāṇa passage on Viṣṇu’s energy in the sun and the Padma Purāṇa’s eight auspicious items. Names 758–760 have no comment.

Tryambakā: three eyes, or the three gods. Triguṇātmikā: her body is the balance of sattva, rajas and tamas.

📜 Looking deeper

Trivarga is the three goals of life short of liberation: dharma, artha, kāma. Tryambakā is the feminine of Tryambaka, Śiva’s name in the Mṛtyuñjaya prayer. The three guṇas, sattva, rajas, tamas, make up nature, and she is their self.

Tryambaka is Śiva’s name in the Mahāmṛtyuñjaya mantra of the Ṛg Veda. As Tryambakā she shares the name and its promise of protection from death.

🌱 For today

Right living, wealth and love are all within her gift.

ŚLOKA 147 · DEEP DIVE

HEAVEN AND RELEASE

🔎 In context

Heaven is the reward of good deeds, liberation the end of rebirth; she gives both. Her colour is that of the japā, the red hibiscus, offered at Devī temples throughout India.

📖 What the commentary adds

Japāpuṣpanibhākṛti: he describes the ajapā mantra from the Dakṣiṇāmūrti Saṃhitā. Ojovatī: ojas as the eighth bodily essence in Vedic commentaries.

Yajñarūpā: the Taittirīya Saṃhitā says yajña is Viṣṇu. Priyavratā: the Bhaviṣyottara Purāṇa says a vow to any deity is devotion to Śiva-Śakti; Priyavrata is also a king.

📜 Looking deeper

Yajñarūpā: she is the sacrifice itself. Priyavratā: she loves vows, and those who keep them are dear to her. Ojas, vigour, is a word Āyurveda uses for the essence of vitality.

Heaven is temporary in Indian thought; liberation is final. She gives both, according to what the devotee seeks.

🌱 For today

Keeping a small vow builds strength.

ŚLOKA 148 · DEEP DIVE

ON MOUNT MERU

🔎 In context

Durārādhyā seems to contradict sukhārādhyā, “easy to worship”, in Part 7. Commentators reconcile them: she is easy for those who love her and hard for those whose minds are unsteady.

📖 What the commentary adds

Durārādhyā and durādharṣā: hard for fickle minds. Mahatī: also Nārada’s vīṇā; he cites Yāska. Merunilayā: the Tantrarāja’s sixteen Nityās and the three aspects of the Śrīcakra.

Mandārakusumapriyā: mandāra is the heavenly tree, or the white arka.

📜 Looking deeper

Meru is the cosmic mountain at the centre of the world. The three-dimensional Śrīcakra, the Meru-prastāra, is named after it. Pāṭalī and mandāra are flowers offered to her; mandāra is a tree of Indra’s heaven.

The Meru form of the Śrīcakra, built in three dimensions as a mountain, is worshipped in some temples and homes. Name 775 names her home on the cosmic mountain.

🌱 For today

Easy or hard depends on the heart.

WORSHIPPED BY HEROES

🔎 In context

Virāṭ is the cosmic body of the Puruṣa Sūkta, the whole universe as one person. The Bhagavad Gītā’s chapter 11 shows it as Arjuna sees it. Then the opposite: pratyagrūpā, the inmost Self.

📖 What the commentary adds

Vīrārādhyā: he quotes a description of the vīras. Viśvatomukhī: Śvetāśvatara 3.3; form, he says, is created by meditation. Parākāśā: ether as Brahman (Chāndogya, Brahma Sūtras).

Prāṇadā: breath as Brahman (Brahma Sūtra 1.1.23; Kauṣītaki). Prāṇarūpiṇī: Chāndogya 4.10.5 and Manu 12.123; the Nitya Tantra on keeping time by breaths.

📜 Looking deeper

She is the whole cosmos outside and the innermost self inside. Prāṇadā and prāṇarūpiṇī: she gives life and is life.

Vīra in Tantra also means a practitioner of a certain grade, the “heroic” disposition. Commentators give both senses.

🌱 For today

The vast and the inmost are one.

KĀMADHUK

🔎 In context

Satya, jñāna, ānanda: the Taittirīya Upaniṣad defines Brahman as satya, jñāna, ananta; the hymn gives bliss. Sāmarasya is the perfect blending of Śiva and Śakti, like milk and water: the goal of Śrīvidyā practice.

Satyajñānānandarūpā: Taittirīya 2.1 and Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.9.28. Kapardinī: ka as Gaṅgā’s water in Śiva’s matted hair; the Devī Purāṇa on Chāgalaṇḍa. Kalāmālā: kalā as beauty, mā as brilliance.
