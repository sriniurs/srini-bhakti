LALITĀ SAHASRANĀMA

The thousand names · rasa, rest, music, the inward turn

HYMNS OF THE GODDESS

HOW THIS WORKS: All the ślokas in one go for chanting, then every śloka on two pages: the śloka, a picture, its meaning and every name in the word table; then a deep dive.

WHERE WE ARE

Part 9 covers ślokas 152–167, names 797–905. It begins with the arts: she is the treasure of rasa, the art of poetry. Then she is the vastest light and the smallest atom, holds the noose of bondage and cuts it, and is named Brahman, the Mother.

At its heart are two of the tenderest names: she gives rest to those scorched by birth, death and old age (851), and she loves music (857). Then the method in two names: worshipped by those who turn inward, hard to reach for those who look only outward (870–871).

It ends with Vaiṣṇavī, nāda, and the bindu throne. Three counting points: brahma-jananī (822–823), ajā (866) and akāntā (860) follow the standard count, not our text’s printing.

ZAPPY: Rasa means flavour in art. Is that all?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya gives both: the rasas of drama, and rasa as the nectar of Brahman, citing Taittirīya 2.6–7, “he is rasa”. The Brahma Purāṇa links rasa to the life-breath.

Treasure-house of rasa.

ŚLOKA 152 · DEEP DIVE

TREASURE OF THE ARTS

🔎 In context

Rasa is the central idea of Indian aesthetics: the flavour or essence of an experience that a poem, a dance or a song gives. She knows rasa, and she is its treasure-house. The Taittirīya Upaniṣad says Brahman is rasa itself.

The Taittirīya Upaniṣad (2.7) says raso vai saḥ, “He is rasa”: having tasted it, one becomes blissful. The hymn gives the Goddess the same title.

📖 What the commentary adds

Kāvyakalā links poetic inspiration to Tantric meditation and to a kalā called Amṛtasañjīvinī. Rasajñā: knower of the dramatic rasas, and the sense of taste. Puṣṭā: her body is the 36 tattvas.

Puṣkarā: puṣka is nourishment; Puṣkarā is the deity of Puṣkara tīrtha; or puṣkalā, complete. Puṣkarekṣaṇā: he cites the Padma Purāṇa on a rare planetary conjunction called puṣkara, and reads kṣaṇa as festivity (Amarakośa).

📜 Looking deeper

Kāvyakalā, the art of poetry, connects to name 613 in Part 7: she delights in poetry, and she is poetry. Name 800 falls on rasa, a fitting centenary.

Indian aesthetics names nine rasas. Abhinavagupta, a great Śaiva teacher, held that rasa at its peak is a taste of the bliss of the Self.

🌱 For today

Beauty is one of the ways to her.

ŚLOKA 153 · DEEP DIVE

SUPREME LIGHT

🔎 In context

She is the vastest (supreme light, supreme abode) and the smallest (paramāṇu, the subtlest atom). The Kaṭha Upaniṣad says the same of the Self: smaller than the small, greater than the great.

📖 What the commentary adds

Paraṃjyoti: the Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Kaṭha on the supreme light. Paraṃdhāma: Gītā 15.6 and Gauḍapāda’s Kārikā; Kaṭha 3.9, Viṣṇu’s highest abode. Paramāṇu: the logicians’ atoms are her forms.

Parātparā: above Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra; para is also Brahmā’s lifespan. Pāśahastā: the noose is in her left lower hand. Paramantravibhedinī: mantra as a king’s three powers, lordship, counsel and force, which she breaks in enemies.

📜 Looking deeper

Names 810–811 are a lovely pair: she holds the noose and she cuts it. The noose of attachment is in her hand, from Part 1 onwards; she binds in love and releases from bondage. Name 812 says she breaks the spells others cast.

Kaṭha Upaniṣad 2.20: aṇor aṇīyān mahato mahīyān, “smaller than the small, greater than the great”. Names 806–809 echo it.

🌱 For today

What binds can also be undone by the same hand.

ŚLOKA 154 · DEEP DIVE

THE SWAN IN THE SAGE’S MIND

🔎 In context

With form and without form: the hymn keeps both, as worship and philosophy need both. Anityatṛptā is read two ways: content without transient things, or, kindly, pleased even with our perishable offerings of flowers and food.

📖 What the commentary adds

Mūrtā and amūrtā: earth, water and fire have form, air and space do not; the Viṣṇu Purāṇa on Brahman’s two forms. Munimānasahaṃsikā: the swan of Lake Mānasa; or a dancer with ankle-bells delighting the sages.

Satyavratā: several readings, including a Devī Bhāgavata story of a Brahmin named Satyavrata who gained poetic skill from a single syllable. Satyarūpā: what persists through past, present and future. Satī: Dakṣa’s daughter (Brahma Purāṇa).

📜 Looking deeper

The swan in the sages’ minds matches name 372, the swan in devotees’ minds, in Part 5. Antaryāmin, the inner controller, is a famous section of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad. Satī closes: Dakṣa’s daughter, and also “she who is”.

The Antaryāmi Brāhmaṇa of the Bṛhadāraṇyaka (3.7) describes the inner ruler “whom the earth does not know, whose body the earth is”. Name 819 makes her that ruler.

🌱 For today

Small offerings please her too.

ŚLOKA 155 · DEEP DIVE

BRAHMAN, MOTHER

🔎 In context

Our text prints “brahmajananī”, mother of Brahmā, as one word; the standard count, following Bhāskararāya, reads it as two names, brahma and jananī: “Brahman” and “the Mother”. Both readings are beautiful. The standard reading puts the two great words side by side.

📖 What the commentary adds

Brahmāṇī: the tail of the bliss-sheath, or she who gives life to Brahmā (Devī Purāṇa). Brahma: reached by the liberated (Viṣṇu Purāṇa). Jananī: creator of all; one may also read brahmajananī as one word.

Bahurūpā: many forms through her deeds, as in the war with Bhaṇḍa. Pracaṇḍā: Taittirīya 2.8 (the wind blows from fear of him), Kaṭha 6.2 (a raised thunderbolt). Ājñā: command; or jña, the knower (Śvetāśvatara 6.2). Pratiṣṭhā: a sixteen-syllable metre, and a kalā of the water element.

📜 Looking deeper

Ājñā, the command, is the divine order that the whole world obeys. Pracaṇḍā, the fierce, sits next to prasavitrī, who gives birth: fierce and tender together, as a mother is.

The split into brahma and jananī puts the impersonal absolute and the personal Mother side by side, the whole theology of Śrīvidyā in two words.

🌱 For today

A mother can be fierce in her love.

ŚLOKA 156 · DEEP DIVE

FIFTY SEATS

🔎 In context

The fifty pīṭhas are the fifty letters of the alphabet, each a sacred seat, matching the fifty-one Śakti Pīṭhas where Satī’s body fell. Name 577 in Part 6 called her the letters; here the letters are her seats.

The lists of Śakti Pīṭhas vary: 51, 52, 108. Name 833 follows the scheme in which each of the fifty letters is a seat of the Goddess.

📖 What the commentary adds

Prāṇeśvarī: prāṇa as the senses (Brahma Sūtra 2.4.14). Prāṇadātrī: giver of life to the eleven senses including mind.

Viśṛṅkhalā: fetters are the bondage of action; or, read otherwise, “unclothed”. Viviktasthā: in solitary places (Hārīta Smṛti), or in discrimination between self and not-self. Vīramātā: mother of heroes, or of chief devotees; a Padma Purāṇa story of Vīra as her son. Viyatprasūḥ: Taittirīya 2.1.

📜 Looking deeper

Viśṛṅkhalā means without chains: she is free, absolutely. Viviktasthā, who dwells in solitude: she is found by those who seek a quiet place, or in the discerning mind. Viyatprasūḥ: she gives birth to space, the first element.

Vivikta means both “solitary” and “discriminating”. The Gītā (13.10) praises a taste for solitary places; the hymn says she lives there.

🌱 For today

Solitude can be a meeting place.

Healer of the disease of becoming.

ŚLOKA 157 · DEEP DIVE

HEALER OF THE DISEASE OF BIRTH

🔎 In context

Mukunda is a name of Viṣṇu, “giver of mukti”; here it is feminine. Mūlavigraha, the root image, is the original form from which all her other forms come: the commentators identify it with Lalitā herself.

📖 What the commentary adds

Muktinilayā: abode of the five kinds of liberation. Mūlavigraharūpiṇī: the root form, Rājarājeśvarī, from whom Bālā, Bagalā and other śaktis arise. Bhāvajñā: Yāska’s six modifications of being; Pāṇini and the Yoginīhṛdaya.

Bhavarogaghnī: he cites the Rāmāyaṇa (Book 7) on Śiva as the only remedy, and the Śiva Purāṇa. Bhavacakrapravartinī: he discusses whether the heart lotus is a cakra or a yantra; the Viṣṇu Purāṇa defines cakra as the mind.

📜 Looking deeper

Bhāvajñā, who knows every feeling: nothing in the heart is hidden from her. Bhavaroga, the disease of becoming, is the cycle of birth and death; she heals it. She also turns the wheel. Again the hymn holds both.

Bhavaroga, the disease of becoming, makes the Goddess a physician; Vaidya is also a name of Viṣṇu in his Sahasranāma. Healing is a shared divine role.

🌱 For today

She knows what you feel before you say it.

ŚLOKA 158 · DEEP DIVE

ESSENCE OF THE VEDAS

🔎 In context

Chandas, the metres, stands for the Vedas themselves. She is the essence of the Vedas, of all scripture, of all mantras. Between these grand names sits a beauty name, slender-waisted, as the hymn often does.

📖 What the commentary adds

Chandas can also mean desire, icchā-śakti. Śāstrasārā: śāstra is the Veda (Brahma Sūtra 1.1.3), with Tantric and Mīmāṃsā texts; he quotes the Bhāmatī’s definition. Mantrasārā: Vedic mantras, Tantric mantras, or the 64 books on mantras.

Talodarī: read a-talodarī, the Atala world is her waist in the cosmic form. Udārakīrti: the golden person in the sun (Chāndogya 1.6.6). Varṇarūpiṇī: the 63 or 64 letters of the Pāṇinīya Śikṣā form her body.

📜 Looking deeper

Varṇarūpiṇī: her form is the letters, again. Name 577 in Part 6, the pīṭhas in name 833, and this name all say that language is her body.

Chandas also means “what covers”: the metres are said to have covered and protected the gods. She is the essence they protect.

🌱 For today

The essence of all learning is one.

ŚLOKA 159 · DEEP DIVE

REST FOR THE WEARY

🔎 In context

Name 851 is one of the tenderest in the hymn. Birth, death and old age burn people like the sun, and she is the cool shade where they rest. It is often recited for the old and the dying.

The five kalās of Śaiva teaching are nivṛtti, pratiṣṭhā, vidyā, śānti and śāntyatītā: the soul’s steps back to Śiva. The last is beyond peace itself.

📖 What the commentary adds

Name 851: viśrānti means happiness in oneself. Sarvopaniṣadudghuṣṭā: “Upaniṣad” brings the self near Brahman; he cites Brahma Sūtra 3.3.1 on the identity of meditations across the Upaniṣads.

Śāntyatītakalātmikā: the highest of the five kalās, in the space element (Śaivāgamas).

📜 Looking deeper

Every Upaniṣad proclaims her. Śāntyatītā, beyond peace, is the highest of the five kalās, the stages of the soul’s return, in Śaiva teaching: peace, and then beyond even peace.

Name 851 is often recited at the bedside of the old and the dying. Viśrānti, rest, is also a term for final repose in consciousness.

🌱 For today

Rest is a gift, not a weakness.

↑ ŚLOKA 159 · Rest for the weary.

ŚLOKA 160

LOVER OF MUSIC
gambhīrā, gaganāntaḥsthā, garvitā, gānalolupākalpanārahitā, kāṣṭhā, kāntā, kāntārdha vigrahā ‖160‖
The deep; dwelling within the sky; the proud; who loves music; free of imagining; the goal; the beyond-desire; whose body is half her beloved’s.
WordMeans
(854) gambhīrāthe deep
(855) gagana-antaḥ-sthādwelling within the sky
(856) garvitāthe proud
(857) gāna-lolupāwho loves music
(858) kalpanā-rahitāfree of imagining
(859) kāṣṭhāthe goal, the limit
(860) akāntābeyond desire; who removes sin (our text: kāntā, the lovely)
(861) kānta-ardha-vigrahāwhose body is half her beloved’s

ZAPPY: What music does she love?

PROF. BOLT: All four kinds of instruments, says Bhāskararāya: stringed, drums, metal and wind. Or the songs of the Sāmaveda.

🔗 ALSO SEE: The Aṣṭāvakra Gītā, cited for 858, pictures consciousness as an ocean on which the world is waves.
She loves music.

ŚLOKA 160 · DEEP DIVE

LOVER OF MUSIC

🔎 In context

Gānalolupā, “greedy for music”, is the name every musician who sings to her cherishes. The Carnatic tradition, and Muttusvāmi Dīkṣitar above all, offered her music. Our Navāvaraṇa kṛti guides will come to that.

📖 What the commentary adds

Gambhīrā: the great lake (Śiva Sūtra 1.23). Gaganāntaḥsthā: the heart, the elemental sky, or the supreme sky (Śvetāśvatara 3.9). Garvitā: the supreme I-ness of creating the world.

Kalpanārahitā: the world is waves imagined on consciousness (Aṣṭāvakra Gītā). Kāṣṭhā: a measure of time; or the last word of Vedānta; or Bhīma-Śiva’s wife (Liṅga Purāṇa). Akāntā: aka is sin and pain. Kāntārdhavigrahā: half Śiva’s body.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 860 is read as akāntā in the standard count; our text prints kāntā. One says she is beyond desire, the other that she is lovely; both fit. Name 861 is Ardhanārīśvara again, as in name 392.

Gānalolupā has made the hymn dear to Carnatic musicians; Dīkṣitar’s kṛtis to Kamalāmbā are a musical walk through the Śrīcakra.

🌱 For today

Music reaches where words do not.

↑ ŚLOKA 160 · She loves music.

ŚLOKA 161

THE PLAYFUL BODY
kāryakāraṇa nirmuktā, kāmakeli taraṅgitākanat-kanakatāṭaṅkā, līlāvigraha dhāriṇī ‖161‖
Free of cause and effect; rippling with the play of love with Kāmeśvara; with glittering gold earrings; who takes on bodies in play.
WordMeans
(862) kārya-kāraṇa-nirmuktāfree of cause and effect
(863) kāma-keli-taraṅgitārippling with the play of love
(864) kanat-kanaka-tāṭaṅkāwith glittering gold earrings
(865) līlā-vigraha-dhāriṇīwho takes on bodies in play

ZAPPY: Who is Līlā?

PROF. BOLT: In the Yogavāsiṣṭha, Queen Līlā, wife of King Padma, whose story teaches that worlds within worlds are made of mind. Bhāskararāya mentions her here.

🔗 ALSO SEE: The Līlā story is the first long tale of the Yogavāsiṣṭha’s Utpatti section.
Bodies taken in play.

ŚLOKA 161 · DEEP DIVE

THE PLAYFUL BODY

🔎 In context

She is beyond cause and effect, and yet she plays. Līlā, play, came in name 648 of Part 7: she arranges the universes in play. Here she takes on bodies in play: every avatāra and every form of the Goddess is līlā.

📖 What the commentary adds

Kāryakāraṇanirmuktā: kārya is the evolutes from mahat, kāraṇa primal nature; a śruti says “neither cause nor effect”. Kāmakelitaraṅgitā: Kāma is Kāmeśvara.

Līlāvigrahadhāriṇī: Queen Līlā of the Yogavāsiṣṭha; or effortless play; or the avatāras.

📜 Looking deeper

Her gold earrings are the tāṭaṅkas of the Saundaryalaharī (verse 28), which keep Śiva alive. The love-play of name 863 is read as the joy of Śiva and Śakti in each other, which makes the world.

To be beyond cause and effect and yet to play is the paradox of līlā: the free act, done for its own joy.

🌱 For today

Play is serious business for the gods.

↑ ŚLOKA 161 · Bodies taken in play.

ZAPPY: Can a householder be liberated?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya says yes, under name 867: kṣaya can mean “house”, and he cites his own Śiva stuti to show freedom while living in a home.

🔗 ALSO SEE: Saundaryalaharī 95, cited for 871, says she is hard to reach for those whose senses waver.

ŚLOKA 162 · DEEP DIVE

INWARD WORSHIP

🔎 In context

Our text runs ajā and kṣayavinirmuktā together; the standard count makes two names. Then mugdhā, innocent and charming, and kṣipraprasādinī, quick to grace, as in name 383.

📖 What the commentary adds

Ajā: Śvetāśvatara 4.5 and Gītā 2.27. Mugdhā: beautiful and innocent (Viśva). Kṣipraprasādinī: the Saura and Śiva Purāṇas on quick liberation through devotion; the Tantrarāja on irregular worship still bearing fruit.

Antarmukhasamārādhyā: worshipped by the introspective. Bahirmukhasudurlabhā: Saundaryalaharī 95.

📜 Looking deeper

Names 870–871 are among the most quoted: she is found by turning inward, and very hard to find by those who only look outward. This is the whole method of the inner worship the hymn has hinted at since Part 5.

Kaṭha Upaniṣad 4.1: the senses face outward, so we see outward; a wise one, seeking the deathless, turns the gaze inward. Names 870–871 compress this.

🌱 For today

What you are looking for may be inside.

↑ ŚLOKA 162 · Found by turning inward.

ZAPPY: A garden of her own?

PROF. BOLT: One of Bhāskararāya’s readings of svātmārāmā: sva-ārāmā, “her own garden”. He also cites Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.3: the One, alone, divided itself into two.

🔗 ALSO SEE: Tripuramālinī, goddess of the eighth enclosure, is in our Khaḍgamālā guides.
Delighting in her own Self.

ŚLOKA 163 · DEEP DIVE

TRAYĪ

🔎 In context

Trayī is the three Vedas, Ṛg, Yajus and Sāman. Tripuramālinī is the presiding deity of the eighth enclosure of the Śrīcakra in our Khaḍgamālā and Āvaraṇa guides.

📖 What the commentary adds

Trayī: the three Vedas (Kūrma, Padma, Devī Purāṇas). Trivarganilayā: dharma, wealth and desire, or the three times, or the three parts of Oṃ. Tristhā: the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa’s long list of triads.

Nirālambā: she supports all and needs no support, which avoids infinite regress. Sudhāsṛti: the nectar from the moon at the crown flows through kuṇḍalinī; the Vāyu Purāṇa gives fifteen streams.

📜 Looking deeper

Svātmārāmā: she delights in her own Self, needing nothing outside. Nirālambā: she rests on nothing. Sudhāsṛtiḥ, the stream of nectar, is read by yogis as the nectar that flows from the crown in deep meditation.

Ātmārāma, one who delights in the Self, is a famous phrase of the Bhāgavata (1.7.10): even the ātmārāmas love Hari. The hymn gives the delight to her.

🌱 For today

Joy that needs nothing outside is the steadiest.

↑ ŚLOKA 163 · Delighting in her own Self.

ŚLOKA 164

LIFTING US FROM THE MUD

WordMeans
(880) saṃsāra-paṅka-nirmagna-samuddharaṇa-paṇḍitāskilled in lifting out those sunk in the mud of worldly life
(881) yajña-priyāwho loves sacrifice
(882) yajña-kartrīwho performs sacrifice
(883) yajamāna-svarūpiṇīwhose form is the sacrificer

ZAPPY: Does remembering her keep one out of the mud?

PROF. BOLT: The Kūrma Purāṇa, cited here: those who remember the Goddess do not fall into the ocean of saṃsāra.

🔗 ALSO SEE: Śiva’s eight forms, the eighth being the sacrificer, are in the Liṅga and Vāyu Purāṇas.

ŚLOKA 164 · DEEP DIVE

LIFTING US FROM THE MUD

🔎 In context

Name 880 is a picture: people sunk in the mud of saṃsāra, and she, skilled at the work, pulling them out. Paṇḍitā means expert: rescue is her craft.

📖 What the commentary adds

Yajñapriyā: the śruti says yajña is Viṣṇu. Yajamānasvarūpiṇī: dīkṣā is a form of Śiva, and the eighth of his forms is the self, the sacrificer (Liṅga, Vāyu Purāṇas).

Name 882, performer of sacrifice, he leaves without further comment.

📜 Looking deeper

Then sacrifice: she loves it, performs it, and is the sacrificer. In the Vedic view the world itself is a sacrifice, and she is every part of it: the offering, the doer, the one for whom it is done. Śiva’s eight forms, named in Part 7, include the sacrificer.

The Gītā (4.24) says “the offering is Brahman, the oblation is Brahman, poured by Brahman into the fire of Brahman”. Names 881–883 say the same of the Goddess.

🌱 For today

Even in the mud, help comes.

↑ ŚLOKA 164 · She lifts us from the mud.

ZAPPY: Worshipper and worshipped not different?

PROF. BOLT: Bhāskararāya says so on dhanādhyakṣā: she is Kubera, and the worshipper and the worshipped are not two.

🔗 ALSO SEE: The Kālikā Purāṇa story of Viṣṇu’s confusion at Kāmarūpa is cited for 889.

ŚLOKA 165 · DEEP DIVE

WEALTH AND THE LEARNED

🔎 In context

Dharma comes first, then wealth: the order matters. Dhanadhānyavivardhinī, increasing wealth and grain, is a name farmers and households pray with. Vipra means a learned person, one who knows the Vedas.

📖 What the commentary adds

Dharmādhārā: dharma is custom not contrary to the Veda (Saṃvarta Smṛti); or dhārā, a flowing stream. Viprapriyā: types of learned Brahmins (Brahmavaivarta). Viprarūpā: the gods dwell in the learned (śruti); the Parāśara and Āpastamba Smṛtis.

Viśvabhramaṇakāriṇī: Śvetāśvatara 6.1 and Gītā 18.61 on the turning wheel of the world, and the Kālikā Purāṇa story of Viṣṇu bewildered at Kāmarūpa.

📜 Looking deeper

Name 889 is a fine image: she sets the universe whirling, like a potter’s wheel or the turning sky. The commentators also read it as the wandering of souls through births, which only she can stop.

The Gītā (18.61) says the Lord dwells in all hearts, “whirling all beings as if mounted on a machine, by his māyā”. Name 889 echoes it.

🌱 For today

Wealth rests best on right living.

↑ ŚLOKA 165 · Who increases wealth and grain.

ŚLOKA 166 · DEEP DIVE

VAIṢṆAVĪ

🔎 In context

She swallows the universe at the end of time, as Mahākālī did in Part 8. Then she is Vaiṣṇavī and Viṣṇu himself. The Sahasranāma always unites: Viṣṇu is Hayagrīva, who teaches this hymn.

📖 What the commentary adds

Viśvagrāsā: Kaṭha 2.25 and Brahma Sūtra 1.2.9 on the eater as the highest Self. Vaiṣṇavī: four meanings from the Devī Purāṇa. Viṣṇurūpiṇī: the Lalitopākhyāna, the Kūrma Purāṇa (Himavat’s praise), the Padma and Vāmana Purāṇas.

Ayoni: yoni as cause or abode. Yoninilayā: Muṇḍaka 3.1.3; the eight cakras and nine doors of the body-city Ayodhyā. Kūṭasthā: Gītā 12.3; kūṭa means anvil, peak, deception (Viśva). Kularūpiṇī: kula as the Kaula path, family, or conduct.

📜 Looking deeper

She has no origin and is the origin of all. Kūṭasthā is the Gītā’s word (6.8, 12.3) for the unchanging, like an anvil that stays still while everything is hammered on it.

Vaiṣṇavī is also one of the Seven Mothers, the Mātṛkās, carrying Viṣṇu’s conch and discus. The Devī Māhātmya shows her in battle.

🌱 For today

Stay steady while things change around you.

ŚLOKA 167 · DEEP DIVE

THE BINDU THRONE

🔎 In context

Name 900 is naiṣkarmya, beyond action, the Gītā’s ideal of acting without being bound by action. Nāda is the primal, unstruck sound from which mantras come; she is it.

📖 What the commentary adds

Vīrā: the Nāmamālā defines a vīrā as a woman with husband and sons. Naiṣkarmyā: the Kaivalya Upaniṣad, Gītā 5.10, and Yoga Sūtra 1.24. Nādarūpiṇī: nāda above Oṃ (Svacchanda Tantra).

Vijñānakalanā: vijñāna is direct experience of Brahman; kalanā is making it one’s own. Kalyā: able to create (Viśva). Baindavāsanā: he cites a Bindu Upaniṣad on seeing Brahman as one and many.

📜 Looking deeper

The part ends at baindavāsanā: seated on the bindu, the central point of the Śrīcakra, where our Khaḍgamālā and Āvaraṇa guides end too. Part 10, the last, begins at śloka 168 with Tattvādhikā.

Naiṣkarmya is the Gītā’s goal (3.4, 18.49): not idleness but action without bondage. Sureśvara’s Naiṣkarmyasiddhi takes its title from the word.

🌱 For today

The centre is where everything rests.

↑ ŚLOKA 167 · Seated on the bindu.
