ŚRĪ SAUNDARYALAHARĪ · PART 2 OF 5 · VERSES 21–41

SAUNDARYALAHARĪ

The wave of bliss · Ānandalaharī completed

HYMNS OF THE GODDESS

HOW THIS WORKS: All twenty-one verses in one go for chanting, then every verse on two pages: the verse, a picture, its meaning and key words; then a deep dive into the ideas and stories behind it.

WHERE WE ARE

Part 2 completes the Ānandalaharī, the “wave of bliss”, verses 1–41 of the Saundaryalaharī. It contains some of the best-loved verses of the hymn: “bhavāni tvam” (22), where two words of prayer win union with the Goddess, and “japo jalpaḥ” (27), which turns every act of the day into worship.

It also contains the hymn’s most technical teaching: Śiva and Śakti as one (34–35), the Śrīvidyā tantra (31), and a descent through the six centres of the body, from the brow to the root, with Śiva and the Goddess honoured in each (36–41).

Verses 32 and 33 refer to an initiatory mantra of Śrīvidyā and name its parts by symbolic words. We translate the words and do not decode them. As in Part 1, the yantras and uses traditionally attached to each verse are left out.

VERSE 21

HER DIGIT ABOVE THE SIX
taṭillekhātanvīṃ tapanaśaśivaiśvānaramayīṃniṣaṇṇāṃ ṣaṇṇāmapyupari kamalānāṃ tava kalāmmahāpadmāṭavyāṃ mṛditamalamāyena manasāmahāntaḥ paśyanto dadhati paramāhlādalaharīm ‖21‖
Your digit, slender as a streak of lightning, made of sun, moon and fire, seated above even the six lotuses in the great lotus forest: the great ones, seeing it with minds from which the stain of māyā has been rubbed away, hold a wave of supreme joy.
WordMeans

VERSE 21 · DEEP DIVE

HER DIGIT ABOVE THE SIX

🔎 In context

Verse 21 continues the theme of the six centres from verses 9 and 10. Above all six, in the thousand-petalled lotus, shines the Goddess’s kalā, a single digit of light. It is made of the three lights of Śrīvidyā: sun, moon and fire.

📜 Looking deeper

The image of lightning is apt: brilliant, instant and too thin to hold. The great see it not with the eyes but with a mind cleaned of māyā, the illusion that makes the world seem separate from her. Their reward is not a thing but a state: a wave of the highest joy, which is what the first half of this hymn is named for.

🌱 For today

What is finest is often seen only after the mind has been cleaned.

↑ VERSE 21 · A flash of her light above all six centres.

VERSE 22

“BHAVĀNI TVAM”
bhavāni tvaṃ dāse mayi vitara dṛṣṭiṃ sakaruṇā-miti stotuṃ vāñchan kathayati bhavāni tvamiti yaḥtadaiva tvaṃ tasmai diśasi nijasāyujyapadavīṃmukundabrahmendrasphuṭamakuṭanīrājitapadām ‖22‖
If someone begins to praise you, “Bhavāni, you, cast your compassionate glance on me, your servant,” and gets only as far as “Bhavāni tvam”, at that very moment you grant him union with you, your feet lit by the crowns of Mukunda, Brahmā and Indra.
WordMeans
bhavāni tvam“O Bhavānī, you…”, or “may I become you”
dāse mayion me, your servant
dṛṣṭiṃ sakaruṇāma compassionate glance
nija-sāyujya-padavīmthe state of union with you
nīrājita-padāmwhose feet are lit as if with lamps
Two words, and she gives herself.

VERSE 22 · DEEP DIVE

“BHAVĀNI TVAM”

🔎 In context

This is one of the best-loved verses of the hymn, and it rests on a pun. Bhavāni tvam is the opening of a prayer, “O Bhavānī, you…”, but read as a verb it means “may I become you”. The devotee meant to ask only for a glance; the Goddess hears the second meaning and grants it at once.

📜 Looking deeper

Commentators enjoy what it says about grace: the Goddess does not wait for the prayer to be finished, and she gives more than was asked. The crowns of the great gods, bowed at her feet, shine on them like the lamps waved in worship, which is the meaning of nīrājita.

🌱 For today

Sometimes the first two words are enough. Begin the prayer.

↑ VERSE 22 · Two words, and she gives herself.

VERSE 23

MORE THAN HALF
tvayā hṛtvā vāmaṃ vapuraparitṛptena manasāśarīrārdhaṃ śambhoraparamapi śaṅke hṛtamabhūtyadetattvadrūpaṃ sakalamaruṇābhaṃ trinayanaṃkucābhyāmānamraṃ kuṭilaśaśicūḍālamakuṭam ‖23‖
Having taken Śambhu’s left half, your mind still not satisfied, I suspect you took the other half too. For this form of yours is red all over, three-eyed, bending under the weight of the breasts, and crowned with the crescent moon.
WordMeans
vāmaṃ vapuḥthe left half of the body
aparitṛptena manasāwith a mind not satisfied
śaṅkeI suspect
sakalam aruṇābhamred all over
kuṭila-śaśi-cūḍāla-makuṭamwith the crescent moon in the crown
Not half: the whole of Śiva.

VERSE 23 · DEEP DIVE

MORE THAN HALF

🔎 In context

Ardhanārīśvara, Śiva who is half woman, is a famous image: the left half is the Goddess, the right half Śiva. The poet teases: her form is entirely red, three-eyed and crowned with the moon, the marks of both. She must have taken his other half as well.

📜 Looking deeper

Behind the playfulness lies the Śākta teaching that the Goddess is not merely half of Śiva but the whole. Lalitā has Śiva’s third eye and his crescent; the two are one form. The poet’s “I suspect” keeps the tone light while making a serious point.

🌱 For today

A good joke can carry a deep truth more easily than a lecture.

↑ VERSE 23 · Not half: the whole of Śiva.

VERSE 24

FIVE ACTS AT HER COMMAND
jagatsūte dhātā hariravati rudraḥ kṣapayatetiraskurvannetatsvamapi vapurīśastirayatisadāpūrvaḥ sarvaṃ tadidamanugṛhṇāti ca śiva-stavājñāmālambya kṣaṇacalitayorbhrūlatikayoḥ ‖24‖
Brahmā creates the world, Hari protects it, Rudra destroys it; Īśa, withdrawing all this, conceals even his own body; and Sadāśiva blesses all of it: all of them obeying your command, given by a moment’s movement of your creeper-like brows.
WordMeans

tiraskurvan … tirayaticoncealing, withdraws
sadāpūrvaḥ śivaḥSadāśiva

VERSE 24 · DEEP DIVE

FIVE ACTS AT HER COMMAND

🔎 In context

Śaiva and Śākta thought speak of five divine acts: creation, preservation, destruction, concealment and grace. Each has its god: Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Rudra, Īśvara and Sadāśiva. The verse makes all five servants of a single flicker of the Goddess’s eyebrows.

📜 Looking deeper

The same five gods appear in the image of Lalitā’s throne: four of them form the legs of her couch and Sadāśiva its seat. The Sahasranāma calls her pañcabrahmāsanasthitā, “seated on the five Brahmas”. A movement of her brow is enough to set the whole cosmic order working.

🌱 For today

Great things can follow from a very small sign, given by the right person.

↑ VERSE 24 · Five gods, one flick of her brow.

VERSE 25

WORSHIP OF HER FEET IS ENOUGH
trayāṇāṃ devānāṃ triguṇajanitānāṃ tava śivebhavet pūjā pūjā tava caraṇayoryā viracitātathā hi tvatpādodvahanamaṇipīṭhasya nikaṭesthitā hyete śaśvanmukulitakarottaṃsamakuṭāḥ ‖25‖
Worship offered to your feet, O Śivā, is itself worship of the three gods born of the three guṇas. For they stand always near the jewelled footstool that bears your feet, their hands folded like buds above their crowns.
WordMeans
triguṇa-janitānāmborn of the three guṇas
caraṇayoḥ pūjāworship of your feet
maṇi-pīṭhathe jewelled footstool
mukulita-karahands folded like buds
śaśvatalways
Worship her feet, worship them all.

VERSE 25 · DEEP DIVE

WORSHIP OF HER FEET IS ENOUGH

🔎 In context

The three gods, Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra, are linked with the three guṇas: rajas, sattva and tamas. They stand at her footstool with joined hands. So flowers offered at her feet fall on their heads too, and one act of worship reaches all three.

📜 Looking deeper

The verse is practical devotion. Anyone worried that worshipping one deity neglects the others is reassured: honour the Goddess’s feet and the whole divine court is honoured with them. It is a vivid way of stating the Śākta view that she is the source of every god.

🌱 For today

Go to the source and the branches are watered too.

↑ VERSE 25 · Worship her feet, worship them all.

VERSE 26

THE GREAT DISSOLUTION
viriñciḥ pañcatvaṃ vrajati harirāpnoti viratiṃvināśaṃ kīnāśo bhajati dhanado yāti nidhanamvitandrī māhendrī vitatirapi sammīlitadṛśāmahāsaṃhāre'smin viharati sati tvatpatirasau ‖26‖
Brahmā goes to his death, Hari comes to an end, Yama himself perishes, Kubera meets his end, and the line of Indras too close their eyes; in this great dissolution, O faithful one, your husband alone plays on.
WordMeans

dhanadaḥKubera, lord of wealth

VERSE 26 · DEEP DIVE

THE GREAT DISSOLUTION

🔎 In context

At the end of a cosmic cycle, the Purāṇas say, even the gods dissolve: Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Yama, Kubera, the Indras. Only Śiva remains. The verse adds a quiet word, sati, “O faithful one”: he survives because of his wife.

📜 Looking deeper

This prepares verse 28, which gives the reason more fully. Commentators read the line as praise of the Goddess’s auspiciousness as a wife: her husband cannot perish while she is with him. Śiva’s immortality is her gift.

🌱 For today

Lasting things last because something holds them together.

↑ VERSE 26 · All end; her Lord plays on.

VERSE 27

LET MY LIFE BE WORSHIP
japo jalpaḥ śilpaṃ sakalamapi mudrāviracanāgatiḥ prādakṣiṇyakramaṇamaśanādyāhutividhiḥpraṇāmassaṃveśassukhamakhilamātmārpaṇadṛśāsaparyāparyāyastava bhavatu yanme vilasitam ‖27‖
Let my chatter be japa, all my handiwork mudrā, my walking circumambulation, my eating and the rest oblation, my lying down prostration; let all my pleasures, seen as offering of myself, be your worship. Whatever I do, let it be a form of your worship.
WordMeans
japo jalpaḥlet my chatter be japa
mudrā-viracanāmaking of mudrās
prādakṣiṇyacircumambulation
ātmārpaṇa-dṛśāwith the vision of self-offering
saparyā-paryāyaḥa form of worship
A whole life as worship.

VERSE 27 · DEEP DIVE

LET MY LIFE BE WORSHIP

🔎 In context

Perhaps the most widely quoted verse of the hymn, and the easiest to live. Every ordinary act becomes a ritual act: talk becomes recitation, gestures become sacred hand-signs, walking becomes circling the shrine, eating becomes offering into the fire, sleep becomes a bow.

📜 Looking deeper

The key word is ātmārpaṇa, offering of oneself. Without that attitude, chatter is just chatter. With it, the whole day is worship. Many teachers give this verse to beginners, and many devotees recite it at the end of formal worship, offering everything else they do that day.

🌱 For today

Nothing you do needs to be outside your prayer.

↑ VERSE 27 · A whole life as worship.

VERSE 28

HER EARRINGS
sudhāmapyāsvādya pratibhayajarāmṛtyuhariṇīṃvipadyante viśve vidhiśatamakhādyā diviṣadaḥkarālaṃ yatkṣvelaṃ kabalitavataḥ kālakalanāna śambhostanmūlaṃ tava janani tāṭaṅkamahimā ‖28‖
Even after drinking the nectar that removes the fear of old age and death, the gods, Brahmā, Indra and the rest, perish. That Time does not end Śambhu, who swallowed the dreadful poison, is, O Mother, the glory of your earrings.
WordMeans
sudhām āsvādyahaving tasted nectar
jarā-mṛtyu-hariṇīthat removes old age and death
kṣvelamthe poison (halāhala)
kāla-kalanāthe reckoning of Time, death
tāṭaṅka-mahimāthe glory of your earrings
Her earrings keep Śiva alive.

VERSE 28 · DEEP DIVE

HER EARRINGS

🔎 In context

When the ocean was churned, a deadly poison came up before the nectar, and Śiva swallowed it to save the world. The gods drank the nectar and still die at the end of the cycle; Śiva drank poison and does not. Why? Because his wife wears her earrings: in Indian custom, the ornaments of a married woman are signs that her husband lives.

📜 Looking deeper

The tāṭaṅka, the earring, links this verse to a famous temple story. At Tiruvānaikkāval, tradition says Ādi Śaṅkara placed Śrīcakras in the earrings of the Goddess Akhilāṇḍeśvarī to calm her fierce form. That temple has its own guide later in this series.

🌱 For today

Auspiciousness is a kind of protection, and it is often worn quietly.

↑ VERSE 28 · Her earrings keep Śiva alive.

VERSE 29

MIND THE CROWNS
kirīṭaṃ vairiñcaṃ parihara puraḥ kaiṭabhabhidaḥkaṭhore koṭīre skhalasi jahi jambhārimukuṭampraṇamreṣveteṣu prasabhamupayātasya bhavanaṃbhavasyābhyutthāne tava parijanoktirvijayate ‖29‖
As you rise to greet Bhava coming home, your attendants’ cry rings out: “Avoid Brahmā’s crown in front of you! You will trip on the hard crown of Kaiṭabha’s slayer! Keep clear of Indra’s crown!” For those gods were lying there, bowed low.
WordMeans
kirīṭaṃ vairiñcamBrahmā’s crown

VERSE 29 · DEEP DIVE

MIND THE CROWNS

🔎 In context

A charming domestic scene. The great gods are prostrate at the Goddess’s feet when Śiva arrives. She jumps up to greet him, and her attendants cry out in alarm: mind Brahmā’s crown, don’t trip over Viṣṇu’s, step around Indra’s.

📜 Looking deeper

The humour serves the theology. The crowns of the gods are just obstacles on the floor of her room; their wearers are bowed so low that she might trip over them. Yet she rises at once for her husband. Devotion and love meet in one moment.

🌱 For today

Even the most powerful know where to bow.

↑ VERSE 29 · The gods’ crowns, underfoot.

VERSE 30

“I AM YOU”
svadehodbhūtābhirghṛṇibhiraṇimādyābhirabhitoniṣevye nitye tvāmahamiti sadā bhāvayati yaḥkimāścaryaṃ tasya trinayanasamṛddhiṃ tṛṇayatomahāsaṃvartāgnirviracayati nīrājanavidhim ‖30‖
Whoever always meditates on you, eternal one, surrounded on every side by rays born from your body, Aṇimā and the rest, thinking “I am you”: what wonder is it that the fire of the great dissolution waves lights before him, who counts even the wealth of the three-eyed god as straw?

“I am you”: the highest meditation.

VERSE 30 · DEEP DIVE

“I AM YOU”

🔎 In context

Aṇimā, the power to become tiny, and the other attainments (siddhis) are pictured as goddesses around the Goddess; on the Śrīcakra they stand in the outermost enclosure. Here they are rays from her body. The meditation is one of identity: “I am you”.

📜 Looking deeper

The fruit is huge. Such a person cares nothing even for Śiva’s wealth, and the fire that ends the world honours him with ārati instead of burning him. Śrīvidyā’s highest teaching is this identity of the worshipper with the Goddess; the verse states it plainly.

🌱 For today

Who you take yourself to be changes everything you meet.

↑ VERSE 30 · “I am you”: the highest meditation.

VERSE 31

THE SIXTY-FOUR TANTRAS
catuṣṣaṣṭyā tantraiḥ sakalamatisandhāya bhuvanaṃsthitastattatsiddhiprasavaparatantraiḥ paśupatiḥpunastvannirbandhādakhilapuruṣārthaikaghaṭanā-svatantraṃ te tantraṃ kṣititalamavātītaradidam ‖31‖
Paśupati, having bound the whole world with sixty-four tantras, each devoted to producing its own particular powers, stood still; then, at your insistence, he brought down to earth this tantra of yours, independent, which alone brings together all the aims of life.
WordMeans

VERSE 31 · DEEP DIVE

THE SIXTY-FOUR TANTRAS

🔎 In context

Tradition speaks of sixty-four tantras, each giving particular powers. This verse says Śiva taught them first, and they kept the world busy. Then, because the Goddess insisted, he taught her own tantra, which gives all four aims of life, dharma, wealth, pleasure and liberation, together.

📜 Looking deeper

Commentators take “your tantra” to be Śrīvidyā itself. Lakṣmīdhara, the best-known commentator, names the sixty-four and criticises some of them. We give the verse’s claim, which is a strong one: other systems give partial results; this one gives everything.

🌱 For today

A path that leaves out part of life will not satisfy all of it.

↑ VERSE 31 · Sixty-four tantras, then hers.

VERSE 32

THE LETTERS OF HER NAME

Śiva, Śakti, Kāma, Earth; then Sun, the cool-rayed Moon, Smara, Haṃsa, Śakra; and after that Parā, Māra and Hari: these letters, joined at their ends by three hṛllekhās, become the parts of your name, O Mother.

VERSE 32 · DEEP DIVE

THE LETTERS OF HER NAME

🔎 In context

Commentators read this verse as a code: each word stands for a letter, and together, in three groups, they make up an initiatory mantra of Śrīvidyā. In keeping with this series we translate the words but do not decode them. That mantra is given only by a teacher.

📜 Looking deeper

Even without the code, the verse is beautiful: Śiva, Śakti, love, the earth, the sun and moon, the swan and the king of gods, all becoming syllables of the Goddess’s name. The whole cosmos, the poet says, is spelled out in her name.

🌱 For today

Some knowledge is kept by being passed on in person. Respect the door.

↑ VERSE 32 · The cosmos spells her name.

VERSE 33

Some, O eternal one, placing these three, Smara, Yoni and Lakṣmī, at the beginning of your mantra, delighting in endless great enjoyment, worship you with rosaries of wish-gems, offering hundreds of streams of fragrant ghee into the fire of Śiva.

VERSE 33 · DEEP DIVE

THE ETERNAL WORSHIPPERS

🔎 In context

Like verse 32, this verse refers to the mantra and names three seed-sounds by their symbolic names. We give the meaning of the names only. The picture is of the most devoted worshippers, adorned with gem rosaries, offering ghee into the sacred fire without end.

📜 Looking deeper

Commentators link the “fire of Śiva” both to the outer fire ritual and to the inner fire of awareness. The phrase “endless great enjoyment” is typical of Śrīvidyā, which does not set enjoyment against liberation but sees both as gifts of the Goddess.

🌱 For today

Ritual and inner life are two sides of one offering.

↑ VERSE 33 · Worship without end.

VERSE 34

BODY AND SELF
śarīraṃ tvaṃ śambhoḥ śaśimihiravakṣoruhayugaṃtavātmānaṃ manye bhagavati navātmānamanaghamataśśeṣaśśeṣītyayamubhayasādhāraṇatayāsthitaḥ sambandho vāṃ samarasaparānandaparayoḥ ‖34‖
You, with sun and moon for breasts, are the body of Śambhu; and your self, I think, is the flawless Navātman. So the relation of principal and dependent is shared equally by you both, who are one in taste and given to supreme bliss.
WordMeans

VERSE 34 · DEEP DIVE

BODY AND SELF

🔎 In context

Here the poet works out the relation of Śiva and Śakti with philosophical care. She is his body; he is her self. So each is principal from one side and dependent from the other. Neither is above the other; they are samarasa, of one taste.

📜 Looking deeper

Navātman, the ninefold self, is a name of Śiva linked to the Śrīcakra’s nine parts. The verse is a reply, in four lines, to anyone who asks which comes first, Śiva or Śakti: the question has no answer, because they are one.

🌱 For today

In the best partnerships, each is the other’s support.

↑ VERSE 34 · Body and self: one taste.

VERSE 35

YOU ARE EVERYTHING

You are mind, you are space, you are air, you are fire, the charioteer of air; you are water, you are earth. When you have become all this, there is nothing beyond. To turn yourself into the form of the universe, O young wife of Śiva, you take on, by your will, the form of consciousness and bliss.

VERSE 35 · DEEP DIVE

YOU ARE EVERYTHING

🔎 In context

The six elements of verse 9, mind and the five, are named one by one, and the Goddess is each of them. Nothing is left over: “there is nothing beyond.” This is the hymn’s clearest statement that the Goddess is the universe itself.

📜 Looking deeper

The last line says she becomes the universe by first taking the form of consciousness and bliss. In Śākta thought the world is not an illusion but her real transformation (pariṇāma), as milk becomes curd. The verse prepares the descent through the centres that follows.

🌱 For today

If everything is her, nothing need be treated as worthless.

↑ VERSE 35 · Mind to earth: all of it, her.

VERSE 36

IN THE ĀJÑĀ

I salute the supreme Śambhu in your ājñā centre, bearing the brightness of millions of suns and moons, his side joined by the Supreme Consciousness. Whoever worships him with devotion lives in that world of light which is beyond the reach of sun, moon and fire, a world needing no light.

VERSE 36 · DEEP DIVE

IN THE ĀJÑĀ

🔎 In context

Verses 36 to 41 descend through the six centres, from the ājñā between the eyebrows to the mūlādhāra at the base. In each, a form of Śiva with his Śakti is honoured. Here, at the top, it is the supreme Śiva joined with Parā, supreme consciousness.

📜 Looking deeper

The world beyond sun, moon and fire recalls the Kaṭha and Muṇḍaka Upaniṣads: there the sun does not shine, nor the moon, nor fire; by its light all this shines. The poet draws on that famous image for the highest centre.

🌱 For today

Some light does not depend on anything else to shine.

↑ VERSE 36 · Beyond sun, moon and fire.

VERSE 37 · DEEP DIVE

IN THE VIŚUDDHI

🔎 In context

The throat centre is linked with space, the first of the elements. Śiva here is crystal-clear and begets space; the Goddess equals him. Their light is like moonlight, and the world drinks it like the cakora, a bird that poets say lives on moonbeams.

📜 Looking deeper

Verse by verse, the descent shows the same pair, Śiva and Śakti, in each centre, each time in a form suited to its element. Commentators note how the colours change: here, crystal and moonlight, cool and clear.

🌱 For today

Clarity spreads; people near it see better too.

VERSE 38

THE PAIR OF SWANS

I worship that wondrous pair of swans, delighting only in the honey of the blossoming lotus of knowledge, moving in the Mānasa of the great; from whose talk come the eighteen sciences, and who take what is good from what is faulty, as swans take milk from water.

VERSE 38 · DEEP DIVE

THE PAIR OF SWANS

🔎 In context

This is the heart centre, anāhata, though the verse does not name it. Śiva and Śakti appear as two swans. Mānasa is the sacred lake of the swans in the Himalaya, and also means “the mind”: they swim in the minds of the great.

📜 Looking deeper

Indian poetry credits the swan with the power to separate milk from water, a symbol of discrimination. The divine pair take only good from what is mixed. From their conversation come the eighteen branches of learning. The verse is a picture of wisdom as a loving dialogue.

🌱 For today

Learn to keep the good and let the rest flow away.

↑ VERSE 38 · Two swans: wisdom and discernment.

VERSE 39

VERSE 39 · DEEP DIVE

FIRE AND COMPASSION

🔎 In context

Consistent with verse 9, the svādhiṣṭhāna here is the centre of fire. Śiva appears as Saṃvarta, the fire that ends the worlds; the Goddess as Samayā. When his anger burns, her compassion cools.

📜 Looking deeper

The name Samayā is important. Lakṣmīdhara belonged to the Samaya school of Śrīvidyā, which stresses inner worship, and he reads this hymn in its light. Samayā here is the Goddess as the partner equal to Śiva. The pattern of fire and cooling recurs through the hymn.

🌱 For today

Where there is anger, be the cool glance.

VERSE 40 · DEEP DIVE

THE DARK CLOUD

🔎 In context

In this school the maṇipūra is the centre of water. Śiva is a dark rain cloud, the Goddess his lightning, and her jewels make a rainbow. After the fire of the previous verse, the cloud rains on the three worlds and cools them.

📜 Looking deeper

The image is one of the most beautiful in the hymn and a favourite of painters: a monsoon sky with lightning and a rainbow. Its theme is renewal: what dissolution has scorched, grace waters again. Readers in India know exactly how welcome that first rain is.

🌱 For today

After every burning season, rain comes.

VERSE 41

THE DANCE AT THE ROOT

In your mūlādhāra I meditate on Navātman, dancing the great tāṇḍava full of the nine rasas, together with Samayā, who is devoted to the lāsya. This world has these two, joined in compassion for the sake of creation, as its father and mother.

VERSE 41 · DEEP DIVE

THE DANCE AT THE ROOT

🔎 In context

At the base of the body, the descent ends in a dance. Śiva dances the vigorous tāṇḍava, the Goddess the graceful lāsya, and between them they express all nine rasas, the moods of Indian aesthetics. From their dance the world is born.

📜 Looking deeper

This is the end of the Ānandalaharī, the “wave of bliss”, the first part of the hymn. It closes not with doctrine but with parents: the world, the poet says, is not an orphan. It has a father and a mother, joined in compassion. The next verses turn to her beauty, from crown to feet.

🌱 For today

You were not made by accident; you were made in a dance of compassion.

↑ VERSE 41 · The world has a father and a mother.

THE HYMN ON ONE PAGE

Part 2 in five lines
21–23. Her lightning digit above the six centres; “Bhavāni tvam” wins union; she is not half of Śiva but the whole.
24–26. Five gods act at a flick of her brow; worship of her feet is worship of all; only her Lord survives the dissolution.
27–30. Let my whole life be worship; her earrings keep Śiva alive; the gods’ crowns lie at her feet; “I am you”.
31–35. Her own tantra; the letters of her name; worship without end; body and self, one taste; she is every element.
36–41. Down through the centres, Śiva and Śakti in each, to the dance at the root that makes them the world’s father and mother.
