The body as the Śrīcakra

BHĀVANOPANIṢAD

FOREWORD

The Bhāvanopaniṣad is the shortest key to the Śrīcakra: about thirty-five sūtras that read every enclosure as part of your own body and mind, and end by saying that your own blissful Self is Lalitā.

This guide goes through the sūtras from the outside in, beside the yantra, with plain-English meanings.

AT A GLANCE

The body as the Śrīcakra
WhatA short Upaniṣad, linked to the Atharva Veda, of about 35 brief sūtras
TeachingThe Śrīcakra is your own body and mind; its Goddess is your own Self
MethodBhāvanā: contemplating each part of the yantra as a part of yourself
SchoolKādi, by its own closing sūtra
CommentatorsBhāskararāya, Upaniṣadbrahmayogin, and modern teachers
Edition usedThe text with the Aruṇalaharī commentary (Shastra Nethralaya, Rishikesh)
Our ruleThe sūtras are public scripture; we give meanings, not rites

ZAPPY: The Śrīcakra is my body?

PROF. BOLT: That’s the whole Upaniṣad in one line, Zappy. The rest is the details.

The Bhāvanopaniṣad is a short Upaniṣad of about thirty-five sūtras, linked to the Atharva Veda. Its single subject is bhāvanā: contemplating the Śrīcakra as one’s own body and mind, and its Goddess as one’s own Self.

It moves through the yantra from the outside in, matching each enclosure with parts of the person, until at the centre it says plainly that one’s own blissful Self is Lalitā.

Bhāskararāya wrote a commentary on it, and so have later teachers. This guide follows the edition supplied for the series, with the Aruṇalaharī commentary.

WHAT KIND OF TEXT

Bhāvanā, the highest worship
NameBhāvanā (contemplation) + upaniṣad (sitting close to the truth)
Outer worshipBahir-yāga: rites with an image or yantra
Inner worshipAntar-yāga: worship in the mind
BhāvanāSeeing the yantra as one’s own being; called the highest
FormSūtras: few words, no doubt, wide meaning
MovementFrom a symbol to the Self, “not different from the Goddess”

ZAPPY: Is this a ritual manual?

PROF. BOLT: The opposite. It takes the ritual and turns every part of it inward.

Śrīvidyā tradition speaks of three ways of worship. Bahir-yāga is outer worship with an image or a yantra; antar-yāga is worship performed in the mind; bhāvanā is the contemplation in which the worshipper sees the yantra and mantra as his or her own being.

The commentary calls bhāvanā the highest of the three, because it gathers the other two into the body itself. It is not a ritual manual; it takes the language of ritual and turns every part of it inward.

The text is written in sūtras: very short sayings, packed with meaning. Our renderings open them up in plain English.

THE GURU AND THE BODY

Sūtras 1–3
Sūtra 1śrīguruḥ sarvakāraṇabhūtā śaktiḥ — The guru is the power that causes everything
Sūtra 2tena navarandhrarūpo dehaḥ — So the body, with its nine openings, is that power’s form
Sūtra 3navacakrarūpaṃ śrīcakram — The Śrīcakra, of nine enclosures, is that same body
Nine openingsTwo eyes, two ears, two nostrils, mouth, and the two lower openings
First contemplationSee the body as the guru’s form
EchoThe “city of nine gates” of the Gītā and Upaniṣads

ZAPPY: Why start with the guru?

PROF. BOLT: Because in this tradition the guru is the Goddess made visible. The body you have is her gift and her form.

The first sūtra names the guru as the power that causes everything. In Śrīvidyā, as in other tantric traditions, the guru is the Goddess made visible.

From that, the second sūtra concludes that the body, with its nine openings, is the guru’s own form; the third, that the Śrīcakra with its nine enclosures is that same body.

The image of the body as a “city of nine gates” is old: it appears in the Gītā and the Upaniṣads. Here it becomes the doorway into the yantra.

FATHER, MOTHER AND OCEANS

Sūtras 4–5
Sūtra 4vārāhī pitṛrūpā kurukullā balidevatā mātā — Vārāhī is the father’s part, Kurukullā the mother’s
Read asBone and marrow from the father; flesh and blood from the mother
VārāhīThe boar-faced commander of Lalitā’s army
KurukullāThe red goddess of the moat around Śrīpura
Sūtra 5puruṣārthāḥ sāgarāḥ — The four aims of life are the oceans
OceansDharma, wealth, desire and liberation surround the island

ZAPPY: Goddesses as parents?

PROF. BOLT: Your body is built from two inheritances. The Upaniṣad names them as goddesses of Lalitā’s court.

See: Vārāhī · Kurukullā

Sūtra 4 names Vārāhī as the father’s part of the body and Kurukullā as the mother’s. Commentators read these as the tissues inherited from each parent: bone and marrow from the father, flesh and blood from the mother.

Vārāhī, boar-faced, is Lalitā’s commander; Kurukullā, red and fierce, guards the moat of Śrīpura. Both have their own guides in this series.

Sūtra 5 adds that the four aims of life, dharma, wealth, desire and liberation, are the oceans around the island.

THE ISLAND OF GEMS

Sūtras 6–9
Sūtra 6deho navaratnadvīpaḥ — The body is the island of nine gems
Sūtra 7tvagādisaptadhāturomasaṃyuktaḥ — Made of skin and the other bodily tissues, with hair
Sūtra 8saṅkalpāḥ kalpataravas tejaḥ kalpakodyānam — Wishes are the wish-trees; the mind’s radiance is their garden
Sūtra 9rasanayā bhāvyamānā … ṣaḍ ṛtavaḥ — The six tastes, sensed by the tongue, are the six seasons
LalitopākhyānaŚrīpura stands on such an island in an ocean of nectar
ShiftFrom the yantra’s parts to the landscape of her city

ZAPPY: My skin is part of a magic island?

PROF. BOLT: In the story Lalitā lives on a jewelled island. The Upaniṣad says: you’re standing on it.

See: Lalitopākhyāna 3: Śrīpura

In the Lalitopākhyāna, Lalitā’s city stands on an island of nine gems in an ocean of nectar, surrounded by gardens of wish-fulfilling trees. Sūtras 6 to 9 place all of this in the body.

The body is the island; its tissues are the wish-trees; its wishes and intentions are their fruit; the mind’s radiance is the garden. The six tastes known by the tongue are the six seasons of the island.

The effect is to turn the mythical landscape of Śrīpura into an inner landscape one can contemplate.

WHAT WORSHIP REALLY IS

Sūtra 10
Sūtra 10jñānam arghyaṃ jñeyaṃ haviḥ jñātā hotā … — Knowing is the water-offering, the known is the oblation, the knower is the priest
ThenSeeing knower, knowing and known as one is “worship of the Śrīcakra”
Key sūtraEvery later sūtra fills in this one
Ritual wordsArghya, havis, hotṛ: taken from the fire rite
MeaningWorship is not an act on an object; it is the end of the gap
Gītā“Brahman is the offering, Brahman the fire”, 4.24

ZAPPY: So what is the pūjā?

PROF. BOLT: Noticing that the one who knows, the knowing and what is known are one awareness. That’s it.

Sūtra 10 is the key to the text. It takes the words of the Vedic fire rite, the water offering, the oblation and the priest, and applies them to knowing: knowledge is the offering, the known is the oblation, the knower is the priest.

Seeing knower, knowing and known as one, it says, is the true worship of the Śrīcakra. Every later sūtra fills in this one.

The Bhagavad Gītā (4.24) uses the same idea: Brahman is the offering, Brahman the fire, Brahman the one who offers.

ENCLOSURE 1: THE SQUARE

Sūtras 11–12
Sūtra 11niyatiḥ śṛṅgārādayo rasāḥ aṇimādayaḥ … — Destiny and the nine moods are the siddhi-goddesses
Same sūtraDesire, anger, greed, delusion, pride, envy, merit and sin are Brāhmī and the eight mothers
Sūtra 12ādhāranavakaṃ mudrāśaktayaḥ — The nine supports of the subtle body are the mudrā-goddesses
Three linesSiddhis, mothers and mudrās: the three lines of the square
LessonEven our faults stand at her gate as goddesses
EnclosureTrailokyamohana, “enchanter of the three worlds”

ZAPPY: Anger is a goddess?

PROF. BOLT: At the outer gate, yes. Seen as her power, it stops owning you.

See: Āvaraṇa 1

The outer square of the Śrīcakra has three lines of goddesses: the siddhi-goddesses, the eight mothers beginning with Brāhmī, and the mudrā-goddesses. Sūtras 11 and 12 match them with parts of us.

Destiny and the nine aesthetic moods are the siddhis; desire, anger, greed, delusion, pride, envy, merit and sin are the eight mothers; the nine supports of the subtle body are the mudrās.

The striking point is that our faults are not thrown out. They stand at the outer gate as her attendants, and seeing them so loosens their grip.

ENCLOSURES 2 AND 3: THE LOTUSES

Sūtras 13–14
Sūtra 13pṛthivy-ap-tejo-vāyv-ākāśa … — Five elements, ten senses and the mind’s changes
AreThe sixteen attracting goddesses, Kāmākarṣiṇī and her sisters
Sūtra 14vacanādāna-gamana-visargānanda-hānopādānopekṣākhya-buddhayaḥ — Speaking, taking, going, excreting, pleasure, rejecting, accepting, indifference
AreThe eight goddesses Anaṅgakusumā and her sisters
16 petalsWhat you are made of and sense with
8 petalsWhat you do, and how you respond

ZAPPY: Why are the senses ‘attractors’?

PROF. BOLT: Because each one pulls the mind toward its object. Seen as her, the pull leads back to her.

See: Āvaraṇa 2 · Āvaraṇa 3

The sixteen-petalled lotus holds the sixteen “attracting” goddesses, Kāmākarṣiṇī and her sisters. Sūtra 13 matches them with the five elements, the ten organs of sense and action, and the mind.

The eight-petalled lotus holds Anaṅgakusumā and her seven sisters. Sūtra 14 matches them with eight functions: speaking, taking, going, excreting, enjoying, rejecting, accepting and remaining indifferent.

Together the two lotuses hold what we are made of and what we do.

ENCLOSURE 4: FOURTEEN CHANNELS

Sūtra 15
Sūtra 15alambusā kuhūr viśvodarā … iḍā piṅgalā suṣumnā — Fourteen nāḍīs, the channels of the subtle body
AreThe fourteen goddesses, Sarvasaṅkṣobhiṇī and her sisters
Best knownIḍā, piṅgalā and the central suṣumnā
OthersAlambusā, Kuhū, Viśvodarā, Vāraṇā, Hastijihvā, Yaśovatī, Payasvinī, Gāndhārī, Pūṣā, Śaṅkhinī
RingThe fourteen triangles
MeaningThe pathways of life-force are her attendants

ZAPPY: Fourteen pipes inside me?

PROF. BOLT: Channels of life-energy, in yoga’s map. Here each one is a goddess in the ring.

See: Āvaraṇa 4

The fourteen triangles of the fourth enclosure hold fourteen goddesses beginning with Sarvasaṅkṣobhiṇī. Sūtra 15 matches them with fourteen nāḍīs, the channels of the subtle body in yoga.

The best known are iḍā, piṅgalā and the central suṣumnā; the others are named in the sūtra.

Our guide to the fourth enclosure gives the goddesses’ names; this sūtra adds the bodily reading.

ENCLOSURES 5 AND 6: BREATH AND FIRE

Sūtras 16–19
Sūtra 16prāṇāpāna-vyānodāna-samāna-nāga-kūrma-kṛkara-devadatta-dhanañjayāḥ — The ten vital airs are the goddesses of the outer ten
Sūtra 17… pañcadhā jaṭharāgnir bhavati — Joined with the airs, the digestive fire works in five ways
Sūtra 18kṣārakaḥ udgārakaḥ kṣobhakaḥ jṛmbhakaḥ mohakaḥ … — Five more powers digest the five kinds of food
Sūtra 19etā daśa vahnikalāḥ … antardaśāragā devatāḥ — These ten powers of fire are the goddesses of the inner ten
Five foodsChewed, eaten, sucked, licked and drunk
MeaningBreathing and digesting are worship going on all the time

ZAPPY: Digestion is worship?

PROF. BOLT: Every meal, in this reading, is an offering into her fire. Eat accordingly.

See: Āvaraṇa 5 · Āvaraṇa 6

The outer ring of ten triangles holds ten goddesses beginning with Sarvasiddhipradā. Sūtra 16 matches them with the ten vital airs: the five main airs and five minor ones.

Sūtras 17 and 18 describe how, joined with these airs, the digestive fire works in five ways, and five further powers digest the five kinds of food: what is chewed, eaten, sucked, licked and drunk.

Sūtra 19 calls these ten powers of fire the goddesses of the inner ring of ten, beginning with Sarvajñā. Breathing and digesting become worship going on all the time.

ENCLOSURE 7: THE PAIRS AND THE GUṆAS

Sūtra 20
Sūtra 20śītoṣṇa-sukha-duḥkhecchāḥ sattvaṃ rajas tamaḥ — Cold, heat, pleasure, pain, desire, and the three guṇas
AreThe eight goddesses of speech, Vaśinī and her sisters
VāgdevatāsSaid to have composed the Lalitā Sahasranāma
RingThe eight triangles
MeaningThe swings of experience are where speech and praise arise
LinkOur Sahasranāma guide tells their story

ZAPPY: Why put pain beside the goddesses of speech?

PROF. BOLT: Because what we feel is what we speak from. Turned to her, even pain becomes praise.

See: Āvaraṇa 7

The eight triangles of the seventh enclosure hold the eight goddesses of speech, beginning with Vaśinī. Tradition says they composed the Lalitā Sahasranāma at her command.

Sūtra 20 matches them with cold, heat, pleasure, pain, desire, and the three guṇas of sattva, rajas and tamas.

The pairing suggests that our feelings and moods are where speech arises; turned toward her, they become praise.

THE FOUR WEAPONS

Sūtras 21–24
Sūtra 21śabdāditanmātrāḥ pañca puṣpabāṇāḥ — The five subtle elements are the five flower arrows
Sūtra 22mana ikṣudhanuḥ — The mind is the sugarcane bow
Sūtra 23rāgaḥ pāśaḥ — Attachment is the noose
Sūtra 24dveṣo ’ṅkuśaḥ — Aversion is the goad
Subtle elementsSound, touch, form, taste and smell
MeaningWhat binds you is held in her hands

ZAPPY: The goddess holds my attachments?

PROF. BOLT: As her noose and goad. Held by her, they steer you home instead of dragging you away.

Lalitā holds four weapons: five flower arrows, a sugarcane bow, a noose and a goad. Sūtras 21 to 24 read them inwardly.

The arrows are the five subtle elements, sound, touch, form, taste and smell; the bow is the mind that shoots them toward objects; the noose is attachment; the goad is aversion.

This is the reading our Lalitā Tripurasundarī guide follows. What usually binds us is pictured as held in her hands.

ENCLOSURE 8: THE TRIANGLE

Sūtra 25
Sūtra 25avyakta-mahad-ahaṅkārāḥ kāmeśvarī-vajreśvarī-bhagamālinyaḥ — The unmanifest, the great principle and the ego are the three goddesses of the triangle
KāmeśvarīThe unmanifest
VajreśvarīMahat, cosmic intelligence
BhagamālinīAhaṅkāra, the sense of “I”
SāṅkhyaThe first three principles of creation
LinkKāmeśvarī and Bhagamālinī are also Nityās 1 and 2

ZAPPY: The ego is a goddess too?

PROF. BOLT: At the triangle, closest to the centre. Even the sense of ‘I’ is her, one step from the bindu.

See: Āvaraṇa 8

The central triangle holds three goddesses: Kāmeśvarī, Vajreśvarī and Bhagamālinī. Sūtra 25 matches them with the first three principles of creation in Sāṅkhya: the unmanifest, mahat (cosmic intelligence) and ahaṅkāra (the sense of “I”).

Kāmeśvarī and Bhagamālinī are also the first two Tithi Nityās, linking this enclosure to the cycle of the moon.

Even the ego stands here, one step from the centre, as a goddess.

THE CENTRE

Sūtras 26–28
Sūtra 26nirupādhikasaṃvid eva kāmeśvaraḥ — Awareness free of all limits is Kāmeśvara
Sūtra 27sadānandapūrṇaḥ svātmaiva paradevatā lalitā — One’s own Self, full of bliss, is the supreme Goddess Lalitā
Sūtra 28lauhityam etasya sarvasya vimarśaḥ — The redness of all this is vimarśa, self-reflecting awareness
Śiva and ŚaktiPure light, and its power to know itself
RedLalitā’s colour, read as awareness turned toward itself
The pointThe bindu is you

ZAPPY: Lalitā is me?

PROF. BOLT: Your own Self, full of bliss. That’s what sūtra 27 says, plainly.

See: Āvaraṇa 9 · Kāmeśvara

At the centre, sūtras 26 to 28 make the Upaniṣad’s boldest statements. Awareness free of all limits is Kāmeśvara. One’s own Self, full of bliss, is the supreme Goddess Lalitā.

And the red colour that pervades everything is vimarśa, awareness reflecting on itself. In Śrīvidyā thought, Śiva is pure light (prakāśa) and Śakti its power to know itself (vimarśa).

The bindu, in other words, is not a point on a diagram. It is the one reading it.

THE INNER PŪJĀ

Sūtras 29–32
Sūtra 29ananyacittatvena ca siddhiḥ — Attainment is a mind that sees nothing other
Sūtra 30bhāvanāyāḥ kriyā upacāraḥ — The acts of contemplation are the offerings
Sūtra 31ahaṃ tvam asti nāsti … vikalpānām ātmani vilāpanaṃ homaḥ — Dissolving “I, you, is, is not, must, must not” into the Self is the fire-offering
Sūtra 32bhāvanāviṣayāṇām abhedabhāvanaṃ tarpaṇam — Seeing all objects of contemplation as one is the libation
CommentaryEach of the sixteen offerings is given an inner meaning
Our ruleMeanings only; no rite is described

ZAPPY: What do I offer into the fire?

PROF. BOLT: Your opinions. ‘I’, ‘you’, ‘should’, ‘shouldn’t’: sūtra 31 burns the lot.

Sūtras 29 to 32 turn the parts of worship inward. Attainment is a mind that sees nothing other; the offerings are acts of contemplation; the fire-offering is dissolving all the mind’s distinctions, “I, you, is, is not, must, must not”, into the Self; the libation is seeing all objects of contemplation as one.

The commentary goes through the sixteen traditional offerings and gives each an inner meaning: bathing, clothing, flowers, light, food, praise, circumambulation and salutation all become ways of resting in the Self.

In keeping with this series’ rule, we give the principle and not the detail of any rite.

TIME AND THE NITYĀS

Sūtras 33–35
Sūtra 33pañcadaśatithirūpeṇa kālasya pariṇāmāvalokanam — Watching time change as the fifteen lunar days
Fifteen daysThe fifteen Nityās, round Lalitā, the sixteenth
Sūtra 34evaṃ muhūrtatritayaṃ … jīvanmukto bhavati — One devoted to this, even for a short while, is freed in life
ŚivayogīSuch a person is called a Śiva-yogī
Sūtra 35kādimatenāntaścakrabhāvanāḥ pratipāditāḥ — The inner contemplations of the cakra, by the Kādi school, are taught
Close“Whoever knows this…”

ZAPPY: Free in a few hours?

PROF. BOLT: Not by the clock. When the seeing is complete, it doesn’t need a lifetime.

See: The Tithi Nityās

Sūtra 33 widens the contemplation to time: one watches time change as the fifteen lunar days, which in Śrīvidyā are the fifteen Nityā goddesses round Lalitā, the sixteenth.

Sūtra 34 says that one who is absorbed in this contemplation, even for a short while, becomes free in this life, and is called a Śiva-yogī.

The last sūtra names the teaching as the inner contemplation of the cakra in the Kādi school, and ends with the traditional “whoever knows this”.

THE WHOLE MAP

Body and yantra side by side
Outside inFrom moods and faults at the gate to pure awareness at the centre
Gross to subtleTissues, senses, actions, channels, breath, fire, feelings, principles, Self
Every partIs a goddess; none is left out
UseRead the āvaraṇa guides with this map beside them
PurposeTo see the body as sacred and the Goddess as near
NotA medical or anatomical claim

ZAPPY: Do I have to memorise all that?

PROF. BOLT: No. Remember the direction: from the outside in, all the way to you.

Taken together, the Upaniṣad moves from the outside in. At the gate stand our moods and faults; then senses and actions; then channels, breath and fire; then feelings; then the first principles of creation; and at the centre, awareness itself.

Every part of a person is included, and every part is a goddess.

The correspondences are a traditional contemplation, not anatomy. They are meant to make the body sacred and the Goddess near.

WHAT IT TEACHES

For the reader
NearnessThe Goddess is not far; she is the body and the Self
Nothing excludedEven anger and ego are her attendants
InwardOuter worship ripens into contemplation
OnenessKnower, knowing and known are one
FreedomIn life, through seeing
GentleNo one is asked to deny the body

ZAPPY: What’s the one thing to remember?

PROF. BOLT: Sūtra 27: your own Self, full of bliss, is Lalitā.

The Bhāvanopaniṣad teaches nearness. The Goddess is not far away in a jewelled city; the city is the body and the Goddess is the Self.

It excludes nothing: anger, ego and digestion are all given a place in the yantra. And it shows how outer worship ripens into contemplation.

Its teaching matches the Tripurā Rahasya, which says the same thing through stories.

Prakāśa and vimarśa, light and self-reflection, are the two aspects of awareness in Śrīvidyā and Kashmir Śaiva thought. Kāmeśvara is prakāśa; Lalitā is vimarśa; the Upaniṣad’s red colour is vimarśa’s mark.
