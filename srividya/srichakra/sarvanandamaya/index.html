Sarvānandamaya

The bindu at the heart of the Śrīcakra.

Where it sits

Sarvānandamaya is the ninth and innermost enclosure. Its geometry is the bindu, a single point inside the central triangle. A drawn dot gives the eye a place to rest, while the point it represents has no area. The triangle around it is the eighth enclosure.

Presiding deity and attendants

Mahātripurasundarī presides at the centre. The Khaḍgamālā guide also names the Parāpararahasya Yoginī and addresses Lalitā by the honorific Śrī Śrī Mahābhaṭṭārikā. In the image of Lalitā with Kāmeśvara, the pair makes visible the unity of Śakti and Śiva.

The name means “consisting of all bliss.” The bindu can be read in two directions: as the point from which the diagram unfolds, and as the point to which the sequence of enclosures returns. The Bhāvanopaniṣad gives this inward reading a contemplative dimension by relating the diagram to awareness.

What it signifies

The royal imagery of the Lalitopākhyāna and the central point of the Śrīcakra speak in different forms. One shows the Goddess enthroned, while the other marks her presence with the simplest possible figure. Read both alongside the Foundations guide on Śiva and Śakti.
