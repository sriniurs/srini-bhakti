Śrīcakra · Āvaraṇa 7 of 9

Sarvarogahara

The eight triangles and the goddesses of speech.

Where it sits

Sarvarogahara is the seventh enclosure. Its eight small triangles sit between the inner ring of ten triangles and the single central triangle. When reading the diagram, count eight triangular compartments, distinct from the eight lotus petals farther out.

Presiding deity and attendants

Tripurāsiddhā is the presiding form of Lalitā. The enclosure’s attendants are the Rahasya Yoginīs, the eight Vāgdevīs or goddesses of speech.

Read the names in the order used by our Khaḍgamālā guide: Vaśinī, Kāmeśvarī, Modinī, Vimalā, Aruṇā, Jayinī, Sarveśvarī, and Kaulinī. The Kāmeśvarī here belongs to the group of speech goddesses. The same name also appears in the distinct group of lunar Nityās.

What it signifies

The name means “remover of all ills.” The Bhāvanopaniṣad reads the eight goddesses through pairs of experience such as heat and cold, pleasure and pain. Here, speech matters in particular: the Sahasranāma tradition credits Vaśinī and her companions with composing the thousand names.
