Start Here

A reading path and quick reference for Śrī Vidyā.

A reading path

  1. Meet Lalitā and learn the basics. Read What is Śrī Vidyā? and Lalitā Tripurasundarī.
  2. Get oriented in the diagram. Open the Śrīcakra overview, then use the table below to compare enclosures.
  3. Read the story and the names together. Begin with Lalitopākhyāna · 6 parts and Lalitā Sahasranāma · 10 articles. Use Devī Khaḍgamālā · 13 articles beside the enclosure guides.
  4. Explore the philosophical readings. Continue with Bhāvanopaniṣad, Saundaryalaharī · 5 articles, and Tripurā Rahasya · 4 articles.

Glossary

Āvaraṇa
An enclosure or layer of the Śrīcakra. The nine guides follow the order from the outer square to the centre.
Bindu
The central point, counted as the ninth enclosure.
Cakreśvarī
The presiding form of the Goddess for a particular enclosure.
Bhāvanā
Contemplation or sustained imaginative identification. In the Bhāvanopaniṣad, the body and Śrīcakra are read in relation to each other.
Dīkṣā
Initiation into a lineage.
Nityā
One of the goddesses associated with the lunar days in Śrī Vidyā.
Sampradāya
A lineage or stream of transmitted teaching.
Stotra
A hymn of praise. Sahasranāma and triśatī refer here to hymns of a thousand and three hundred names, respectively.
Vāgdevī
A goddess of speech. The eight Vāgdevīs are associated with the seventh enclosure.
Yantra
A sacred diagram. The Śrīcakra is the central diagram of the tradition.
Yoginī
A term with several meanings. In the enclosure guides it appears in the names or classifications of attendant goddesses.

Where accounts vary

The Foundations introduction discusses Samaya and Kaula. These labels identify streams of interpretation and practice whose boundaries can vary. The table follows Bhakti’s sequence. Spelling, deity order, orientation, and finer correspondences may differ in particular editions and lineages.

Bhakti’s public guides cover history, stories, texts, and symbolism. A field study of contemporary Śrīvidyā offers further context on variation among practitioners.

Sources and editions

  • Devī Khaḍgamālā. Bhakti’s source note identifies Srini’s Śrī Devī Khaḍgamālā booklet as the basis for its names and placement of the deities.
  • Lalitā Sahasranāma and Saundaryalaharī. The Bhakti study notes use the Sanskrit texts published by Vaidika Vignanam for the Lalitā Sahasranāma and Vaidika Vignanam for the Saundaryalaharī. The English explanations are Bhakti’s own.
  • Lalitopākhyāna. Bhakti’s retelling identifies G. V. Tagare’s translation of the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa as a source. The Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams edition is a separate online reading.
  • Bhāvanopaniṣad. Bhakti follows the supplied Shastra Nethralaya edition with the Aruṇalaharī commentary. See the article’s source note. Compare Vaidika Vignanam’s Sanskrit transcription. Passage numbering can differ.
  • Tripurā Rahasya. Bhakti follows the chapter numbers of Swami Sri Ramananda Saraswathi’s Sri Ramanasramam translation of the Jñāna Khaṇḍa. See the edition note in Part 1.
  • Further reading. André Padoux and Roger-Orphé Jeanty’s The Heart of the Yogini is an English translation of the Yoginīhṛdaya. The Bhakti texts guide describes additional works and commentaries.
