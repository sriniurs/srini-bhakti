HOW THIS WORKS: A reading path through this library for anyone starting out: ten pages in order, from lighting a lamp at home to reading a temple wall, with what each teaches, why it comes at that point, and where to go after.

WHERE WE ARE

This library holds hundreds of illustrated guides. If you are new, that is too many doors at once. Here is a path: ten pages, in an order that builds, so that each one makes the next easier.

Read one a week, or one a day. Each step below says what the page teaches, why it comes at that point, and what to notice. For quick answers to common questions, keep Questions Newcomers Ask open alongside.

STEP 1 · A SIMPLE HOME PŪJĀ

Read: A Simple Home Pūjā

What it teaches. How to set up a small shrine and offer a lamp, flowers, water and a few words, the sixteen traditional offerings in simple form.

Why first. Hindu worship is practised before it is studied. A child learns to fold its hands before it learns a single doctrine. Start by doing.

Notice. Each offering is the welcome given to an honoured guest: water for the feet, a seat, food, light. The whole rite is hospitality.

Then try. Light a lamp each evening for a week before moving on.

ZAPPY: I can do this today?

PROF. BOLT: Tonight, Zappy. That’s the point of step one.

Begin by doing.

STEP 2 · HOW TO READ IAST

Read: How to Read IAST

What it teaches. The marks this library puts on letters, and how to pronounce every Sanskrit word and verse.

Why second. Every page from here on uses IAST. Five minutes now saves confusion on every page later.

Notice. The difference between ś and s, between ṭ and t: Sanskrit is precise, and the marks keep that precision.

Then try. Read the name Kṛṣṇa aloud correctly, and Śiva, and Gaṅgā.

ZAPPY: Kṛṣṇa, not Krishna?

PROF. BOLT: Same person, Zappy, spelled so you can hear him exactly.

Learn the key.

STEP 3 · WHO IS VIṢṆU

Read: Who Is Viṣṇu

What it teaches. Viṣṇu the preserver: his form, his consort Lakṣmī, Garuḍa, his ten avatāras and the traditions that worship him as supreme.

Why here. Rāma and Kṛṣṇa, the heroes of India’s two great epics, are Viṣṇu’s avatāras. Knowing him opens most of the stories.

Notice. The idea of avatāra: God coming down whenever dharma declines (Gītā 4.7–8).

Then. The Daśāvatāra series, one avatāra at a time.

ZAPPY: Why does he keep coming back?

PROF. BOLT: Because the world keeps needing him, Zappy. That’s the avatāra idea.

The god who comes down.

STEP 4 · WHO IS ŚIVA

Read: Who Is Śiva

What it teaches. Śiva the yogī, the dancer, the destroyer and renewer; the liṅga; Pārvatī, Gaṇeśa and Skanda; the traditions that see him as supreme.

Why here. Most temples in Karnataka and the South are Śiva’s, and the Pradoṣa, Śivarātri and Kārtika calendars are his.

Notice. How one god holds opposites: ascetic and householder, fierce and gentle.

Then. Śiva’s Family and Symbols and Kāla Bhairava.

ZAPPY: Destroyer sounds scary.

PROF. BOLT: He clears the old so the new can grow, Zappy. Every spring owes him something.

The yogī who dances.

STEP 5 · DURGĀ AND PĀRVATĪ

Read: Durgā and Pārvatī

What it teaches. The Devī, the Goddess, as Pārvatī the mother and Durgā the warrior who slays Mahiṣa; the Śākta view of her as the supreme power.

Why here. With Viṣṇu and Śiva known, the Devī completes the three great streams of Hindu worship.

Notice. Śakti: power, energy, the active side of the divine without which, the Śāktas say, Śiva himself could not move.

Then. Devī Māhātmya, Navarātri, and Lakṣmī and Sarasvatī.

ZAPPY: The Goddess is the most powerful?

PROF. BOLT: In the Śākta view, yes, Zappy. Other traditions honour her as consort or as power.

The power behind all.

STEP 6 · WHO IS GAṆEŚA

Read: Who Is Gaṇeśa

What it teaches. Gaṇeśa’s birth, his elephant head, his mouse, and why every rite begins with him.

Why here. Now that you know his parents, Śiva and Pārvatī, his stories make full sense; and he will be invoked at the start of almost everything you read next.

Notice. The remover of obstacles is also the placer of obstacles, so that we pause before acting.

Then. The full Gaṇeśa collection: forms, stories, hymns, temples and festivals.

ZAPPY: Why does everything start with him?

PROF. BOLT: He is the lord of beginnings, Zappy. You ask him first so the rest goes smoothly.

Begin with Gaṇeśa.

STEP 7 · THE BHAGAVAD GĪTĀ

Read: The Bhagavad Gītā: Overview

What it teaches. The Gītā in brief: its setting at Kurukṣetra, its eighteen chapters, its paths of action, knowledge, devotion and meditation.

Why here. With the gods met, this is the central teaching. Nearly every tradition treats the Gītā as foundational.

Notice. Arjuna’s question is a real one: what should I do when every choice costs something?

Then. One chapter a week, starting with chapter 2; and Gītā Jayantī.

ZAPPY: Is it hard?

PROF. BOLT: Some verses, Zappy. But many are as clear as advice from a friend.

The teaching at the centre.

STEP 8 · THE GĀYATRĪ MANTRA

Read: The Gāyatrī Mantra

What it teaches. The Veda’s best-known prayer, to the Sun as Savitṛ, asking for a stirred and clear mind; its words, metre and meaning.

Why here. After the Gītā’s teaching, a single verse to hold each day. It connects everything to the Veda itself.

Notice. It asks for nothing material: only that the mind be awakened.

Then. Ratha Saptamī for the Sun, and Upākarma for the Vedic year.

ZAPPY: Just one verse?

PROF. BOLT: One verse, Zappy, said by millions every dawn for three thousand years.

One verse for every day.

STEP 9 · THE PAÑCĀṄGA

Read: The Pañcāṅga

What it teaches. The five limbs of the Hindu almanac, how months and fortnights work, and a table of every festival by month and tithi.

Why here. Now the festivals will make sense: you will know why Dīpāvali moves and why Ekādaśī comes twice a month.

Notice. Time is sacred: each day has its own quality, and the year is a cycle of stories.

Then. Keep the next festival using the festivals collection.

ZAPPY: Do I need to read an almanac?

PROF. BOLT: Just know how it works, Zappy. The calendar becomes a story you live through each year.

Time as a story.

STEP 10 · HOW TO READ A HINDU TEMPLE

Read: How to Read a Hindu Temple

What it teaches. A twelve-part series: the gateway and the sanctum, the skyline, the hall, the tank, the chariot, carvings, paintings and inscriptions.

Why last. A temple gathers everything you have read: the gods, their stories, the calendar, the rites. Now you can read its walls.

Notice. The temple is a map: you walk from the world to the centre, from the many to the one.

Then. Visit our Around Mysuru, Karnataka and Jyotirliṅga guides.

ZAPPY: I can read a temple now?

PROF. BOLT: You can start, Zappy. Every visit after this will show you something new.

Read the stones.
