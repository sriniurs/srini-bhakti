TEMPLES OF ANDHRA PRADESH · PART 8 OF 10 · RATNAGIRI

Annavaram Satyanārāyaṇa

The temple of the vrata · explained and illustrated

The temple of the true promise

HOW THIS WORKS: Zappy and Prof. Bolt climb Ratnagiri at Annavaram, where a temple built as a chariot holds Satyanārāyaṇa, and families come in their thousands to perform his vrata, the worship of truth.

WHERE WE ARE

In the Kakinada district of coastal Andhra, on a hill called Ratnagiri above the little river Pampā, stands the temple of Śrī Vīra Veṅkaṭa Satyanārāyaṇa Svāmī at Annavaram.

Across India, families perform the Satyanārāyaṇa vrata at home: a worship of Viṣṇu as the Lord of Truth, with a story and a sweet prasādam. Annavaram is the great temple of this vrata.

Every day hundreds of couples perform it here, and on auspicious days thousands. Newlyweds especially come to begin married life with it.

ZAPPY: Is Satyanārāyaṇa a different god?

PROF. BOLT: He is Viṣṇu, worshipped as Satya, Truth. At Annavaram he is shown with Śiva and Lakṣmī, a rare grouping.

ZAPPY: Why do newlyweds come?

PROF. BOLT: The vrata is a vow of truth and blessing. Beginning married life with it, at its own temple, is a strong tradition in Andhra.

HOW THE TEMPLE BEGAN

A dream and a hill

The story

The hill. The temple’s story says the hill Ratnagiri was a devotee, Ratnākara, son of the mountain Meru, who did penance so that Viṣṇu would live on him.
The dream. In 1891, temple tradition says, the god appeared in a dream to local devotees and told them where his image lay on the hill.
The image found. The image was found and installed on the hill, and worship began in a small shrine.
Growth. As the fame of the vrata grew, the temple was enlarged, and the present chariot-shaped temple was built in the 20th century.
Young and old. So Annavaram is a young temple compared with most in this series, but it has become one of the most visited in Andhra.

ZAPPY: A temple from 1891 in the same series as Tirumala?

PROF. BOLT: Age is not everything. What matters is the faith of millions, and Annavaram has that.

Annavaram: a young temple of an old vow.

A TEMPLE BUILT AS A CHARIOT

Wheels at the corners

Chariot form. The main temple is designed as a chariot, with wheels at the four corners of its base: the god travels to his devotees.

Two levels. The building has two levels. The main sanctum is on the upper level, with a lower level below.

A meaning. The chariot recalls the Kaṭha Upaniṣad’s image of the body as a chariot, with the Self as its master.

Compare. Compare with the chariot temples of How to Read Part 10, Konārak and Dārāsuram: an old idea in a modern building.

Around it. Around the main temple are halls for the vrata, the kitchen, offices, and shrines of other deities.

ZAPPY: Is it old like Konārak?

PROF. BOLT: No, it is modern. But it follows the same idea: the temple as the god’s vehicle.

THREE IN ONE SANCTUM

Satyanārāyaṇa, Śiva and Lakṣmī

Satyanārāyaṇa. In the centre stands Viṣṇu as Satyanārāyaṇa, the Lord of Truth.

Śiva. On one side is Śiva, as a liṅga. Devotees see this as a sign that Hari and Hara are one.

Anantalakṣmī Satyavatī. On the other is the goddess, called Anantalakṣmī Satyavatī.

Brahmā below. Temple tradition says Brahmā is present in the lower level, under the main image, so that the three gods of the trinity are worshipped together.

A unifying temple. This makes Annavaram dear to Vaiṣṇavas and Śaivas alike.

ZAPPY: Why put Viṣṇu and Śiva together?

PROF. BOLT: Many Andhra and Tamil temples blend traditions. Here the god of truth is shown as one with Śiva.

THE SATYANĀRĀYAṆA VRATA

How families perform it

The vow. The Satyanārāyaṇa vrata is a worship of Viṣṇu as Truth, described in the Skanda Purāṇa (Revā Khaṇḍa) as giving peace and prosperity.

The ritual. A couple or family sits before a kalaśa and an image of the god. A priest leads the worship: Gaṇeśa first, the navagrahas, then Satyanārāyaṇa.

Five stories. The priest reads five short stories of people who kept or broke the vow, and what followed. The lesson: keep your word.

The prasādam. The vrata ends with a sweet prasādam of wheat (or semolina), ghee, sugar and banana, shared with all.

At Annavaram. The temple has large halls where many vratas run at once, in batches, with tickets for different sizes of ritual. On busy days, thousands are performed.

ZAPPY: Can I do it at home?

PROF. BOLT: Yes, and most families do: on a full moon, a housewarming, a birthday. Annavaram is where people come to do it at its source.

THE WHEAT PRASĀDAM

In a leaf, still warm

Godhuma prasādam. Annavaram’s prasādam is a sweet made from wheat rava, ghee and sugar, flavoured with cardamom, cooked in the temple kitchen.

In a leaf. It is packed in leaves, often a leaf of the local tree, and handed out warm. The leaf gives it a special aroma.

Famous. Pilgrims carry it home for family and neighbours. For many, the taste of Annavaram is this prasādam.

Like the vrata. It is close to the vrata’s own prasādam, a sign of the temple’s focus.

Buy at the counter. Buy prasādam only at official temple counters.

ZAPPY: Is it different from Tirupati’s laddu?

PROF. BOLT: Very different: soft, warm and spooned rather than a ball. Each temple’s prasādam has its own fame.

THE DIVINE WEDDING

Kalyāṇotsavam in Vaiśākha

When. The annual Kalyāṇotsavam, the divine wedding of Satyanārāyaṇa and Anantalakṣmī, is celebrated in Vaiśākha (April–May), around Ekādaśī of the bright fortnight.

Festival days. The wedding is part of a festival of several days, with processions on vehicles.

Kārttika. In the month of Kārttika (October–November) vratas multiply, as it is a month of special devotion.

Weddings below. On auspicious wedding days, many couples marry in the town of Annavaram and then climb the hill.

Theppa on the Pampā. Some festivals include a float or bathing ritual at the Pampā river below.

ZAPPY: Do people really marry there?

PROF. BOLT: Yes, many weddings take place in halls near the temple, especially for families from the region.

THE PAMPĀ AND THE HILL

Steps, river and town

Steps and road. A long flight of steps climbs Ratnagiri, with a ghat road for buses and cars.

The Pampā. The small river Pampā flows at the foot of the hill; a reservoir on it adds to the scenery.

A town of the temple. The town of Annavaram has grown around the temple: lodges, choultries, vrata supplies and wedding halls.

Other shrines. On the hill are shrines of other deities, and the temple runs a Vedic school and charitable services.

National highway. Annavaram lies on the main highway and railway between Visakhapatnam and Rajahmundry, so pilgrims stop on journeys.

ZAPPY: Pampā like Sabarimala?

PROF. BOLT: Same name, different river. The name recalls the sacred lake of the Rāmāyaṇa.

A TEMPLE OF TRUTH

What the vrata teaches

Satya. The vrata’s message is simple: satya, truth, and keeping one’s promises.

The stories. In the five stories a poor man, a woodcutter, a merchant, a king and others prosper when they keep the vow and suffer when they forget it.

The prasādam. One story warns against refusing the prasādam: the shared sweet is part of the vow.

Community. The vrata is a family and community ritual: neighbours are invited and share the prasādam.

A modern devotion. Its huge popularity in modern Andhra shows how an old Purāṇic vow can become a living practice.

ZAPPY: Is the vrata only about prosperity?

PROF. BOLT: Prosperity follows, the stories say, but the heart of it is truthfulness and gratitude.

HOW TO VISIT

Practical notes
Getting there. Annavaram has a railway station on the Visakhapatnam–Vijayawada line and lies on the national highway; Rajahmundry and Visakhapatnam have airports.
Booking the vrata. Vrata tickets are sold at the temple and online through official channels; choose the type and time.
Timings. The temple opens early. Vratas run in batches through the day.
Dress. Traditional dress is preferred for the vrata: dhoti or kurta for men, sari or churidar for women.
Busy days. Kārttika Mondays, full moons and wedding seasons are very busy.

ZAPPY: How long does the vrata take?

PROF. BOLT: About an hour, including the stories and prasādam. Allow time for queues.
