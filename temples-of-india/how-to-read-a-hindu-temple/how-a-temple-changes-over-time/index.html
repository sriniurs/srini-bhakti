A SITE HAS MORE THAN ONE DATE

The date announced at a temple entrance can be the date associated with a founding patron or consecration. Start by asking which architectural element or event that date identifies. A later hall, painted phase or enclosure can be centuries younger than the shrine. Treat the age of the temple as a question about relationships between components.

ZAPPY: If the inscription gives the date, why can't I put it on every building?

PROF. BOLT: Write down what the inscription dates. A gift, a shrine and a later repair are three different events.

MAKE A SEPARATE MARK FOR EVIDENCE AND INFERENCE

When a later-looking wall meets an earlier-looking surface, identify the physical join on your drawing. Then mark the interpretation in a separate annotation. Similar colours can conceal construction phases and different colours may reflect weather or cleaning. IGNCA's mapped documentation at Thanjavur illustrates why inscriptions and their architectural positions should be studied together. [1]

CHANGES IN USE LEAVE THEIR OWN TRACES

A closed stair, barrier or covering may tell you about maintenance or present conditions. It rarely tells you on its own what happened in the past. Ask whether you are reading a fabric change, an access change or a shift in ritual practice. Those can occur independently. Build your time line from records that say which change took place and when.

CONDITION AND INTEGRITY ARE DIFFERENT QUESTIONS

UNESCO describes Rani-ki-Vav as retaining key components even though some pavilion storeys are missing. A site can communicate its type and historic design through surviving relationships. Conversely, a tidy surface may cover important evidence. Record the survival of parts and how legible the ensemble is as two distinct observations. [3]

A LIVING PLACE HAS PEOPLE RESPONSIBLE FOR IT

The World Heritage description of Ramappa names the Archaeological Survey of India and consultation with religious and community authorities. For a reference article, verify who documents or manages each aspect before treating a conservation statement as the whole account of modern use. Add dated observations and use the official documentation to explain why a feature may be temporarily unavailable to inspect. [2]

FIELD EXERCISE

A sequence you can follow on a drawing or at the site

  2. 02Later addition
  3. 03Recorded condition
  4. 04Current observation

Try a phase drawing with distinct outlines for the earliest unit you can document, dated later additions and elements with no secure date. Leave the last category visible. An honest blank can help the next researcher see which claims require a photograph, an inscription or a conservation report.

A replacement can make a building usable while removing evidence of how an older piece was fitted. A surviving fragment can preserve that evidence even when it no longer works in place. Record both physical context and continuing use before assessing the significance of a change.

ZAPPY: Is the newest-looking stone always new?

PROF. BOLT: Weathering and cleaning can alter appearances. A dated repair record or a documented join is stronger evidence than colour alone.
