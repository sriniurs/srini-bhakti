THE WALL IS PART OF THE DOCUMENT

Start by writing down where an inscription sits. The north wall of the principal shrine, the face of a plinth and the side of a gateway are different contexts. IGNCA's account of inscription 1 at Thanjavur describes nine sections on the north wall and four on the west. A visitor looking at one panel is seeing only part of the record. Search with the editor's inscription number as well as the temple's present name. [1][2]

ZAPPY: Why can't I read the whole inscription from one photograph?

PROF. BOLT: First check how many walls it occupies. A close-up can make a fragment look complete.

SEPARATE OPENING PRAISE FROM ADMINISTRATIVE DETAIL

A record may begin with royal praise or a formula before it moves to what was ordered, given or recorded. Find where the subject changes. Mark names, dates and terms of obligation separately. At Thanjavur the account identifies the temple with Rājarāja and its name Rājarājēśvara. A historical name in a text gives you a way to recognise references that modern signs spell differently. [1]

HOW TO READ A GIFT FOR WORSHIP

If a record mentions a recurring service, ask what was given, who was responsible, and how the service was to be maintained. A lamp needs fuel after the dedication ceremony. Land, animals, money or produce can relate to recurring supplies in different ways. Use the terms in the edition before inferring a financing mechanism. A grant describes what someone arranged or prescribed; evidence of repeated performance would require another record or independent testimony.

READ THE GATEWAY WITH THE SHRINE WALL

IGNCA's gateway documentation identifies grants connected with annual festivals and daily services. Those two time scales lead to different research questions. A festival concentrates resources and labour around an occasion. Daily service calls for a repeating supply. Compare what the gateway record says with inscriptions mapped elsewhere before attributing every grant to the same donor, date or purpose. [2][3]

MAKE A CITATION THAT SOMEONE CAN RETRACE

Record the temple, architectural location, inscription number, edition and section or line where available. If a reading is disputed, keep alternatives attached to the visible or transcribed words that generated them. A translation can be accessible while preserving technical terms whose exact local value is uncertain. In a reference article, write the inference in a separate sentence so another reader can check the step between text and interpretation.

FIELD EXERCISE

A sequence you can follow on a drawing or at the site
  1. 01Find the wall
  2. 02Locate the record
  3. 03Check the edition
  4. 04Read its terms
  5. 05State the limit

Read the printed edition beside the mapped wall, rather than working from an isolated quotation. Copy the clause that identifies the act and the clause that identifies the beneficiary. They may be separated by titles or qualifiers. Paraphrase the two clauses in ordinary language, then ask what falls outside their wording.

Imagine an entry that assigns a recurring offering to the income from a grant. To establish whether it was supplied over ten years, you would look for accounts, renewals, complaints or later references. The grant alone tells you something narrower and valuable: the service was provisioned in the terms written there.

ZAPPY: Which part of a royal inscription is the most useful?

PROF. BOLT: It depends on the question. A donor's title can help date a record, while a measure or recurring obligation helps explain the resources behind worship.
