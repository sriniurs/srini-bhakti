ŚRĪ MAHĀBAḶĒŚVARA · RĀVAṆA AND THE ĀTMALIṄGA · A REFERENCE GUIDE

GŌKARṆA

The liṅga that even Rāvaṇa could not lift

TEMPLES OF KARNATAKA

FOREWORD

On the coast of Uttara Kannaḍa, a liṅga sits in a square stone base with only its tip showing. Tradition says it is Śiva’s ātmaliṅga, his very self, won by Rāvaṇa and lost on this beach.

Rāvaṇa pulled until he fell, and named the god Mahābala, “of great strength”. The pieces he tore away are said to have made four more shrines along the coast.

This guide visits the temple of Mahābaḷēśvara: its liṅga and its forty-year unveiling, the Kadamba temple, the holy tank and the rites for the dead, the poets who praised it, and how it is run today.

Om Namaḥ Śivāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Isn’t this the Gaṇeśa story?

PROF. BOLT: From Gaṇeśa’s side, yes: the site has that full story. This guide is Śiva’s side: the liṅga, its temple and what happened after Rāvaṇa lost.

The essential facts on one page

PlaceGōkarṇa, Kumṭā tālūk, Uttara Kannaḍa, Karnataka
SettingOn the Arabian Sea, between the mouths of the Gaṅgāvaḷi and the Aghanāśinī
TempleDravidian, granite; ascribed to the Kadamba king Mayūravarma (Mayūraśarma), 4th century, much of it later
The liṅgaSet in a square pīṭha; only its top shows through an opening
TitlesBhūkailāsa, Kailāsa on earth; Dakṣiṇa Kāśī; one of Karnataka’s seven mukti kṣētras
Hymned inThe Tamil Tēvāram; Kālidāsa’s Raghuvaṁśa
Great festivalMahāśivarātri, with a chariot procession

ZAPPY: Is it one of the twelve jyotirliṅgas?

PROF. BOLT: No, it isn’t on that list. Its claim is different and, to devotees, just as great: Śiva’s own ātmaliṅga.

WHERE IT IS

Between two rivers, facing the sea

The town. A temple town on the coast of Uttara Kannaḍa, in Kumṭā tālūk, with the sea on its west.

Two rivers. It lies between the mouths of the Gaṅgāvaḷi to the north and the Aghanāśinī to the south.

The temple. Mahābaḷēśvara faces the main beach; the Mahāgaṇapati shrine stands close by on the same street.

THE COW’S EAR

Gō + karṇa

🐄 The words

Gō, cow, and karṇa, ear. Gōkarṇa is “the cow’s ear”.

🗿 The liṅga’s shape

In one telling, Rāvaṇa’s twisting crushed the liṅga into the shape of a cow’s ear.

🌍 The earth’s ear

In another, Śiva rose from the earth, pictured as a cow, through her ear at this spot.

🌊 The land’s shape

Some point to the shape of the land between the two river mouths. The name is far older than all these explanations.

ZAPPY: Three reasons for one name?

PROF. BOLT: Old names collect stories. What they agree on is that the cow and the liṅga belong together here.

THE STORY IN BRIEF

How the ātmaliṅga came to the coast
The prizeRāvaṇa won Śiva’s ātmaliṅga by fierce penance at Kailāsa
The ruleWherever it was first set on the ground, there it would stay for ever
The trickViṣṇu dimmed the sun to make a false dusk, so Rāvaṇa had to stop for his evening rites
The boyGaṇeśa, as a cowherd boy, agreed to hold it, called Rāvaṇa three times and set it down
The resultThe liṅga rooted itself at Gōkarṇa

The full telling. Rāvaṇa’s mother, the penance, the false dusk, the boy and the blow are told step by step in Rāvaṇa and Gōkarṇa, and the boy-Gaṇeśa’s shrine in Gōkarṇa Mahāgaṇapati. This guide picks up where Rāvaṇa returns.

MAHĀBALA

The god of great strength
Too late. Rāvaṇa came back from his rites to find the liṅga already on the ground.
The pull. He seized it with all his arms and pulled. The earth shook, but the liṅga did not rise.
The fall. He strained until he fainted, beaten by a stone he had carried most of the way from Kailāsa.
The name. Coming round, he named the god Mahābala, “of great strength”: Mahābaḷēśvara, the Lord of Great Strength.
In anger. Unable to take the liṅga, he tore at its coverings and flung them away down the coast.

ZAPPY: The strongest king in the world, beaten by a liṅga?

PROF. BOLT: That is the point of the name. Rāvaṇa’s strength was real, and still it was nothing against Śiva’s. He was the first to praise it.

FIVE PLACES OF ONE LIṄGA

The pañca kṣētras of the coast
GōkarṇaThe liṅga itself: Mahābaḷēśvara, first and chief
SajjēśvaraThe case that held it, flung north to Sajjēśvara near Kāravāra
DhārēśvaraNear Kumṭā; said to be the string of the case, or a sharp-edged piece (dhārā, an edge)
GuṇavantēśvaraNear Iḍaguñji; the lid of the case
MurḍēśvaraOn Kanduka hill by the sea; the cloth that wrapped the liṅga. See Murḍēśvara

ZAPPY: Do pilgrims visit all five?

PROF. BOLT: Many do, as one pilgrimage along the coast. Murḍēśvara is famous; the others are small and quiet.

THE LIṄGA IN ITS PĪṬHA

What you can and can’t see

Enclosed. The ātmaliṅga stands inside a square stone base, a pīṭha of śāligrāma stone, with a small opening in the top. Through it, devotees see only the very top of the liṅga.

Within reach. Unlike many great shrines, devotees here may enter the sanctum and touch the liṅga through the opening.

ZAPPY: So no one sees the whole liṅga?

PROF. BOLT: Almost never. That is what makes the next page so special.

ONCE IN FORTY YEARS

The aṣṭabandha kumbhābhiṣēka
The seal. The liṅga is set in its base with aṣṭabandha, a binding paste of eight ingredients, the traditional way of fixing a sacred image in its seat.
Renewal. At long intervals the old seal is removed and renewed in a great reconsecration, the aṣṭabandha kumbhābhiṣēka.
The unveiling. Only then, about once in forty years, is the whole ātmaliṅga shown to devotees.
A lifetime’s chance. For most pilgrims, seeing the full liṅga is something that happens once in a life, if at all.

ZAPPY: Why seal it at all?

PROF. BOLT: Fixing an image firmly in its seat is part of consecration everywhere in the South. At Gōkarṇa the custom has a special edge: the liṅga stays hidden between renewals.

THE TEMPLE

Kadamba to Vijayanagara
4th c. Tradition and later accounts credit Mayūravarma (Mayūraśarma), founder of the Kadamba dynasty, with building a temple over the liṅga, dated to 345–365 CE.
1336–1646 Much of the present large complex belongs to the Vijayanagara period.
Later The Nāyakas of Keḷadi add to it.
1665 Śivājī worships at Mahābaḷēśvara after disbanding his army at Gōkarṇa.
1676 The English traveller John Fryer visits during Śivarātri and writes about the festival.
StyleDravidian, in granite
PlanA gōpura, halls and a circumambulatory path around the sanctum
FacesThe beach and the Arabian Sea

SHRINES OF THE TOWN

Gōkarṇa is more than one temple

🐘 Mahāgaṇapati

By custom, pilgrims bathe in the sea, worship the boy-Gaṇeśa first, and only then go to Mahābaḷēśvara.

🌺 Tāmragaurī

Pārvatī’s shrine, “the copper-hued Gaurī”, close to Mahābaḷēśvara.

🔥 Bhadrakāḷī

The fierce Goddess, worshipped as Umā, counted with the liṅga and the cow’s ear among Gōkarṇa’s sacred trio.

🕉️ And more

Ādi Gōkarṇēśvara, Caṇḍikēśvara, Gōkarṇanāyakī and Dattātrēya, among others.

The maṭha. Tradition says Ādi Śaṅkara founded a maṭha at Aśōke near Gōkarṇa, the origin of today’s Rāmacandrāpura maṭha, which takes the title of Gōkarṇa maṇḍalādhīśvara.

A PLACE OF RELEASE

Kōṭitīrtha and the rites for the dead

💧 Kōṭitīrtha

The large holy tank of the town, “the bathing place of a crore”, used for ritual bathing and for the rites for the dead.

🕯️ Rites for the dead

Many Karnataka families perform obsequies for their departed here, believing the place helps the dead to release.

🗺️ Seven mukti kṣētras

Gōkarṇa, Uḍupi, Kollūr, Subrahmaṇya, Kumbhāśi, Kōṭēśvara and Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa.

🏔️ Bhūkailāsa

“Kailāsa on earth”: Śiva’s own self is believed to be here, so the place is treated as his mountain home.

ZAPPY: Why would the dead go to Gōkarṇa?

PROF. BOLT: If Śiva’s own self is here, then rites done in his presence are held to carry the dead furthest. That belief keeps the town busy with families all year.

GŌKARṆA IN THE TEXTS

A very old holy place
RāmāyaṇaNames Gōkarṇa as a place of penance: Bhagīratha’s, to bring down the Gaṅgā, and that of Rāvaṇa and his brothers
MahābhārataLists Gōkarṇa among the holy places pilgrims visit
KālidāsaThe Raghuvaṁśa speaks of the sage Nārada travelling to serve the “Lord of Gōkarṇa” by the southern sea
TēvāramPraised by the Tamil Śaiva saints, Appar and Sambandar; one of the 275 Pāḍal Peṭra sthalams
TravellersJohn Fryer (1676) describes Śivarātri here

Far-famed. A Kannaḍa temple sung by Tamil saints and named by a Sanskrit poet: Gōkarṇa was a pan-Indian holy place long before its beaches became famous.

ŚIVARĀTRI

The great night

When. Mahāśivarātri, on the fourteenth night of the dark half of Māgha, about February–March: the great festival of Gōkarṇa.

The chariot. A ratha yātrā sets out from the Mahāgaṇapati temple at the end of the main street, known as Car Street.

The crowd. Pilgrims come from across Karnataka and beyond to bathe, worship and keep the night’s vigil.

WHO RUNS THE TEMPLE

A long dispute, briefly
2008 The Karnataka government removes the temple from the list of state-managed temples and hands its management to the Rāmacandrāpura maṭha.
2018 The Karnataka High Court quashes that order.
Apr 2021 Hearing the maṭha’s appeal, the Supreme Court places the temple under an overseeing committee chaired by retired Supreme Court judge Justice B. N. Śrīkṛṣṇa, as an interim arrangement.
2024 After the chairman reports deadlock in the committee, the Supreme Court gives him a veto over its decisions and orders it reconstituted.
Since Worship continues as before; the dispute over management remains before the court.

ZAPPY: Whose side is right?

PROF. BOLT: This guide doesn’t say. The dates and orders are facts; the arguments on each side belong to the court.
