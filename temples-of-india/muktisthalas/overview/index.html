SAPTA MUKTISTHALA · PARAŚURĀMA KṢETRA · AN OVERVIEW

THE SEVEN MUKTISTHALAS

Seven places of release on Paraśurāma’s coast

TEMPLES OF KARNATAKA

FOREWORD

Between the Western Ghāṭs and the Arabian Sea runs a strip of land that tradition says Paraśurāma won from the ocean with a throw of his axe. On it, coastal Karnataka counts seven holy places as Muktisthalas, places of mukti, release.

They are a remarkable set: a child Kṛṣṇa, a Goddess inside a liṅga, a serpent-lord Subrahmaṇya, a self-born Gaṇapati, a liṅga of ten million, Śiva and Viṣṇu in one stone, and Śiva’s own ātmaliṅga.

Each has its own full guide on this site. This overview shows them together: the land, the idea, the map, the gods, the year, and what holds the seven together.

Namō Bhārgavāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Seven places, seven different gods?

PROF. BOLT: That’s the beauty of it. The set isn’t built around one deity. It’s built around one land, and one hope: release.

The names. Muktisthala or mukti kṣētra, “place of release”; the set is also called the seven Paraśurāma kṣētras or puṇya kṣētras. No single fixed order is given in the sources.

ZAPPY: Five of seven in one district?

PROF. BOLT: Yes. Uḍupi district is the heart of the set, with Gōkarṇa to the north and Kukke inland to the south-east as its two ends.

LAND FROM THE SEA

Paraśurāma Kṣetra
No land of his own. Having given the whole earth away after his wars against tyrant kings, Paraśurāma had nowhere left to perform his rites.
The throw. He asked Varuṇa, lord of the sea, for land, and threw his axe from the Sahyādri hills; the sea drew back as far as it flew.
The coast. The land that rose runs, in the tradition, from Gōkarṇa to Kanyākumārī: Paraśurāma Kṣetra.
Seven places. On its northern part, coastal Karnataka, he is said to have established seven sacred places for release.

More on him. The full story of the sixth avatāra is in Paraśurāma.

WHAT IS MUKTI?

Release, and why places can give it

🔓 The word

Mukti, also mōkṣa: release from saṁsāra, the round of birth and death. The last and highest of the four aims of life.

🧭 Schools differ

For Advaita, release is knowing oneself as Brahman. For Dvaita, as at Uḍupi, it is the soul’s eternal joy in Viṣṇu’s presence.

🛕 A kṣētra

A kṣētra is a field of the sacred, where a god is held to be especially present and acts done there bear more fruit.

🕯️ In practice

Pilgrimage, worship, a holy bath, and, at some of the seven, rites for the dead, believed to carry them toward release.

ZAPPY: Does visiting a temple give you liberation?

PROF. BOLT: The tradition says such places make release nearer: they turn the mind toward God. Every school still asks for devotion, knowledge or grace beyond the journey itself.

WHERE THEY ARE

Gōkarṇa to Kukke

The spread. From Gōkarṇa in Uttara Kannaḍa in the north to Kukke Subrahmaṇya in Dakṣiṇa Kannaḍa in the south-east, roughly 250 km.

Coast and Ghāṭs. Four lie on or near the coast (Gōkarṇa, Kōṭēśvara, Kumbhāśi, Uḍupi); three inland at the foot of the Ghāṭs (Kollūr, Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa, Subrahmaṇya).

Close neighbours. Kōṭēśvara and Kumbhāśi lie a few kilometres apart near Kundāpura; Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa and Kollūr lie inland of them.

UḌUPI

Kṛṣṇa the child, in a monks’ house

🙏 The god

Bālakṛṣṇa, the child Kṛṣṇa holding a churning rod, worshipped in the Śrī Kṛṣṇa Maṭha.

🚢 How he came

The image, coated in gōpīcandana clay, came by ship from Dvārakā; Madhvācārya recovered and installed it in the 13th century.

🪟 Famous for

Darśan through the Kanakana kiṇḍi, the window of the poet-saint Kanaka, and the turn-by-turn rule of the Aṣṭa Maṭhas.

📖 Full guide

Uḍupi Śrīkṛṣṇa

KOLLŪR

The Goddess in the liṅga

🙏 The god

Mūkāmbikā, whose self-born liṅga is split by a golden line: Śakti on one side, Śiva on the other.

👹 Her name

From Mūkāsura, the demon she struck dumb (mūka) and then slew.

🏔️ Famous for

The five-metal image set up, by tradition, by Śaṅkara, who meditated on Kodachādri above; and children’s first letters at Navarātri.

📖 Full guide

Kollūr Mūkāmbikā

KUKKE SUBRAHMAṆYA

Lord of the serpents

🙏 The god

Subrahmaṇya as lord of the nāgas, with Vāsuki and Śēṣa, on the Kumāradhārā below Kumāra Parvata.

🐍 The story

Vāsuki took refuge here from Garuḍa, under the god’s protection; Skanda’s wedding with Dēvasēnā is also placed here.

🔥 Famous for

Rites for the serpents: sarpa saṁskāra, āśleṣā bali and nāga pratiṣṭhā.

📖 Full guide

Kukke Subrahmaṇya

KUMBHĀŚI

The elephant hill

🙏 The god

Siddhi Vināyaka, a self-born standing Gaṇapati with four arms, at Ānegudde, “elephant hill”.

✨ His title

Sarva Siddhi Pradāyaka, “giver of every success”: people come before any new venture.

🛕 Famous for

A gold face and silver armour on the image, and the ancient Harihara and Sūryanārāyaṇa temples nearby.

📖 Full guide

Kumbhāśi Ānegudde

KŌṬĒŚVARA

The liṅga of ten million

🙏 The god

Kōṭiliṅgēśvara, Śiva with a silver face, whose name means a crore of liṅgas in one.

🏷️ Old name

The town was once Dhvajapura, “town of the banner”.

💧 Famous for

A Kerala-style coastal temple and its great tank, Kōṭi Tīrtha, of about four acres.

📖 Full guide

Kōṭēśvara

ŚAṄKARANĀRĀYAṆA

Śiva and Viṣṇu in one stone

🙏 The god

Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa: Śiva and Viṣṇu as one, in a self-born liṅga below the sanctum floor.

😠 Another name

Krodha Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa, and the place Krodha Kṣētra; the village itself carries the god’s name.

📜 Famous for

Praise in the Padma Purāṇa and an 11th–12th-century temple at the foot of the Sahyādri; family deity of the Pañcagrāmi Brahmins.

📖 Full guide

Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa

GŌKARṆA

Śiva’s own self

🙏 The god

Mahābaḷēśvara, the ātmaliṅga that Rāvaṇa won and Gaṇeśa set down on this shore.

💪 The name

Rāvaṇa could not lift it, and named it Mahābala, “of great strength”.

🕯️ Famous for

A liṅga seen only through an opening in its pīṭha, rites for the dead, and praise in the Tēvāram and by Kālidāsa.

📖 Full guide

Gōkarṇa Mahābaḷēśvara

A DIVINE FAMILY

Who is worshipped across the seven

Śiva’s household. Five of the seven belong to Śiva’s family: Śiva twice, the Goddess once, and both their sons.

Viṣṇu. Uḍupi is the one wholly Vaiṣṇava shrine, and it is the most famous of all.

The bridge. Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa joins the two great traditions in one image.

ZAPPY: So the set covers almost every major god?

PROF. BOLT: Śiva, Viṣṇu, the Goddess, Gaṇapati and Subrahmaṇya: the five gods of the old pañcāyatana worship, with Skanda in place of Sūrya. A complete pilgrimage in one region.

SEVEN UNUSUAL FORMS

None is an ordinary image
HiddenGōkarṇa: a liṅga sealed in its base, shown whole about once in forty years
Below the floorŚaṅkaranārāyaṇa: a self-born liṅga set low in the ground
Two in oneKollūr: one liṅga, divided by a golden line into Śakti and Śiva
Many in oneKōṭēśvara: a single liṅga named for a crore
Self-bornKumbhāśi: a standing Gaṇapati that no sculptor made
Sea-borneUḍupi: a child found inside a lump of sacred clay
A refugeKukke: a god who shelters the serpents rather than one shown as a serpent

ZAPPY: Is that a coincidence?

PROF. BOLT: Hard to say. Coastal shrines often grew around something found, not something made: a stone, a spring, a self-born form. The temples were built around the find.

THE TEACHERS

Great names tied to the seven
Paraśurāma In tradition, the founder of the whole coast and of the seven.
Śaṅkara 8th c. by common reckoning: linked by tradition with Kollūr, where he is said to have set up the image, and with Gōkarṇa through the maṭha at Aśōke.
Madhva 13th c.: founds the worship at Uḍupi; his Tantrasāra Saṅgraha governs the rites at Kukke.
Vādirāja 16th c.: gives Uḍupi its two-year Paryāya.
Tamil saints The Śaiva poets of the Tēvāram sing of Gōkarṇa.
Kanaka The 16th-century poet-saint whose window at Uḍupi is still the way to see Kṛṣṇa.

ZAPPY: Advaita and Dvaita, side by side?

PROF. BOLT: Yes. Kollūr and Gōkarṇa carry Śaṅkara’s memory; Uḍupi and Kukke, Madhva’s. The set belongs to both.

THE FESTIVAL YEAR

The great day of each
JanUḍupi: Paryāya, the hand-over between the Aṣṭa Maṭhas (every two years). Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa: ten-day jātre with chariot
Feb–MarGōkarṇa: Mahāśivarātri and ratha yātrā
MarKollūr: rathōtsava in Phālguna
Aug–SepUḍupi: Kṛṣṇa Janmāṣṭamī. Kumbhāśi: Gaṇeśa Caturthī
Sep–OctKollūr: Navarātri, with first letters on Vijayadaśamī
Nov–DecKōṭēśvara: Koḍi Habba, the chariot festival in Vṛścika. Kukke: Campā Ṣaṣṭhī. Kumbhāśi: rathōtsava in Mārgaśīrṣa

ZAPPY: So winter is the busiest?

PROF. BOLT: November to March holds most of the chariot festivals, before the heat and the monsoon. That’s the coast’s festival season generally.

WHAT THEY TEACH

The lesson of the set

🌊 Land is a gift

The coast itself is held to be won from the sea for worship. The ground is sacred before any temple stands on it.

🤝 Many paths

Śaiva, Vaiṣṇava and Śākta, Advaita and Dvaita: seven doors to the same release.

🪨 God is found

Self-born stones and found images: the divine is discovered more than made.

🚶 Go on foot

A kṣētra asks you to come. The journey between them is part of the meaning.

ZAPPY: What’s the one-line lesson?

PROF. BOLT: Release has many doors, and this coast keeps seven of them open.

THE SEVEN ON ONE PAGE

One land, seven doors

SOURCES & NOTES

Where the facts come from

Sources: Templenet, “Seven Mukti Stalas” (the seven names; all called Paraśurāma kṣetras, on land reclaimed by Paraśurāma); Wikipedia, “Shankaranarayana” (one of seven Muktikṣētras; also called seven puṇya kṣetras of Paraśurāma land); Karnataka temple-tour pages and Kukke Devasthānam pages (the seven together make Paraśurāma Kṣetra; compared with the seven holy cities of the north; Paraśurāma said to have established them); Hindu Mirror (no single fixed order); Wikipedia, “Kumbashi” (one of seven Mukti Sthalas). Facts about each temple come from this site’s full guides, listed on the previous pages, and their sources.

Sources differ: the spelling of Kōṭēśvara (Kōḍēśvara); whether the seven are called mukti, puṇya or Paraśurāma kṣetras; whether Paraśurāma founded each temple or only sanctified the land.

Not verified: any early Sanskrit text naming exactly these seven; the comparison with the Sapta Purī is drawn by modern regional writers. The map and the family tree are simplified. Zappy and Prof. Bolt are fictional.
