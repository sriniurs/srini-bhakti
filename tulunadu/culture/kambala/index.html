THE BUFFALO RACE OF TUḶUNĀḌU · A REFERENCE GUIDE

KAMBAḶA

A race through the mud that began as a rite of the field

TEMPLES OF DAKṢIṆA KANNAḌA

FOREWORD

Most people know Kambaḷa as a spectacle: two buffaloes thundering down a flooded track, a runner flying behind them, a sheet of mud thrown high into the floodlights.

But the race grew out of the paddy field and the shrine. Before it was a sport, it was a way of preparing the land, honouring its guardians and showing loyalty to the great house of the village.

This guide follows Kambaḷa from the post planted in the mud and the night before, to the race itself, its runners, its court cases and its new life in the city.

To the field and its guardians,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: A rite, with betting and floodlights?

PROF. BOLT: Both live side by side. Some kambaḷas are still temple and house rites; others are big competitive meets. Many carry a bit of each.

AT A GLANCE

The essential facts on one page
WhatA race of yoked pairs of buffaloes down a flooded, muddy paddy track, with a man running or riding behind
WhereDakṣiṇa Kannaḍa, Uḍupi and Kāsaragōḍ
SeasonAbout November to March, from the Tuḷu month Jārde to Suggi
How manyOver 45 major meets a year on the coast
HostsLandholding houses, temples and kambaḷa committees (samitis)
TrackThe kare, usually about 135–145 m, often paired
RuleNever a single buffalo; always a yoked pair
StatusBanned in 2014 with jallikaṭṭu; made lawful again by a Karnataka amendment, 2017–18

ZAPPY: Buffaloes, not bulls?

PROF. BOLT: He-buffaloes, the coast’s field animals. They are built for wet paddy, and they love the mud the race runs through.

THE NAME

Three readings

🌾 Kampa-kaḷa

The commonest reading: kampa, slushy mud, and kaḷa, a field: “the mud field”.

🪵 Kamba

Another links it to kamba, a pole: the post in the field, or the pole the splash must reach.

🗣️ Spellings

Kambaḷa, Kambla, Kambula: all the same word, in Kannaḍa and Tuḷu forms.

🏷️ Many kambaḷas

Each kind carries a qualifier: pookare, bāre, kōri, arasu, dēvere, bāle; the name tells you whose and what kind it is.

ZAPPY: So “kambaḷa” is really the field?

PROF. BOLT: In the oldest sense, yes: a field prepared and honoured. The race is what happens on it.

FOUR KINDS OF KAMBAḶA

From rite to sport
PookareA rite around the decorated post in a field, tied to the worship of nāgas and daivas; no race
BāreAnother ritual form honouring the nāgas and daivas; no race
Arasu or DēvaraThe kambaḷa of a king or a god: buffaloes run in the field of a ruling house or temple
ĀdhunikaThe modern competitive race, run by committees with categories and prizes

The old way. Traditional kambaḷa was not a contest. Pairs ran one at a time, each simply completing its run. Racing two pairs side by side for a prize is the modern form.

Loyalty run. At a king’s or god’s kambaḷa, everyone who owed allegiance to the deity or the house brought their animals, and each pair ran one round there before going to work in its own fields, even if only as a token.

THE POOKARE

The post that marries the field
Felled and carved. An areca palm is felled and laid at the head of the field; craftsmen of the carpenter community, the ācāris, shape the post from it.
Dressed. Washermen, the Maḍivāḷas, dress it with flowers from around the village.
Planted. The head of the guttu leads its carrying, installation and worship. Often two poles stand together, a plain one counted male and a decorated one female.
A marriage. The planting is read as a marriage: the field the bride, the post the groom, so that the land will bear.
Guarded. The night before, the field is watched against spirits, and against rival houses who might try to carry the pookare off.

KŌRI: THE FIELD RITE

When the village ploughs together

What it is. Kōri kambaḷa, or kanda kōri, is a day of collective ploughing and planting, fixed for the main paddy crop. Teams of buffaloes from many households work one field together.

Why it mattered. It turned the race’s energy to the field’s purpose: the trampling and churning prepared the mud for transplanting, and the shared labour bound households to the great house and to each other.

The post first. Before sowing, the pookare kamba is set in the middle of the field. In some houses the season of field worship begins right after Dīpāvaḷi.

ZAPPY: So the old kambaḷa actually ploughed the field?

PROF. BOLT: That was part of its value. Running buffaloes up and down a flooded field is not far from preparing it for planting.

A Tuḷu world. Field, buffalo, nāga, daiva and house belong together in Tuḷu life. The kambaḷa is the moment they meet in public.

THE DAIVAS OF THE RACE

Who watches from the edge of the field

🐃 Mahiṣandāya

A daiva in buffalo form, central to the daivada kambaḷa. His shrine stands at the guttu house, and the day begins with his worship.

🌾 Bermer

Scholars describe the old kambaḷa as a fertility rite dedicated to Bermer, the Tuḷu Brahma of the land, and his attendant daivas.

🥁 Yēru Baṇṭa

An attendant daiva who, by tradition, beat a drum to announce a coming kambaḷa.

🛕 Dēvara kambaḷa

At Tiruvailu the deity’s festival image is carried to the field to “watch”; at Tonnase (Hebri) the kambaḷa is linked with Dhūmāvatī and Gōpālakṛṣṇa; at Vandāru farmers race to thank the deity for keeping their animals free of disease.

Before running. Pairs are taken to the daivasthāna first, and the owner touches kuṅkuma from the shrine to their foreheads.

THE NIGHT BEFORE

The Koragas’ vigil
Pāni kuḷḷunu. On the night before the race, the Koraga community keeps a vigil in the open field. The name means “sitting under the dew”.
Dance and drum. Led by their headman, the Mukhāri, the men dance around a fire to their own drums, the ḍōlu and the ḍuḍi; the drumming, the ḍōlu sēve, goes on until the kambaḷa is over.
Their daiva. They sing to their own daiva, Nīca, and offer toddy and a rice pudding cooked in a big clay pot; the pot is broken so the pudding stands as one mass, the kandēl addyē.
Their due. At the end the host family gives them betel leaf and areca, marking the close of their service.

THE TRACK

Twin lanes of slush

🛤️ The kare

A flooded paddy lane kept soft and wet. Many meets build two side by side, a joḍukare, so two pairs race at once; some famous pairs of lanes are named Sūrya and Candra, Sun and Moon.

🏁 Mañjoṭṭi

The mud pit and raised end of the track, where handlers wait to slow, calm and wash the pair after the dash.

🚩 Niśāne

A tall pole with white cloth near the end, the target the mud-spray must reach in the splash race.

⏱️ Speed

Top pairs cover the track in about 12–15 seconds.

FOUR WAYS TO RACE

The categories of the modern kambaḷa
Negilu (nēgil)The pair drags a light plough; the runner holds it down without lifting it. Entry level, with junior and senior rounds.
Hagga (bal)A rope joins runner and pair; he runs behind holding its knots. For experienced pairs. First to the finish wins.
Aḍḍa halageThe runner stands on a crosswise plank dragged behind the pair, holding ropes and tails. Hard, so not run everywhere.
Kaṇe halageThe runner rides a plank with two holes, on one leg; water jets up through the holes. Judged by the height of the splash against the niśāne, not by speed.

Age groups. Pairs are also graded by age, judged from their teeth, so young animals race young animals: in effect a junior and senior ladder inside each category.

ZAPPY: A race you win without being first?

PROF. BOLT: Kaṇe halage is the showpiece. The crowd waits for the moment the spray hits the white cloth.

RACE DAY

From the procession to the prize
Day before Trials, the kuḍi kambaḷa, test the track and the pairs; the Koragas’ vigil starts at dusk.
Morning A procession of the pairs, dressed with a forehead rope, the netti baḷḷa, some with silver ornaments and a cloth across the back, the pāvaḍe; a wooden staff, the nōga, lies across the shoulders.
Heats The announcer clears the track and calls category, owner and runner. At the cry “Āle buḍiyer!” the pairs are released, a kombu horn blaring as they run.
Night At many meets the races run on under floodlights through the night, with bands and crowds of 15,000–20,000 at large meets.
Finals By the next afternoon the winners in each class are settled.
Prizes Once coconuts and bananas; now medals, gold and silver coins and cash. A champion pair may collect hundreds of medals in its life.

THE BUFFALOES

Athletes of the mud
6 amHay and water, then ground horse gram mixed with palm jaggery
7:30 amGrazing on wet ground and a long wallow in mud
10:30 amA bath and an oil massage with herbal and coconut oils
4 pmHorse gram again, a warm-water bath, a hay snack
SummerFans in the sheds, and in some, water dripping over them to keep cool
HandlingA nose rope for control; trained caretakers who live with the pair

Status. A great pair is a family’s pride. Some owners have built swimming pools for their animals.

Fewer houses. Supporters estimate that the houses keeping racing pairs today are a small fraction of what they were fifty years ago.

THE RUNNERS

The men behind the pair
Who they are. Usually young men of farming families, skilled at keeping their feet and their line in knee-deep slush while holding rope or plough.
2020. At Aikaḷa, Srīnivāsa Gauḍa of Mūḍabidri was timed over 142.5 m in 13.62 seconds. Social media worked out a “100 m in 9.55 s”, faster than Usain Bolt’s record.
The caution. The president of the Kambaḷa Academy, Prof. K. Guṇapāla Kaḍamba, declined the comparison: the timing is not done to athletics standards, and the runner is pulled along by the pair. Gauḍa was offered a national trial and chose to stay with kambaḷa.
Records fall. Within weeks, another runner, Niśānt Śeṭṭi, was reported faster still.

ZAPPY: So was he faster than Bolt?

PROF. BOLT: Not in any way that can be compared. Different ground, different timing, and a buffalo pair doing a lot of the work. What is real is how hard it is to run that fast in that mud.

IN THE COURTS

Ban and return
Concern Animal-welfare groups object to whipping and to driving animals hard; organisers say the animals are cared for as family and the race is tradition.
2014 In cases brought by animal-welfare groups, the Supreme Court bans jallikaṭṭu and similar events; kambaḷa stops.
Jan 2017 After protests in Tamil Nadu, jallikaṭṭu is restored; the coast demands the same for kambaḷa.
Jul 2017 The Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (Karnataka Amendment) Ordinance makes kambaḷa lawful, with the President’s assent.
Feb 2018 The amending Act receives presidential assent, settling the legal basis.
After Welfare groups report continued whipping; officials press for gentler handling.

ZAPPY: Is the argument over?

PROF. BOLT: The law is settled for now; the welfare debate isn’t. This guide records both sides as they stand, without taking one.

KAMBAḶA TODAY

From village field to the capital

💡 Floodlit nights

Many meets run day and night under floodlights; Jeppinamogaru, in Maṅgaḷūru, is one of the places known for them.

🏙️ Bengaluru, 2023

On 25–26 November 2023 the first kambaḷa outside the coast was held at Bengaluru’s Palace Grounds, on a 155 m track, with well over a hundred pairs brought up from the coast.

🎬 On screen

The 2022 film Kantara features a kambaḷa and drew new interest from outside Tuḷunāḍu.

🌊 Neighbours

Kerala has a related race, the māramaḍi.

Old kambaḷas. Some of the oldest, such as the king’s and god’s kambaḷa at Kadri, once under Āḷupa patronage and called both Arasu and Dēvere Kambaḷa, remain famous names; sources differ on how they run today.
