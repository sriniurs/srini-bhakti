Daiva & Nāga Traditions of Tulu Nadu

The coastal daivas, their oral epics, Bhūta Kōla and the Nāgamaṇḍala serpent rite.

← Tulu Nadu Temples Culture Festivals

  • Bhūta Kōla

    1 article

    Bhūta kōla in full: the performer families (Nalike, Pambada, Parava), the bhaṇḍāra, costume from face paint to aṇi and gaggara, the bronze masks, the stages of a night, the nuḍi and the daiva's court.

  • Bobbarya and Kordabbu

    1 article

    Bobbarya, the sea daiva of the fishermen, born of a Muslim father and a Jain mother, and Kordabbu, Babbu Svāmi, the orphan healer whom the royal daivas made Daivarāja: their stories, their meeting, and their worship on the coast.

  • Guḷiga

    1 article

    Guḷiga, the stone-born daiva of Tuḷunāḍu: found in Pārvatī’s ash and thrown by Śiva, born again from a serpent, the endless hunger that only Viṣṇu’s little finger could satisfy, the fight with Pañjurli, the pact with Koragajja, and the guardian of boundaries worshipped as a stone.

  • Jumādi

    1 article

    Jumādi, Dhūmāvatī of Tuḷunāḍu: the demon Dhūmāsura whom neither man nor woman could kill, Pārvatī’s hunger and the swallowing of Śiva, the joined god with a man’s face and a woman’s body, the faithful Baṇṭe, and Jumādi’s kōla.

  • Kalkuḍa and Kallurṭi

    1 article

    Kalkuḍa and Kallurṭi, the sculptor and his sister: the Gommaṭa of Kārkaḷa, the king who cut off the master’s hand and leg, the greater statue at Yēnūr, a sister’s oath of revenge, and the two daivas of justice.

  • Koragajja

    1 article

    Koragajja, Koraga Taniya: the orphan boy of the Koraga community who became the most beloved daiva of Tuḷunāḍu, his foster mother, the pact with Guḷiga, the Kuttār Ādisthaḷa and the seven kōla sites, his kōla and offerings, and the finder of lost things.

  • Kōṭi and Cennaya

    1 article

    Kōṭi and Cennaya, the twin heroes of Tuḷunāḍu: their mother Deyi Baiḍeti the healer, the king of Paḍumale and his minister Budyanta, the twins’ fight for justice and their death near Eṇmūr, and their worship with Bermer in the garaḍis as Brahma Baidarkaḷa.

  • Nāgamaṇḍala

    1 article

    Nāgamaṇḍala, the all-night serpent rite of Tuḷunāḍu: why it is held, the five-colour floor drawing, the Vaidya as nāgakannikā with the ḍakke, the Brahmin pātri and the areca flower, the night hour by hour and the erasing at dawn.

  • Pañjurli

    1 article

    Pañjurli, the boar daiva of Tuḷunāḍu: the piglet Pārvatī adopted at Kailāsa and Śiva sent to earth, guardian of crops and boundaries, the boar mask, the Barne Korpuni offering of paddy, his many local forms, and the Varāha question.

  • Siri

    1 article

    Siri of Tuḷunāḍu: the princess born from areca flowers, her wronged marriage and walk into divinity, her daughter Sonne and the twins Abbaga and Dāraga, the 15,683-line Siri pāḍdana, the Ādi Āḷade shrines and the Siri jātre where women enter trance.
