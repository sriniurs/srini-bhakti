Temples of Tulu Nadu

Shrines of the coast, from Maṅgaḷūru and Dharmasthaḷa to Uḍupi and Kārkaḷa.

← Tulu Nadu Culture Festivals Daiva & Nāga Traditions

  • Ānegudde Vināyaka, Kumbhāśi

    1 article

    Ānegudde Śrī Vināyaka of Kumbhāśi, a Muktisthala of Paraśurāma Kṣetra: Agastya’s sacrifice, Bhīma and the demon Kumbhāsura, the self-born Gaṇapati in gold and silver, the Harihara shrine and Vādirāja’s birthplace.

  • Bappanāḍu Durgāparameśvarī

    1 article

    Bappanāḍu Durgāparameśvarī at Mulki: the Goddess as a liṅga on the Śāmbhavī, the seven Durgās and the sandalwood boat, the Muslim trader Bappa whose boat stopped in a red river, the Jain Sāvanta who built the temple, the great ḍōlu and the Meena jātre.

  • Dharmasthaḷa Mañjunātha

    1 article

    Śrī Kṣetra Dharmasthaḷa: Mañjunātha on the Netrāvatī, the four Dharma Daivas and Annappa, the Jain Heggaḍe family and Mādhva priests, the Bāhubali of Ratnagiri, and the Lakṣa Dīpotsava.

  • Kadri Mañjunātha

    1 article

    Kadri Mañjunātha, Maṅgaḷūru: Paraśurāma’s Kadaḷī Kṣetra, the Buddhist Kadarika Vihāra, the 968 bronze Lokeśvara, the Nātha Jōgi Maṭha, the Gōmukha spring and its tanks, the Pāṇḍava caves and the January jātre.

  • Kārkaḷa: Ananta Padmanābha and Veṅkaṭaramaṇa

    1 article

    Two Viṣṇu temples of Jain Kārkaḷa: the reclining Ananta Padmanābha found in a lake and installed in 1567 for a Śṛṅgeri pontiff, and Padu Tirupati, the Veṅkaṭaramaṇa of the Gauḍa Sārasvatas who fled Goa, with the idol lost in a Mulki well, the cow that chose the site, Vīra Māruti and the carved Garuḍa pillars.

  • Kaṭīl Durgāparameśvarī

    1 article

    Kaṭīl Durgāparameśvarī: the Goddess on an island in the Nandinī, Jābāli and the cursed cow-daughter, Bhrāmarī and the bees that killed Aruṇāsura, the fire ritual of Attūr and Koḍettūr, and the Kaṭīl Yakṣagāna Mela.

  • Kōṭeśvara · Kōṭiliṅgeśvara

    1 article

    Kōṭiliṅgeśvara of Kōṭeśvara, a Muktisthala of Paraśurāma Kṣetra: a crore of sages and a crore of Śivas, the thousand liṅgas under the pedestal, King Vasu of Basrūr and the sugarcane chariot of the Koḍi Habba, and the great Kōṭi Tīrtha.

  • Kudroḷi Gokarṇanātha

    1 article

    Kudroḷi Śrī Gokarṇanātha Kṣetra, Maṅgaḷūru: the temple of 1912 consecrated by Nārāyaṇa Guru for all, Koragappa and the Billava community, the Navadurgās and Śāradā of the Maṅgaḷūru Dasarā, and women priests.

  • Mandārthi Durgāparameśvarī

    1 article

    Mandārthi Durgāparameśvarī near Brahmāvara: the five serpent princesses and Mandarati, King Devavarma and the Goddess found in the river, the robber Surgōḷi Antu and the anklets, the five harake Yakṣagāna troupes booked years ahead, the kenda sēve fire-walk and the Kumbha jātre.

  • Maṅgaḷādevī

    1 article

    Maṅgaḷādevī of Bōḷāra, the Goddess who gave Maṅgaḷūru its name: the princess Premaladevi and Matsyendranātha, King Kundavarman and the Nātha sages, the Kerala-style shrine, and Navarātri with the chariot to Mārnamikaṭṭe.

  • Poḷali Rājarājeśvarī

    1 article

    Poḷali Rājarājeśvarī: the great clay image of the Goddess said to be made by King Suratha of the Devī Māhātmya, the lēpāṣṭagandha that renews it, the Poḷali Ceṇḍu ball game and the month-long jātre on the Phalguṇī.

  • Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa

    1 article

    Krodha Śaṅkaranārāyaṇa, a Muktisthala of Paraśurāma Kṣetra: Śiva and Viṣṇu in one hidden self-born liṅga under water, the sage Krodha and the demons Khara and Raṭṭa, Kāmadhenu’s footprints, the Kōṭi Tīrtha and the January jātre.

  • Sowthaḍka Mahāgaṇapati

    1 article

    Sowthaḍka Mahāgaṇapati: the Gaṇapati who chose the open sky, the cowherds who carried him to a cucumber meadow, the bells of answered vows, avalakki pañcakajjāya and the Mūḍappa sēve that buries him in appa.

  • Uḍupi Śrīkṛṣṇa

    1 article

    Śrī Kṛṣṇa Maṭha, Uḍupi: Bālakṛṣṇa with the churning rod, Madhvācārya and the ship from Dvārakā, Kanakana Kiṇḍi, the Aṣṭa Maṭhas and the two-year Paryāya, Saptotsava and Viṭla Piṇḍi.

  • Uppinaṅgaḍi Sahasraliṅgeśvara

    1 article

    Uppinaṅgaḍi Sahasraliṅgeśvara, the Dakṣiṇa Kāśī: the meeting of the Netrāvatī and Kumāradhārā, Bhīma and the Puruṣamṛga, the thousand liṅgas in the riverbed and the buried original, the monsoon saṅgama at the temple door, Mahākāḷī and the Makhe jātre.
