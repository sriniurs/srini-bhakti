ŚRĪ RĀJARĀJEŚVARĪ · POḶALI · A REFERENCE GUIDE

POḶALI

The Goddess shaped from the earth

TEMPLES OF DAKṢIṆA KANNAḌA

FOREWORD

Most great goddesses of the coast are stone, or rose from the rock on their own. At Poḷali, on the Phalguṇī, the Mother is earth: a towering seated image moulded from clay, and renewed with clay.

Her story reaches back to the Devī Māhātmya, to a king who lost everything and made an image of the Goddess from the soil of a riverbank. And once a year her festival turns the field before the temple into the scene of a great ball game between villages.

This guide tells both: the image and the game.

Śrī Rājarājeśvaryai namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: A clay goddess that lasts centuries?

PROF. BOLT: It lasts because it is cared for. Every few years it is coated again with a special earth, so the image you see is both very old and freshly made.

AT A GLANCE

The essential facts on one page
DeityŚrī Rājarājeśvarī, a form of Lalitā Tripurasundarī
ImageA seated figure about nine to ten feet tall, of specially prepared clay
FounderBy tradition King Suratha of the Devī Māhātmya
PlacePoḷali, Kariyaṅgaḷa village, Baṇṭvāḷa tālūk, about 19 km from Maṅgaḷūru
RiverThe Phalguṇī (Gurupura)
Great festivalA month-long jātre from Mīna saṅkramaṇa, mid-March
Famous forThe Poḷali Ceṇḍu, a five-day ball game played as a rite
Old namesPural; in epigraphs the goddess is Poraḷa Dēvi

ZAPPY: Pural?

PROF. BOLT: The old name of the place. It is linked with the Tuḷu word for a flute. Poḷali is the later form.

WHERE IT IS

Hills, paddy and the Phalguṇī

The setting. Poḷali lies in Kariyaṅgaḷa village of Baṇṭvāḷa tālūk, ringed by low hills and wide paddy fields.

The river. The Phalguṇī runs from the east along the north side of the temple, about a furlong away, and turns south behind it on the west.

The field. Before the temple lies the open ground where the Ceṇḍu is played, with a platform at its edge called the Sultān Kaṭṭe.

RĀJARĀJEŚVARĪ

Queen of the kings of kings

👑 The name

Rājarājeśvarī: “Sovereign of the King of Kings”. A name of Lalitā Tripurasundarī, the Goddess as supreme ruler of the worlds.

🌸 In Śrīvidyā

In the Śrīvidyā · 130 articles tradition, Lalitā after her coronation is hailed as Rājarājeśvarī, and she is worshipped as the full form of Bhuvaneśvarī.

🛡️ At Poḷali

Here she sits with Durgāparameśvarī, Mahāgaṇapati, Subrahmaṇya and Bhadrakāḷī, the family of shrines consecrated together.

⚔️ A king’s goddess

Fitting for a temple said to be founded by a king who prayed to win back his throne.

ZAPPY: Why is Subrahmaṇya named with her?

PROF. BOLT: Some records call the deity “Rājarājeśvarī Subrahmaṇya”. Subrahmaṇya’s clay image stands among the temple’s chief images, and Ṣaṣṭhī is one of its festivals.

KING SURATHA

The story from the Devī Māhātmya
A king undone. In the frame story of the Devī Māhātmya · 19 parts, King Suratha loses a war, and his own ministers seize his treasury. He rides alone into the forest.
The hermitage. He comes to the hermitage of the sage Medhas (Sumedhas), where he meets a merchant, Samādhi, cast out by his own family.
The teaching. The sage tells them the great deeds of the Goddess: the slaying of Madhu and Kaiṭabha, of Mahiṣa, of Śumbha and Niśumbha.
The image of earth. The two men go to a riverbank, make an image of the Goddess from earth, and worship it for three years with flowers, incense and fasting.
The boon. The Goddess appears. Suratha asks for his kingdom back, and for a future birth as the Manu Sāvarṇi; Samādhi asks for wisdom.

At Poḷali. Local tradition places that riverbank and forest here, makes Suratha the shaper of this clay image, and says he set his own jewelled crown on its head.

THE CLAY IMAGE

Earth, herbs and time

🏺 Material

Clay from different soils, prepared with herbal ingredients to give it strength; often described today as stucco over the clay core.

🌿 Lēpāṣṭagandha

Every twelve years or so the images receive a coat of earth mixed with eight fragrant and medicinal substances. The prepared earth is old stock, kept from long ago, not made new each time.

👥 The family

Beside her are clay images of Subrahmaṇya, Bhadrakāḷī and Mahāgaṇapati; some sources add Sarasvatī.

📏 Rare

A clay image of this size is described as probably the only one of its kind in Karnataka.

RECORDS AND RULERS

What the stones and visitors say
8th c. An inscription found near the temple is cited for the building of a temple around the clay image.
Āḷupas The early rulers of Tuḷunāḍu are credited with promoting the worship of Rājarājeśvarī.
Many houses Kadambas, Cāḷukyas, Rāṣṭrakūṭas, Hoysaḷas, Vijayanagara, the Nāyakas of Keḷadi and Mysore all ruled the region; many gave land to the temple.
1448 The Persian envoy ʿAbd al-Razzāq describes a brass temple near the coast with a deity whose eyes were rubies; the temple’s own account identifies it with Poḷali.
17th c. Queen Cennammāji of Keḷadi visits and has a grand chariot made for the Goddess.
18th c. Tradition says Ṭīpu Sultān admired the Ceṇḍu and had a platform built to watch it: the Sultān Kaṭṭe.
2019 After renovation, the temple holds its Brahmakalaśa reconsecration on 13 March, fifteen years after the last.

Wood, copper and a lion tower
MukhamaṇṭapaThe front hall; its roof is carved in wood with figures of gods and goddesses
CopperSheets cover the roofs over the sanctum, the flag-mast and the lamp-pillar
TowerNamed Siṁha, the lion
TankNamed Phalguṇī, after the river
Flag-mastRenewed for the 2019 reconsecration; the teak was given by the Billava community

PRIESTS AND HOUSES

Who serves and who manages
Four chief priests. The worship is in the hands of four head priests, the pradhāna arcakas, who take turns in four terms, the paryāya.
The eldest. The office of Pavitrapāṇi, the senior priest, goes to the eldest of them, not from father to son.
Hereditary rights. The right to worship itself is inherited; priests are not hired. The line is counted at over thirteen generations.
The great houses. For centuries the temple’s affairs have been in the hands of the heads of the Ammuñje guttu, the Uḷipāḍi guttu and the Cēra family.
The realm. At great festivals, a vow of restraint is asked of everyone within the temple’s traditional bounds, the sāvira sīme, “the thousand boundaries”: no meat or liquor, no weddings, until the rites are done.

THE MONTH-LONG JĀTRE

Mīna saṅkramaṇa to the final bath
Day 1 The festival begins on Mīna saṅkramaṇa, the sun’s entry into Pisces, in mid-March.
Day 4 The festival image, set on a circular crown-frame called the prabhāvaḷi, is carried to the Siṁhāsana Kaṭṭe, a platform about 100 m from the temple, for a special worship.
Weeks Nightly processions, music, Yakṣagāna and a large fair around the temple.
Late The five days of the Ceṇḍu begin seven days before the final bath.
Ratha The great chariot festival, the rathōtsava.
End The avabhṛtha, the closing bath of the festival image, in mid-April.

ZAPPY: Who comes?

PROF. BOLT: Devotees from across the coast. With its fair, plays and the ball game, it is one of the great spring gatherings of Tuḷunāḍu.

THE POḶALI CEṆḌU

A ball made for the Goddess
The name. Ceṇḍu in Kannaḍa, cend in Tuḷu: a ball. Locally the rite is also Pural da Ceṇḍu, “the ball of Pural”.
The makers. A hereditary cobbler family of Mijār stitches the leather ball each year; it is their right and honour.
The carriers. An oil-miller family of Kaḍapu Kariya fetches the ball from Mijār and announces the day the game will begin.
The first resting place. They set it in the freshly cleaned front yard of the Maḷali Ballāḷ house.
On the gateway. On the first evening the cobbler family places the ball and a palm-leaf umbrella on top of the temple gateway; after worship, it goes to the field.

HOW THE GAME GOES

Two villages, one ball, five days
WhenFive days, starting seven days before the avabhṛtha
WhereThe open field in front of the temple
SidesTwo villages, one on each side of the temple
PlayersNo fixed number; up to about five hundred have joined
StartThe guttu families wait at the Sultān Kaṭṭe; a man who holds the hereditary right throws the ball in
AimTo drive the ball to the opponents’ side
MeaningDharma against adharma; the chariot festival that follows marks the victory

ZAPPY: Is it like modern football?

PROF. BOLT: Only loosely. No referee, no fixed team size, no clock. Trustees recall crowds so thick no one could see the ball once play began.

THROUGH THE YEAR

Other days of the Goddess

🌾 Harvest

Kadiru habba, the first sheaves of the new crop brought to the temple.

🪔 Lights

Navarātri, Dīpāvaḷi, and the Lakṣa Dīpōtsava, a hundred thousand lamps, on the new moon of Kārtika.

🐍 Subrahmaṇya

Subrahmaṇya Pañcamī and Ṣaṣṭhī, for the god who shares the shrine.

🌙 More

Siṁha saṅkramaṇa, Saura Ugādi, Gōkulāṣṭamī, Gaṇeśa Caturthī, Dhanurmāsa and Mahāśivarātri.

Every day. Worship three times a day, and a free meal, anna prasāda, for all who come.
