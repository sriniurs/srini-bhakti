GARUḌA PURĀṆA · PART A · 1 OF 8 · THE BOOK AND ITS BIRD

GARUḌA PURĀṆA

What Viṣṇu told his eagle, and why the book is more than death

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya

FOREWORD

Say “Garuḍa Purāṇa” in most Indian homes and people go quiet. It is the book a priest reads in the thirteen days after a death, the book of Yama’s road and the hells.

That book is real, but it is a small part of the whole. Most of the Garuḍa Purāṇa is about living: how to worship, how to test a gem, how to treat a fever, how a king should rule, how to sit in yoga and know the self.

The series comes in two folders: Part A, eight guides to the Purāṇa itself, and Part B, the Sārōddhāra read at funerals, one guide per chapter. This first guide is the map: the bird who heard it, the three sections, the famous digest read at funerals, and what scholars and tradition say about its age.

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: A Purāṇa people are scared to open?

PROF. BOLT: Mostly because they know one slice of it. Open the whole thing and it reads more like an encyclopedia than a ghost story.

AT A GLANCE

The Garuḍa Purāṇa in facts
KindOne of the eighteen Mahāpurāṇas; usually counted seventeenth
GodViṣṇu; a Vaiṣṇava Purāṇa
SpeakerViṣṇu to Garuḍa, his eagle mount
AuthorBy tradition, Vyāsa
Size19,000 verses by tradition; about 8,000 in surviving manuscripts
SectionsPūrva (Ācāra) Kāṇḍa; Uttara (Prēta) Kāṇḍa; Brahma Kāṇḍa in one edition
ClassSāttvika, “of goodness”, in the Padma Purāṇa’s grouping
LanguageSanskrit verse

ZAPPY: Why does the size drop from 19,000 to 8,000?

PROF. BOLT: The 19,000 is the figure the Purāṇa lists give. What survives in manuscripts is much smaller, and the versions differ from each other a lot.

WHO IS GARUḌA?

The eagle who carries Viṣṇu

👪 Family

Son of the sage Kaśyapa and Vinatā, so called Vainatēya. His elder brother Aruṇa drives the Sun’s chariot.

🦅 Form

Part eagle, part man: a beak and wings, a human body with joined hands, gold and red in colour.

🏳️ Role

Viṣṇu’s vāhana, his mount, and the emblem on his banner: Garuḍadhvaja.

🐍 Enemy of

The nāgas, the serpents, sons of Kadrū. Mantras to Garuḍa are said to cure snakebite.

Titles. Suparṇa (“fine-winged”), Tārkṣya, Khagēśvara (“lord of birds”), Pakṣirāja. In temples he stands facing the sanctum with joined hands.

THE BIRD WHO STOLE AMṚTA

How Garuḍa became Viṣṇu’s mount
The wager. Kaśyapa’s wives Vinatā and Kadrū bet on the colour of the divine horse Uccaiḥśravas’s tail. Kadrū’s serpent sons blackened it, and Vinatā lost and became her slave.
The price. The serpents would free her only for amṛta, the nectar of immortality, kept by the gods.
The theft. Garuḍa fought past the gods, took the pot, yet did not drink a drop.
The boon. Pleased by that restraint, Viṣṇu made him his mount and banner; his mother was set free.

Where it is told. The full story is in the Mahābhārata’s Ādi Parva; the Purāṇa takes Garuḍa’s place beside Viṣṇu for granted.

HOW THE TEACHING TRAVELLED

From Viṣṇu to us

Why “Garuḍa”. The Purāṇa is named for the one who first heard it and passed it on, not for the one who taught it.

Two frames. The outer frame is the usual Purāṇa setting: the bard Sūta reciting to the sages of the Naimiṣa forest. Inside it, Garuḍa questions Viṣṇu and Viṣṇu answers.

Lineage. Accounts give the middle links in slightly different order; all agree it began with Viṣṇu speaking to Garuḍa.

WHAT MAKES A PURĀṆA

Where this one fits among eighteen
Sargacreation of the world
Pratisargaits dissolution and re-creation
Vaṁśalineages of gods and sages
Manvantarathe ages of the Manus
Vaṁśānucaritathe histories of royal dynasties

The five marks. The table gives the classic pañca lakṣaṇa, five topics a Purāṇa is meant to cover. The Garuḍa has them, briefly, and then runs far beyond them into practical knowledge.

🧭 Six of goodness

The Padma’s six sāttvika: Viṣṇu, Nārada, Bhāgavata, Garuḍa, Padma, Varāha. The Garuḍa’s own list swaps in Matsya, Kūrma and Vāyu.

📚 Its neighbour

Only the Agni Purāṇa is as encyclopedic; both read like libraries of their time.

THREE KĀṆḌAS

How the book is divided

📗 Pūrva or Ācāra

The “earlier” or “conduct” section. About nine-tenths of the text.

📕 Uttara or Prēta

The “later” or “departed” section, also Prēta Kalpa or Dharma Kāṇḍa.

📒 Brahma

Only in the printed Veṅkaṭēśvara edition.

🔢 Numbers vary

Chapter counts differ between manuscripts and editions; the bar uses the usual ranges.

THE ĀCĀRA KĀṆḌA

An encyclopedia of right living
🌍Cosmos: creation by Viṣṇu with Lakṣmī, the avatāras, the worlds, the body as a small cosmos
🪔Worship: pūjā of Viṣṇu, Śiva, Sūrya and Devī, mantras, vratas, holy places
🏛️Temples: grid-based floor plans and dozens of temple styles
💎Gems: how to test, clean and wear them
🌿Medicine: a long Dhanvantari section on disease, herbs and diet; even horses’ ailments
⭐Signs: astrology, body marks, omens
⚖️Dharma: duties, gifts, purification, much of it shared with the Yājñavalkya Smṛti
👑Nīti: maxims on kings, officials, friends and enemies
🧘Yoga: the closing chapters on meditation, the self and release

ZAPPY: Medicine and temple plans in a holy book?

PROF. BOLT: A Purāṇa was a library for people who had no other library. If it mattered for life, it went in.

THE PRĒTA KĀṆḌA

The section everyone knows

❓ The question

Garuḍa has seen the worlds and asks Viṣṇu what becomes of a person after death, and why some suffer.

🛤️ The journey

The dying moment, Yama’s messengers, the year-long road to Yama’s city, the river Vaitaraṇī.

🔥 The rites

Cremation, the daily piṇḍas, the ten days, joining the ancestors, the yearly śrāddha.

👻 The prēta

The soul that lingers as a prēta, and how gifts and rites free it.

A loose book. Ludo Rocher calls this section entirely unsystematic, and three quite different versions are known. Funeral practice in India takes from it but does not follow it line for line.

THE BRAHMA KĀṆḌA

A late, southern addition

🗣️ The speakers

Kṛṣṇa and Garuḍa, rather than Viṣṇu in his four-armed form.

🏔️ A shrine

Praise of Veṅkaṭēśvara and his hill at Tirupati.

📜 A school

It upholds Madhvācārya’s Dvaita and argues against some Advaita teachings.

🕰️ Its age

Other Purāṇas’ descriptions of the Garuḍa do not mention it, which marks it as later.

ZAPPY: So the third part has a Karnataka flavour?

PROF. BOLT: Its Mādhva theology does. Uḍupi’s tradition would find itself at home there.

THE SĀRŌDDHĀRA

The book read in the house of mourning
Ch. 1–7The road to Yama, the hells, and the deeds that lead there
Ch. 8–13Rites for the dying and the dead, day by day
Ch. 14Yama’s city and court
Ch. 15–16Good rebirth, yoga and final release

What it is. Garuḍa Purāṇa Sārōddhāra, “the essence drawn out”, is a 16-chapter digest with commentary by Naunidhirāma. It draws on a version of the Prēta Kāṇḍa that no longer survives, and on other texts.

The mix-up. Its 1911 English translation by Ernest Wood and S. V. Subrahmanyam was long taken for the Garuḍa Purāṇa itself. Albrecht Weber first noticed the error.

The shape. It ends not with hell but with yoga and liberation.

HOW OLD IS IT?

Scholars do not agree
Tradition Composed by Vyāsa, like all the Purāṇas.
Rocher An early core possibly in the first centuries CE, with chapters added through the 6th century or later.
Gietz et al. A first version somewhere between the 4th and 11th centuries.
Leadbeater About 900 CE, given its yoga and tantra chapters.
Pintchman The surviving text about 850–1000 CE.
Dalal About 800–1000 CE, with later sections added in the second millennium.
Hazra Not earlier than about the 10th or 11th century in its present form.

ZAPPY: Why such a spread?

PROF. BOLT: A Purāṇa grows. Each scholar is dating a different layer: the core, the first version, or the text as printed today.

OTHER NAMES

Each name is one of Garuḍa’s
Sauparṇafrom Suparṇa, “fine-winged”; named so in the Bhāgavata Purāṇa (12.13)
Tārkṣyafrom Tārkṣya, a Vedic name for him; recorded by the 11th-century scholar Al-Bīrūnī
Vainatēyafrom Vinatā, his mother; used in the Vāyu Purāṇa

Same book? Manuscripts under these titles exist. Whether each was the text we now call the Garuḍa Purāṇa, or a different work of the same name, is not settled. Quotations from a “Garuḍa Purāṇa” in old digests do not always match the surviving text.

HOW IT IS USED

A text that lives at funerals

🏠 The reading

In many families, especially in North India, a priest reads the Prēta section or the Sārōddhāra at home during the mourning days.

🎯 The purpose

The text says the departed soul stays near, hears, and understands where it is going; the family learns the rites.

🔀 Not everywhere

Many families and regions read other texts at a death, such as the Bhagavad Gītā or the Bhāgavata, or none.

📖 The rest

The Ācāra Kāṇḍa has been read as a manual: its medicine and gem chapters were studied for their own sake.

KEEP IT AT HOME?

A belief and its answer

The belief. Many people hold that the Garuḍa Purāṇa must not be kept in the house or read except after a death, for fear of inviting it.

The text. The Purāṇa itself contains no such rule. It praises hearing and reading it, like every Purāṇa does.

Teachers. Traditional scholars quoted in Telugu and Kannada editions call the belief a misunderstanding and say the book may be kept and read on any auspicious day.

Why it arose. Almost certainly from its funeral use: a book heard only in grief came to feel like grief itself.

ZAPPY: So I can read it today?

PROF. BOLT: Yes. Most of it is about living well, and the death chapters are written to make you live better, not to frighten you.

THE TWO FOLDERS

Part A: the Purāṇa · Part B: the Sārōddhāra
A1The Book and Its Bird

A3Worship, Vratas, Tīrthas
A4Ratna Parīkṣā: Gems
A5Dhanvantari’s Medicine
A6Stars, Signs, Omens
A7Garuḍa Nīti
A8Yoga and Knowledge

Part B. Sixteen guides, B1 to B16, one for each chapter of the Sārōddhāra: the road to Yama, the hells, birth and rebirth, the rites from deathbed to anniversary, Yama’s city, and the law of liberation.

Why two folders. Part B is the text heard in a house of mourning; it can be read on its own, or left aside, without the rest.

WHY IT MATTERS

What the whole book is for

🧩 Life is one

Worship, health, wealth, rule and death in one book: no part of life is outside dharma.

🪞 Death as teacher

The afterlife chapters look forward but speak backward: they are about how to live now.

🤲 Care for the dead

It gave families a clear order of duties at the hardest time.

🕊️ Release last

Both halves end in yoga and mukti, not in fear.

ZAPPY: One line?

PROF. BOLT: The Garuḍa Purāṇa is not a book about dying. It is a book about the whole of life, with death as one chapter.
