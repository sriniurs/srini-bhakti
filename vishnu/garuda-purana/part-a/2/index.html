GARUḌA PURĀṆA · PART A · 2 OF 8 · CREATION AND AVATĀRAS

THE WORLD BEGINS

How the Garuḍa Purāṇa tells creation, the families of beings, and Viṣṇu’s descents

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya

FOREWORD

Every Purāṇa must tell where the world came from and who peopled it. The Garuḍa does this quickly, almost like a reference book, and then gets on with practical things.

Its first chapters say how the Purāṇa itself began: Garuḍa’s boons, and a burnt tree that came back to life. Then come the nine creations, Dakṣa’s daughters, and Kaśyapa’s many wives, mothers of gods, demons, serpents and birds.

Much later in the book, the avatāras return with three of India’s great stories compressed into a chapter each: the Rāmāyaṇa, Kṛṣṇa’s life and the Mahābhārata.

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: The whole Mahābhārata in one chapter?

PROF. BOLT: One chapter. The Garuḍa assumes you know the stories; it just wants them in the library.

GARUḌA’S FIVE BOONS

The Purāṇa’s own account of its birth
Free my mother. Release for Vinatā from slavery to the serpents.
The nectar. Strength to defeat the gods and bring back the amṛta.
Your carrier. To become Viṣṇu’s mount.
Power over serpents. Strength, knowledge of all things, and power to afflict the nāgas.
Author. To compose Purāṇas and saṁhitās.

The occasion. Garuḍa worships Viṣṇu with austerities on earth, and Viṣṇu, pleased, offers a boon.

The answer. Viṣṇu grants all five, and adds that the Purāṇa Garuḍa composes will bear his name and be celebrated among all Purāṇas.

ZAPPY: So Garuḍa is the author, not just the listener?

PROF. BOLT: In this chapter, yes: his omniscience is a gift, and the book is its fruit. Other passages make him the one who heard it from Viṣṇu.

THE BURNT TREE

Proof of the Purāṇa’s power

The story. Viṣṇu tells that Garuḍa recited the Purāṇa to the sage Kaśyapa, and that Kaśyapa, by its power, brought a burnt tree back to life.

The point. The knowledge is shown to be life-giving from its first chapter, long before any talk of death.

Serpent lore. The tie between Kaśyapa, Garuḍa and the defeat of snake venom runs through the Purāṇa’s later chapters on mantras against poison.

THE PURĀṆA’S OWN LINEAGE

Chapter 2’s chain of tellers

Rudra’s questions. Śiva, meditating on Viṣṇu, asks who the supreme god is, how to worship him, what vows to keep, and how the world is made and unmade. The whole Purāṇa is Viṣṇu’s answer.

Two routes. Here the teaching reaches Vyāsa through Brahmā; the Garuḍa–Kaśyapa line runs beside it as a second stream.

Śiva as student. A Vaiṣṇava Purāṇa that puts Śiva in the questioner’s seat.

ONE GOD, THREE ROLES

Viṣṇu behind the trimūrti

🔁 The cycle

At the end of each kalpa, a day of Brahmā, the world is withdrawn; it is sent out again at the next dawn.

👫 Two powers

Lakṣmī is the creative power of nature, Viṣṇu the conscious spirit: two aspects of one reality.

ZAPPY: Śiva as a form of Viṣṇu?

PROF. BOLT: In a Vaiṣṇava Purāṇa, yes. The Śaiva Purāṇas say the reverse. Both agree the three are one power doing three jobs.

NINE CREATIONS

The sargas, in order
1 Mahatcosmic intelligence, the first stir of nature
2 Bhūtathe subtle elements
3 Vaikārikathe senses and their gods
4 Mukhyathe unmoving: mountains, trees
5 Tiryakanimals, moving horizontally
6 Dēvathe gods
7 Mānuṣahuman beings
8 Anugrahabeings of mixed light and dark nature
9 Kaumārathe eternal youths, Sanaka and his brothers

Two kinds. The first three come from nature itself (prākṛta); the rest are Brahmā’s work (vaikṛta). The ninth is counted as both. The same scheme appears in the Viṣṇu Purāṇa.

THE FIRST COUPLE

When mind-born creation was not enough
Mind-born sons. Brahmā first creates sages from his mind: Marīci, Atri, Aṅgiras, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu and others. They do not multiply.
Divided. Brahmā splits into male and female: Svāyambhuva Manu and Śatarūpā.
Their children. Two sons, Priyavrata and Uttānapāda, and daughters married to sages.
Dhruva. Uttānapāda’s son by Sunīti, who won the fixed place in the sky; the Purāṇa traces a dynasty from him.

ZAPPY: Why do the sages fail to multiply?

PROF. BOLT: Mind-born beings are complete in themselves. Ongoing life needs two, so the story moves from thought to couples.

DAKṢA’S SIXTY DAUGHTERS

Mothers of almost everything

Reborn Dakṣa. Born again as son of the Pracētas and Māriṣā, Dakṣa finds his mind-creation blocked, and fathers sixty daughters on Asiknī.

Dharma’s ten. Arundhatī, Vasu, Yāmī, Lambā, Bhānu, Marutvatī, Saṅkalpā, Muhūrtā, Sādhyā, Viśvā, mothers of the Vasus, Sādhyas, Viśvēdēvas and others.

Counts differ. Some versions give fourteen to Kaśyapa and twenty-eight to the Moon.

ZAPPY: Gods and demons are half-brothers?

PROF. BOLT: Cousins at least: same father, different mothers. Their wars are family quarrels, which is why they keep making truces and breaking them.

9Pṛthu: milked the earth, as a cow, to end famine

Same as the Bhāgavata. The list matches Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.3; translations number Rāma, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma slightly differently. The ten best known have their own pages: Daśāvatāra · 10 avatāras + overview.

WHY HE DESCENDS

Chapter 142’s account

🛡️ The reason

To protect the good, destroy the asuras and keep the Vēdic order alive; “unborn and undying,” he takes forms for his creation’s sake.

🐟 The flood

Here Matsya carries the Vēdas and slays the demon Hayagrīva who stole them.

👑 Four as one

Viṣṇu divides himself into all four sons of Daśaratha: Rāma, Bharata, Lakṣmaṇa and Śatrughna.

⚕️ From the ocean

Dhanvantari rises from the churning with the nectar; Viṣṇu as a woman shares it among the gods.

ZAPPY: Twenty-two in one place, ten in another?

PROF. BOLT: Ten is the famous shortlist. The Purāṇas also say the descents are countless; any list is a selection.

THE WIFE WHO STOPPED THE SUN

A story inside chapter 142
A cruel husband. Kauśika, a leprous Brahmin of Pratiṣṭhāna, treats his wife harshly; she serves him still, and even carries him to a courtesan’s house.
The curse. On the way, in the dark, she brushes against the sage Māṇḍavya, impaled on a false charge of theft. In pain he curses her: she will be a widow by sunrise.
No dawn. She declares that the sun shall not rise, and it does not. All creation is in darkness.
Anasūyā. Brahmā sends the gods to Anasūyā, the model of fidelity, who persuades her. Her husband is granted life and health, and the sun rises.

The lesson drawn. Brahmā says the power of a faithful wife overcame the power of a sage’s austerity. The chapter places Sītā’s faithfulness in Rāvaṇa’s house alongside Anasūyā’s.

THE RĀMĀYAṆA IN A CHAPTER

Chapter 143
Lineage Brahmā, Marīci, Kaśyapa, Sūrya, Manu, Ikṣvāku, down to Daśaratha.
Youth With Viśvāmitra, Rāma kills Tāṭakā and Subāhu, and wins Sītā.
Exile Kaikēyī’s demand: fourteen years in the forest; Daśaratha dies of grief; Bharata rules with Rāma’s sandals.
Loss Śūrpaṇakhā, Khara and Dūṣaṇa, the golden deer, Sītā taken; Jaṭāyu dies fighting.
War Sugrīva’s alliance, Vālī slain, Hanumān’s leap and Laṅkā aflame, Vibhīṣaṇa’s defection, the bridge, Rāvaṇa slain.
Return Sītā’s ordeal by fire; Ayōdhyā.
Reign Eleven thousand years, ten horse sacrifices, Lava and Kuśa crowned.

ZAPPY: Nothing new in it?

PROF. BOLT: The events are Vālmīki’s. The interest is the scale: an epic of 24,000 verses kept as a single chapter, so the library is complete.

KṚṢṆA IN A CHAPTER

Chapter 144: the Harivaṁśa

👶 Childhood

Born to Vasudēva and Dēvakī; Pūtanā, the cart demon, the twin trees, Kāḷiya, Dhēnuka; Gōvardhana lifted.

⚔️ Battles

Ariṣṭa and Kēśī killed, Kaṁsa overthrown; later Naraka, Śiśupāla and others.

👪 Family

Eight chief queens, Rukmiṇī and Satyabhāmā among them; Pradyumna, Aniruddha and Uṣā, and Vajra.

🌸 Wonders

The pārijāta tree brought from heaven; his teacher Sāndīpani’s dead son restored to life.

The ending. After Kṛṣṇa leaves the world, his great-grandson Vajra rules at Mathurā. The fuller story is in Kṛṣṇa.

THE MAHĀBHĀRATA IN A CHAPTER

Chapter 145
Line Brahmā, Atri, the Moon, Purūravas, Āyu, to Śantanu and Bhīṣma.
Rivals Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s sons against Pāṇḍu’s; Draupadī; Indraprastha.
Dice The kingdom lost, exile, a year hidden at Virāṭa’s court.
War Bhīṣma, Drōṇa, Karṇa and Śalya fall in turn; Bhīma kills Duryōdhana; Aśvatthāmā’s night raid.
After Yudhiṣṭhira’s horse sacrifice; Parīkṣit crowned.

Why it was fought. The chapter says Kṛṣṇa backed the Pāṇḍavas to lift the burden of wrong from the earth.

The bridge. It closes with Dhanvantari teaching medicine to Suśruta, and the very next chapters open the Purāṇa’s medical section.

WHAT IT TEACHES

Why creation and epics sit here

🌳 Everyone is kin

Gods, demons, serpents, birds and cattle share one grandfather. Creation is a family tree.

🔁 Nothing is final

Kalpas end and begin; the world is always being remade.

🙏 God keeps coming

Twenty-two descents and more: Viṣṇu does not create and walk away.

📚 Stories are dharma

The epics are retold not as entertainment but as teaching, with merit promised to the hearer.

ZAPPY: One line?

PROF. BOLT: The world comes from God, its peoples are one family, and when it goes wrong he comes back.
