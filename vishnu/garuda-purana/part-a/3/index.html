GARUḌA PURĀṆA · PART A · 3 OF 8 · WORSHIP, VRATAS, TĪRTHAS

HOW TO WORSHIP

The Purāṇa’s handbook of gods, vows and holy places

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya

FOREWORD

A large share of the Ācāra Kāṇḍa is a priest’s handbook. It tells which god to worship, with which syllables, on which day, and where.

It is wider than its Vaiṣṇava label suggests. Śiva, Sūrya, Durgā, Gaṇapati and the Mothers all get their rites, and Śivarātri gets one of its best-loved stories.

This part walks through the worship chapters, the twenty-odd chapters of vratas, the list of ninety-four holy places, and Gayā, the place the Purāṇa ranks above all others for the dead.

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: A Viṣṇu Purāṇa that teaches Śiva pūjā?

PROF. BOLT: Over and over. The Purāṇa’s own preface calls Viṣṇu supreme, then gives everyone’s rites. That was how a household actually worshipped.

ZAPPY: That’s nearly half the Ācāra Kāṇḍa’s early chapters.

PROF. BOLT: Yes. The medicine and gems get the fame, but worship takes more space.

THE GODS OF THE RITES

Who gets a chapter

🔵 Viṣṇu

Many forms: as Hayagrīva, the horse-headed lord of learning; as Gōpāla; through his discus, Sudarśana.

☀️ Sūrya

Sun worship with its own mantras; the Purāṇa is noted for its chapters on the Sun.

🔱 Śiva

Several forms of Śiva pūjā, including the five-faced Śiva.

🌺 The Goddess

Durgā, Gāyatrī and Tripurā, each with seed syllables and a diagram.

The frame of daily life. Beside these sit initiation (dīkṣā), the twilight prayers (sandhyā), and the worship of the teacher’s sandals.

A THOUSAND NAMES

Viṣṇu Sahasranāma in the Garuḍa

📿 What it is

A hymn of a thousand names of Viṣṇu, given in the Purāṇa’s opening chapters on his worship.

🔀 Not the famous one

The one sung in homes and temples is from the Mahābhārata, taught by Bhīṣma. The Garuḍa’s is its own list.

🔁 Many thousands

Several Purāṇas carry thousand-name hymns of Viṣṇu, Śiva and Devī; the form was a way to praise every quality at once.

📖 Related

The Mahābhārata hymn: Viṣṇu Sahasranāma · 10 parts.

ZAPPY: Two different thousand-name hymns to the same god?

PROF. BOLT: More than two. Each Purāṇa that praised Viṣṇu wanted its own; the Mahābhārata’s just became the one everyone sings.

MOTHERS AND BHAIRAVAS

A tantric chapter inside a Vaiṣṇava book
The gaṇas. Chapter 24 opens with worship of Śiva’s attendants under Gaṇapati.
Durgā. Her mantra is placed on the worshipper’s heart and limbs; eight fierce Śaktis are called.
The Mothers. In a lotus diagram: Brāhmī, Māhēśvarī, Kaumārī, Vaiṣṇavī, Vārāhī, Cāmuṇḍā and Caṇḍikā, then the eight Bhairavas.
Inner worship. A triangle with a lotus is pictured in the heart, holding Kāma, Rati, the Yōginīs, Durgā and others.
Siddhi. A hundred thousand repetitions of the mantra, with fire offerings, bring Tripurā’s power.

Why it matters. Scholars date the Purāṇa partly by such Tantric chapters: they belong to a later layer of Indian worship.

AGAINST POISON

Garuḍa’s own speciality

🐍 The problem

Snakebite was a daily danger; the Purāṇa gives mantras and rites to remove venom.

🦅 The logic

Garuḍa is the serpents’ enemy, so his name and form are the remedy. Healers who treat bites by mantra are called gāruḍikas.

🛡️ Armour

A Vaiṣṇava kavaca, a protective hymn, appears in the Purāṇa’s medical half.

⚕️ Both ways

The medicine chapters also treat poison with drugs; mantra and medicine stand side by side.

ZAPPY: Does it work?

PROF. BOLT: The text claims so; medicine today says antivenom. Historically these chapters show how seriously the danger was taken.

HOUSE, TEMPLE, IMAGE

Chapters 46–48

📐 Vāstu

The site is a grid of 64 squares; deities guard each square, Brahmā at the centre, and are worshipped before building.

🏛️ Temple styles

Temples are classed by shape and proportion into many named types.

🪔 Installation

Pratiṣṭhā: rites that bring life into a new image before it is worshipped.

🏠 Houses too

Palaces and dwellings get rules of their own.

How to keep a vow. Fast, take one meal at night, or eat only fruit, and give gifts. Hari is to be worshipped in every month and on every weekday.

EKĀDAŚĪ

The eleventh day, both fortnights

👑 The example

King Māndhātā became lord of three worlds by keeping Ekādaśī, so both eleventh days are to be fasted.

❄️ The claim

As cold removes heat, Ekādaśī removes sin, even the gravest sins named in the law books.

🌙 Bhaimī

The Ekādaśī of Māgha is singled out as Bhaimī, fasted and broken on the twelfth.

⏳ Timing

Rules cover the case when the eleventh day overlaps the tenth or twelfth.

ZAPPY: Why the eleventh day?

PROF. BOLT: Every school gives its reason, but they agree on the practice: on Ekādaśī, the body rests and the mind turns to Viṣṇu.

ŚIVARĀTRI

Chapter 124: the rite
When The fourteenth night of the waning moon between Māgha and Phālguna.
The day before Self-control, and a vow to fast, keep vigil and worship.
The night The liṅga bathed with pañcagavya and pañcāmṛta, worshipped with “Ōṁ namaḥ Śivāya”; rice, barley and sesame with ghee offered in the fire.
Watches Worship in each quarter of the night; the vow’s story heard from a priest.
Dawn Pardon asked; the merit given to Śiva.
Years Kept for twelve years; it ends with feeding twelve ascetics and lighting the temple lamps.

Its source. Brahmā says Gaurī learned this vow from Mahādēva himself.

THE HUNTER AND THE BILVA

The story told on Śivarātri night
A cruel king. Sundarasēna of Arbuda goes hunting with his dogs and finds nothing by nightfall.
An accident of worship. Resting beside a pool, he shakes down bilva leaves onto a liṅga, splashes it with water, and an arrow falls against it, all on Śivarātri, all unknowing.
The end. Years later Yama’s messengers come; Śiva’s attendants drive them off and take him, and his faithful dog, to Śiva’s world.

MORE VOWS

A few of the twenty-odd vrata chapters
Anaṅga TrayōdaśīBright 13th of Mārgaśīrṣa; Śiva worshipped with dhattūra flowers; health, wealth, a good spouse, children
Akhaṇḍa DvādaśīA vow that keeps the merit of seven past lives “unbroken”; in Mārgaśīrṣa one lives on the five cow products
Śravaṇa DvādaśīThe twelfth under the Śravaṇa star; worldly joy and release
Bhīṣma PañcakaFive days, Kārttika bright 11th to full moon; ancestors honoured, then Viṣṇu
Madana TrayōdaśīŚiva worshipped with damanaka leaves on the bright 13th, sacred to Kāma

ZAPPY: The rewards are very worldly.

PROF. BOLT: Sons, land, wealth, a spouse, then heaven or release. The Purāṇa makes no apology: vows serve this life and the next.

NINETY-FOUR HOLY PLACES

Chapter 81: the whole map of India in a list

🌊 First

The Gaṅgā, then Haridvār, Prayāga, Gaṅgāsāgara and Vārāṇasī.

🏔️ North

Kurukṣētra, Kēdāra, Badarī, Naimiṣa, Puṣkara, Ayōdhyā, Mathurā, Gōvardhana, Kāmarūpa, Paśupati.

🌅 West

Prabhāsa, Dvārakā, Ujjayinī, Nāsika, Māhiṣmatī.

🛕 East

Puruṣōttama (Purī), Ēkāmra (Bhubaneswar), Viraja, Mahēndra hill, Puṇḍravardhana.

Rivers as tīrthas. Nearly a quarter of the list are rivers: Kāvērī, Gōdāvarī, Kṛṣṇā, Tāpī, Gaṇḍakī, Śōṇa and more. The list ends by naming Gayā, which gets the next five chapters.

GAYĀSURA

Chapter 82: how Gayā became holy
A too-holy asura. Gaya’s austerities are so great they trouble the universe; the gods run to Viṣṇu.
The fault. Gaya picks lotuses from Viṣṇu’s waters, meant for Śiva, and makes them his bed in the land of Kīkaṭa.
The fall. Viṣṇu’s mace strikes him down.
The gift. Viṣṇu stays at Gayā on the asura’s body to give release to pilgrims; Brahmā and Śiva take their places there too.

WHAT GAYĀ PROMISES

Śrāddha at Gayā

🕊️ Four roads

Release comes by knowledge of Brahman, śrāddha at Gayā, death in a cowshed, or dwelling at Kurukṣētra.

🍚 For all the dead

Śrāddha there helps even those who died without rites, by theft, by wild beasts or by snakebite.

🪙 Brahmā’s curse

He curses Gayā’s priests for hoarding gold, then softens it: they will live on pilgrims’ fees and śrāddha gifts.

📍 Five chapters

Ch. 83–86 name the spots in and around Gayā and the rite and merit at each.

ZAPPY: The priests are cursed in the scripture itself?

PROF. BOLT: And allowed their living in the same breath. The story explains how a pilgrim town’s priesthood earned its keep.

WHAT IT TEACHES

The logic of the handbook

🧭 Time is sacred

Each lunar day has its god; the calendar itself is a path of worship.

🗺️ Space is sacred

Rivers, hills and towns across India are mapped as one holy land.

🙏 Grace beats intention

The hunter meant nothing and won Śiva’s world; the act counts.

🤝 All gods, one house

A Vaiṣṇava text gives Śiva, Sūrya and Devī their full due.

ZAPPY: One line?

PROF. BOLT: Pick your day, your place and your god: the Garuḍa has a rite for each.

Three ways to worship
The Garuḍa’s worship has three coordinates: a god to worship, a day to keep, and a place to go, with Gayā crowning them all for the dead. 🙏
