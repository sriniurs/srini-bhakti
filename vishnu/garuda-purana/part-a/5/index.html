GARUḌA PURĀṆA · PART A · 5 OF 8 · DHANVANTARI SAṀHITĀ

THE PURĀṆA OF HEALING

Dhanvantari teaches Suśruta: diagnosis, disease and cure

Ōṁ Namō Bhagavatē Dhanvantarayē

FOREWORD

The largest single block of the Ācāra Kāṇḍa is not about gods at all. It is a medical textbook, set in the mouth of Dhanvantari, physician of the gods, teaching his pupil Suśruta.

It runs disease by disease: causes, early signs, full signs, course, and which cases cannot be cured. Then it turns to treatment, diet and long lists of recipes.

Scholars have shown that much of it follows the classical Āyurveda texts closely. Its value is that a Purāṇa, heard by ordinary people, carried a doctor’s knowledge into every household that owned it.

Ōṁ Dhanvantarayē Namaḥ,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: A medical textbook inside a Purāṇa?

PROF. BOLT: Fifty-odd chapters of one. Read it as a window on how classical Indian medicine was taught.

AT A GLANCE

The medical chapters, in Dutt’s numbering
Ch. 146General pathology: diagnosis and the doṣas
Ch. 147–191Disease by disease: fever first, then bleeding, cough, urinary disease, abscess, ear, nose, eye, fistula, poison, ulcers, fractures
Ch. 192Principles: doṣas, tissues, tastes, the four pillars, diet
Ch. 193 onTreatments, then recipe after recipe
LaterHorses, elephants, plants, protective hymns

Its name. The block is called the Dhanvantari Saṁhitā. Other editions number it from about 146 to 218.

ZAPPY: Diagnosis first, treatment after?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly like a modern textbook: pathology section, then therapeutics.

WHO TEACHES WHOM

A chain of physicians

🌊 Dhanvantari

Rose from the churned ocean with the nectar; counted among Viṣṇu’s avatāras.

🔪 Suśruta

The name of the great surgical classic, the Suśruta Saṁhitā.

Borrowed learning. Ludo Rocher traced parts of this section to Vāgbhaṭa’s Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya; others trace its food lore to the Suśruta Saṁhitā.

SIX STEPS OF DIAGNOSIS

Chapter 146
Nidāna. The cause that sets the disease off.
Pūrvarūpa. Vague early signs, before the disease declares itself.
Rūpa. The full, distinguishing signs.
Upaśaya. What makes it better or worse: drug, food, habit. A test by trial.
Saṁprāpti. How it develops and where it settles in the body.
Vijñāna. The diagnosis itself.

ZAPPY: It says five and lists six?

PROF. BOLT: The text does announce five. Many traditions count the first five as the pentad and treat the sixth, diagnosis, as their result.

THE THREE DOṢAS

What upsets each, and when it peaks
Upset byWorst in
Vāyuover-exertion, dry or hot food, fearsummer; after meals; end of day and night
Pittapungent, sour, hot food; irritabilityautumn; midday and midnight
Kaphasweet, salty, cold, heavy food; sitting; day-sleepspring; early day and night

The model. The three doṣas, out of balance, spoil the blood and tissues; each disease is then read by which doṣa leads. Chapter 192 adds the seven tissues (dhātu) and the wastes (mala).

ZAPPY: Seasons and times of day too?

PROF. BOLT: Yes: the same doṣa that is harmless at noon may flare at dusk. Timing is part of diagnosis.

WHY PEOPLE FALL ILL

Chapter 146’s list of causes

🍽️ Food

Irregular or clashing meals, stale, dried or raw food.

🌦️ Season

Unseasonal weather, the east wind, sudden changes of habit.

🧠 Mind

Suppressed urges and dishonest conduct.

⭐ Fate

The birth star’s influence; the strain after childbirth.

A mix. Diet, environment, behaviour and astrology sit side by side. The text does not separate what we would call medical and moral causes.

FEVER, LORD OF DISEASES

Chapter 147

🔥 Its birth

Fever arose from Rudra’s anger when he was insulted at Dakṣa’s sacrifice.

👑 Its titles

“Lord of diseases” and “lord of death”.

🐘 In animals

Pākala in elephants, abhitāpa in horses, alarka in dogs.

😢 Other roots

Injury, poison, burns and contagion; and anger, fear, grief and frustrated desire.

Seasons. Vāta fevers in the rains, Pitta in autumn, Kapha in spring. Recovery shows as fading heat and pain, returning appetite and a calm mind.

FEVERS THAT COME AND GO

Five patterns, deeper and deeper

Five types. Continuous, twice-daily, daily, every third day and every fourth day: the old names for what later medicine called remittent, tertian and quartan fevers.

Into the tissues. Each lodges deeper, from plasma and blood through flesh and fat to marrow and semen. The deepest are called incurable.

A fatal one. Haridrābha, a fever with yellowing, is described as deadly.

ONE ORGAN: THE NOSE

Chapter 170, as a sample
Pīnasablocked, dry nose
Pūtināsyafoul-smelling discharge
Nāsāpākainflamed, pustular nose
Kṣavathusneezing
Nāsāśōṣadryness that hampers breathing
Pratiśyāyacatarrh: the main subject

Catarrh’s triggers. Holding back natural urges, dust, cold water, mist, and weeping.

If neglected. It may lead to deafness, cough, eye disease and loss of smell; polyps and nosebleeds are also listed.

ABSCESS, FISTULA, ULCER

The surgical side

🫧 Vidradhi

Abscess, classed by doṣa; blamed on stale or hot food and hard bedding. Scrotal swellings and internal masses (gulma) follow.

🕳️ Bhagandara

Fistula-in-ano, five kinds, one from a thorn prick. Cases discharging stool or urine through the tract are called incurable.

🩹 Vraṇa

Ulcers: from within (śārīra) or from weapons (āgantuka), each doṣa giving a distinct look and discharge.

⚠️ Marma

An ulcer on a vital point, or one that will not heal with proper care, is incurable.

ZAPPY: They told you when not to treat?

PROF. BOLT: Constantly. Knowing the incurable case was part of a good physician’s honesty.

SIX JOINT INJURIES

Chapter 191: dislocations and fractures at the joint
Utpiṣṭabone ends crushed together; swelling on both sides; pain worse at night
Viśliṣṭathe joint loosened apart
Vivartitatwisted; severe pain on each side
Tiryaggatadisplaced sideways at an angle
Kṣiptathrown out; the two limbs unequal in length
Adhaḥkṣiptadropped below the socket; ends grating

Common signs. A loose limb that cannot bend or straighten, tenderness, swelling, and a grating sound when the break is pressed.

TWELVE SHAFT FRACTURES

Named by the shape of the break

The full twelve. Karkaṭaka, Aśvakarṇa, Vicūrṇita, Picchita, Challika, Kāṇḍabhagna, Atipātita, Majjāgata, Sphuṭita, Vakra, and two kinds of Chinna.

By bone type. Soft bone bends, hollow long bone bursts, flat skull bone breaks, tooth-like bone splinters.

Prognosis. Poor with fever, weak digestion, bloating or retention, and above all if the fracture was not reduced properly at first. Sacral injuries, shattered thighs and badly set fractures are called incurable. The chapter gives no treatment.

FOUR PILLARS OF TREATMENT

Chapter 192

🧮 Weigh first

Season, place, age and constitution before choosing a remedy.

⏳ When to stop

Treat only while the patient’s strength is not at its lowest; the signs of approaching death are listed.

Also here. The six tastes and their actions, four kinds of indigestion, and the classic daśamūla, the “ten roots” preparation.

FOOD AS MEDICINE

The survey that ends chapter 192

🌾 Grains & pulses

Each rice, barley and lentil rated for the doṣa it raises or calms.

🥬 Vegetables & fruit

Leaves, roots and fruits by taste and effect.

🧂 Salts & spices

Their heat or coolness and use.

🥛 Waters, milk, oils

Different waters, dairy, oils, sweeteners and soups.

ZAPPY: So every food has a doṣa effect?

PROF. BOLT: In this system, yes. Diet is the first and daily medicine; drugs come after.

RECIPES AND CHARMS

The later chapters

🌿 Remedies

Pastes for teeth, juices for ear pain and discharge, pills for indigestion and cholera, eye applications.

🔮 Charms

Mixed among them: spells and preparations for attraction, favour at court, quarrel, invisibility.

👶 Fertility

Preparations said to aid conception or to prevent it.

🎙️ The voice

These later recipe chapters are spoken by Hari, not Dhanvantari.

Read as history. Medicine and magic share the same pages, as in many pre-modern texts everywhere. None of it is a prescription.

PLANTS AND ANIMALS

Beyond human medicine

🌱 Herbs

Lists of more than 450 plants and herbs with their uses.

🐎 Horses

Their diseases and treatment.

🐘 Elephants

A royal animal, given its own medicine.

🛡️ Kavaca

A Vaiṣṇava protective hymn and Garuḍa mantras sit inside the medical block: prayer as part of care.

ZAPPY: Veterinary chapters in scripture?

PROF. BOLT: Horses and elephants were a king’s army. Their health was a matter of state.

WHAT IT TEACHES

Medicine as dharma

🩺 Look before you treat

Six steps of diagnosis before any remedy.

⚖️ Balance is health

Disease is a disturbed balance; cure restores it.

🤲 Honest limits

Name what cannot be cured; do not torment the dying.

🍲 Daily care

Food, season and habit come before drugs.

ZAPPY: One line?

PROF. BOLT: The body is a duty too: to keep it is dharma, and the Purāṇa gives the manual.
