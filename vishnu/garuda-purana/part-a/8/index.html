GARUḌA PURĀṆA · PART A · 8 OF 8 · YOGA AND KNOWLEDGE

THE WAY OUT

How the first half of the Purāṇa ends: yoga, devotion and knowing the self

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya

FOREWORD

After rites, gems, medicine, omens and law, the Ācāra Kāṇḍa turns inward. Its closing chapters ask: what is all this for, and how does one get free of the round of birth and death?

The answer comes in three voices. The yogin says: still the mind and know the self. The devotee says: remember Hari’s name at every moment. The philosopher says: see that the self was never bound.

The section ends with a short Bhagavad Gītā of its own and a promise to whoever has heard the Purāṇa this far.

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: So the first half ends with release, not with death.

PROF. BOLT: Right. The famous death chapters come after this. The living half has already pointed the way out.

Numbers. Dutt’s translation runs to 248 chapters here; most editions close the Ācāra Kāṇḍa between 229 and 243. Some call these last chapters the Brahma Gītā.

THE HOUSEHOLDER’S DAY

Chapter 229
Before dawn Rise; cleanse the body with clay and water; clean the teeth.
Bath Several kinds of bath, each with its merit.
Sandhyā Twilight prayer and the Gāyatrī.
Fire Offering into the home fire.
Five offerings The five daily yajñas: to the Vēda, the ancestors, the gods, all beings, and guests.
Ancestors Water offered to them.
Meal Food shared first, then eaten in the proper order.

Sources of duty. Vēdic rites first; failing that, the Smṛtis; failing that, good conduct. Truth, charity, self-control, non-violence and not stealing bind everyone.

THE WHEEL OF EXISTENCE

Chapter 234: saṁsāra cakra

The round. After death a subtle body is led to Yama over twelve days, fed by the water and sesame its family offers. Then heaven or hell, then a womb, birth, infancy, youth, old age, death: like an hourglass turned again and again.

Carried over. Those fresh from hell tend to cruelty and meanness; those from heaven, to kindness and truth.

The exit. The eight-limbed yoga brings final dissolution of bondage.

THE TREE OF EGO

Chapter 235: Dattātreya teaches Alarka

The root. The sense of “I” and “mine” is a tree grown from ego. Its branches, leaves, flowers and fruits are our attachments and what they bring.

The axe. Knowledge cuts it down, and the one who is free merges in Brahman.

The teacher. Dattātrēya, honoured as the guru of eight-limbed yoga, teaches King Alarka.

EIGHT LIMBS

Chapters 49, 235 and 248
Yamanon-harm, truth, not stealing, continence, not grasping
Niyamapurity, contentment, austerity, study, devotion to Hari
Āsanasteady posture
Prāṇāyāmaregulated breath
Pratyāhārasenses drawn back from their objects
Dhāraṇāholding the mind on one point
Dhyānameditation
Samādhiabsorption

A Vaiṣṇava touch. Devotion to Vāsudēva sits inside the niyamas: yoga and bhakti are not rivals here.

BREATH AND FOCUS

Details from chapter 235

🌬️ Three grades

Breath control of 10, 20 and 30 units: light, middle and full.

😴 Obstacles

Drowsiness, trembling and heat arise in turn and must be passed.

🎯 Ten points

Hold the mind on one of ten places: navel, heart, throat, tip of the nose, between the brows and others.

🧭 Two meditations

First on Brahman with form, then on Brahman without form.

ZAPPY: Very close to Patañjali.

PROF. BOLT: The same eight limbs. The Purāṇa makes them a household practice and adds devotion.

I AM THE LIGHT

Meditation on Ōṁ

The syllable. Ōṁ, made of A, U and M, is repeated as the sound of the universal self.

The affirmation. The meditator then says, step by step, that he is the supreme light, free of body, senses, elements, breath and ignorance.

An earlier echo. Chapter 49 had already ended its yoga with Tat tvam asi, “That thou art”: the self is Brahman.

THE POWER OF THE NAME

Chapters 236–237

🙇 A bow

One prostration to Kṛṣṇa outweighs many horse sacrifices.

📿 Always

Say “Namō Nārāyaṇāya” in every posture; it is a pity to choose hell while one can still speak it.

🕯️ At the end

Even a dying person who sings the names is freed of sin; those who call Acyuta, Ananta or Vāsudēva are never taken to Yama.

🌊 Two sounds

“Ha” and “ri”, said with love, carry one across the ocean of birth.

ZAPPY: That’s a long way from the hell chapters.

PROF. BOLT: It’s the answer to them. The same Purāṇa that describes Yama’s road says the name keeps you off it.

FAITH OVER RITUAL

Chapters 238–239
Simple offerings. Flowers or water with the Puruṣa Sūkta are enough; sincere devotion counts for more than costly gifts.
Worth more than pilgrimage. Visiting sixty-six thousand holy places is worth less than a sixteenth of one act of devotion.
The one atonement. Remembering Viṣṇu clears sins that bathing in holy water cannot.
Inner ages. The golden age is Kali for one who forgets Gōvinda; Kali is golden for one who holds Acyuta in the heart.
At home. Devotees can be freed without giving up household life.

A turn in the book. After hundreds of chapters of rites and pilgrimages, the Purāṇa ranks them all below remembrance.

A TRUE VAIṢṆAVA

Chapters 239–240

👅 Use the body

The tongue is for praising Hari, the heart for holding him, the hands for offering.

⚕️ Like a doctor

Sins vanish at contact with Kēśava as disease yields to a good physician.

🤝 No outcastes

A devotee born among the lowest equals a Brahmin in purity; even birds and insects that turn to Hari are raised.

🎁 Offer the act

Actions offered to God bind no one.

ZAPPY: Equal to a Brahmin?

PROF. BOLT: In devotion, yes, says the text. It sits uneasily beside the caste rules of earlier chapters; the Purāṇa holds both.

FOUR HYMNS

Chapters 241–244
241Hymn to Nṛsiṁha
242–243Hymns to Viṣṇu, one composed by Mārkaṇḍēya
244Acyuta Stōtra: Brahmā’s hymn to the “one who never falls”, taught to Nārada

The Acyuta hymn. A long chain of salutations: to the bearer of Śrīvatsa, the Man-lion, the Boar, the protector of Gōkula. Viṣṇu is the teacher, the mantra, the rite and every being.

Its promise. Said at the end of worship or thrice daily: freedom from rebirth, and a son, health, wealth, freedom or learning for whoever lacks it.

A confession. The singer admits his worship has been imperfect and asks forgiveness.

KNOWLEDGE OF THE SELF

Chapters 245–246: Brahmā teaches Nārada

🧘 Two paths

Advaita yoga and Sāṅkhya yoga, both needing a fully gathered mind.

🔥 Karma burned

Knowing the self destroys the fruits of past, present and future acts.

🌙 Three states

Waking, dream and deep sleep belong to māyā; the self is beyond them, formless and actionless.

🚪 Rites can’t free

Rites may purify the mind but do not liberate; release comes by knowledge and the end of desire.

ZAPPY: So devotion and knowledge both claim to be the way?

PROF. BOLT: Side by side, a few chapters apart. The Purāṇa doesn’t choose; it offers both doors.

THE GĪTĀ IN A CHAPTER

Chapter 247: Gītāsāra

The core. Realising the self is the highest good of life. The self is not body, ego, mind or senses; it is the witness, a smokeless flame in the heart.

The mirror. When sin is cleared, the self is reflected in the mind as in a mirror, and right knowledge dawns.

Dissolution. Each layer merges into the next until the self knows itself as Brahman.

Related. The full Gītā: Bhagavad Gītā · 18 chapters + overview.

THE HEARER’S REWARD

Chapter 248: how the first half closes

🪷 The last teaching

The eight limbs once more, and meditation first on Brahman with form, then without.

🎧 The promise

Vyāsa says that reciting or hearing the Garuḍa Purāṇa brings merit.

🎁 The reciter

Whoever recites it should be honoured with gifts.

📖 Next

The Prēta Kāṇḍa follows: Garuḍa’s questions about death.

ZAPPY: A blessing for reading it, at the end of the living half.

PROF. BOLT: Every Purāṇa has its phalaśruti. This one sits before the death chapters, not after them: the hearer is blessed for learning how to live.

WHAT IT TEACHES

Three doors out of the wheel

🧘 Yoga

Still the mind, cut the tree of ego.

🙏 Bhakti

Hold Hari in the heart at every moment.

💡 Jñāna

Know the self as never bound.

🏠 Anywhere

All three are open to the householder.

ZAPPY: One line?

PROF. BOLT: The wheel turns until you stop it from within, by stillness, love or understanding.
