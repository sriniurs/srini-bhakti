GARUḌA PURĀṆA · PART B · SĀRŌDDHĀRA · CHAPTER 1 OF 16

THE MISERIES OF THE SINFUL

From the deathbed to the first step on Yama’s road

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya

FOREWORD

This folder, Part B, takes the Garuḍa Purāṇa Sārōddhāra one chapter at a time. It is the book priests read in homes during the days after a death, and the one most people mean when they say “Garuḍa Purāṇa”.

Chapter 1 sets the scene and follows a sinful man from his last illness to the thirteenth day after his death, when he sets out on the road to Yama. Its sixty verses are walked through here in order.

The language is harsh in places. It is meant to be: the book wants the hearer to change before the same road opens for him.

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Should I read Part A first?

PROF. BOLT: Not needed. Part B stands on its own. Part A explains the larger Purāṇa it comes from.

WHAT THE SĀRŌDDHĀRA IS

“The essence drawn out”

✍️ Its author

Navanidhirāma, son of Śrī Hari Nārāyaṇa, writing at Jhuñjhunū in Rajasthan under a ruler named Sukhalāla.

🎯 Its aim

The essence of the Garuḍa Purāṇa for readers who could not follow the older works; he says it is his own work, from long study.

📚 Its shape

Sixteen chapters: 1–7 the hells and rebirth, 7–13 the rites, 14 Yama’s city, 15–16 yoga and release.

🏠 Its use

Read across India at funerals; some avoid it at other times as inauspicious.

ZAPPY: So it isn’t the Purāṇa itself?

PROF. BOLT: It’s a digest with its own arrangement, drawing on a version of the Prēta Kāṇḍa and other texts. Scholars long confused the two.

THE TRANSLATORS’ VIEW OF HELL

From the 1911 introduction

🔧 Reform, not revenge

Hell’s pains are corrective and temporary, leaving a lasting mark on conscience.

🔢 How many

Yājñavalkya counts 21 hells; the Viṣṇu Smṛti 22.

🧭 Where

They place hell in a subtle region toward the south pole, heaven toward the north.

💧 Repentance first

Repentance is the highest penance; bodily penance is secondary. They criticise priests who sold penances.

Earthly hells. They also point to prison and social exclusion as hell-like suffering in this world.

THE MADHUSŪDANA TREE

Verse 1: the opening blessing
RootDharma, the law
Trunkthe Vēdas
Branchesthe Purāṇas
Flowerssacrifices
Fruitliberation

The tree is Viṣṇu. Madhusūdana, “slayer of Madhu”, is pictured as a single tree in which all of scripture grows.

The point. A book about death opens with a picture whose fruit is mōkṣa. The aim is set before the first word about hell.

ZAPPY: The Purāṇas are just branches?

PROF. BOLT: Branches of one tree. They spread the Vēda’s teaching wide, but the fruit they bear is the same.

THE SAGES ASK

Verses 2–7
Naimiṣa. Sages led by Śaunaka have been offering sacrifice for thousands of years in the Naimiṣa forest, seeking heaven.
Their question. After the morning offerings they ask Sūta about the fearsome way of Yama, the miseries of the changing world, and how its pains end.
Sūta’s promise. He will describe the hard road, “happiness to the virtuous, misery to the sinful”.
His source. He will repeat what Viṣṇu once told Garuḍa.

Three questions in one. The sages ask about death, about life’s suffering, and about the way out. The book answers all three, in that order.

GARUḌA IN VAIKUṆṬHA

Verses 8–13

🙇 He bows

Garuḍa approaches Hari in Vaikuṇṭha with reverence.

❓ He asks

He has heard the paths of devotion; now he asks about the way of Yama, travelled by those who turn from devotion.

His puzzle. Hari’s name is so easy to say, he observes, that he scorns those who still end in hell. What happens to them, and how?

WHO ARE THE SINFUL?

Verses 14–18
😈They delight in wrongdoing
💔They lack compassion and righteousness
👥They cling to the wicked
📕They reject scripture and good people
🪞They are self-satisfied and proud of wealth
🌀They are tangled in delusion and desire

The two ends. The wise reach the highest goal; the sinful suffer Yama’s torments. Viṣṇu says he will tell first their suffering in this world, then after death.

ZAPPY: It’s about character, not single acts.

PROF. BOLT: Yes. The list describes a way of living. The specific sins come later, in chapter 4.

THE LAST ILLNESS

Verses 19–25
19 Past actions bring disease.
20 Death comes suddenly, “like a serpent”, to a man still hoping to live.
21–22 Unready, he lies at home, tended by dependents, his body failing.
23–24 He eats little, barely moves; eyes watering, breath clogged, a rattle in the throat; he cannot answer those who speak.
25 His mind on his family, his senses unruly, he dies in pain while relatives weep.

ZAPPY: Painfully realistic.

PROF. BOLT: Anyone who has sat at a deathbed will recognise it. The text uses that recognition to make its point land.

THE FINAL MOMENT

Verses 26–29

👁️ A vision

All the worlds appear to him as one, but he cannot speak of it.

🧠 Fading

Senses decay, intellect numbs; the messengers arrive; life goes.

⏳ An age

The moment feels endless, like the sting of a hundred scorpions.

🚪 The exit

The life-breaths leave by the lower opening.

A contrast to come. Chapter 9 says the breaths of the righteous leave by the upper openings, and a yōgin’s by the crown.

THE MESSENGERS

Verses 30–36
Their look. Two, naked and fierce, black as crows, hair on end, teeth grinding, nails like weapons, carrying nooses and rods.
Drawn out. The man, now the size of a thumb, is dragged from his body while he watches it.
Bound. They wrap him in a body made for suffering, tie a noose round his neck, and lead him “like a convict”.
Threats. “Hurry, wicked one”: they name Kumbhīpāka and the other hells ahead. Hearing his family weep, he wails, and is beaten.

THE FIRST JOURNEY

Verses 37–40

😰 Fear

His heart failing, he trips on thorns and remembers his misdeeds.

🔥 Heat

Scorching sun, forest fires, hot winds; burning sand with no shade or water.

🌑 Darkness

Falling, fainting, rising again, he is led through the dark.

🏛️ A preview

At Yama’s abode the messengers show him the tortures of hell.

ZAPPY: He goes all the way to Yama on the first day?

PROF. BOLT: A quick first visit. He sees what awaits, then is sent back. The real journey starts later.

BACK THROUGH THE AIR

Verses 41–46
Sent back. After seeing Yama, he is ordered home and returns through the air with the messengers.
Longing. Held by old tendencies and the noose, he craves a body; hungry and thirsty, he weeps.
No comfort. His children’s piṇḍas and gifts reach him, but a sinful man is not satisfied by them; even eating, he stays hungry.
Worse without. Those who get no offerings at all wander an empty forest until the end of the age.
Karma’s rule. Unspent karma never fades; without suffering it, a being does not win human birth again.

FOUR SHARES OF A PIṆḌA

Verses 47–49

Ten days. The son offers a piṇḍa every day for ten days, each divided into four.

The tenth day. After nine days and nights of offerings, on the tenth the departed has a full body and strength.

ZAPPY: The messengers get a share?

PROF. BOLT: Their share, in effect. The text simply assigns it: even the escorts on the road are provided for.

THE BODY OF TEN DAYS

Verses 50–54
Day 1head
Day 2neck and shoulders
Day 3heart
Day 4back
Day 5navel
Day 6hips and private parts
Day 7thighs
Day 8–9knees and feet
Day 10hunger and thirst
Day 11–12the departed eats

Its size and use. The old body is burned; the new one, a hand’s length, is made of the offerings, and in it the departed tastes the good and evil of the road.

THE THIRTEENTH DAY

Verses 55–57

🐒 Departure

Bound by Yama’s servants, he walks the road alone “like a captured monkey”.

📏 Length

86,000 yōjanas, not counting the Vaitaraṇī.

🚶 Pace

247 yōjanas a day, without stopping day or night.

🗓️ The sum

About 348 days: the year of rites the family keeps.

ZAPPY: Why does mourning end on the thirteenth day in many families?

PROF. BOLT: This verse is one reason: the departed has left the house and set out.

SIXTEEN CITIES AHEAD

Verses 58–60

The list. Saumya, Sauripura, Nāgēndrabhavana, Gāndharva, Śailāgama, Krauñca, Krūrapura, Vicitrabhavana, Bahvāpada, Duḥkhada, Nānākrandapura, Sutaptabhavana, Raudra, Payōvarṣaṇa, Śītāḍhya, Bahubhīti.

The close. Held by Yama’s noose and crying out, the sinner leaves his house for the road. Chapter 2 walks it city by city.

READING IT TODAY

How families and teachers use chapter 1

📅 A calendar

It explains the ten daily piṇḍas and why mourning ends on the thirteenth day.

🪞 A mirror

Its portrait of the sinful is a warning to the listening family, not a verdict on the one who died.

🕯️ A comfort

It assures mourners that their offerings reach and help the departed.

🧭 Views differ

Some take it literally, others as a moral picture; both use the same rites.

ZAPPY: Isn’t it hard to hear this about a parent who just died?

PROF. BOLT: That’s why many priests say plainly: this describes the sinful as a lesson for the living. The rites are done for everyone, good or not.

WHAT IT TEACHES

Chapter 1 in four lessons

⏰ Death is sudden

It comes like a serpent to one still hoping to live.

🧳 Only deeds go

Family weeps, wealth stays; the self goes alone with its karma.

🤲 The family can help

Ten days of offerings build the body for the road.

🌳 The fruit is mōkṣa

The first verse sets the goal beyond all this.

ZAPPY: One line?

PROF. BOLT: Death comes without notice, deeds travel with us, and the living can still lend a hand.
