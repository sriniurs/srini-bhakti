GARUḌA PURĀṆA · PART B · SĀRŌDDHĀRA · CHAPTER 4 OF 16

THE SINS THAT LEAD TO HELL

Who walks the southern road, and why

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya

FOREWORD

Chapters 1 to 3 described what happens to “the sinful”. Chapter 4 says who they are: 64 verses of named wrongs, each matched to a stage of punishment.

The list is long and mixed. Some sins are crimes in any age: murder, theft, poisoning, arson. Some are betrayals of trust: an unpaid debt, a broken promise, a deposit kept. Some are failures of care. And some belong to the social order of the time the digest was written.

The chapter ends with the way back: through every form of life, to human birth once more.

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Is this a list to be scared of?

PROF. BOLT: It’s a list to learn from. Most of it, read today, is simply a description of how to wreck trust between people.

Structure. The sins are grouped by where they lead: the road, the river, the tree, and the hells beyond.

THREE GATES AND ONE

Verses 1–4
Garuḍa’s question. Which sins lead onto the great road, into the Vaitaraṇī, and into hell?
From fear to fear. Those who love wrong and spurn good go “from hell to hell, from misery to misery, from fear to fear”.
The gates. The righteous enter Yama’s city by three gates; sinners only by the southern road.
The river. The Vaitaraṇī lies on that road alone.

ZAPPY: So the good never see the river?

PROF. BOLT: Not on this account. The river is on the southern road only.

Why the south is singled out. The gate gives the catalogues a direction. They explain which conduct the text associates with the difficult approach we have already followed in Chapter 2. Chapter 14 will describe the other approaches in terms of hospitality, study, worship and care for others.

THE GREAT SINS

Verses 5–7: those who weep on the road
KillingA Brahmin, a child, a woman; causing abortion
DrinkDrinking intoxicants
Secret sinWrongs done in hiding
TheftFrom a teacher; from a temple or the learned; from women; from children
TrustNot repaying debts; keeping a deposit; betraying confidence
PoisonKilling with poisoned food

Notice. Stealing from the weak (women, children) and from those who trust you (a teacher, a depositor) are named beside murder.

SINS OF ATTITUDE

Verses 8–12

🔍 Fault-finding

Belittling merit, envy, preferring the wicked, shunning the good.

🙄 Scorn

Mocking holy places, good people, teachers, gods, the Vēdas, the Purāṇas and the schools of thought.

😈 Spite

Enjoying others’ pain; making the happy miserable; evil speech; malice.

🪨 Stubbornness

Ignoring good advice; self-satisfied, rigid, foolish, falsely learned.

ZAPPY: Attitudes, not acts?

PROF. BOLT: The text counts them as sins. A sneering, envious mind is where the acts begin.

INTO THE RIVER I

Verses 13–19
EldersDishonouring parents, teachers and elders
SpouseAbandoning a faithful, virtuous wife
The goodSlandering the virtuous
PromisesBreaking a promise to a Brahmin; sending one away empty-handed
GiftsTaking back a gift; regretting it; stopping others giving
LivelihoodDepriving someone of their living
Common landMoving field boundaries; ploughing up pasture
WorshipObstructing a sacrifice or a reading of sacred stories

Consequences begin on the way. The Vaitaraṇī recalls the physical hardships of the preceding journey. By bringing it into a catalogue of conduct, the chapter invites readers to work backward from a frightening scene to decisions made in familiar places: at home, on a road, or in an exchange with another person.

INTO THE RIVER II

Verses 23–26

💔 Others’ spouses

Coveting the king’s wife; abducting another’s wife.

🚫 The young

Lust toward unmarried girls.

🗣️ Slander

Defaming virtuous women.

🌊 Thrown back

After the road, these are hurled back into the Vaitaraṇī, “foremost among hells”.

Twice punished. These sinners face the river on the road and are thrown into it again at Yama’s order.

BREAKING THE OLD ORDER

Verses 20–22

For Brahmins. Selling liquor; eating meat and drinking; killing animals for pleasure outside sacrifice; abandoning one’s duties and study.

For others. A Śūdra who studies the Vēda, wears the sacred thread, or crosses certain marriage lines is also named.

Of its time. These verses enforce the caste rules of the age in which the digest was written. Excluding people from scripture or marriage by birth is rejected by modern Indian law and by many traditional teachers; the verses are shown here because they are in the text.

ZAPPY: So some of these “sins” aren’t sins now?

PROF. BOLT: Some are, some aren’t. Killing for pleasure is still wrong. Studying the Vēda is not. Reading honestly means telling which is which.

AT THE TREE

Verses 27–31
The cow not given. Those who gave no black cow and kept no rites for the ancestors suffer in the river, then reach a tree on its bank.
Liars. False witnesses, fake piety, cheats, thieves.
Destroyers. Those who cut down great trees, gardens and forests; who neglect vows and pilgrimage.
Betrayal. Violating a widow; a spouse who despises the other and wants someone else.
Onward. Beaten down at the silk-cotton tree, they are thrown into the other hells.

AGAINST THE PUBLIC

Verses 33, 35 and 42

💧 Water

Destroying wells, tanks, ponds; dumping waste in water, fire, gardens, roads or cow-pens.

🛣️ Roads

Blocking roads with posts, mounds, timber, stones or thorns.

🏚️ Buildings

Destroying shrines and homes.

🌳 Green

Cutting down great trees and forests (v. 29).

Harm that outlasts an encounter. A shared resource serves people who may never meet the person who damages it. That is what gives these examples their particular weight. The harm continues after the immediate act, and it can fall on anyone who later depends on what has been lost.

AGAINST THE HOUSEHOLD

Verses 32, 34, 36, 39–41
Eating firstEating while wife, children, servants, teacher, ancestors and gods go without
IngratitudeDenying scripture, greed, hypocrisy, ingratitude
No worshipNever honouring Śiva, Śivā, Hari, Sūrya, Gaṇēśa, the wise or good teachers
DiscordStirring quarrels among the learned; enjoying cow-fights
ConductUnions forbidden by time or place: on śrāddha days, during menstruation, by day, in water

ZAPPY: Eating first is a sin?

PROF. BOLT: Feeding others before yourself was a householder’s first duty. Eating while dependents go hungry turns that upside down.

FORBIDDEN TRADES

Verses 43–44

🏹 Weapons

Making and selling swords, bows and arrows.

☠️ Poison

Selling poison.

🧳 Leather

Trading in leather, for a Vaiśya.

💇 Hair

Women selling their hair.

The logic. Trades that profit from harm, or from parts of bodies, are listed together. Armies needed weapons, but the text still names the trade a sin.

Why occupations enter the list. The examples place responsibility inside ordinary transactions. An occupation gives someone access to goods, information or duties that another person may have to trust them with. The chapter asks what happens when that trust becomes an opportunity for private advantage.

CRUELTY AND BETRAYAL

Verses 45–50
The helpless. No pity for the helpless; hating the good; punishing the innocent.
The guest. Turning away a hopeful guest though food is cooked.
All creatures. Distrusting, deceiving or being cruel to any living being.
Vows. Taking a vow and dropping it for lack of self-control.
Teachers. Disrespecting teachers of the self and reciters of the Purāṇas.
Friends. Betraying a friend, breaking a friendship, destroying another’s hope.

Who can bear the cost. Some examples concern people or creatures with little power to resist. The chapter brings such actions into the same account as more publicly visible wrongs. Its categories are historical, but the question its examples raise is concrete: who suffers when someone stronger chooses to take advantage?

A HELL OF NO RETURN

Verse 51

Who goes there. Those who break up marriages, disrupt processions of the gods, or attack groups of pilgrims.

Why so severe. Each attacks a bond many people share: a family, a festival, a pilgrimage. The harm spreads beyond one victim.

A puzzle. Chapter 3 says every soul returns from hell purified; this verse speaks of a hell without return. The digest does not reconcile the two.

ZAPPY: Breaking up a marriage is that serious?

PROF. BOLT: In this text, yes: it’s grouped with attacks on festivals and pilgrims, as a harm to many at once.

THE ARSONIST

Verses 52–57: one punishment in full
52 Whoever burns a house, village or forest is roasted in pits of fire.
53 Burned, he begs for shade; he is led to the sword-leaf forest.
54 The blades slash him; “rest in the cool shade,” the messengers mock.
55 Parched, he begs for water; he is given boiling oil.
56–57 Offered “food” and “drink”, he collapses, burning inside, too weak to speak.

The pattern. Each plea is answered with a mirror of his crime: he spread fire, so relief itself burns.

THE LONG CLIMB BACK

Verses 58–64
Until the deluge. Thousands are punished in these hells until the pralaya; the torments are too many to list.
Down to stone. Their karma spent, they are born as unmoving things: trees, shrubs, creepers, rocks, grasses.
Upward. Then insects, birds, animals, fish, through 84 lakh kinds of birth.
Human again. At last human, but in hard circumstances, some bearing illness or blindness as marks of past sin.

Reading a catalogue with care. The list brings together injury, deception and violations of ritual or social duties. These categories reflect the world of the text and should remain distinguishable in reading it. Keeping them in view helps us see both the chapter’s moral concerns and the particular social order through which it expresses them.

READING IT TODAY

Sorting the list
Still crimesMurder, theft, poisoning, arson, abduction, abuse, false witness, fraud
Still wrongBetraying trust, unpaid debts, cruelty, ingratitude, slander, harming the environment
Religious dutyWorship, pilgrimage, rites for ancestors, vows
Of its timeCaste and marriage rules; restrictions on who may study

On illness and disability. The text links some conditions to past sin. Most teachers today warn against using this to judge anyone; it is read as a warning about the deed, not an explanation of a person.

WHAT IT TEACHES

Chapter 4 in four lessons

🤝 Trust is sacred

Debts, deposits, promises and friendships are named again and again.

🌍 The commons count

Water, roads, trees and pasture belong to all.

🍽️ Others first

Feed guests, dependents and animals before yourself.

🧗 The way back is long

Every form of life, before human birth comes again.

ZAPPY: One line?

PROF. BOLT: Keep faith with people, with the land and with your word, because the road back is very long.
