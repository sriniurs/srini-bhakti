GARUḌA PURĀṆA · PART B · SĀRŌDDHĀRA · CHAPTER 6 OF 16

THE MISERIES OF BIRTH

The child who remembers, prays, and forgets

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya

FOREWORD

After hell and the long climb through animal births, the soul comes back to a human womb. Chapter 6, 43 verses, tells what it goes through there and after.

Its heart is a prayer. In the seventh month the unborn child remembers its past lives, weeps, and vows to Viṣṇu that if it is released it will seek only liberation. Then it is born, forgets everything, and the old round begins again.

The chapter is less about punishment than about forgetting, and about the rare chance a human life offers.

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: Everyone forgets the vow?

PROF. BOLT: That’s the chapter’s sting. If anyone remembered it, says verse 27, no one would remain bound.

GARUḌA ASKS

Verses 1–2

The question. How does a soul coming back from hell take shape in the womb, and what does it suffer there?

The answer begins. Viṣṇu explains how a body forms when the male and female elements join.

The frame. This is the birth of “the sinful”. Chapter 15 describes the birth of those who have done good, in a very different tone.

ZAPPY: So there are two birth chapters?

PROF. BOLT: Yes: this one, heavy and painful, and chapter 15, which is about a good birth and how to use it.

CONCEPTION

Verses 3–6
By karma. The soul enters the womb according to its past deeds.
The first night. A lump.
The fifth night. A round form.
The tenth day. Hard, like a jujube fruit; then an egg of flesh.

Of its time. Verses 3–4 tie conception to old rules about a woman’s monthly impurity, linked to a myth of Indra. They reflect ancient purity codes and are not repeated here in detail.

MONTH BY MONTH

Verses 7–8
Month 1the head
Month 2arms and limbs
Month 3nails, hair, bones, skin, the openings
Month 4the seven body-tissues
Month 5hunger and thirst
Month 6wrapped in the membrane, moving to the left

A chronology with a purpose. The sequence of months makes the story slow down after the speed of the road to Yama. The text follows the growth of a body so that it can ask what it means to enter one again. Its description belongs to a historical account of embodiment and conveys a religious argument through successive stages.

A PARROT IN A CAGE

Verses 9–12

🍲 Fed

Its body is built from what the mother eats and drinks.

🐛 Bitten

Worms bite its soft body; it faints again and again.

🌶️ Burned

The mother’s sharp, bitter, salty and sour foods pain it.

🦜 Caged

Head bent to belly, limbs unable to move, “like a parrot in a cage”.

ZAPPY: Is this meant as biology?

PROF. BOLT: As experience. It tells what being unborn might feel like, to make the point that every birth begins in confinement.

What the enclosure does in the story. The image of confinement turns a description of the womb into a scene with a point of view. We are asked to imagine a being that remembers and wants release but can do very little. That helplessness explains why the next scene takes the form of prayer.

MEMORY

Verses 13–15
Divine sight. By a power not its own, the unborn child remembers the deeds of hundreds of past lives.
Weeping. It sobs, and nothing relieves it.
Folded hands. Bound by seven bonds, trembling, it pleads with the One who placed it there.
The seventh month. From then on it is fully aware, shrinking from the pressing winds of the womb.

Why memory changes the prayer. The being sees the present predicament in relation to earlier choices. This makes the prayer more than a plea to escape discomfort. It is also a wish to interrupt the pattern that brought the speaker to this point.

THE PRAYER I

Verses 16–19: what the child says

🙏 Refuge

“I take refuge in Viṣṇu, Lakṣmī’s lord, upholder of the world, who protects all who come to him.”

🌀 Deluded

“Your māyā made me think of body, son and wife as mine; ego kept me wandering.”

⚖️ Unfair bargain

“I did good and bad for those who depended on me. I suffer the results; they enjoyed them.”

📿 The vow

“If I am freed, I will bow at your feet and follow the path to liberation.”

The third card. The same point is made in B1 and B3: family enjoys the wealth; the one who earned it answers for how.

THE PRAYER II

Verses 20–23: a change of mind
When? Trapped in filth and the belly’s heat, it asks when it will be free.
No more. It will take refuge in the Lord, and asks never to be born again.
A reversal. Then it wishes never to leave the womb at all: out there, its sins will bring only misery.
Better here. It would rather stay, clinging to Viṣṇu’s feet, detached from the changing world.

ZAPPY: It doesn’t want to be born?

PROF. BOLT: Not after remembering what life brought last time. Inside, it is at least turned toward God; outside, it fears forgetting.

BIRTH

Verses 24–26

🌬️ Pushed out

After ten months, still praying, it is driven out head-first by the winds of birth.

😮‍💨 Breathless

It emerges anxious and gasping.

🫥 Memory gone

Everything it remembered is lost.

😭 The first cry

It falls to the ground, squirming, and cries, “deprived of knowledge”.

The first cry. The text reads the newborn’s cry as grief for what it has just forgotten.

What is lost at birth. The chapter gives the unborn speaker a clarity that the newborn does not retain. The change makes the question of remembrance central: how can a person recover an insight that seemed obvious in distress once attention is again taken up by ordinary life?

FOUR MOMENTS OF CLARITY

Verses 27–28

The verse. The insight that comes in the womb, in illness, at the cremation ground, or while hearing the Purāṇas would free everyone, if only it lasted.

Why it fades. Once karma is worked out and the child is born, Viṣṇu’s māyā clouds it again.

ZAPPY: The cremation-ground feeling.

PROF. BOLT: Everyone knows it: standing at a pyre, everything is clear. Two days later, it’s gone. This verse names that.

INFANCY

Verses 29–32

🗣️ Unheard

Helpless and unable to speak, it depends on others for everything.

🍼 Misread

Those who care for it don’t know what it wants, and give it what it doesn’t.

🛏️ Stuck

It lies on a soiled bed, unable to scratch, sit, stand or move.

🦟 Bitten

Mosquitoes, gnats and bugs bite its tender skin.

YOUTH

Verses 33–35
Bad habits. Growing up, he takes to harmful ways.
Bad company. He broods on wrong, keeps wicked company, scorns scripture and good people.
Desire. Lust takes him over.
The moth. Captivated, he plunges into darkness “like a moth into a flame”.

FIVE SENSES

Verse 36

The verse. The deer, elephant, bird, bee and fish are each destroyed through a single sense. How can one who indulges all five escape?

The usual pairings. In the wider tradition: the deer lured by sound, the elephant by touch, the moth or bird by sight, the bee by scent, the fish by taste. The verse itself does not say which.

From helplessness to distraction. The later stages of life bring new abilities and new objects of desire. The irony is that greater freedom of movement does not guarantee greater freedom from attachment. This is the thread linking infancy, youth and pride back to the promise made in the womb.

PRIDE AND QUARRELS

Verses 37–38

🤲 Craving

He wants what he cannot have.

😠 Anger

Ignorance turns to anger and grief.

📈 Growing

Pride and anger grow as his body grows.

🐘 Defeated

He fights rivals and is destroyed by stronger men, as one elephant brings down another.

NECTAR DROPPED

Verses 39–40

The worst fool. Nothing is more sinful than wasting a rare human birth on the senses.

Its rarity. Human birth takes hundreds of lives to reach.

The image. The one who spends it on pleasure “drops nectar from his hand”.

ZAPPY: Same idea as chapter 16?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly. Chapter 16 opens with it again: guard this body for duty, duty for knowledge, knowledge for release.

A counterpart in Chapter 15. Chapter 15 also asks what takes place in human birth, but its starting point is a person of good deeds. Both chapters eventually ask what a human life makes possible. Reading them together brings that shared question into view without erasing their different accounts of how the life begins.

AGE AND THE NOOSE

Verses 41–43
Old age. Serious disease comes; at death he falls into hell once more.
The noose. Bound by the endless noose of karma and deluded by māyā, the sinful are never freed on their own.
No rites. Viṣṇu adds that sinners who receive no funeral offerings go to hell, and asks Garuḍa what more he wants to hear.

The bridge. That last line leads straight into chapter 7: a man with no one to perform his rites, and the king who did it for him.

THE SAME PRAYER ELSEWHERE

An old and widespread image

📗 Bhāgavata

In Kapila’s teaching to his mother, the unborn child in the seventh month prays to God and vows to seek release, then forgets at birth.

🪷 Upaniṣad

The Garbha Upaniṣad also has the embryo remember past births and resolve to seek liberation.

🎵 Song

Saint-poets across India sang of the vow made in the womb and broken in the world.

📕 Here

The Sārōddhāra gives it as the turning point between hell and the next life.

WHAT IT TEACHES

Chapter 6 in four lessons

🧠 We knew once

The text says everyone, before birth, saw clearly and vowed to seek God.

🌫️ We forget

Māyā clouds that vow; clarity returns only in hard moments.

🦋 Senses can trap

One sense can undo a creature; five can undo a person.

💎 Life is rare

Human birth is nectar in the hand; don’t drop it.

ZAPPY: One line?

PROF. BOLT: You promised before you were born; the rest of your life is the chance to remember.
