GARUḌA PURĀṆA · PART B · SĀRŌDDHĀRA · CHAPTER 8 OF 16

THE GIFTS FOR THE DYING

The holy name, the eight great gifts, and the cow that carries across

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya

FOREWORD

The first seven chapters described the road. From chapter 8 the Sārōddhāra turns to what the living can do, beginning before death. This is its longest chapter: 118 verses.

It has three themes. The holy name: Hari’s name, said or heard at the end, keeps Yama’s servants away. The eight great gifts, each answering a danger on the road, with the Vaitaraṇī cow as the boat across. And the reason to give now: body, wealth and family do not come along.

Its tone is hopeful. After the hells, here are the ways out.

Ōṁ Namō Nārāyaṇāya,
srini.presents 🙏

ZAPPY: So the name and the gifts undo the road?

PROF. BOLT: That is the promise of this chapter. Chapters 1–7 were the warning; 8 onward is the remedy.

The question. Garuḍa asks for all the rites for those departing, and how sons should perform them (vv. 1–2).

PREPARING TO DIE

Verses 3–8
Notice. When age, illness and bad planetary signs show death is near, stay fearless and alert.
Atone. Make amends for wrongs done knowingly or unknowingly.
Worship. Bathe; worship Viṣṇu as a śālagrāma with scent, flowers, saffron, tulasī, incense, lamp and food.
Give. Make gifts to the learned and feed them.
Mantras. Repeat the eight-syllable and twelve-syllable mantras of Viṣṇu.
The names. Remember and hear the names of Viṣṇu and Śiva.

The time of giving. The chapter puts gifts at the edge of a transition. Some are described as acts a person can undertake before death, even though the benefits are imagined on the road that follows it. That interval gives the chapter its urgency and recalls the messengers’ warning in Chapter 2 about setting out without provisions.

TRUE RELATIVES

Verses 9–11

No wailing. Relatives should not mourn aloud near the dying, but remember the Lord’s name again and again.

Ten names. Meditate on the ten avatāras: Fish, Tortoise, Boar, Man-lion, Dwarf, Paraśurāma, Rāma, Kṛṣṇa, Buddha, Kalki.

Real kin. Those who recite these names beside the dying are their true relatives.

THE POWER OF THE NAME

Verses 12–15 and 21–23

⚡ Swift

The name “Kṛṣṇa” destroys great sins quickly.

👦 Ajāmila

Dying, he called his son, whose name was Nārāyaṇa, and reached heaven. How much more for one who calls in faith!

🔥 Like fire

Fire burns even when touched by accident; Hari’s name removes sin even from an evil mind.

🍶 The liquor pot

Penances cannot purify one who turns from Nārāyaṇa, as rivers cannot clean a liquor pot.

ZAPPY: Ajāmila only meant to call his son.

PROF. BOLT: That’s the point of the story, told in full in the Bhāgavata: even an unintended name of God protected him.

YAMA’S ORDER

Verses 16–20

🚫 Keep away

Yama tells his servants not to bring those who meditate on Hari, take refuge in Viṣṇu, and are free of sin.

📿 His names

Acyuta, Kēśava, Nārāyaṇa, Kṛṣṇa, Dāmōdara, Vāsudēva, Hari, Śrīdhara, Mādhava and more.

✅ Seize these

Those who neglect Viṣṇu’s feet and are bound only by household desire.

✅ And these

Those who never praise his name, never meditate, never bow, never worship.

In a dream. Whoever remembers Kṛṣṇa’s name never sees Yama or his servants, not even in a dream (v. 23).

FOUR AIDS AT THE END

Verses 24–27

The four. The Ekādaśī fast, the Gītā, Gaṅgā water and tulasī leaves; with them, the water from Viṣṇu’s feet and his names.

Also. Hear the Gītā and the Thousand Names. Give a cow while saying “Nandanandana”, and the Vaitaraṇī will not take you.

Then. Offer food with ghee and gold to a learned man; give cows with their calves.

THE SON’S PART

Verses 28–31

✅ Approve

Any gift the dying man makes, large or small, becomes imperishable if the son approves it.

🎁 Give

A good son makes gifts for his father in those last days.

🙅 Don’t covet

Sons should not covet wealth the father earned himself; only ancestral property is theirs to expect.

❤️ The effect

A son’s gifts may lengthen his father’s life and free him from misery hereafter.

ZAPPY: The son mustn’t eye the father’s savings?

PROF. BOLT: Verse 30 says exactly that. The text sees the deathbed as a time to give, not to divide the estate.

THE EIGHT GREAT GIFTS

Verses 32–34
1Sesame
2Iron
3Gold
4Cotton cloth
5Salt
6The seven grains
7Land
8A cow

When. In illness or calamity, and especially in the last days, these eight rank above all other gifts and remove great sins. Each is explained in the pages that follow.

Objects and the needs they answer. The list moves among materials used in worship, things of value and things that sustain life. Read as a group, the gifts link giving to bodily and social needs. The text then assigns them effects in the world of the departed, where the objects reappear as forms of protection or assistance.

SESAME AND IRON

Verses 35–40

⚫ Sesame’s origin

White, black and brown kinds, born from the Lord’s sweat.

⚫ Its effect

It satisfies even the demons and removes sins of speech, thought and act.

⛓️ Iron, how

Given while touching the ground.

⛓️ Iron, why

It placates Yama’s weapons; his fierce servants become kind. The giver avoids Yama’s road.

ZAPPY: Iron for Yama’s weapons?

PROF. BOLT: Like for like: weapons are iron, so iron given calms them. Each gift answers a danger on the road.

A small gift can carry a large meaning. Sesame is easy to overlook among the more costly offerings. Its place in the list shows that the chapter distinguishes ritual significance from market value. A material used repeatedly in the rites can carry a particular meaning even when the amount given is small.

GOLD, CLOTH, SALT, GRAIN

Verses 41–48
GoldPleases the three worlds, Brahmā, the sages and Dharma’s court; heaven, then rebirth as a good and prosperous king
Cotton clothNo fear of Yama’s messengers
SaltNo fear of Yama
Salt, cloth, sesame, goldTogether they please Citragupta and the officers of Yama’s city
Seven grainsRice, barley, wheat, beans, black gram, millet and peas; they please the gatekeepers

LAND

Verses 49–55

🐄 A cow-hide’s size

Even land the size of a cow’s hide, given to a worthy person, absolves the gravest sin.

👑 For kings

Land expiates a king’s sins; a king who withholds it is reborn a beggar.

🌾 With crops

Land full of grain leads to Indra’s world.

📈 It grows

Unlike other gifts, its merit increases every day the land is farmed.

For others. For those who are not kings, a cow is equal to land.

A gift with a life beyond the moment. Land differs from something consumed in a single meal or offering. Its use can continue long after the giver has died. This helps explain the prominence the chapter gives it within a discussion of acts whose effects are expected to outlast the moment of giving.

THE COW

Verses 56–64

🐄 Its power

A cow gift overcomes death, clears debts and wins release; given for the Vaitaraṇī, it carries one past three hells.

🐄 The best

A tawny milch cow with its calf, to a virtuous learned man: it clears sins of every age of life, every hour, and thought, word and deed.

Given in full health1 cow
Given in illness100 cows
Given when dying1,000 cows
Given after death100,000 cows
The verseall four are equal in merit

ZAPPY: So giving early is worth more?

PROF. BOLT: Exactly what the scale says: one cow given in health equals a hundred thousand given for you after death.

WHO SHOULD RECEIVE

Verses 64–68
Worthy. One learned in scripture, who keeps the sacred fire and lives simply; a gift to such a one multiplies a hundred-thousandfold.
Unharmed. Such a recipient is not tainted even by a gift of jewel-filled earth.
Unworthy. A gift to the undeserving takes the giver to hell and troubles the receiver’s family for a hundred generations.
Whole. A cow goes to one person; sold or shared, it troubles the family for seven generations.

What the choice of recipient tells us. The chapter pays attention to the recipient as well as the gift. Giving is described as a relationship involving intention, the thing offered and the person who receives it. That helps explain why the narrative pauses over conditions that would be unnecessary if all that mattered were the object’s price.

THE VAITARAṆĪ COW

Verses 69–76
The cow. Black or reddish; horns tipped with gold, hooves with silver; two black cloths; a bell at the neck.
On the cloth. A covered bronze vessel of ghee, a golden image of Yama and an iron rod.
The raft. Sugarcane bound with silk, floated in a water-filled pit.
The act. Hold the cow’s tail, set a foot in the raft, honour a learned man, and recite the mantra.

The river from the giver’s side. Chapter 2 introduces the cow gift from the far side of death: the ferrymen ask whether it was made. Here the focus shifts to the person giving and the words that accompany the act. The same river therefore connects a scene of preparation with a scene of need.

THE MANTRA

Verses 77–86, in paraphrase

To the Lord. Saviour of those drowning in the ocean of sorrow, lift me up.

To the river. Salutations to Vaitaraṇī.

To the cow. Look kindly on me at Yama’s gate. May cows be before me, behind me, and in my heart. You are prosperity itself: take away my sins.

The gift. With folded hands, honour Yama in the form of the cow, walk around the offerings, and give them. The giver goes to Yama’s court by the righteous road.

When. Do it in health or illness; the text calls the cow a symbol.

SACRED TIMES

Verses 87–89

🌊 Places

Bathing places such as the Gaṅgā; the homes of the learned.

🌘 Sky

Solar and lunar eclipses; the sun entering a new sign; new moon; equinoxes and solstices.

📅 Calendar

Vyatīpāta, the first days of the ages, and other holy days.

💗 Any moment

Verse 89: the sacred time is whenever faith arises and a worthy person is present.

ZAPPY: So the calendar doesn’t really matter?

PROF. BOLT: Verse 89 quietly says so. Faith plus a worthy recipient makes any moment the right one.

GIVE WHILE ALIVE

Verses 90–100
Nothing lasts. Body passing, wealth unstable, death always near: store up righteousness.
By your own hand. Give what you can; even a small gift from your own hand never ends.
Provisions. Gifts are food for the great road; without them the traveller starves.
The rare birth. A human life is won by great merit; use it for dharma.
The only kin. Relatives’ love fades after death; one’s own self is the true kinsman.

STICKS IN A RIVER

Verses 101–112

🪵 The image

Family meet like sticks drifting together in a river, or creatures in one water-tank, then part. No one truly belongs to anyone.

🔥 At the pyre

Relatives leave the body at the cremation ground; only dharma and one’s deeds go on.

🍃 A leaf will do

The Lord accepts a leaf, a flower, a fruit or water given in devotion; he does not weigh size.

💰 Past gifts

Present wealth comes from past giving; righteousness conquers desire and leads to release.

THE CLOSING VERSES

Verses 113–118

The good son. Honoured by the gods; he urges his ailing father to give, and the family is ennobled.

Multipliers. A gift made through a father counts a hundredfold, a mother a thousandfold, a sister ten thousandfold, a brother beyond counting.

The giver. Faces no hell, no trouble, no fear of Yama’s messengers.

The miser. One who withholds in illness grieves after death; kin who give nothing for a dying man are counted as grave sinners.

WHAT IT TEACHES

Chapter 8 in four lessons

📿 Say the name

It keeps Yama’s servants away; recite it for the dying.

🎁 Give early

One cow in health equals a hundred thousand after death.

🍃 Size doesn’t matter

A leaf in devotion is accepted.

🪵 Hold lightly

Family and wealth are sticks in a river.

ZAPPY: One line?

PROF. BOLT: Say his name, give with your own hand, and give now.
