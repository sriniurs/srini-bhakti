VIṢṆU · THE BELOVED

Rādhā

The Beloved of Kṛṣṇa · explained and illustrated

Love as the highest devotion

HOW THIS WORKS: Rādhā, Kṛṣṇa’s beloved: where she appears in the texts, from a single early verse to Jayadeva’s Gītagovinda, her birth at Barsānā, the rāsa dance, how different traditions see her, from hlādinī śakti to supreme goddess, the arts she inspired, and Rādhāṣṭamī, her birthday.

WHERE WE ARE

In northern India people greet each other with “Rādhe Rādhe”. In Vṛndāvana her name is painted on walls, sung in every lane, and spoken before Kṛṣṇa’s. Yet she is one of the most surprising figures in Hinduism: the great Bhāgavata Purāṇa, which tells Kṛṣṇa’s childhood in detail, never names her.

This guide explains who Rādhā is, how she grew from a hint into a goddess, what she means to different traditions, and how her birthday, Rādhāṣṭamī, is kept. For Kṛṣṇa himself, see Kṛṣṇa.

FROM A HINT TO A GODDESS

Where Rādhā appears

The Bhāgavata’s silence. In the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, during the rāsa dance, Kṛṣṇa vanishes with one favoured gopī. She is not named. Later commentators, such as Viśvanātha Cakravartī, read her as Rādhā.

First names. The earliest clear mention is in the Prākṛt Gāthā Saptaśatī, a collection of love verses. She appears now and then in poetry after that.

The Gītagovinda. Jayadeva’s 12th-century Sanskrit song-poem makes Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa’s longing, quarrel and reunion its whole subject. It is still sung at Jagannātha in Purī.

Purāṇas. The Brahmavaivarta Purāṇa raises her to the supreme goddess, the root of creation (Mūlaprakṛti).

ZAPPY: So poets made her famous before theologians?

PROF. BOLT: Pretty much, Zappy. Then the theologians caught up.

RĀDHĀ OF BARSĀNĀ

Her story in Vraja

The story

Vraja tradition and later Purāṇas; tellings vary.

Tradition says the cowherd chieftain Vṛṣabhānu found a baby girl on a lotus in the Yamunā; she was raised by him and Kīrtidā at Barsānā, near Kṛṣṇa’s village.
As children and young people, Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa met in the groves of Vṛndāvana: games, teasing, flowers, the sound of his flute.
On autumn full-moon nights the gopīs danced the rāsa with Kṛṣṇa, who multiplied himself so each felt he was hers alone; Rādhā was the heart of it.
When Kṛṣṇa left for Mathurā, Rādhā stayed behind. Her longing in separation (viraha) became the deepest theme of devotional poetry.
Some texts tell of a secret marriage before he left; others keep her love outside marriage. Traditions differ.

ZAPPY: He left and never came back?

PROF. BOLT: In most tellings, Zappy. The longing is the point.

HOW TRADITIONS SEE HER

Many views of one beloved

💫 Gauḍīya (Caitanya)

Rādhā is Kṛṣṇa’s hlādinī śakti, his own power of bliss; Caitanya is seen as Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in one form.

💍 Nimbārka and others

Rādhā is Kṛṣṇa’s eternal consort, worshipped together with him; the Nimbārka tradition was one of the first to do so.

👑 Rādhāvallabha

Rādhā herself is supreme; Kṛṣṇa is her devoted lover.

🏠 Puṣṭimārga (Vallabha)

She is honoured as Svāminī, the queen of Kṛṣṇa’s love.

ZAPPY: Is she Lakṣmī?

PROF. BOLT: Some say so, Zappy, and some say Lakṣmī is a portion of her. See Sampradāyas.

One love, many views.

LOVE AS DEVOTION

Why Rādhā matters

The model devotee. Rādhā’s love asks for nothing. She is the ideal of prema, pure love of God.

Mādhurya. Of the moods of devotion (servant, friend, parent, lover), the lover’s mood, mādhurya bhāva, is held by many traditions to be the most intimate; Rādhā embodies it.

Not ordinary romance. Teachers insist her love is spiritual: the soul longing for God, not desire as we know it. (Traditional reading.)

Viraha. Longing in separation is valued as highly as union; it keeps the heart turned to God.

ZAPPY: A love story as a prayer?

PROF. BOLT: The oldest kind, Zappy. Many saints wrote that way.

Love that asks nothing.

RĀDHĀ IN THE ARTS

Song, dance and painting

🎶 Songs

The Gītagovinda’s aṣṭapadīs, Vidyāpati, Caṇḍīdās, Sūrdās, Mīrābāī and the Haveli music of Nāthdwārā.

💃 Dance

Odissi, Manipuri Rāsa Līlā and Kathak tell her story; Vraja’s Rāslīlā plays are staged by boy actors.

🖼️ Painting

Kangra, Basohli and Rajput miniatures show Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in groves, storms and moonlight.

🌈 Holi

Barsānā’s Laṭhmār Holi, where women playfully beat the men of Nandgaon with sticks, recalls her play with Kṛṣṇa.

PROF. BOLT: At Barsānā’s Holi, Zappy, all in fun.

Love in every art.

RĀDHĀṢṬAMĪ

Her birthday

When. Bhādrapada Śukla Aṣṭamī, about fifteen days after Janmāṣṭamī.

At Barsānā. The hilltop Lāḍlī-jī temple celebrates with abhiṣeka at noon, songs and huge crowds; Vṛndāvana’s temples do the same.

At home. A fast till midday, worship of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa together, her names sung, and sweets offered.

Her feet. At Vṛndāvana’s Rādhāramaṇa and Bāṅke Bihārī traditions, some temples show Rādhā’s feet only on this day. (Practices vary.)

KNOWING RĀDHĀ TODAY

A practical note

Her name first. Devotees say “Rādhe-Kṛṣṇa” or “Rādhe-Śyām”, her name before his, because, they say, he loves to hear it.

Read the poems. A translation of the Gītagovinda or Sūrdās’s songs shows why poets loved her.

In the South. Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa bhajans are sung widely, though South Indian temples more often show Kṛṣṇa with Rukmiṇī and Satyabhāmā.

Respect differences. Some traditions emphasise her, others honour her less; both are part of Hinduism.

PROF. BOLT: Sing one “Rādhe-Kṛṣṇa” bhajan this week, Zappy, and listen to the words.
