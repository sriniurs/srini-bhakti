Who Is Viṣṇu?

The All-Pervading Preserver · explained and illustrated

The preserver

HOW THIS WORKS: Who Viṣṇu is: the Vedic god of three strides, his conch, discus, mace and lotus, Vaikuṇṭha, his forms and avatāras, four great stories, worship, temples and the teachers of Vaiṣṇavism.

WHERE WE ARE

Viṣṇu is the god who keeps the world going: the preserver, who rests on the cosmic serpent on the ocean of milk and comes down, age after age, whenever goodness is in danger.

This guide answers the question who is Viṣṇu? from the Veda to the temples: his name, his emblems, his home and companions, his forms, four of his stories, how he is worshipped and the teachers who made his path.

His ten descents each have their own guide in our Daśāvatāra · 10 avatāras + overview series.

THE NAME AND THE VEDA

The god of three strides

The name. Viṣṇu is usually explained as “the all-pervading”, from a root meaning to spread or enter.

In the Ṛgveda. Only five of its 1,028 hymns are addressed to him, but he is the friend of Indra and the god of three strides who measures out earth, sky and heaven.

Rise. In later times he merged with Nārāyaṇa, the cosmic person, and Vāsudeva-Kṛṣṇa, and became for millions the Supreme God.

Other names. Hari (“who takes away”), Nārāyaṇa, Keśava, Govinda: a thousand of them in the Viṣṇu Sahasranāma · 10 parts.

ZAPPY: Only five hymns at the start?

PROF. BOLT: Small seeds, Zappy. Look at the tree now.

The god of three strides.

HIS EMBLEMS

Conch, discus, mace, lotus

🐚 Conch

Pāñcajanya: its sound is the call of dharma and the first sound of creation.

☸️ Discus

Sudarśana, “beautiful to see”: the mind, swift as light, that cuts ignorance.

🔨 Mace

Kaumodakī: strength, knowledge and the power of time.

🪷 Lotus

Purity and unfolding: beauty untouched by the mud it grows in.

💙 Dark-blue body

The colour of the sky and the deep sea: vast and infinite.

💎 Kaustubha and Śrīvatsa

The jewel on his chest and the mark of Lakṣmī there; a garland of forest flowers, yellow silk.

ZAPPY: Why blue?

PROF. BOLT: Like the sky and the ocean, Zappy: no edge to him.

Four hands, four gifts.

VAIKUṆṬHA AND THE MILK OCEAN

Śeṣa, Garuḍa and Lakṣmī

Anantaśayana. Between ages he reclines on Śeṣa (Ananta, “endless”), the thousand-headed serpent, on the ocean of milk.

Creation. From his navel rises a lotus, and on it Brahmā, who creates the world.

Lakṣmī. His consort sits at his feet or on his chest; read our Lakṣmī guide.

Garuḍa. The eagle king is his mount and banner; read our Garuḍa Purāṇa · 24 articles series.

Vaikuṇṭha. His heaven, the goal of the devotee: its doors open on Vaikuṇṭha Ekādaśī.

ZAPPY: Brahmā comes out of his navel?

PROF. BOLT: On a lotus stalk, Zappy. Creation begins in Viṣṇu.

Rest that holds the world.

The word avatāra means “descent”. Viṣṇu descends in a form suited to each crisis.

📜 Why he comes

In the Gītā he says he is born age after age to protect the good and restore dharma.

🏹 Rāma and Kṛṣṇa

🐎 Kalki

The one still to come, at the end of this age.

STORY 1 · THE THREE STEPS

Vāmana and Bali

The story

From the Bhāgavata Purāṇa.

The generous demon-king Bali had won the three worlds.
Viṣṇu came as Vāmana, a dwarf boy, and asked for just three steps of land.
Bali agreed. The boy grew vast: one step covered the earth, the second the heavens.
Nothing was left; Bali offered his own head for the third step.
Viṣṇu sent him to the netherworld as its king and became his guard. Read our Vāmana guide.

ZAPPY: Bali lost everything?

PROF. BOLT: And gained Viṣṇu at his door, Zappy.

Three steps.

STORY 2 · GAJENDRA

The elephant who called

The story

From the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Book 8.

Gajendra, king of elephants, went to bathe in a lake.
A crocodile seized his leg. They struggled for a very long time; the elephant’s strength failed.
At last he gave up his pride, raised a lotus in his trunk and called on the Lord.
Viṣṇu came at once on Garuḍa and cut the crocodile with his discus.
The elephant was freed. The story is told as the power of surrender. (Gajendra Mokṣa.)

ZAPPY: Why didn’t he call earlier?

PROF. BOLT: He was trying alone, Zappy. The story says: call.

Call, and he comes.

STORY 3 · MADHU AND KAIṬABHA

Before creation

The story

From the Devī Māhātmya, chapter 1, and the Purāṇas.

Before creation Viṣṇu slept on Śeṣa, and Brahmā sat on the lotus from his navel.
Two demons, Madhu and Kaiṭabha, born of his earwax, rose to kill Brahmā.
Brahmā praised Yoganidrā, the Devī as Viṣṇu’s sleep, and she left him.
Viṣṇu woke and fought them for five thousand years; at last they asked to be killed where there was no water.
He killed them on his thighs. Read our Devī Māhātmya · 19 parts series.

ZAPPY: Even Viṣṇu needed the Devī to wake him?

PROF. BOLT: The two work together, Zappy.

He wakes to protect.

STORY 4 · THE UNIVERSAL FORM

Chapter 11 of the Gītā

The request. On the battlefield Arjuna asks Kṛṣṇa to show his true form.

The vision. Given divine sight, he sees all worlds, gods and beings in one body, and time devouring the armies.

Fear and love. Arjuna trembles and begs to see his friend’s gentle form again.

Read it. Our Gītā chapter 11, verse by verse.

ZAPPY: He wanted his friend back?

PROF. BOLT: Infinite is hard to love, Zappy. So God comes as a friend.

Infinite, yet a friend.

HOW HE IS WORSHIPPED

Ekādaśī, tulasī and the name

Ekādaśī. The eleventh lunar day, twice a month, is his fast; read our Ekādaśī · 28 articles guides.

Tulasī. The holy basil is dearest to him; no worship is complete without a leaf. Read our Tulasī guide.

The name. Chanting his names, especially the thousand names · 10 parts, is the simplest worship.

Mantras. Oṃ Namo Nārāyaṇāya and Oṃ Namo Bhagavate Vāsudevāya are the best known.

Morning. Many begin the day with the Śāntākāram verse.

ZAPPY: Just saying his name?

PROF. BOLT: The Kali age’s easiest path, Zappy, so the teachers say.

Chant the name.

HIS DEVOTEES AND TEACHERS

Āḻvārs and ācāryas

The Āḻvārs. Twelve Tamil poet-saints whose hymns spread love of Viṣṇu across the south.

Rāmānuja. The teacher of Viśiṣṭādvaita, “qualified non-dualism”, who lived at Mēlukōṭe for years.

Madhva. The Dvaita teacher of Udupi; the Haridāsas of Karnataka, Purandara and Kanaka, sang in his tradition.

The north. Nimbārka, Vallabha and Caitanya, with their love of Kṛṣṇa; Rāmānanda and Tulsīdās, of Rāma.

ZAPPY: So many schools!

PROF. BOLT: One Viṣṇu, many ways to love him, Zappy.

Many teachers.

WHAT VIṢṆU TEACHES

Preservation and care

Preserving. He keeps the world going, quietly and constantly. Many read his rest on Śeṣa as calm in the middle of action. (Interpretation.)

He comes. The avatāras say God does not stay far away: he enters the world when it needs him.

Surrender. Gajendra and Bali teach that letting go of pride brings him close.

With Lakṣmī. Abundance and goodness belong together.

ZAPPY: Calm in the middle of action?

PROF. BOLT: Resting on the snake, Zappy, holding the world.

Care that never stops.
