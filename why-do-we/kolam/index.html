WHY DO WE… · KOLAM AND RANGOLI

Kolam

Why We Draw at the Doorstep · explained and illustrated

Art at the doorstep

HOW THIS WORKS: Why Hindu homes are drawn with kolam, rangoli or rangavalli every morning: rice flour that feeds ants and birds, a welcome for Lakṣmī, dots and endless lines, the great kolams of Mārgaḻi, the regional styles, and the surprising mathematics inside them.

WHERE WE ARE

Walk down a South Indian street at dawn and you will see it: in front of almost every door, freshly swept and sprinkled with water, a white pattern of dots and looping lines. It is called kolam in Tamil, rangoli or rangavalli in Kannada, muggu in Telugu, and it is drawn every single morning, usually by the women of the house.

By evening it is scuffed, walked on and half eaten by ants. Tomorrow it will be drawn again. This guide explains why.

AT THE THRESHOLD

The daily ritual

Sweep and sprinkle. The day starts by sweeping the doorstep and sprinkling it with water, often mixed with cow-dung in villages, which settles the dust and is held to purify.

Draw. Then, with a pinch of rice flour held between thumb and finger, the design flows out in a thin line: first a grid of dots, then lines looping round them.

Before sunrise. Traditionally it is finished before the sun comes up, before anyone leaves or arrives, so the house greets the day prepared.

Gone by evening. Feet, wind and ants wear it away. Tomorrow it is drawn afresh, never quite the same.

ZAPPY: Every single day?

PROF. BOLT: In many homes, yes, Zappy, for generations.

A fresh start every dawn.

WHY RICE FLOUR?

Feeding a thousand souls

Food for small creatures. Rice flour feeds ants, insects and birds. Feeding other beings every day is one of the householder’s five daily duties (pañca mahāyajña): to gods, sages, ancestors, people and creatures (bhūta yajña).

A daily act of kindness. Grandmothers often say the kolam is “so the ants don’t go hungry”. The whole family’s food is shared, a little, with the smallest neighbours.

Chalk is not the same. White stone powder is sometimes used because it lasts longer, but rice flour is the traditional choice for this reason.

Impermanence. Because it is eaten and worn away, the kolam teaches that beauty need not last to be worth making. (Interpretation.)

ZAPPY: So the ants get breakfast first!

PROF. BOLT: Before you do, Zappy. That’s good manners in the old books.

Share with the smallest.

A WELCOME FOR LAKṢMĪ

The threshold as sacred

Lakṣmī comes where it is clean and beautiful. A swept, decorated doorstep is a welcome for Lakṣmī, goddess of prosperity; an untidy one, it is said, sends her away.

Guarding the threshold. The threshold (hosilu in Kannada) is a boundary between outside and inside. The closed lines of the kolam are traditionally said to keep evil out.

A signal. A missing kolam can tell neighbours something: in many communities none is drawn in a house mourning a death.

Festival kolams. Larger designs, colours, lamps and footprints of Lakṣmī walking in appear for Varamahālakṣmī, Dīpāvalī and weddings.

ZAPPY: No kolam means something is wrong?

PROF. BOLT: In many homes, Zappy. So neighbours notice and come to help.

Beauty invites blessing.

KINDS OF KOLAM

Dots, lines and colours

⚫ Puḷḷi kolam

A grid of dots, connected or circled by lines. Grids grow from 3 × 3 to huge 21 × 21 designs.

➰ Sikku / kambi kolam

Lines that weave around the dots without touching them, often a single unbroken loop that returns to its start.

🌸 Free designs

Flowers, lamps, conch and wheel, peacocks: drawn freehand, especially on festival days.

🎨 Colour rangoli

Bright coloured powders, more common for festivals and in North and West India.

ZAPPY: Which is hardest?

PROF. BOLT: The single unbroken loop round a big dot grid, Zappy. Experts plan it in their heads.

One line, no end.

MĀRGAḺI KOLAMS

The great season

❄️ The month

Mārgaḻi (December–January), the month of Dhanurmāsa, is the great kolam season.

🏁 Street contests

Young women traditionally competed to draw huge kolams across the village street; colleges and towns still hold kolam contests.

🌼 A flower on top

In many homes a pumpkin flower set in cow-dung is placed on the kolam each Mārgaḻi morning.

🎶 With Tiruppāvai

The dawn kolam goes with Āṇḍāḷ’s songs; see Tiruppāvai.

ZAPPY: A flower in the middle of the kolam?

PROF. BOLT: In Tamil homes especially, Zappy. It’s Mārgaḻi’s signature.

The kolam month.

ACROSS INDIA

Many names, one idea

🟫 South

Kolam in Tamil Nadu, rangavalli in Karnataka, muggu in Andhra and Telangana: mostly white rice flour, drawn daily.

🌈 West

Rangoli in Maharashtra and Gujarat: bright colours, especially at Dīpāvalī.

🌼 Kerala

The pookalam, a circle of flowers, made for Onam.

⚪ East and North

Alpona in Bengal and aripan in Mithila, made with rice paste, often on walls and floors for festivals.

ZAPPY: Kerala uses flowers instead of flour!

PROF. BOLT: For Onam, Zappy. The idea is the same: beauty to welcome a guest.

Many names, one welcome.

THE MATHS OF KOLAM

A pattern-language

Rules without books. Women learn kolam from mothers and neighbours, by eye and hand, yet the designs follow strict rules of symmetry and closure.

One line. Many sikku kolams are a single closed curve that passes every dot exactly as required: a problem mathematicians study in graph theory.

Studied by science. Researchers have analysed kolams’ mathematical properties and used them in computer algorithms and pattern generation.

Memory and skill. Holding a 15 × 15 design in mind while drawing it smoothly is real mental training. (A common observation.)

ZAPPY: Grandma is a mathematician!

PROF. BOLT: A pattern genius, Zappy, without a single equation.

Art and maths together.

DRAW YOUR FIRST KOLAM

A 3 × 3 to begin

Dots. Place nine dots in a 3 × 3 square, evenly spaced, with chalk or rice flour.

Loop. Draw a diamond-shaped loop that circles all nine dots without touching any; keep the line smooth.

Add petals. Draw small loops round the corner dots. You now have a simple flower kolam.

Practise. Try a 5 × 5 next. Draw it each morning for a week and it will start to flow.

ZAPPY: Can boys draw kolam too?

PROF. BOLT: Of course, Zappy. Anyone with a pinch of flour and patience.

Begin with nine dots.
